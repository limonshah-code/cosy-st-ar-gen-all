## Discover the Stunning Appearance of a Long Haired German Shepherd

Imagine a loyal and powerful dog with a magnificent, flowing coat. This is often the image of a long haired German Shepherd. These beautiful dogs capture hearts with their striking appearance. They possess a distinctive look that sets them apart from their more commonly seen short-haired relatives. People often wonder about the specific traits that define a long haired German Shepherd. This article will explain exactly what a long haired German Shepherd looks like. We will cover their unique coat, body structure, common colors, and more. Join me as we explore the captivating beauty of these impressive canines.

### Takeaway:

*   Long haired German Shepherds have a luxurious, flowing outer coat and a dense undercoat, giving them a distinct fluffy appearance.
*   They share the classic German Shepherd body shape but appear larger due to their voluminous fur.
*   Grooming is essential to maintain their coat and prevent matting.
*   Their temperament is similar to standard German Shepherds: intelligent, loyal, and protective.

**What does a long haired German Shepherd look like?** A long haired German Shepherd displays a distinctive, flowing coat that covers its body, including feathering on the ears, legs, and tail. Their fur is noticeably longer than the standard type, giving them a grand and impressive silhouette while maintaining the classic German Shepherd build and alert expression.

### The Distinctive Appearance of a Long Haired German Shepherd

A long haired German Shepherd possesses a truly regal look. Their most striking feature is, of course, their coat. This coat is not just long; it flows gracefully, giving the dog an almost majestic presence. I find their luxurious fur adds to their powerful outline. This is quite different from the sleek appearance of their short-haired cousins.

#### Coat Characteristics and Texture

The long haired German Shepherd has a coat that is soft and often slightly wavy. It can be quite thick and dense. The hair extends significantly beyond the body. It forms beautiful feathering on the back of their legs, behind their ears, and along their belly. Their tails are particularly fluffy, resembling a plume or a brush. This length of hair is what truly defines their appearance. It creates a striking visual difference compared to other German Shepherds.

#### Body Structure and Proportions

Beneath all that beautiful fur, the long haired German Shepherd retains the classic German Shepherd build. They have a strong, muscular body. Their frame is athletic and well-proportioned. They typically have a deep chest and a straight back, though some lines may show a slight slope. Their powerful legs allow for agility and speed. The long hair makes them appear larger and more substantial than they actually are. This contributes to their commanding presence.

### Understanding the German Shepherd Coat Types

German Shepherds come in different coat types. The most common is the standard, medium-length coat. There are also long-haired varieties. These differences come from genetics. A specific gene determines coat length in German Shepherds.

#### Standard vs. Long Coat German Shepherds

Standard German Shepherds have a medium-length double coat. The outer coat is dense and straight. It lies close to the body. They have a softer, thick undercoat. This protects them from various weather conditions.

Long haired German Shepherds have a significantly longer outer coat. This coat can be soft or slightly harsh. They usually have a dense undercoat as well. Sometimes, a long-haired German Shepherd might lack an undercoat. This can affect their ability to tolerate extreme weather. You can learn more about identifying coat types by asking [how do I know if my German Shepherd is long-haired](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-do-i-know-if-my-german-shepherd-is-long-haired).

#### The Recessive Gene Explained

The long coat in German Shepherds is due to a recessive gene. This means a puppy needs to inherit the gene from both parents to have long hair. If a puppy gets only one copy, it will have a standard coat but can still pass the long-hair gene to its offspring. This genetic trait explains why some litters have both long and short-haired puppies. If your German Shepherd does not have long hair, it simply means it did not inherit two copies of this recessive gene. ### Color Variations and Markings in Long Haired GSDs

Long haired German Shepherds come in a wide range of colors. The long hair can make their colors appear richer or slightly muted. Their markings are usually distinct and well-defined. I often admire the varied palette they display.

#### Classic Black and Tan

The black and tan pattern is the most famous for German Shepherds. This color combination is also very common in long haired varieties. The black typically covers the back, often extending to the neck and tail. Tan or reddish-brown colors appear on the legs, chest, and sometimes parts of the face. The contrast between these two colors is striking. The long hair enhances this contrast, making the dog look even more impressive.

#### Sable and Bi-Color Varieties

Sable is another common and beautiful color for long haired German Shepherds. Sable coats have individual hairs with multiple color bands. This gives the coat a grizzled or shaded appearance. The overall look can range from light silver sable to dark black sable. Bi-color German Shepherds are mostly black with tan markings on their lower legs, chest, and under their tail. The black covers most of their body. The long fur makes the transition between colors look very soft and flowing.

#### Solid Colors and Rarities

While less common, long haired German Shepherds can also come in solid colors. Solid black is one such example. These dogs have a deep, uniform black coat from head to tail. White German Shepherds also exist, but they are a separate variation. Their white color is due to a different gene. Sometimes, blue or liver-colored German Shepherds appear. These colors are rarer and considered faults in some breed standards. Their long coats make these rare colors even more unique.

### Facial Features and Expressive Eyes of Long Haired German Shepherds

The face of a long haired German Shepherd is intelligent and noble. Their facial features contribute greatly to their overall look. They have a very expressive gaze. I always feel drawn to their alert eyes.

#### Head and Muzzle Shape

Long haired German Shepherds possess a strong, well-defined head. Their skull is typically broad, tapering slightly towards the muzzle. The muzzle is long and powerful, designed for gripping. Their nose is always black. The long hair around their face, particularly on their cheeks and behind their ears, can make their head appear slightly wider. This framing of the face by their fur adds to their appealing appearance.

#### Alert Ears and Expressive Eyes

Their ears are usually erect and pointed. They sit high on the head. These ears are typically covered with longer hair, especially on the edges. This gives them a slightly feathered look. The ears swivel, showing the dog's attentiveness. Their eyes are almond-shaped and dark. They convey intelligence, loyalty, and a keen awareness of their surroundings. The dark eyes against their varied coat colors create a captivating contrast. This makes their expressions very clear and engaging.

### Size, Weight, and Proportions of Long Coat GSDs

Long haired German Shepherds generally conform to the standard size guidelines for the breed. Their long coat can make them appear larger. However, their actual bone structure and muscle mass are similar. Knowing their typical measurements helps in understanding their build.

#### Male vs. Female Size Differences

Male long haired German Shepherds are typically larger than females. Males usually stand between 24 to 26 inches tall at the shoulder. They weigh between 65 to 90 pounds. Females are slightly smaller. They usually measure 22 to 24 inches tall. Their weight ranges from 50 to 70 pounds. These ranges are typical for healthy adults. The long fur gives them an imposing presence. Their size, combined with their flowing coat, creates a powerful visual. ### Grooming Needs for a Lush Long Haired German Shepherd Coat

A long haired German Shepherd's beautiful coat requires regular care. This is crucial for their health and appearance. Proper grooming keeps their fur free of mats and tangles. It also helps manage shedding. I know from experience that a well-groomed coat is a happy coat.

#### Brushing and Detangling

Daily brushing is often necessary for a long haired German Shepherd. This prevents mats from forming, especially behind the ears, under the legs, and on the tail. A slicker brush and a long-toothed comb work best. Brushing removes loose hair and dirt. It also distributes natural oils, keeping the coat shiny. My dog loves the attention during brushing. Regular detangling prevents painful knots from forming deep within the fur.

#### Managing Seasonal Shedding

Like all German Shepherds, long haired ones shed. They typically shed heavily twice a year, during spring and fall. This is when they "blow their coat" to adapt to changing temperatures. During these periods, brushing should increase. Daily brushing, sometimes twice a day, helps remove the vast amounts of loose fur. This reduces hair around your home. It also prevents skin irritation for the dog. Regular grooming helps keep their coat healthy and beautiful year-round.

### Temperament and Personality of Long Haired German Shepherds

Beyond their striking looks, long haired German Shepherds share the same wonderful temperament as their short-haired counterparts. They are known for their intelligence, loyalty, and protective nature. These traits make them excellent companions and working dogs. I find their dedication truly admirable.

#### Loyalty and Protectiveness

Long haired German Shepherds are incredibly loyal to their families. They form strong bonds with their owners. This loyalty often translates into a protective instinct. They are watchful and will alert their family to anything unusual. They can be wary of strangers at first. Once they accept someone, they are usually friendly. This protective nature makes them great guardians for a home.

#### Intelligence and Trainability

These dogs are highly intelligent. They are eager to please their owners. This combination makes them very trainable. They excel in obedience training, agility, and other dog sports. They thrive on having a job to do. Mental stimulation is just as important as physical exercise for them. I enjoy teaching mine new commands. Early socialization and consistent training are key to raising a well-behaved and balanced long haired German Shepherd.

### Distinguishing Long Haired German Shepherds from Similar Breeds

Sometimes, people confuse long haired German Shepherds with other breeds. Their similar appearance can be misleading. It is important to know the key differences. This ensures correct identification.

Some people might confuse a long haired German Shepherd with a King Shepherd. While they share some ancestry and looks, they are distinct breeds. A King Shepherd is generally much larger and heavier. Their coat can also be longer and thicker. The King Shepherd is a relatively new breed. It was developed in the United States. It mixes German Shepherds, Alaskan Malamutes, and Great Pyrenees. However, their body structure is often lighter. Their heads are usually more refined. Belgian Shepherds typically carry their tails lower. Their overall build is more graceful compared to the robust German Shepherd. They also have different ear sets and facial expressions. The Belgian Shepherd has a finer bone structure. Their temperament can also differ slightly. Knowing these subtle differences helps tell them apart. ### FAQ Section

#### Are long-haired German Shepherds rare?
Long haired German Shepherds are less common than standard coat German Shepherds. However, they are not rare. The gene for long hair is recessive, meaning both parents must carry it to produce long-haired offspring. Breeders who specifically aim for long coats often produce them. They are increasing in popularity among enthusiasts.

#### Do long-haired German Shepherds shed more?
Long haired German Shepherds do shed a lot, especially during seasonal coat changes. Their longer hair often makes the shedding more noticeable than with short-haired varieties. Despite the length, the amount of hair shed is comparable to other German Shepherds in terms of volume. Regular grooming helps manage this shedding.

#### Are long-haired German Shepherds recognized by the AKC?
The American Kennel Club (AKC) only recognizes the standard, medium-length coat for German Shepherds. They consider the long coat a disqualifying fault in the show ring. However, long-haired German Shepherds are recognized as a distinct variety in some other countries, like Germany.

#### Are long-haired German Shepherds healthier?
There is no scientific evidence suggesting long haired German Shepherds are inherently healthier or less healthy than standard coated ones. Their health issues are similar to all German Shepherds. This includes concerns like hip and elbow dysplasia. Their coat length does not impact general health. Good breeding practices are crucial for health.

#### How can you tell if a German Shepherd puppy is long-haired?
You can usually tell if a German Shepherd puppy is long-haired by a few weeks of age. Look for noticeably longer fur around their ears, on their legs (feathering), and especially on their tail, which often looks very fluffy. The hair on their body will also be softer and have more volume. For a detailed guide on identifying long hair in puppies, you can refer to [how can you tell if a German Shepherd puppy is long-haired](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-can-you-tell-if-a-german-shepherd-puppy-is-long-haired).

#### What is a plush coat German Shepherd?
A plush coat German Shepherd has a coat that is longer than the standard coat but shorter than a true long coat. It has a very dense undercoat, giving it a soft, plush feel and a fuller appearance. It’s often considered an intermediate length between the two extremes.
