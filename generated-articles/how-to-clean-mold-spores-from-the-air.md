## Understanding Airborne Mold Spores and Their Impact

Mold is a type of fungus. It grows in damp, warm environments. Mold reproduces by releasing tiny spores into the air. These spores are invisible to the naked eye. They float freely and can land anywhere.

Once mold spores land on a damp surface, they can start a new mold colony. This cycle makes mold a persistent problem. My goal is to help you break this cycle. You need to understand how these spores behave.

### What are Mold Spores?

Mold spores are reproductive units. They are similar to seeds for plants. Each spore is incredibly small, often between 2 to 100 microns. You cannot see them without a microscope. This tiny size allows them to spread easily.

Spores become airborne with even slight air currents. Walking across a room can stir them up. Opening a door or window also moves them. This means mold from one area can quickly affect your entire home.

### Health Effects of Airborne Mold

Exposure to mold spores can cause various health problems. Many people experience allergic reactions. These include sneezing, runny nose, red eyes, and skin rash. Asthma sufferers might experience worsened symptoms.

Some mold types produce mycotoxins. These are toxic compounds. Exposure to mycotoxins can lead to more severe health issues. These include neurological problems or respiratory distress. It is important to address mold quickly. I want your home to be safe for everyone.
