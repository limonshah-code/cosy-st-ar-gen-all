## The Signature Coat Colors and Patterns of Full Grown Australian Shepherds

The coat of a full grown Aussie is arguably its most defining feature. It is striking and comes in many variations. This double coat serves a functional purpose. It also gives the Aussie its distinct beauty. I am always amazed by the range of colors and patterns. Each one seems to tell a unique story.

Their coat is medium length. It can be straight or wavy. The texture feels coarse to the touch. This helps shed water and dirt. Underneath, there is a soft, dense undercoat. This provides warmth in cold weather. It also helps cool them in hot weather.

### Primary Coat Colors in Full Grown Aussies

Aussies come in four primary coat colors. These are black, blue merle, red, and red merle. Each color has its own charm.
*   **Black:** Solid black Aussies have rich, dark coats. They often have white or tan markings. These markings appear on the face, chest, and legs.
*   **Red:** Red Aussies range from a light cinnamon to a deep liver color. Like black Aussies, they usually have white or tan markings.
*   **Blue Merle:** This pattern is complex. It involves patches of black on a gray base. These patches can vary in size and shape. The gray areas often look mottled. White and tan markings usually accompany the blue merle pattern.
*   **Red Merle:** Similar to blue merle, red merle shows patches of red on a lighter, sometimes pinkish, base. The red patches can be various shades. White and tan markings are also common.

These four colors form the basis of all Aussie coats. They are recognized by breed standards. I particularly love the merle patterns. They are truly unique to each dog.

### Understanding Merle Patterns and Markings

Merle is a genetic pattern. It dilutes the base color in irregular patches. This creates a mottled, marbled effect. No two merle Aussies look exactly alike. This makes each one special. White markings are common. They appear on the muzzle, forehead, chest, and legs. They must not exceed a certain percentage of the body. Excessive white can indicate health issues, especially around the ears and eyes.

Tan markings are also common. They appear as copper points. These are usually on the cheeks, over the eyes, on the legs, and around the vent. These markings enhance the dog's expression. They add depth to the coat. Understanding these patterns helps in identifying a true full grown Aussie. It also highlights the breed's diverse genetics.
