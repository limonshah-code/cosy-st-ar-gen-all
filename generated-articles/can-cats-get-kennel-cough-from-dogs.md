## Understanding Kennel Cough in Dogs

Kennel cough is a highly contagious respiratory disease in dogs. It causes inflammation of the windpipe and voice box. The common name comes from how easily it spreads in places where dogs gather. This includes kennels, dog parks, and training classes.

The disease typically involves several pathogens. The main one is the bacterium *Bordetella bronchiseptica*. Viruses like parainfluenza virus and adenovirus also contribute. These agents attack the respiratory lining. They cause irritation and lead to a persistent cough.

Dogs usually catch kennel cough through airborne droplets. An infected dog coughs or sneezes. These droplets then spread to other dogs. Direct contact with infected surfaces also transmits the disease. Other signs include sneezing, a runny nose, and eye discharge. Transmission usually occurs through direct contact. An infected dog might sneeze or cough. The bacteria then spread through the air in tiny droplets. Cats can inhale these airborne particles. Sharing food bowls, water bowls, or bedding can also facilitate spread. This is especially true in homes where dogs and cats live closely.

Cats infected with *Bordetella* often show different symptoms than dogs. While dogs typically present with a harsh, dry cough, cats may have a more subtle illness. A1: Kennel cough in cats, caused by *Bordetella bronchiseptica*, can range from mild to severe. Most healthy adult cats experience mild upper respiratory symptoms. However, kittens, older cats, or those with weakened immune systems can develop serious conditions like pneumonia. Always consult a vet if your cat shows signs of illness.

**Q2: Can vaccinated dogs still spread kennel cough to cats?**
A2: Yes, vaccinated dogs can still potentially spread *Bordetella bronchiseptica*. The kennel cough vaccine for dogs reduces the severity of symptoms and the likelihood of infection. It does not always prevent infection or shedding of the bacteria. Therefore, a vaccinated dog might still carry and transmit the bacteria to a cat.

**Q3: How long does kennel cough last in cats?**
A3: The duration of *Bordetella* infection in cats varies. Mild cases might resolve in 7-10 days with supportive care. More severe infections, especially those requiring antibiotics, might take several weeks to clear completely. Cats can also shed the bacteria for weeks after symptoms disappear.
