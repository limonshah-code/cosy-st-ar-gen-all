## FAQ Section

### Q1: What is the safest concentration of hydrogen peroxide to buy for general household use?

The safest and most common concentration for general household use is 3% hydrogen peroxide. This solution is widely available in pharmacies and supermarkets. It is effective for minor first aid, surface cleaning, and laundry tasks. Always follow label directions for safe application. Avoid contact with eyes and prolonged skin exposure.

### Q2: Can I use hydrogen peroxide for cleaning all surfaces?

No, you cannot use hydrogen peroxide on all surfaces. While it is great for many, avoid using it on certain materials. This includes marble, granite, or some delicate fabrics. Test a small, inconspicuous area first. This helps to prevent discoloration or damage. Always check the manufacturer's recommendations for your specific surfaces.

### Q3: Is food-grade hydrogen peroxide different from regular hydrogen peroxide?

Yes, food-grade hydrogen peroxide is different. It undergoes a more rigorous purification process. This removes stabilizers and contaminants present in standard solutions. It is sold in higher concentrations (e.g., 35%). People use it for specific purposes like sanitizing food contact surfaces or water treatment, always with proper dilution. It is not for direct consumption or typical household use without extensive dilution and understanding.

### Q4: How should I store hydrogen peroxide safely at home?

Store hydrogen peroxide in its original, opaque bottle. Keep it in a cool, dark place. A medicine cabinet or pantry shelf works well. Avoid direct sunlight or heat, as these cause it to break down. Keep the cap tightly sealed. Store it out of reach of children and pets. Proper storage ensures its effectiveness and safety.

### Q5: Does hydrogen peroxide expire?

Yes, hydrogen peroxide does expire. Over time, it naturally breaks down into water and oxygen. The expiration date on the bottle indicates its maximum potency. Once opened, its effectiveness can decrease more quickly. Most 3% solutions remain effective for about six months to a year after opening. If it stops fizzing, it has likely lost its potency.

### Q6: Can hydrogen peroxide be used to whiten teeth?

Some people use very diluted hydrogen peroxide solutions for teeth whitening. However, dentists generally advise caution. Excessive or improper use can damage tooth enamel or irritate gums. It is best to consult your dentist before using hydrogen peroxide for oral care. They can recommend safe and effective whitening methods.
