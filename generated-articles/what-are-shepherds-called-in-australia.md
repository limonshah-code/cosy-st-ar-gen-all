## The Historical "Alsatian" Moniker and Its Origins

The term "Alsatian" has a significant place in the history of **shepherds called in Australia**. During and after the World Wars, anti-German sentiment was strong globally. To avoid association with Germany, many countries changed the breed's name. They called the German Shepherd Dog the "Alsatian Wolf Dog" or simply "Alsatian."

This name gained traction in Australia. It was a way to distance the breed from its German roots during sensitive times. Breeders and kennel clubs adopted this name for several decades. My grandparents sometimes still refer to them as Alsatians. This shows how deeply the name became ingrained.

The "Alsatian" name refers to the Alsace region. This area lies on the border of France and Germany. It served as a neutral geographical reference. The change was purely political and did not affect the dog's genetics. The dogs themselves remained the same. Their temperament and appearance were unchanged.

After decades, the German Shepherd Dog name began to regain favor. This happened as geopolitical tensions eased. Today, the ANKC and most breeders use "German Shepherd Dog." However, older generations might still use "Alsatian." You might encounter this name if you chat with long-time dog enthusiasts. It's a reminder of a bygone era. ## Beyond the Breed: Human Shepherds and Their Terminology

While the German Shepherd dog breed is often implied, the term "shepherd" also refers to people. These are individuals who care for sheep. In Australia, the terminology for human **shepherds called in Australia** can be more diverse than just "shepherd." This reflects the vastness of the land and the scale of farming.

On smaller properties, a person tending sheep might simply be called a shepherd. This traditional term is still in use. However, on larger stations or ranches, other terms are common. "Stockman" is a broad term for someone working with livestock. This includes sheep, cattle, and horses. A stockman is skilled in horsemanship and animal husbandry.

Another specific term is "jackaroo" for a young male trainee. A "jillaroo" is the female equivalent. These individuals learn the ropes of station life. They work under experienced stockmen and station managers. They gain practical skills in herding, mustering, and general farm work. These roles are integral to Australia's agricultural sector.

These human shepherds often rely on working dogs. Breeds like Australian Kelpies and Border Collies are popular. These dogs are essential tools for moving large mobs of sheep. The human-dog partnership is crucial for efficient livestock management. So, while we often think of the dog breed, the human role of a shepherd is also a vital part of Australia's rural heritage.
