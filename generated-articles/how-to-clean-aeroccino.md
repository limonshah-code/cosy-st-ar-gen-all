## How To Clean Your Aeroccino for Perfect Froth

Do you love creamy milk froth for your coffee? The Aeroccino makes it easy. But what happens when dried milk leaves residue? Cleaning this device keeps your drinks tasting fresh. It also helps your frother last longer.

Learning how to clean Aeroccino models properly is simple. I want to help you keep your frother in top condition. This article provides clear steps for both daily and deep cleaning. You will learn how to handle stubborn milk stains and maintain all parts. We will cover everything you need to know for a clean Aeroccino.

### Takeaway: Keep Your Aeroccino Clean and Functional

*   Rinse immediately after each use.
*   Wipe the interior with a soft, damp cloth.
*   Disassemble the whisk and lid for thorough washing.
*   Address stubborn residue with gentle cleaning agents.
*   Follow manufacturer guidelines for deep cleaning to ensure longevity.

Cleaning an Aeroccino involves quick rinsing after each use and a deeper wash when needed. Gently wipe the interior with a soft cloth and mild soap. Rinse all parts completely and let them air dry. This prevents milk buildup and keeps your frother working well.

### Daily Cleaning Steps for Your Aeroccino

Daily cleaning keeps your Aeroccino ready for its next use. It is a quick and simple process. I recommend doing it right after you finish making your drink. This prevents milk from drying inside. Dried milk is much harder to remove.

First, unplug the Aeroccino from the power base. Never immerse the base in water. The electrical components are sensitive. You want to handle it with care. This step ensures safety during cleaning.

Immediately after emptying the frother, rinse the inside with cold water. Cold water is better for milk proteins. Hot water can cook milk onto the surface. This makes it stick even more. A quick rinse helps dislodge loose milk.

Next, use a soft, damp cloth or sponge. Wipe the interior of the jug. Pay attention to the bottom and sides. Remove any remaining milk film. You can add a tiny drop of mild dish soap if needed. Rinse the jug thoroughly after using soap.

Do not use abrasive sponges or cleaners. These can scratch the non-stick coating. Scratches can lead to milk sticking more easily. They also make the frother harder to clean in the future. Always be gentle with the interior.

Finally, dry the inside of the jug. A soft towel or paper towel works well. You can also let it air dry completely. Make sure all parts are dry before storing. This prevents water spots and any moisture-related issues. Following these steps daily keeps your frother in top shape.

### Deep Cleaning Your Aeroccino for Longevity

Sometimes, daily cleaning is not enough. You might notice a film or some residue building up. This is when deep cleaning your Aeroccino becomes important. I usually deep clean mine once a week or every two weeks. This helps to keep it looking and working like new.

To start, unplug the Aeroccino. Remove the lid and the whisk. These parts need individual attention. You can wash the lid and whisk in warm, soapy water. Use a small brush to get into crevices of the whisk. Make sure to rinse them thoroughly.

For the jug itself, fill it with warm water and a small amount of mild dish soap. Let it soak for about 10-15 minutes. This helps loosen any stuck-on milk. Soaking is an effective way to break down residue. It makes scrubbing much easier.

After soaking, use a soft bottle brush or a non-abrasive sponge. Gently scrub the inside of the jug. Pay close attention to the heating element area at the bottom. This is where milk often burns or sticks. A gentle scrub removes most residue.

Once clean, rinse the jug several times with fresh water. Make sure no soap residue remains. Any leftover soap can affect the taste of your milk. I often use warm water for rinsing. It helps to dissolve any remaining soap film.

Finally, dry all parts completely. You can use a soft cloth or air dry them on a dish rack. Ensure the frother is fully dry before putting it back together. ### Handling Stubborn Milk Residue and Stains

Stubborn milk residue can be frustrating. Sometimes, despite regular cleaning, milk might burn onto the bottom. This often happens if the milk is left in the frother for too long after use. I have dealt with this many times myself. But do not worry, there are gentle ways to remove it.

One effective method is a baking soda paste. Mix a small amount of baking soda with water to form a thick paste. Apply this paste directly onto the stubborn stains. Let it sit for about 15-20 minutes. Baking soda is a natural abrasive that is gentle.

After letting it sit, use a soft brush or non-abrasive sponge. Gently scrub the affected area. The baking soda helps lift the burnt milk. Rinse thoroughly with water. Repeat if necessary. This method works well without damaging the coating.

Another option is to use white vinegar. Fill the Aeroccino with a solution of half water and half white vinegar. Let it sit for about 30 minutes to an hour. Vinegar is acidic and helps break down milk proteins. It is also good for removing odors.

After soaking with vinegar, empty the solution. Scrub lightly with a soft brush. Rinse the frother several times to remove all vinegar smell. You can also boil plain water in the frother afterward. Always avoid harsh chemicals or metal scrubbers. These can permanently damage the non-stick interior. Patience and gentle cleaning agents are best for stubborn stains. A little effort can bring your Aeroccino back to its clean state.

### Cleaning Aeroccino Parts: Whisk and Lid Care

The small parts of your Aeroccino also need attention. The whisk and lid are crucial for proper frothing. Milk can hide in tiny spaces. Overlooking these parts can lead to hygiene issues. I always make sure these get a good wash.

The whisk is simple to clean. It detaches easily from the bottom of the Aeroccino. After each use, rinse the whisk under running water. Use your fingers to rub away any milk film. If milk has dried on it, let it soak in warm, soapy water for a few minutes.

For dried milk inside the coil of the whisk, use a small brush. A bottle brush or even an old toothbrush works well. Gently scrub the coils to remove all residue. Make sure to rinse the whisk thoroughly afterward. It is important that no soap remains on it.

The lid also collects milk splashes. Wipe the lid with a damp cloth after each use. Pay attention to the rubber seal. Milk can get trapped under the seal. Remove the rubber seal if it comes off easily. Wash it separately with warm, soapy water.

When cleaning the lid, check the small vent hole. This hole prevents pressure buildup. Use a toothpick or a small brush to clear it if it looks blocked. A clear vent helps the frother work correctly. ### Descaling Your Aeroccino (If Needed)

Descaling is a cleaning process that removes mineral deposits. This is usually more relevant for appliances that boil water. Think about kettles or coffee machines. For an Aeroccino, descaling is rarely needed. It only heats milk, not water directly. However, if you use very hard water for rinsing, mineral buildup might occur.

You might notice a white, chalky film inside the jug. This is scale. It is from minerals present in your tap water. While uncommon, it can affect performance. If you see this, a gentle descaling process can help.

To descale, you can use a diluted vinegar solution. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water. Fill the Aeroccino jug with this solution up to the maximum fill line. Let it sit for about 30 minutes. Do not heat the solution in the frother.

After soaking, empty the vinegar solution. Use a soft sponge or cloth to wipe the interior. The scale should come off easily. Rinse the frother thoroughly with fresh water. Make sure no vinegar smell remains. You might need to rinse it several times.

You can also run a cycle with plain water afterward. This helps remove any residual vinegar taste. Do not use commercial descaling agents meant for coffee machines. These can be too harsh for the Aeroccino's coating. Always use mild solutions.

Descaling is a specific type of cleaning. It targets mineral deposits. For most Aeroccino users, daily and deep cleaning is enough. Only descale if you clearly see scale buildup. This keeps your Aeroccino in optimal condition.

### FAQ Section

**How often should I clean my Aeroccino?**
You should rinse your Aeroccino immediately after every use. A more thorough deep cleaning, including the whisk and lid, should happen at least once a week. This prevents milk residue from building up. Regular cleaning keeps your frother hygienic and effective.

**Can I put my Aeroccino in the dishwasher?**
No, you should never put the Aeroccino jug or its power base in the dishwasher. The high heat and harsh detergents can damage the non-stick coating and electrical components. Always hand wash the jug and its parts with mild soap and water.

**What kind of soap should I use to clean my Aeroccino?**
Use a small amount of mild dish soap for cleaning your Aeroccino. Avoid abrasive cleaners or strong chemicals. These can scratch the interior surface or leave harmful residues. Gentle dish soap is effective for removing milk fats and proteins.

**How do I remove burnt milk from the bottom?**
For burnt milk, make a paste of baking soda and water. Apply it to the stain and let it sit for 15-20 minutes. Then, gently scrub with a soft sponge. Alternatively, soak with a half water, half white vinegar solution for 30 minutes. Rinse thoroughly afterward.

**Is it safe to use vinegar to clean my Aeroccino?**
Yes, white vinegar is safe for cleaning your Aeroccino. It helps break down milk proteins and can remove scale. Always dilute vinegar with water (50/50 ratio) and do not heat it in the frother. Rinse the frother thoroughly after using vinegar to remove all scent.

**What if my Aeroccino stops frothing milk properly?**
If your Aeroccino stops frothing, first perform a deep clean. Check the whisk for any blockages or damage. Ensure the whisk is correctly attached. Sometimes, old milk residue affects performance. If issues continue, consult the Nespresso user manual or customer support.
