### What percentage of Aussies are born without a tail?

A small percentage of Australian Shepherds are naturally born without a tail, or with a very short one. This is due to a specific genetic mutation, known as the Natural Bobtail (NBT) gene. Estimates suggest around 10% to 20% of Aussies naturally exhibit this trait. This is distinct from tail docking, which is a surgical procedure performed shortly after birth.

### The Natural Bobtail Phenomenon in Australian Shepherds

The sight of an Australian Shepherd with a short or non-existent tail is common. This appearance can be the result of two different origins. One origin is a natural genetic trait, while the other is a human intervention. I find this distinction very important for understanding the breed fully.

A segment of the Australian Shepherd population carries a gene causing them to be born with a naturally bobbed tail. This means they do not have a full-length tail at birth. This genetic predisposition is fascinating and unique to certain breeds. It sets these dogs apart from those whose tails are shortened manually. This natural occurrence influences breeding decisions.

The gene responsible for natural bobtails is called the Natural Bobtail (NBT) gene. It causes the tail to be naturally short or completely absent. Breeders must understand this gene. Breeding two dogs with the NBT gene can have serious consequences. This specific gene is dominant. However, having two copies of the gene is lethal for puppies. This means that if a puppy inherits the NBT gene from both parents, it will not survive. This information is crucial for ethical breeding practices.

Therefore, breeders carefully manage matings to avoid this outcome. They will typically breed a natural bobtail Aussie with a full-tailed Aussie. This practice ensures healthy litters. It also maintains the natural bobtail trait in the breed. The natural bobtail is a hallmark of the breed's genetic diversity.

Long tails could get caught in brush or fences. They could also be stepped on by livestock. Such injuries were painful and could become infected. Docking tails was seen as a way to prevent these injuries. It protected the dogs as they performed their demanding duties. ### The Genetics of the Natural Bobtail

Understanding the genetics behind the natural bobtail is crucial for breeders and owners. The Natural Bobtail (NBT) gene is responsible for this trait. This gene is very specific in how it affects tail length. I find this genetic mechanism truly fascinating.

The NBT gene is a dominant gene. This means a dog only needs one copy of the gene to express the natural bobtail trait. If a dog inherits one copy of the NBT gene from either parent, it will have a short or absent tail. If a dog does not inherit the NBT gene, it will have a full-length tail. This is a straightforward genetic principle.

However, there is a critical aspect to this gene. The NBT gene is lethal when homozygous. This means that if a puppy inherits two copies of the NBT gene (one from each parent), it will not survive. These puppies typically resorb in the womb or are stillborn. This makes breeding natural bobtail Aussies more complex. Breeders must be very careful.

To avoid producing puppies with two copies of the NBT gene, responsible breeders never breed two natural bobtail Aussies together. They will always breed a natural bobtail Aussie with a full-tailed Aussie. This ensures that no puppy receives two copies of the gene. It prevents lethal outcomes. This practice also helps maintain genetic diversity within the breed.

Genetic testing can identify carriers of the NBT gene. This allows breeders to make informed decisions about mating pairs. It promotes the health and well-being of future generations. Knowing a dog's genetic makeup ensures responsible breeding. It helps preserve the natural bobtail trait safely.

### FAQ Section

#### Are all Australian Shepherds born with a short tail?
No, not all Australian Shepherds are born with a short tail. Only a small percentage, estimated between 10% and 20%, are born with a natural bobtail (NBT) due to a specific genetic trait. Most Aussies with short tails have undergone tail docking, which is a surgical procedure performed shortly after birth.

#### Is tail docking painful for Australian Shepherd puppies?
Tail docking is a surgical procedure performed on very young puppies, typically when they are only a few days old. While breeders often state puppies feel little pain due to their underdeveloped nervous systems, veterinarians generally agree that puppies do experience discomfort and pain during and after the procedure. Many countries have banned it due to animal welfare concerns.

#### Can a naturally bobtailed Aussie have a full-tailed puppy?
Yes, a naturally bobtailed Australian Shepherd can have full-tailed puppies. Natural bobtails carry only one copy of the NBT gene. If they are bred with a full-tailed Aussie (who carries no NBT gene), approximately half of the puppies will inherit the NBT gene and be natural bobtails, while the other half will have full tails.

#### Does a short tail affect an Aussie's balance or communication?
A dog's tail helps with balance, especially during quick movements. While Aussies with short tails often adapt well, their balance might be slightly different. More significantly, tails are crucial for canine communication. Short-tailed Aussies may rely more on other body language cues to express themselves, potentially leading to misunderstandings with other dogs.

#### How can I tell if an Aussie's short tail is natural or docked?
It can be challenging to tell visually. A natural bobtail usually has a blunt, often knobby end due to the undeveloped vertebrae. A docked tail often appears cleaner cut. Sometimes, a veterinarian can confirm by feeling the bone structure. Genetic testing is the most definitive way to determine if an Aussie has the NBT gene for a natural bobtail.

#### Why do some breeders still dock Aussie tails?
Some breeders continue to dock Aussie tails to adhere to historical breed standards or for traditional reasons, believing it prevents injury in working dogs. However, ethical concerns and bans in many countries are leading to a decline in cosmetic tail docking. Responsible breeders now prioritize genetic health and animal welfare.
