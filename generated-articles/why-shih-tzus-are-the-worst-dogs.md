## Discover Why Shih Tzus Might Be The Worst Dogs

Shih Tzus, with their flowing coats and endearing faces, often charm people. These "lion dogs" from Tibet hold a certain appeal. However, many owners discover real challenges once they bring one home. The idea that Shih Tzus are the worst dogs might sound harsh. Yet, for some, their unique traits create significant difficulties. This article will explore common frustrations associated with owning a Shih Tzu. We will look at their stubbornness, high grooming needs, and prevalent health issues. By understanding these points, you can decide if a Shih Tzu truly fits your life.

### Takeaway

*   Shih Tzus pose significant challenges due to their stubborn nature and house-training difficulties.
*   Their extensive grooming and unique health problems demand constant attention and high costs.
*   Frequent barking and potential for separation anxiety can disrupt quiet homes.

Shih Tzus might be considered the worst dogs by some owners due to a combination of factors. These factors include their challenging house-training tendencies, high grooming demands, frequent barking, and predisposition to specific health conditions. These traits require significant time, effort, and financial investment from their owners.

## Their Stubborn Temperament Makes Training Hard

Shih Tzus have a well-known stubborn streak. This trait makes training them a frustrating experience for many owners. They often decide when they want to obey. This is especially true for basic commands. You may repeat a command multiple times without success. Their independent nature means they prefer doing things their way.

House-training a Shih Tzu is particularly difficult. Many owners report prolonged struggles with potty training. Accidents inside the house remain a common issue well into adulthood. This happens despite consistent effort and positive reinforcement. Their small bladders also contribute to this problem. They need very frequent potty breaks. If you do not offer breaks often enough, they will have accidents. This difficulty means you might frequently need to clean up messes. It can feel like an endless battle for some people. This anxiety stems from their desire for constant companionship. They were bred to be lap dogs. This means they bond very closely with their owners. When you leave them alone, they get distressed. Their distress causes loud and destructive behaviors. This makes leaving them for even short periods challenging. If you are not home often, a Shih Tzu might not be the best choice. ## Endless Grooming Needs and High Maintenance

Shih Tzus have long, flowing coats. These coats are beautiful but require immense upkeep. Daily brushing is not an option; it is a necessity. Their hair tangles easily. Mats form quickly if you neglect brushing. These mats can pull on their skin. This causes discomfort and even skin infections. Professional grooming sessions are also essential. You need these every 4-6 weeks. Each grooming session comes with a cost. This adds up over time.

Their unique facial structure creates specific grooming challenges. Shih Tzus have prominent eyes. These eyes often get watery. This leads to tear stains on their fur below the eyes. Cleaning these stains needs daily attention. If you ignore them, they can harbor bacteria. This causes infections and bad odors. You must gently wipe their faces every day. This prevents buildup and keeps them comfortable. Their long hair around the face also needs regular trimming. This prevents hair from poking their eyes.

Dental health is another major concern. Shih Tzus have small mouths and crowded teeth. This makes them prone to dental disease. Tartar builds up quickly. This leads to gum inflammation and tooth loss. Bad breath is a common sign of dental problems. Regular dental cleanings by a vet are often necessary. These cleanings can be expensive. Daily tooth brushing at home helps. However, many Shih Tzus resist this. The amount of time and money spent on grooming and dental care for a Shih Tzu is significant. Many owners find this level of maintenance overwhelming.

## Significant Health Problems and Vet Bills

Shih Tzus, unfortunately, are prone to a range of health issues. Their unique physical characteristics, while charming, lead to these vulnerabilities. Many of these conditions require ongoing veterinary care. This results in substantial vet bills. Owners must be ready for these financial commitments. Knowing [what health problems do Shih Tzus have](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-health-problems-do-shih-tzu-have) is important before getting one.

One major concern is brachycephalic airway syndrome. This condition affects dogs with short noses and flat faces. Shih Tzus often struggle with breathing. Their narrowed nostrils, elongated soft palates, and collapsed tracheas make breathing difficult. They are very sensitive to heat. Even moderate exercise on warm days can lead to overheating and respiratory distress. In severe cases, surgery is necessary. This surgery helps improve their breathing. This is a serious condition that affects their quality of life. Owners must monitor their Shih Tzus carefully.

Eye problems are also very common in Shih Tzus. Their large, protruding eyes are easily injured. Corneal ulcers, dry eye, and cataracts are frequent issues. Their long facial hair can irritate their eyes. This contributes to these problems. Regular eye checks and vigilant cleaning are essential. Some eye conditions may require expensive medications or surgical interventions. These health challenges contribute to their shorter lifespan. For instance, respiratory issues are a common cause of death. Understanding [what is the leading cause of death in Shih Tzus](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-leading-cause-of-death-in-shih-tzus) highlights these risks. Joint issues, such as patellar luxation, also affect them. These problems cause pain and limit their movement.

## Excessive Barking and Noise Levels

Shih Tzus are known for being quite vocal dogs. Their barking can become a major nuisance for owners and neighbors alike. They often bark at anything new or unfamiliar. This includes people walking by the window or strange sounds outside. They act as tiny alarm systems. While this may seem helpful, their barking is often excessive. This constant noise can be very irritating.

They also engage in "demand barking." This means they bark to get attention or a desired outcome. They might bark when they want food. They may bark when they want to play. Or they bark when they want to sit on your lap. This behavior can be difficult to stop once it starts. It reinforces their belief that barking gets them what they want. If you give in, you teach them to bark more. This requires firm and consistent training. Many owners find it challenging to manage this behavior.

Living with a dog that barks frequently affects your peace. It can make apartment living difficult. Neighbors may complain about the noise. It can also disrupt your own quiet time at home. Some Shih Tzus bark out of boredom or anxiety. This highlights their need for mental stimulation and companionship. If they do not get enough attention, they will find ways to get it. Shih Tzus have a reputation for being notoriously difficult to house-train. This is one of the biggest frustrations for many Shih Tzu owners. It is not uncommon for them to have accidents indoors, even well past puppyhood. Their small size contributes to this challenge. They have very small bladders. This means they need frequent potty breaks. They cannot hold it for long periods. If you are busy or cannot take them out every few hours, accidents are likely.

Their stubborn nature also plays a role in house-training difficulties. They may understand where to go but choose not to. Some Shih Tzus prefer to relieve themselves indoors on soft surfaces. This often includes carpets or rugs. This behavior can be very frustrating to correct. It requires immense patience and consistency from the owner. You must establish a strict routine. Reward them heavily for outdoor successes. Clean indoor accidents thoroughly to remove odors. Lingering odors encourage them to go in the same spot again.

Puppy teething can worsen house-training efforts. During this period, puppies may chew more. They might also show more anxiety. This can lead to more accidents indoors. ## Frequently Asked Questions About Shih Tzus

### Are Shih Tzus really difficult to train?
Yes, many owners find Shih Tzus challenging to train due to their inherent stubbornness and independent nature. They often take longer to grasp commands and house-training concepts. Consistency, patience, and positive reinforcement are vital for success. Expect a longer training period compared to some other breeds.

### What are the worst health problems for Shih Tzus?
Shih Tzus are prone to several significant health issues. These include brachycephalic airway syndrome, which causes breathing difficulties, and various eye conditions such as corneal ulcers and dry eye. They also frequently suffer from dental disease due to their crowded teeth. These conditions can lead to ongoing vet care and high costs.

### Do Shih Tzus shed a lot?
Shih Tzus have a double coat that sheds minimally compared to other breeds. However, their long hair requires extensive daily brushing to prevent tangles and mats. While they are considered a good choice for people with allergies, their grooming needs are very high. This is a trade-off for less shedding.

### Why do Shih Tzus bark so much?
Shih Tzus bark frequently for various reasons. They often bark at strangers or unfamiliar sounds as an alarm. They also use barking to demand attention, food, or playtime. This demand barking can become excessive without proper training and consistent boundaries. Boredom or separation anxiety can also trigger excessive barking.

### Are Shih Tzus good with children?
Shih Tzus can be good with older, respectful children who understand how to interact gently with small dogs. However, due to their delicate size and potential for snappiness if startled or mishandled, they are often not recommended for homes with very young children. Supervision is always important to ensure safety for both the dog and child.

### Is grooming a Shih Tzu very hard?
Grooming a Shih Tzu is demanding and time-consuming. Their long double coat mats easily, requiring daily brushing. They also need professional grooming every 4-6 weeks to prevent painful tangles and skin issues. Their eyes need daily cleaning to prevent tear stains and irritation. This commitment is significant for owners.
