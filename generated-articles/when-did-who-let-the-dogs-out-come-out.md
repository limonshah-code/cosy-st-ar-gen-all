### The Anslem Douglas Connection

Anslem Douglas, a Trinidadian calypso artist, wrote and first performed the song in 1998. His version was titled "Dogie." It gained popularity during the Trinidad and Tobago Carnival. Douglas's version had lyrics with a different meaning. It described men acting inappropriately towards women. The famous "woof woof" line was present.

The Baha Men heard Douglas's song. They decided to cover it. They changed the lyrics to be less explicit and more universally appealing. This adaptation was key to its broader success. Douglas received songwriting credits for the Baha Men's version. This acknowledged his original contribution. His influence is undeniable.

### Miami Hoods and Legal Debates

Before the Baha Men's version, another group, Miami Hoods, also released a version of the song. They claimed their version was the first to use the exact "Who Let The Dogs Out" phrase. This led to some legal disputes over authorship and copyright. These claims complicated the song's origin story further.

Despite these disputes, the Baha Men's rendition became the definitive global hit. Their version propelled the song into the mainstream. The legal battles highlight the complexities of music rights.
