## Understanding Why Shih Tzu Are Perceived as 'Worst' Dogs

Many people adore the Shih Tzu. This small dog often sits on laps and brings joy. Yet, some individuals label Shih Tzus as "the worst dog." This strong statement can confuse new dog owners. It often comes from a place of specific breed challenges. I aim to explore these common issues in detail. We will look at grooming needs, health problems, and training difficulties. This article provides a balanced view. It helps you understand the specific traits of the Shih Tzu breed. It helps you decide if a Shih Tzu fits your life.

### Takeaway

*   **High Grooming Demands:** Shih Tzus require frequent, specialized grooming to prevent matting and maintain eye health.
*   **Specific Health Risks:** The breed faces common health problems like brachycephalic syndrome, eye issues, and dental disease.
*   **Training Can Be Hard:** Shih Tzus are often stubborn, making house-training and obedience challenging for some owners.
*   **Prone to Barking:** They can be vocal, barking at new sounds or when seeking attention.
*   **Delicate Nature:** Their small size makes them prone to injuries, especially around active children.
*   **Companionship Needs:** They thrive on human interaction and may develop separation anxiety if left alone often.
*   **Costly Care:** Long-term costs for grooming and vet visits can be high.

### Clear and Concise Answer

Shih Tzus are considered "worst" by some due to their high grooming needs, common health issues like brachycephalic syndrome, stubbornness in training, and tendency to bark a lot. Their delicate nature and strong need for constant companionship can also challenge owners unprepared for these specific breed traits.

## The High Maintenance Coat: A Grooming Challenge

The beautiful long coat of a Shih Tzu is iconic. This coat is also a major reason why some people find the breed hard to manage. Maintaining the long, flowing hair needs much time and effort. It is not a simple daily brush. This coat easily mats and tangles if not cared for properly. Owners must brush their Shih Tzu every single day. They must use specific tools and techniques. Neglecting this leads to painful mats. These mats pull on the dog's skin.

Professional grooming appointments are also necessary. These can be expensive. Many owners choose a shorter "puppy cut" to make grooming easier. This still requires regular trims. Some owners do not realize the extent of this grooming need before getting a Shih Tzu. This lack of awareness can lead to frustration. It can also lead to poor coat condition for the dog. Regular eye cleaning is also important. Hair around their eyes can irritate them or lead to infections. Maintaining good hygiene prevents many issues.

Keeping a Shih Tzu's coat healthy demands consistency. I have seen many Shih Tzus with beautiful, well-kept coats. I have also seen some with severe matting. The difference often comes down to the owner's commitment to daily care. This includes bathing, brushing, and eye cleaning. Without this commitment, the coat becomes a problem. The Shih Tzu's long hair can also pick up dirt and debris. This means more frequent baths are often needed. Owners must also keep the hair out of the dog's eyes. This prevents irritation and infections. Some owners use topknots or clips for this purpose. The grooming needs are real. They take time and money. This can be a deal-breaker for some potential owners. ## Understanding Shih Tzu Health Problems

Shih Tzus are adorable, but they come with specific health predispositions. Their unique body structure, especially their flat faces, contributes to several health concerns. This is a primary reason why some people consider them challenging. They are a brachycephalic breed. This means they have short snouts and flattened faces. This structure can lead to Brachycephalic Obstructive Airway Syndrome (BOAS). Dogs with BOAS can have trouble breathing, especially in hot weather or during exercise. They might snore loudly. They might also struggle to cool down their bodies.

Eye problems are also common in Shih Tzus. Their large, prominent eyes are vulnerable. They can suffer from dry eye, cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy, and proptosis. Proptosis is when the eyeball moves out of its socket. This can happen from a small bump or injury. Regular eye checks are important to catch issues early. Dental problems also affect Shih Tzus. Their small mouths can make teeth overcrowded. This leads to plaque buildup, gum disease, and early tooth loss. Owners need to brush their teeth regularly. Professional dental cleanings are often necessary.

Other common health issues include patellar luxation, hip dysplasia, and kidney problems. Patellar luxation means the kneecap slips out of place. This causes pain and limping. Hip dysplasia affects the hip joint. Kidney problems can impact overall health. These health issues can mean frequent vet visits. They can also mean high medical bills. This financial burden is a major concern for some owners. It is important to research these conditions. Understanding them helps owners prepare for their dog's care. ## Training Challenges and Stubbornness

Training a Shih Tzu can be a test of patience for new owners. While intelligent, Shih Tzus are known for their independent and stubborn streak. This can make house-training especially difficult. Many owners report that their Shih Tzus take a long time to become fully house-trained. Accidents inside the house can be a frequent occurrence, which is frustrating. Consistency and positive reinforcement are key. Punishment does not work well with this breed.

Obedience training also poses challenges. Shih Tzus often have their own ideas about what they want to do. They might ignore commands. They might walk away during training sessions. Short, fun training sessions work best. Keep them engaging. Use high-value treats to motivate them. Early socialization is very important for Shih Tzus. Exposing them to different people, places, and other dogs from a young age helps them grow into well-adjusted adults. Without proper socialization, they can become shy or aggressive.

Their small size can also contribute to training issues. Some owners do not feel the need to train small dogs as rigorously as large ones. This is a mistake. All dogs benefit from training. It helps them understand boundaries. It makes them better companions. A well-trained Shih Tzu is much easier to live with. Their stubborn nature just means owners must be more persistent. I have trained many Shih Tzus over the years. I know they can learn commands and house rules. It just takes more dedication than with some other breeds.

## The Tendency for Excessive Barking

Shih Tzus are small dogs, but they often have big voices. Their tendency to bark excessively is another reason some people find them difficult. They are alert little dogs. They often bark at anything new. This includes people walking by, strange sounds, or the doorbell. This "alert barking" is common. It can be annoying if not managed. Some Shih Tzus also use barking to get attention. This is called "demand barking." They might bark at you for food, for play, or simply to be picked up.

This constant barking can disrupt a quiet home. It can bother neighbors. Training to reduce barking takes time and consistency. Owners must address the root cause of the barking. If it is alert barking, management involves blocking views or teaching a "quiet" command. If it is demand barking, ignoring the barking and rewarding quiet behavior helps. It's important not to accidentally reinforce the barking. Giving in when they bark teaches them it works.

Early training and socialization can help prevent excessive barking from becoming a habit. A bored Shih Tzu is also more likely to bark. Providing enough mental stimulation and physical activity helps. Even small dogs need walks and playtime. Puzzle toys can keep their minds busy. While Shih Tzus are not known for being guard dogs, their barking can serve as an alarm system. For some, this is a benefit. For others, it is a significant drawback. ## Delicate Nature and Compatibility Concerns

Shih Tzus are small and have a delicate bone structure. This makes them more prone to accidental injury than larger, more robust breeds. Their size makes them vulnerable to falls from furniture. They can also be hurt if accidentally stepped on. This delicate nature makes them less suitable for homes with very young children. Small children might not understand how to handle a small dog gently. They could drop the dog or play too roughly. This can lead to serious injuries for the Shih Tzu.

Their small size also means they are not ideal for active, rough-and-tumble play. They enjoy short walks and indoor play. They are not dogs that can accompany you on long hikes or strenuous activities. Owners must consider their lifestyle carefully. A Shih Tzu thrives in a calm, indoor environment. They love to be near their people. Their ideal family is one that can offer gentle companionship. This might not be suitable for every household.

Furthermore, Shih Tzus are not typically outdoor dogs. Their long coats and brachycephalic faces make them sensitive to extreme temperatures. They can overheat quickly in hot weather. Their coats can become soaked and cold in wet weather. This means they need to live indoors. This requires a commitment to indoor living and supervision during potty breaks outside. Their delicate nature impacts their overall compatibility with certain lifestyles. It is important to match the dog's needs with your own.

## The Need for Constant Companionship

Shih Tzus were bred as companion dogs for Chinese royalty. They thrive on human interaction. They love to be near their owners. This strong desire for companionship is a lovely trait for many. However, it can also lead to challenges. Shih Tzus can become very attached to their family members. They often follow their owners from room to room. They love to sit on laps. This need for constant closeness can sometimes be overwhelming for owners who are busy or away from home often.

When left alone for long periods, Shih Tzus can develop separation anxiety. This can manifest in destructive behaviors. They might chew furniture or soil the house. They might also bark or howl excessively. This is their way of expressing distress. Managing separation anxiety requires patience. It can involve crate training, leaving comforting items, or seeking professional help. Owners must understand this need before getting a Shih Tzu.

I believe Shih Tzus are happiest in homes where someone is usually present. This could be a retiree, a work-from-home individual, or a family where different members have varying schedules. They truly are "velcro dogs." They want to be part of everything you do. Their charming personalities make them excellent companions. But their dependency means they are not suitable for people with very demanding work schedules that keep them away for many hours each day. This trait makes them "worst" for specific lifestyles. Their desire for closeness is a key aspect of their personality. ## FAQ Section

### Are Shih Tzus good apartment dogs?
Yes, Shih Tzus generally make good apartment dogs. Their small size means they do not need a large living space. They adapt well to indoor living. They enjoy short walks. However, their tendency to bark can be an issue in close living quarters if not managed. They also need consistent potty breaks, which requires easy access outside.

### Do Shih Tzus shed a lot?
Shih Tzus have a double coat that sheds minimally. They are often considered a good choice for people with allergies. However, their long hair does need extensive grooming to prevent mats. Dead hair gets caught in their coat rather than falling out. This means daily brushing is essential to remove loose hair and keep their coat healthy.

### How long do Shih Tzu dogs live?
Shih Tzus have a relatively long lifespan. They typically live between 10 to 16 years. This longevity means a long-term commitment for owners. Proper care, a healthy diet, regular vet check-ups, and managing any breed-specific health issues can help them live a full and healthy life.

### Are Shih Tzus easy to train?
No, Shih Tzus are not considered among the easiest dogs to train. They are intelligent but can be quite stubborn. House-training is often particularly challenging for this breed. Consistent, positive reinforcement methods work best. Short, engaging training sessions are more effective than long, repetitive ones.

### What is a Shih Tzu a combination of?
The Shih Tzu breed originated in Tibet and was later developed in China. It is believed to be a cross between the Lhasa Apso and the Pekingese. These two breeds contributed to the Shih Tzu's distinctive appearance, long flowing coat, and companionable temperament. They were highly valued by Chinese royalty. You can learn more here: [What is a Shih Tzu a combination of](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-a-shih-tzu-a-combination-of).

### Are Shih Tzus good with kids?
Shih Tzus can be good with older, considerate children. Their delicate size makes them vulnerable to injury if handled roughly. They are not ideal for homes with very young children who might not understand gentle interaction. Supervision is always important to ensure safe play and prevent accidents.
