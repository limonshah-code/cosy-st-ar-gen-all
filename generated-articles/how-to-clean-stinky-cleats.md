## Banish Bad Odor: How To Clean Stinky Cleats

We all recognize that distinct smell. You finish a game, take off your cleats, and a pungent odor fills the air. It is a common problem for athletes. Sweat, dirt, and bacteria combine inside your athletic footwear. This mixture creates a powerful, unpleasant smell. Nobody wants to wear stinky cleats. More importantly, leaving cleats dirty and smelly can impact their lifespan and your foot health.

Keeping your cleats clean is not just about smell. It is about maintaining your gear. Proper cleaning prevents premature wear and tear. It also stops bacteria and fungi from growing, protecting your feet. This guide helps you tackle even the most stubborn cleat odors. We will explore simple, effective methods. You will learn how to clean stinky cleats, remove the smell, and keep them fresh. We will cover everything from basic cleaning steps to advanced odor elimination techniques. Get ready to transform your athletic footwear.

### Takeaway

*   **Start with Dry Cleaning:** Remove loose dirt and debris before any wet cleaning.
*   **Choose the Right Method:** Use baking soda, vinegar, or specialized cleaners based on odor severity.
*   **Wash Gently:** Hand wash when possible; machine wash with care in a mesh bag.
*   **Dry Thoroughly:** Air dry completely to prevent mold and mildew growth.
*   **Prevent Future Odor:** Store properly, use shoe deodorizers, and rotate cleats.

To clean stinky cleats, first remove all dirt and debris. Then, use a mixture of baking soda and warm water to scrub the insides, targeting odor-causing bacteria. Rinse thoroughly and air dry completely away from direct heat to prevent further odor and material damage.

## Understanding Why Cleats Get Stinky

Cleats get stinky for clear reasons. You put your feet through intense activity inside them. Sweat collects inside the shoes. This moisture creates a perfect home for bacteria. These bacteria break down sweat, producing unpleasant smells. Dirt and grass also get trapped in cleats. This organic matter adds to the odor problem.

My own cleats would smell awful after a long soccer match. It was embarrassing to leave them by the door. The primary cause is bacteria. Your feet have millions of sweat glands. When you play sports, these glands work overtime. Sweat itself does not smell bad. But bacteria on your skin and inside the shoe eat the sweat. Their waste products create that strong, foul odor. This process happens quickly in warm, damp environments. Cleats are exactly that.

The materials of your cleats also play a role. Synthetic materials can sometimes trap odors more than natural ones. Leather cleats might breathe better but can also absorb sweat deeply. Rubber soles and synthetic linings hold moisture. They make it hard for the shoe to dry out. When cleats stay damp, mold and mildew can grow. These fungi also contribute to the foul smell. Understanding these causes helps us fight the odor effectively. We can target the source of the problem.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Deep Cleaning Stinky Cleats

Deep cleaning your stinky cleats removes embedded dirt and tackles tough odors. This process requires a bit of time but delivers great results. Follow these steps for fresh-smelling footwear. Remember, patience ensures a thorough clean.

### 1. Prepare Your Cleats

First, get your cleats ready. Take them outside or over a trash can. Bang the soles together to knock off large clumps of mud and dirt. Use your stiff brush or a stick to scrape off any remaining debris from the studs and sides. Removing this loose dirt prevents it from turning into mud later.

Next, remove the laces and insoles. These parts often hold a lot of odor and dirt themselves. Set them aside; we will clean them separately. If your insoles are very old or heavily worn, consider replacing them. New insoles can significantly improve freshness. My dad always told me to do this first, and it really makes a difference.

### 2. Wash Laces and Insoles

Laces can go into a mesh laundry bag. Wash them with your regular laundry. If they are particularly dirty, soak them in warm, soapy water first. For insoles, hand wash them. Mix warm water with a small amount of mild detergent in your basin. Use an old toothbrush to scrub both sides of the insoles. Focus on any stained areas.

Rinse the insoles thoroughly under running water. Squeeze out excess water gently. Lay them flat to air dry completely. Do not put them in direct sunlight or a dryer. Excessive heat can warp or damage the materials. Making sure these parts are clean is crucial for overall freshness. This step is often overlooked.

### 3. Surface Cleaning the Cleats

Now, focus on the outside of the cleats. In your bucket, mix warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap. Dip your soft brush or sponge into the soapy water. Gently scrub the entire outer surface of the cleats. Work in small sections. Pay attention to any scuff marks or caked-on grime.

For tougher spots, you can make a paste. Mix baking soda with a little water. Apply this paste directly to stubborn stains. Let it sit for 5-10 minutes. Then, scrub with your brush. Rinse the outside of the cleats with a clean, damp cloth. Avoid soaking the entire cleat unless it is absolutely necessary. This prevents water from getting trapped in hard-to-dry areas. For more specific advice on different materials, this guide on [how to clean white baseball cleats](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-white-baseball-cleats) offers useful tips for preserving color and fabric integrity.

### 4. Tackling Internal Odor with Baking Soda

Baking soda is a miracle worker for internal odors. After cleaning the outside, it is time to address the inside. Sprinkle a generous amount of dry baking soda inside each cleat. Make sure it coats the entire footbed and sides. You can shake the cleat to distribute it. Let the baking soda sit overnight, or even for 24-48 hours.

The baking soda absorbs moisture and neutralizes odor-causing acids. It pulls the bad smells right out of the material. When ready, shake out all the baking soda. You can use a vacuum cleaner with a hose attachment to remove any residue. The difference in smell will be noticeable. This method is simple and very effective.

### 5. Using Vinegar for Tough Odors

Sometimes baking soda needs a partner. White vinegar is excellent for killing bacteria and fungi. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Lightly mist the inside of your cleats. Do not oversaturate them. The vinegar smell dissipates as it dries. This means you do not need to worry about your cleats smelling like a salad.

Let the vinegar solution sit for about 15-20 minutes. Then, wipe the inside with a clean, damp cloth. Follow this with another application of baking soda if the odor persists. Vinegar is especially useful for older, more ingrained smells. It disinfects the shoe. ### 6. Thorough Drying is Key

Drying your cleats properly is perhaps the most important step. Incomplete drying leads to mold, mildew, and more stink. After cleaning, stuff your cleats with old newspaper or paper towels. These materials absorb internal moisture. Replace the paper every few hours until it comes out dry.

Place your cleats in a well-ventilated area. Avoid direct sunlight or artificial heat sources like radiators. These can shrink, crack, or damage the cleat materials. A fan can speed up the drying process. Aim to have your cleats completely dry within 24-48 hours. Ensure they are bone dry before wearing or storing them. This prevents any new odors from forming. ## Advanced Odor Removal Techniques

Sometimes, basic cleaning is not enough. Stubborn odors might require more intense solutions. These advanced techniques go beyond simple baking soda and vinegar. They target deep-seated smells.

### Essential Oils for Natural Deodorizing

Essential oils offer a natural way to fight odors. They provide a pleasant scent while also having antimicrobial properties. Tea tree oil, eucalyptus oil, and lavender oil work best. Mix a few drops (5-10) of your chosen essential oil with water in a spray bottle. Lightly mist the inside of your cleats after cleaning.

Do not use too much oil directly. It can stain some materials. The diluted spray helps neutralize odors. It leaves a fresh, clean scent. You can also put a few drops on a cotton ball. Place the cotton ball inside each cleat overnight. This method adds a subtle fragrance. It helps keep things smelling good between uses.

### Activated Charcoal Inserts

Activated charcoal is a powerful odor absorber. It has a porous structure that traps odor molecules. You can buy activated charcoal inserts or bags specifically for shoes. Place these inside your cleats when you are not wearing them. They work passively to pull out smells.

Activated charcoal is chemical-free. It is also reusable. You can reactivate it by placing it in direct sunlight for a few hours. This makes it an eco-friendly option. I use charcoal bags in all my athletic shoes. They make a noticeable difference in freshness. They keep the shoes smelling clean.

### UV Shoe Sanitizers

For serious odor problems, consider a UV shoe sanitizer. These devices use ultraviolet light. UV light kills bacteria and fungi. They are often used by athletes to maintain shoe hygiene. You simply place the device inside your cleats. It runs a cycle, typically for 10-15 minutes.

UV sanitizers do not use chemicals. They address the root cause of the odor. They can be an investment. However, they offer a powerful solution. They extend the life of your cleats. They also keep your feet healthier. This is a good option if you frequently deal with very strong odors. ## Preventing Stinky Cleats: Best Practices

Prevention is always better than cure. Taking proactive steps can greatly reduce future odors. Incorporating these habits will keep your cleats fresh for longer. They will save you time and effort in the long run.

### Air Out Cleats After Every Use

This is the simplest yet most effective step. Never leave your cleats stuffed in a gym bag. Always take them out immediately after playing. Loosen the laces and pull back the tongue. This allows air to circulate inside. Place them in a well-ventilated area. A porch or garage corner works well.

Air drying prevents moisture from getting trapped. It stops bacteria from multiplying. This single habit makes a huge difference. I always hang my cleats on a rack after practice. It helps them dry out fully. Do not store them in direct sunlight. This can degrade the materials.

### Use Moisture-Wicking Socks

Your socks play a big role in foot hygiene. Cotton socks absorb and hold sweat. This creates a damp environment for bacteria. Switch to moisture-wicking socks. These socks draw sweat away from your skin. They keep your feet drier. Materials like merino wool or synthetic blends work best.

Dry feet mean less sweat inside your cleats. This directly translates to less odor. It is a small change. However, it has a big impact on overall foot and shoe freshness. Good socks also protect your feet from blisters.

### Rotate Your Cleats

If you play sports frequently, consider owning two pairs of cleats. Rotating them allows each pair to dry completely between uses. Even with air drying, cleats can take a full 24-48 hours to dry inside. Using them while still damp just encourages odor.

Having a second pair gives your primary pair a rest. It prolongs the life of both pairs. It also keeps both sets of cleats smelling fresher. This strategy is an investment. However, it pays off in comfort and durability. ### Regularly Clean and Deodorize

Do not wait for your cleats to become unbearable. Incorporate regular cleaning into your routine. A quick wipe down after each use removes surface dirt. A sprinkle of baking soda inside after a game helps absorb new odors. Once a month, do a deeper clean.

This regular maintenance prevents odor buildup. It keeps your cleats looking good too. Consistent care avoids major cleaning emergencies. It ensures your cleats are always ready for action. Think of it like brushing your teeth. Regular small efforts prevent big problems.

### Store Cleats Properly

How you store your cleats matters. Avoid airtight containers or bags. These trap moisture and odors. Store them in a cool, dry, well-ventilated place. A shoe rack or open shelf is ideal. If you must use a bag, choose one made of breathable mesh.

Inside your cleats, you can place cedar shoe trees. Cedar naturally absorbs moisture and has a pleasant scent. Alternatively, use those activated charcoal inserts mentioned earlier. Proper storage prevents new odors from settling in. It maintains the freshness you worked so hard to achieve.

## Machine Washing Cleats: When and How

Many people wonder if they can machine wash their cleats. The answer is sometimes, but with caution. Machine washing can be harsh on cleats. It can damage certain materials or adhesives. Always check the manufacturer's care instructions first. If they advise against machine washing, then hand wash.

### When to Consider Machine Washing

Machine washing is an option for particularly durable cleats. This applies especially to synthetic models. If your cleats are extremely dirty or have very stubborn odors, it might be worth the risk. It is generally not recommended for leather or cleats with delicate embellishments. It is also a last resort for very old or already damaged cleats. The convenience can sometimes outweigh the potential risks.

My experience with machine washing cleats is mixed. Some pairs came out fine, others showed signs of wear. I only do it if the cleats are synthetic and tough. And even then, I use a very gentle cycle. For specific sport cleats, this guidance on [how to clean soccer cleats that stink](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-soccer-cleats-that-stink) could offer more tailored advice for machine washing if applicable to your cleat type.

### Steps for Machine Washing Cleats

If you decide to machine wash, follow these steps carefully:

1.  **Remove Debris:** Scrape off all mud and dirt from the cleats. Use a brush to get into crevices.
2.  **Remove Laces and Insoles:** Wash these separately as described earlier. Laces can get tangled. Insoles can get damaged.
3.  **Use a Mesh Laundry Bag:** Place each cleat in its own mesh laundry bag. This protects them from hitting the drum. It also prevents damage to your washing machine.
4.  **Add Old Towels:** Put 2-3 old towels into the washing machine. This balances the load. It prevents the cleats from banging around too much.
5.  **Use Cold Water and Gentle Cycle:** Select the cold water setting. Choose a delicate or gentle cycle. This minimizes stress on the cleats.
6.  **Use Mild Detergent:** Add a small amount of mild liquid laundry detergent. Avoid harsh chemicals or bleach.
7.  **No Fabric Softener:** Do not use fabric softener. It can leave a residue on the cleat materials.
8.  **Air Dry ONLY:** **NEVER** put cleats in the dryer. The heat will shrink, crack, and delaminate materials. Air dry them completely in a well-ventilated area. Stuff them with newspaper to absorb moisture.

Machine washing is a shortcut. It carries a risk. Always weigh the benefits against potential damage. Proper hand washing is generally safer and just as effective.

## Tackling Specific Cleat Materials

Different cleat materials require slightly different cleaning approaches. Understanding these differences protects your footwear. It also ensures an effective clean.

### Synthetic Cleats

Most modern cleats are made from synthetic materials. These include various plastics, meshes, and rubbers. Synthetic cleats are generally durable and water-resistant. They are also usually the easiest to clean.

*   **Cleaning:** Use a mild soap and water solution. A soft brush or sponge works well. For stubborn stains, a baking soda paste is effective. Synthetic materials can handle a bit more scrubbing.
*   **Odor:** Baking soda and vinegar sprays are highly effective. Their non-porous nature makes it easier to remove surface odors.
*   **Drying:** Air dry thoroughly. Synthetic materials can sometimes trap moisture inside. Ensure good airflow.

### Leather Cleats

Leather cleats offer a classic feel and can mold to your foot. However, they need more careful handling. Leather can dry out, crack, or stain easily.

*   **Cleaning:** Use a very mild leather cleaner or saddle soap. Apply with a soft, damp cloth. Wipe gently. Avoid excessive water. Immediately wipe away any excess moisture. Do not soak leather cleats.
*   **Odor:** Place dry baking soda inside. Avoid liquid odor removers. Water can damage the leather. For external odor, a light wipe with a damp cloth followed by immediate drying is best.
*   **Drying:** Air dry slowly and completely. Stuff with newspaper to maintain shape and absorb moisture. Apply a leather conditioner once dry. This prevents cracking and keeps the leather supple.

### Mixed Material Cleats

Many cleats combine synthetic uppers with mesh panels or fabric linings. These require a combined approach.

*   **Cleaning:** Focus on the material in each area. Use general cleaning for synthetic parts. Be gentle on mesh. Mesh can be delicate. A soft brush is best for mesh areas.
*   **Odor:** Use dry baking soda inside. For any fabric parts, a diluted vinegar spray can be used. Test a small, inconspicuous area first.
*   **Drying:** Again, thorough air drying is critical. Pay extra attention to mesh areas, as they can sometimes hold moisture.

Knowing your cleat's material helps you choose the right cleaning method. This keeps them looking good and smelling fresh. It prolongs their lifespan. ## When to Consider Replacing Stinky Cleats

Even with the best cleaning efforts, cleats do not last forever. There comes a point when replacement is the best option. Recognizing these signs helps you avoid discomfort and potential injury. It also prevents you from constantly battling an losing battle against odor.

### Persistent Odor

If you have tried every cleaning method, and your cleats still smell terrible, it might be time. Deep-seated bacteria or fungal growth can become impossible to remove. The smell can permeate the materials. This makes them a constant source of unpleasant odor. Sometimes, the materials themselves break down. This contributes to the smell.

A persistent, strong odor despite thorough cleaning is a clear signal. You do not want to be the player everyone avoids in the locker room. Fresh cleats boost confidence. They also signal better hygiene.

### Visible Damage and Wear

Beyond smell, physical damage indicates a need for replacement. Look for:

*   **Cracked or Peeling Uppers:** This means the material is failing. It offers less support.
*   **Worn-Down Studs/Spikes:** Cleats lose their grip. This increases the risk of slipping and injury.
*   **Separating Soles:** The sole detaching from the upper compromises stability.
*   **Holes or Tears:** These allow dirt and moisture in easily. They also reduce foot protection.
*   **Collapsed Arch Support:** Old insoles or weakened internal structure can cause foot pain.

My old cleats once had the sole peeling off. I kept trying to glue it back. It never held. I realized then it was time for a new pair. Playing in damaged cleats can lead to serious ankle or knee problems.

### Impact on Performance and Comfort

Cleats offer essential support and traction. As they age, they lose these qualities. Worn-out cushioning can lead to discomfort and foot fatigue. A lack of proper support can change your gait. This increases strain on your joints. If your cleats no longer feel comfortable or perform well, it is time. New cleats provide the necessary grip and stability. They help you perform your best. They protect your body from injury. This is an investment in your athletic health.

Consider replacing cleats typically every 1-2 seasons. This depends on how often you play. It also depends on the intensity of your use. Regular inspection helps you decide when to say goodbye. ## Long-Term Maintenance for Odor-Free Cleats

Keeping your cleats odor-free is an ongoing process. It involves more than just cleaning after they get stinky. A consistent maintenance routine prevents problems before they start. Implement these habits for lasting freshness.

### Use Shoe Deodorizers Regularly

Make shoe deodorizers a part of your post-game ritual. There are many types available:

*   **Deodorizing Balls:** Small plastic balls filled with deodorizing agents. You twist them open and place them in your cleats. They absorb odors for hours.
*   **Spray Deodorizers:** Commercial sprays designed for athletic footwear. They often contain antibacterial agents. Spray them inside after each use.
*   **Cedar Shoe Trees:** These are excellent. Cedar wood naturally absorbs moisture and imparts a fresh scent. They also help maintain the cleat's shape.
*   **Baking Soda Sachets:** Fill small cloth bags or old socks with baking soda. Tie them closed. Place one in each cleat overnight. These are a DIY, natural option.

I always keep deodorizing balls in my shoes. It is a simple step. It prevents that awful smell from building up.

### Proper Storage Between Seasons

When the season ends, clean your cleats thoroughly. Ensure they are completely dry. Then, store them correctly. Do not just throw them in a dark, damp closet. This creates a breeding ground for mildew.

Stuff your cleats with newspaper or cedar shoe trees. Place them in a breathable bag or box. Store them in a cool, dry, well-ventilated area. An unused shelf in a closet works well. Avoid basements or attics with high humidity. Proper off-season storage keeps them fresh for the next season. It also protects the materials from degradation.

### Address Foot Hygiene

Your feet are the source of the sweat and bacteria. Good foot hygiene directly impacts cleat freshness.

*   **Wash Feet Daily:** Use antibacterial soap. Dry them thoroughly, especially between toes.
*   **Use Foot Powder:** Apply antifungal or deodorizing foot powder before putting on socks. This absorbs sweat. It helps prevent bacteria.
*   **Change Socks Often:** Always wear clean socks for every game or practice. Change them immediately after if they get wet.
*   **Check for Foot Conditions:** Address any athlete's foot or other fungal infections promptly. These conditions contribute significantly to shoe odor.

Taking care of your feet prevents the problem from starting. It is the first line of defense against stinky cleats. It keeps your feet healthy too. This combination of cleat care and personal hygiene ensures long-lasting freshness. It also saves you money on replacements.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I put my stinky cleats in the washing machine?
**A1:** You can, but with caution. Machine washing is generally only for durable, synthetic cleats. Remove laces and insoles first. Place cleats in a mesh bag with old towels. Use cold water on a gentle cycle with mild detergent. Always air dry completely afterwards; never use a dryer as heat can cause significant damage.

### Q2: How do I get rid of the smell from cleat insoles?
**A2:** Remove insoles from cleats. Hand wash them using warm water and mild detergent, scrubbing with an old toothbrush. Rinse thoroughly and squeeze out excess water. Air dry completely. For stubborn odors, sprinkle baking soda on dry insoles overnight before shaking it off.

### Q3: Is baking soda or vinegar better for cleat odor?
**A3:** Both are effective. Baking soda excels at absorbing existing odors and moisture. Vinegar is better at killing odor-causing bacteria and fungi due to its acidic nature. For best results, use them together: baking soda for absorption and vinegar as a disinfectant, ensuring the cleats are completely dry after each treatment.
