## Origin and Appearance of the Ancient Hairless Dog

The Xoloitzcuintli's appearance is as unique as its history, making it easily recognizable as the **Aztec mythical dog**. This long history ties them directly to ancient civilizations like the Aztecs.

The most striking feature of the Xolo is its lack of fur. Most Xolos are hairless, though a coated variety does exist. Their skin can be various colors, from slate grey to liver, often with spots. ## Xolos Today: Legacy of the Aztec Mythical Dog

The legacy of the **Aztec mythical dog** continues strongly today. The Xoloitzcuintli has survived centuries, overcoming near extinction. After the Spanish conquest, their numbers drastically declined. Spanish colonizers saw them as food or pagan symbols, leading to their widespread culling. However, dedicated efforts saved the breed.

In modern Mexico, the Xoloitzcuintli is a national treasure. It represents the rich pre-Hispanic heritage of the country. Artists, cultural icons, and dog enthusiasts celebrate the breed. Mexican artists like Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera famously owned Xolos and featured them in their works. This helped revive interest in the breed.

Today, Xolos are recognized by major kennel clubs worldwide. They are admired for their unique appearance, intelligence, and loyal temperament.
