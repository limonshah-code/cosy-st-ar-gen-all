## Why Ferrets Are Illegal in California

California’s decision to ban ferrets stems from several ecological concerns. The state views ferrets as a potential threat to its native wildlife. This ban is not arbitrary; it is based on scientific assessment. We often consider how a new species might affect local environments.

Ferrets are not native to California. They have the potential to establish wild populations if they escape captivity. An escaped ferret could prey on native birds and small mammals. California has many unique species that are vulnerable to new predators. We must protect these species.

State officials worry that ferrets could spread diseases. They might transmit rabies or other illnesses to native wildlife or even to humans. These diseases could devastate animal populations that have no natural immunity. Preventing such outbreaks is a high priority for public health and ecosystem health.

The California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) regularly assesses species. They look at risks to agriculture and the environment. Ferrets pose a definite risk. This ban reflects a cautious approach to wildlife management.
