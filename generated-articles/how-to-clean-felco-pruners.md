## How To Clean Felco Pruners

Do you love your Felco pruners? I do. These tools are often the workhorses of any garden. Keeping them clean is essential for their performance and longevity. A well-maintained pruner makes gardening tasks easier. It also protects your plants from disease.

This guide helps you understand how to clean Felco pruners effectively. We will cover why cleaning matters, what tools you need, and detailed steps for disassembly, cleaning, and reassembly. You will also learn about sharpening, rust prevention, and routine maintenance. By the end, you will know exactly how to keep your Felco pruners in top condition.

### Takeaway

*   Regularly clean Felco pruners to extend their lifespan and ensure efficient cuts.
*   Disassemble your pruners for a thorough cleaning of all components.
*   Remove sap and grime with appropriate solvents and brushes.
*   Sharpen blades regularly for optimal cutting performance.
*   Lubricate moving parts and protect against rust.
*   Reassemble carefully and test functionality.

To clean Felco pruners, first disassemble the tool. Then, scrub blades and internal parts to remove sap, dirt, and rust. Disinfect the blades to prevent disease spread. Sharpen the cutting edge. Finally, lubricate all moving components and reassemble the pruners. This process ensures your tool remains sharp, efficient, and long-lasting.

### Why Cleaning Your Felco Pruners Matters for Peak Performance

Cleaning your Felco pruners is more than just making them look nice. It directly impacts how well they work. Dirty blades struggle to make clean cuts. This makes your gardening work harder. More importantly, dirty tools can harm your plants.

Sap and plant debris build up on blades. This creates a sticky surface. This surface can harbor bacteria and fungi. When you cut into a healthy plant with a dirty blade, you transfer these pathogens. This leads to plant disease. Regular cleaning prevents this problem. It protects your garden from harmful infections.

Clean pruners also last longer. Dirt, sap, and moisture cause rust and corrosion. These elements damage the metal over time. Rust makes blades dull and weakens the tool's structure. By cleaning and oiling your pruners, you protect the metal parts. This preserves the integrity of your investment.

Think about the effort you put into your garden. Your tools should support that effort, not hinder it. A clean, sharp pruner glides through branches with ease. This reduces hand fatigue. It makes pruning a more enjoyable task. Neglecting your tools means more effort for you and poorer health for your plants. Proper care is a simple step with big rewards.

### Essential Tools and Supplies for Felco Pruner Cleaning

Before you start cleaning, gather all your supplies. Having everything ready makes the process smooth and efficient. You probably have most of these items around your house or garden shed. Do not rush the preparation phase.

First, you need safety gear. Gloves protect your hands from grime and cleaning agents. Safety glasses protect your eyes from debris, especially when brushing or sharpening. I always put my safety glasses on first. These small steps prevent injuries.

Next, focus on cleaning agents. Rubbing alcohol (isopropyl alcohol) is excellent for disinfecting and dissolving sap. Mineral spirits or turpentine can also work for stubborn sap. Some people use specialized tool cleaners. A wire brush or steel wool helps scrub away dried sap and rust. Old toothbrushes are also great for small crevices.

For rust removal, consider a rust eraser or fine-grit sandpaper. A sharpening stone (like a Felco sharpening stone) is crucial for blade maintenance. Finally, you need a rag or old cloth for wiping. Lubricating oil, such as light machine oil or specialized tool oil, is essential for rust prevention and smooth operation. Remember to choose cleaning agents that are safe for your tools. Just like knowing [what you should not clean carpet with](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-should-you-not-clean-carpet-with) to avoid damage, avoid harsh chemicals that could corrode your pruners.

*   **Safety Gear:** Gloves, safety glasses
*   **Cleaning Agents:** Isopropyl alcohol, mineral spirits, tool cleaner
*   **Brushes:** Wire brush, old toothbrush, steel wool
*   **Rust Removal:** Rust eraser, fine-grit sandpaper
*   **Sharpening:** Felco sharpening stone
*   **Lubrication:** Light machine oil, tool oil
*   **Wipes:** Clean rags or old cloths
*   **Disassembly Tool:** Felco adjustment key or appropriate wrench

Having these items ready ensures you can complete the entire cleaning process without interruption. It makes the task much simpler.

### Step-by-Step Guide to Disassembling Your Felco Pruners

To truly clean your Felco pruners, you need to take them apart. This allows you to reach all the hidden spots where sap and grime accumulate. Disassembly might seem daunting at first, but it is straightforward with Felco tools. They are designed for easy maintenance.

First, locate the pivot bolt. This is the main screw that holds the two halves of your pruner together. Most Felco models use a special adjustment key for this bolt. If you do not have the Felco key, a similarly sized wrench might work. Carefully loosen the bolt. Keep track of all the small parts as you remove them.

Once the main bolt is loose, separate the two handles. You will find the spring in the middle. Remove the spring. Sometimes, there are also washers or spacers around the pivot point. Lay out all these components on a clean surface. This prevents losing any crucial pieces.

Each Felco model might have slight variations in its design. Refer to your specific model's manual if you are unsure. However, the basic principle remains the same: identify the main pivot, loosen it, and carefully pull the parts apart. Just like cleaning other mechanical items that have multiple components, such as when you [clean an S&B air filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-s-and-b-air-filter), disassembling helps you clean each part effectively. Taking pictures with your phone during disassembly can also help you remember how everything fits back together.

*   **Secure the Tool:** Hold the pruners firmly on a stable surface.
*   **Locate Pivot Bolt:** Find the main screw holding the two blades.
*   **Use Proper Tool:** Use the Felco adjustment key or a suitable wrench.
*   **Loosen and Remove Bolt:** Turn the bolt counter-clockwise until it comes out.
*   **Separate Handles:** Gently pull the two pruner halves apart.
*   **Remove Spring:** Take out the central spring.
*   **Collect Small Parts:** Keep all washers and spacers in a safe place.

Disassembly is a critical step for a deep clean. It allows you to address every surface. Do not skip this part for best results.

### Thorough Cleaning and Sap Removal Techniques

With your Felco pruners disassembled, you can now focus on deep cleaning each part. This is where you remove all the stubborn sap, dirt, and any rust. Pay close attention to the blades and the internal mechanisms.

Begin with the blades. Use a wire brush or steel wool to scrub off dried sap and grime. For very sticky sap, soak the blade in isopropyl alcohol for a few minutes. Alcohol dissolves sap quickly. You can also use mineral spirits or a specialized tool cleaner. Scrub thoroughly until the metal shines. I find that a dedicated scrubbing session here saves a lot of trouble later.

After removing the visible gunk, disinfect the blades. This is crucial for preventing plant disease. Wipe the blades down with a cloth soaked in rubbing alcohol. You can also use a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 9 parts water) if you rinse very thoroughly afterwards. Disinfecting kills any lingering pathogens. This ensures you start your next pruning job with sterile blades.

For any rust spots, use a rust eraser or fine-grit sandpaper. Gently rub the rusted areas until the rust disappears. Be careful not to damage the blade's edge. Once cleaned, wipe all parts dry. Any moisture left behind can lead to new rust. This thorough cleaning ensures your Felco pruners are pristine and ready for the next step.

#### Removing Stubborn Sap

Sometimes sap is extremely difficult to remove. It can harden into a resin-like substance. For these cases, you might need a stronger approach. Try soaking the affected blade in a small dish of mineral spirits or turpentine for about 15-30 minutes. This helps to soften the sap considerably.

After soaking, use a plastic scraper or an old credit card to gently lift off the softened sap. Follow up with a wire brush or steel wool. Repeat the soaking and scrubbing if necessary. Ensure you work in a well-ventilated area when using these solvents. They are effective but require careful handling.

#### Disinfecting Blades

Disinfecting your blades prevents the spread of plant diseases. Every time you cut into a diseased plant, pathogens can transfer to your tools. A clean blade protects your garden. After removing all physical debris, wipe down your blades with isopropyl alcohol.

For extra assurance, especially after pruning diseased plants, use a 10% bleach solution. Dip the blades in the solution for a few seconds. Immediately rinse the blades with clean water to prevent corrosion. Then dry them completely. This quick step maintains good garden hygiene.

For general metal cleaning and rust removal, especially with organic acids, you might find similarities to [how to clean a still with vinegar](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-still-with-vinegar). Vinegar can be a mild alternative for surface rust or sap, but it needs thorough rinsing and drying to prevent further corrosion on your Felco blades.

### Sharpening Felco Pruner Blades for Precise Cuts

A clean pruner is good, but a clean and *sharp* pruner is even better. Sharpening your Felco blades is vital for making clean, healthy cuts. Dull blades crush plant stems rather than slicing them. This damages the plant and makes it more vulnerable to disease.

Felco pruners typically have a single bevel blade. This means you only sharpen one side, the cutting edge. Hold the sharpening stone at the same angle as the blade's bevel. Maintain a consistent angle throughout the sharpening process. This creates a sharp edge.

Apply moderate pressure and move the stone across the entire cutting edge. Work from the pivot point towards the tip. Repeat this motion several times until you feel a slight burr (a thin ridge of metal) on the flat side of the blade. The burr tells you the edge is sharp.

Once you feel the burr, flip the blade over. Gently remove the burr by running the flat side of the stone along the flat side of the blade once or twice. This creates a clean, razor-sharp edge. Always sharpen away from your body for safety. Sharpening makes a huge difference in cutting efficiency and plant health.

*   **Identify Cutting Edge:** Felco blades have a single bevel.
*   **Hold Stone at Angle:** Match the sharpening stone to the blade's existing bevel angle.
*   **Sharpen Cutting Side:** Move the stone across the blade from pivot to tip, maintaining angle.
*   **Check for Burr:** Sharpen until a slight burr forms on the flat side of the blade.
*   **Remove Burr:** Gently run the flat side of the stone along the flat side of the blade.
*   **Test Sharpness:** Carefully test on paper or a small twig.

A sharp blade cuts cleanly and effortlessly. This prevents plant damage. It also makes your pruning tasks much easier and more enjoyable.

### Rust Prevention and Lubrication for Long-Lasting Tools

Cleaning and sharpening are only part of the maintenance process. Protecting your Felco pruners from rust and ensuring smooth operation are equally important. Rust is a tool's worst enemy. It degrades metal and impairs function.

After cleaning and drying all components thoroughly, apply a thin layer of lubricating oil. Light machine oil, 3-in-1 oil, or specialized tool oil works well. Apply it to the blades, pivot bolt, spring, and any other moving parts. This oil creates a protective barrier against moisture. It also ensures all components glide smoothly against each other.

Pay special attention to the pivot area. This is where most friction occurs. A well-oiled pivot allows the blades to open and close with minimal effort. It prevents wear and tear on the moving parts. Do not over-oil; a thin coat is sufficient. Wipe off any excess oil.

Proper storage also prevents rust. Always store your Felco pruners in a dry environment. Hang them up or keep them in a toolbox. Avoid leaving them exposed to moisture or humidity. Some gardeners use silica gel packets in their toolboxes to absorb moisture. These small steps make a big difference in extending the lifespan of your valuable tools.

*   **Dry Thoroughly:** Ensure all parts are completely dry after cleaning.
*   **Apply Lubricating Oil:** Use light machine oil or tool oil on blades, pivot, and spring.
*   **Focus on Moving Parts:** Ensure the pivot area is well-lubricated.
*   **Wipe Excess Oil:** A thin film is sufficient; excess oil attracts dirt.
*   **Store Dry:** Keep pruners in a dry place, away from moisture.
*   **Consider Desiccants:** Silica gel can help absorb humidity in storage.

Regular lubrication and careful storage keep your Felco pruners in excellent working condition for many years. It is a small investment of time that pays off significantly.

### Reassembling Your Felco Pruners Correctly

Once all parts are clean, sharp, and oiled, it is time to put your Felco pruners back together. Reassembly is basically the reverse of disassembly. However, paying attention to detail here ensures your pruners function perfectly. Do not force any parts into place.

Start by placing the spring back into its position between the handles. Ensure it is seated correctly. Then, align the two halves of the pruner. Make sure the cutting blade and the anvil blade fit together properly. The pivot point should align precisely.

Carefully insert the pivot bolt through the aligned holes. If you had washers or spacers, put them back in their original order. Tighten the pivot bolt. Use the Felco adjustment key or the appropriate wrench. Tighten it until there is no wobble in the blades. However, do not over-tighten. Over-tightening makes the pruners stiff and hard to operate.

After tightening, test the pruners. They should open and close smoothly without any binding. The blades should meet cleanly. Make a few practice cuts on a small twig or piece of paper. Adjust the pivot bolt tension if needed. If they feel too loose, tighten slightly. If too stiff, loosen a bit. Correct reassembly guarantees optimal performance and safety.

*   **Insert Spring:** Place the spring between the handles.
*   **Align Halves:** Match the cutting blade and anvil blade, aligning pivot holes.
*   **Insert Pivot Bolt:** Carefully push the bolt through the aligned holes.
*   **Add Washers/Spacers:** Replace any small parts in their correct order.
*   **Tighten Bolt:** Use the Felco key or wrench to secure the bolt.
*   **Test Functionality:** Open and close the pruners to check for smooth operation.
*   **Adjust Tension:** Fine-tune the pivot bolt for optimal feel and cut.

Proper reassembly ensures your freshly cleaned and sharpened Felco pruners are ready for action. It brings the maintenance cycle to a satisfying close.

### Regular Maintenance Schedule and Tips for Felco Pruners

Keeping your Felco pruners in top condition does not stop after one deep clean. Establishing a regular maintenance schedule is key to their long-term performance and durability. A little care often prevents bigger problems later.

For daily use, perform a quick wipe-down. After each pruning session, wipe the blades with a cloth. Use a little rubbing alcohol to remove sap and dirt immediately. This prevents buildup. It keeps the blades clean and hygienic between uses. This quick step is crucial.

Plan for a weekly or bi-weekly cleaning. This involves a more thorough wipe-down and checking for dullness. If you use your pruners frequently, consider a quick sharpening every few weeks. This maintains a razor-sharp edge. It ensures efficient cuts.

A full deep clean and disassembly should occur seasonally, or at least two to three times a year. This is when you follow all the steps outlined in this article: disassembly, thorough scrubbing, sharpening, and comprehensive lubrication. This prevents rust, addresses any hidden grime, and ensures every component is in excellent shape. Consistent care helps you extend the lifespan of your Felco pruners significantly.

*   **After Each Use:** Wipe blades with an alcohol-soaked cloth to remove sap and dirt.
*   **Weekly/Bi-Weekly:** Perform a more thorough wipe-down; check blade sharpness.
*   **Monthly (Heavy Use):** Consider a quick touch-up sharpening.
*   **Seasonally (2-3 times/year):** Conduct a full deep clean, disassembly, sharpening, and lubrication.
*   **Store Properly:** Always store pruners dry, clean, and lubricated.
*   **Inspect Regularly:** Check for loose bolts, damaged springs, or signs of wear.

A consistent maintenance routine ensures your Felco pruners always perform their best. This small investment of time greatly prolongs the life of your valuable gardening tools.

### FAQ Section

#### How often should I clean my Felco pruners?
You should wipe your Felco pruners clean after every use to remove sap and debris. A deeper clean, involving disassembly and lubrication, is recommended seasonally or at least two to three times a year, depending on how often you use them. Regular maintenance prevents rust and ensures peak performance.

#### Can I use soap and water to clean my Felco pruners?
You can use warm, soapy water for initial cleaning to loosen dirt. However, soap and water alone are not ideal for disinfecting or removing stubborn sap. Always follow up with rubbing alcohol for disinfection and ensure all parts are completely dry afterwards to prevent rust.

#### What if my Felco pruners have rust?
For light rust, use a wire brush, steel wool, or a rust eraser to gently scrub it off. For more significant rust, you might need to use fine-grit sandpaper or a dedicated rust remover. After removing rust, clean the area thoroughly, dry it, and apply lubricating oil to prevent future corrosion.

#### Do I need to sharpen my Felco pruners every time I clean them?
You do not need to sharpen them every single time you clean them. Sharpening is typically done when you notice the blades becoming dull or making ragged cuts. For regular gardeners, sharpening a few times a season or during your deep cleaning sessions is usually sufficient to maintain a keen edge.

#### How do I store my Felco pruners?
Always store your clean, dry, and lubricated Felco pruners in a dry place. Hang them on a hook in your shed or garage, or keep them in a toolbox. Avoid leaving them outdoors or in damp conditions, as this can lead to rust and damage. Proper storage extends their life.
