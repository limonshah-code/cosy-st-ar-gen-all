## How To Clean Smelly Football Cleats: A Fresh Start for Your Gear

There is nothing quite like the smell of victory. There is also nothing quite like the smell of intensely used football cleats after a hard game. If you are reading this, you probably know that second smell very well. Those powerful odors can linger, making your locker room or home smell less inviting.

Cleaning smelly football cleats is a common challenge for players, parents, and coaches alike. We all want our gear to last and perform well. More importantly, we want it to smell fresh. This article will guide you through simple yet effective methods. You will learn how to tackle tough odors, clean away grime, and keep your cleats smelling better for longer. Let's transform your stinky cleats into something you can proudly wear again.

### Takeaway:

*   **Remove Debris:** Start by knocking off loose dirt and mud.
*   **Deep Clean:** Use a brush, mild soap, and water for a thorough wash.
*   **Odor Combat:** Apply baking soda, white vinegar, or commercial odor eliminators.
*   **Proper Drying:** Air dry cleats completely to prevent mildew.
*   **Prevent Future Odors:** Use newspaper, cedar inserts, or regular cleaning.

To clean smelly football cleats, first remove all loose dirt. Then, scrub them with a mild soap solution and a stiff brush. Treat interior odors with baking soda or white vinegar, and ensure they dry completely in a well-ventilated area, away from direct heat or sunlight.

## Understanding Why Football Cleats Get Smelly

Football cleats face harsh conditions on the field. They get covered in mud, grass, and sweat. This combination creates a perfect storm for bad odors. Sweat contains bacteria, and when these bacteria mix with moisture, they multiply rapidly.

The enclosed environment of a cleat traps this moisture and bacteria. This leads to that strong, unpleasant smell we all recognize. If you play often, the problem gets worse. Regular use means more sweat and more chances for bacteria to grow. This is why proper cleaning is so important.

Synthetic materials and leather can both harbor smells. Each material might react differently to cleaning products. Knowing the cause helps us choose the best cleaning methods. We need to attack the bacteria and remove the accumulated grime effectively. This first step helps you understand the battle ahead.

### The Role of Bacteria and Moisture

Bacteria are the main culprits behind the smell. Your feet sweat a lot during physical activity. This sweat is mostly water, but it also contains salts and oils. Bacteria on your skin feed on these substances.

As bacteria break down the sweat, they release waste products. These waste products are what cause the bad smell. Cleats often do not dry out fully between uses. This constant dampness creates an ideal breeding ground for more bacteria. Think of it like a miniature swamp for microbes.

Proper ventilation and drying are key to stopping this cycle. You need to remove both the bacteria and the moisture. This dual approach gives you the best chance to clean smelly football cleats. Ignoring either one means the smell will quickly return.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Deep Cleaning Your Smelly Cleats

Cleaning smelly football cleats requires more than just a quick wipe. A deep clean gets rid of built-up grime and targets the source of the odor. I like to break it down into manageable steps. This ensures no part of the cleat is overlooked.

Follow these steps for a thorough clean:

### 1. Remove Loose Dirt and Debris

First, take your cleats outside. Bang them together gently to dislodge large clumps of mud. Use a stiff brush or an old toothbrush to scrape off any remaining caked-on dirt. Pay attention to the studs and the sides of the cleat. This initial step prevents you from spreading mud around during the wet cleaning process.

You want to get rid of as much dry dirt as possible now. This makes the next steps much easier and more effective. Do not skip this part; it is foundational for a good clean. This is also a good time to remove laces and insoles if they are removable.

### 2. Prepare a Cleaning Solution

Fill a bucket or basin with warm water. Add a small amount of mild soap or laundry detergent. You do not need a lot of soap; a little goes a long way. Mix the solution until it is slightly sudsy. This creates the cleaning power you need.

A gentle soap works best for most cleat materials. Harsh chemicals can damage synthetic fabrics or leather. If you have specific cleaning instructions for your cleats, follow those. For specific advice on similar gear, check out how to clean your football gloves for more general football equipment care.

### 3. Scrub the Exterior

Dip your stiff brush into the cleaning solution. Start scrubbing the exterior of your cleats. Focus on dirty spots and areas with stubborn stains. Work in circular motions to lift the grime.

Do not be afraid to apply a little pressure. The goal is to remove all visible dirt. Rinse the brush as needed to keep it clean. You might need to refresh your cleaning solution if it gets too dirty.

### 4. Clean the Interior and Insoles

The inside of the cleat is where most of the smell comes from. If your insoles are removable, take them out. Wash them separately with soap and water. You can scrub them with an old toothbrush.

For the cleat interior, wipe it down with a damp cloth soaked in your cleaning solution. You can also use a small brush to reach inside. Make sure to get into all crevices. This helps remove sweat and bacteria buildup. This step is critical for truly cleaning smelly football cleats.

### 5. Rinse Thoroughly

Once you have scrubbed everything, rinse your cleats thoroughly with clean water. You can hold them under a faucet or use a hose. Make sure to wash away all soap residue. Leftover soap can attract more dirt later.

Rinse the inside and outside of the cleats until the water runs clear. This step ensures no cleaning product remains. A good rinse prepares the cleats for the important drying process. It helps ensure no sticky residue is left behind.

## Tackling Stubborn Odors with Natural Remedies

Even after a deep clean, some lingering smells might remain. This is where natural odor eliminators come in handy. These remedies are safe, effective, and often already in your home. They work by neutralizing the odor, not just masking it.

I have found these methods to be incredibly powerful. They target the source of the smell directly. You can use them as a follow-up to your deep clean. They are also great for ongoing maintenance.

### Baking Soda Method

Baking soda is a fantastic odor absorber. It works by neutralizing acidic and basic odor molecules. This is why it is a common household deodorizer. You can use it dry or as a paste.

Here is how to use it:

1.  **Dry Powder:** Sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda inside each cleat. Make sure to get it into the toe area.
2.  **Leave Overnight:** Let the baking soda sit inside the cleats for at least 8-12 hours, or overnight. This gives it time to absorb odors.
3.  **Shake It Out:** After several hours, shake out the baking soda. You can use a vacuum cleaner with a hose attachment to remove any residue.

For particularly stubborn smells, you can create a paste. Mix baking soda with a little water. Apply the paste to the inside of the cleats. Let it dry completely, then brush and vacuum it out. This method is also effective for cleaning other gear like if you need to know [how to clean smelly work boots](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-smelly-work-boots).

### White Vinegar Spray

White vinegar is another powerful natural deodorizer. Its acidic nature helps to kill bacteria and fungi. These are common causes of bad smells. The vinegar smell itself dissipates quickly as it dries.

To use white vinegar:

1.  **Dilute:** Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle.
2.  **Spray Inside:** Lightly spray the inside of your cleats with the solution. Do not oversaturate them.
3.  **Air Dry:** Allow the cleats to air dry completely. The vinegar smell will disappear as they dry.

You can also wipe the inside with a cloth dampened with the vinegar solution. This method is especially good for targeting specific smelly areas. Remember to always dilute the vinegar to avoid damaging materials. This is a great tip for cleaning smelly football cleats.

## Advanced Odor Elimination Techniques

Sometimes, natural remedies might not be enough for extremely pungent cleats. In these cases, you might need to bring in stronger tools. These advanced techniques target deep-seated odors and persistent bacteria. They are often a last resort but can be very effective.

Consider these options if the smell persists:

### Commercial Odor Eliminators

Many products are designed specifically for athletic footwear. These sprays and powders often contain powerful antimicrobial agents. They work to kill bacteria and absorb odors. Look for products labeled for sports shoes or cleats.

*   **Sprays:** Apply directly to the interior of the cleats. Follow the product instructions for application and drying times.
*   **Powders:** Sprinkle into the cleats, similar to baking soda. Leave overnight and then shake or vacuum out.
*   **Deodorizing Balls/Inserts:** These small devices absorb moisture and release a fresh scent. You can leave them in your cleats when not in use.

Always test a small, inconspicuous area first. This ensures the product does not stain or damage your cleat material. Using these can give your cleats a truly fresh scent.

### Freezing Method (For Extreme Cases)

Freezing can sometimes kill odor-causing bacteria. This method works by exposing bacteria to extreme cold. It can be helpful for cleats that have a persistent, deep-seated smell.

To use the freezing method:

1.  **Bag Cleats:** Place each clean, dry cleat into a separate sealable plastic bag.
2.  **Freeze:** Put the bagged cleats in the freezer overnight.
3.  **Thaw and Air Out:** Remove them from the freezer and let them thaw and air out.

While some debate its effectiveness, it can be a useful addition. Make sure cleats are completely dry before freezing them. This prevents ice crystal formation and potential material damage.

## The Importance of Proper Drying

Drying your cleats properly is as important as cleaning them. If you do not dry them completely, moisture remains. This moisture creates an ideal environment for bacteria and mildew. The smell will return quickly, even after a thorough clean.

I cannot stress this enough: never put damp cleats away. This mistake is a common reason why cleats continue to smell bad. Proper drying helps to lock in the fresh scent from your cleaning efforts. It prevents new odors from forming right away.

### Air Drying is Best

Always air dry your football cleats. Avoid using direct heat sources. This includes direct sunlight, radiators, or clothes dryers. High heat can damage the materials of your cleats. It can cause them to shrink, crack, or lose their shape.

Find a well-ventilated area for drying. A garage, mudroom, or outdoor patio works well. Ensure good airflow around the cleats. You can place them upside down or on their sides to help air circulate.

### Speeding Up the Drying Process

You can help your cleats dry faster without using heat:

*   **Stuff with Newspaper:** Crumple up old newspapers and stuff them inside the cleats. Newspaper is highly absorbent. It draws moisture out from the interior.
*   **Change Newspaper Regularly:** Replace the damp newspaper every few hours. Continue until the cleats feel dry.
*   **Use a Fan:** Place your cleats near a fan. The moving air will speed up evaporation.
*   **Cedar Shoe Trees:** Cedar naturally absorbs moisture and has a pleasant scent. These can be expensive but are a good long-term investment.

Allow at least 24-48 hours for cleats to dry completely. This time frame ensures all moisture is gone. Patience here pays off in terms of odor prevention. This is a vital step when you clean smelly football cleats.

## Preventing Future Odors in Your Football Cleats

Cleaning smelly football cleats is a reactive task. Preventing them from getting smelly in the first place is proactive. Good preventative measures save you time and effort in the long run. They keep your cleats fresher for longer periods.

Incorporating these habits into your routine will make a big difference. It is about consistent care, not just occasional deep cleans. Think of it as ongoing maintenance for your valuable gear.

### Regular Cleaning Routine

Do not wait for your cleats to become unbearable. A quick clean after every game or practice helps immensely.

*   **Wipe Down:** Use a damp cloth to wipe off mud and grass immediately.
*   **Remove Insoles:** Take out insoles and let them air out separately.
*   **Air Out:** Always air out your cleats after use. Do not toss them into a dark, enclosed bag.

This simple routine stops dirt and sweat from settling in. It prevents bacteria from multiplying rapidly. Consistency is the real key here.

### Odor-Absorbing Inserts

Consider using odor-absorbing inserts. These can be a game-changer for cleat freshness.

*   **Cedar Shoe Trees:** As mentioned, cedar absorbs moisture and smells great.
*   **Charcoal Pouches:** Activated charcoal is a natural deodorizer. Place small pouches inside your cleats when not in use.
*   **Deodorizing Balls:** These small balls contain fresheners or antimicrobials. They fit perfectly inside cleats.

These products work while your cleats are stored. They actively fight against odor buildup. They keep the interior dry and fresh.

### Proper Storage

How you store your cleats affects their smell. Avoid sealing them in airtight bags or containers.

*   **Breathable Bag:** If you must use a bag, choose a mesh or breathable sports bag. This allows air circulation.
*   **Open Space:** Store cleats in a dry, well-ventilated area. A closet with good air flow is better than a sealed locker.
*   **Avoid Heat/Humidity:** Keep cleats away from hot, humid environments. These conditions promote bacterial growth.

### Rotate Your Cleats

If possible, have more than one pair of cleats. Rotating between pairs gives each pair more time to dry completely. This is especially useful for players who train frequently.

When one pair is drying, the other can be in use. This reduces the wear and tear on a single pair. It also drastically cuts down on odor buildup. Your feet will thank you, and so will everyone around your locker.

## Maintaining Specific Cleat Materials

Football cleats come in various materials, each needing slightly different care. Understanding these differences helps to clean smelly football cleats effectively without causing damage. I want to make sure your cleaning method suits your gear.

Different materials react differently to water and cleaning agents. Knowing this helps you choose the right approach.

### Synthetic Cleats

Most modern football cleats are synthetic. These materials are durable and often lightweight.

*   **Cleaning:** Synthetic materials are generally easy to clean. You can use mild soap and water with a brush. They dry faster than leather.
*   **Drying:** Air dry thoroughly. Stuffing with newspaper is very effective. Avoid high heat.

### Leather Cleats

Leather cleats offer a classic feel and can be very comfortable. However, they require more delicate care.

*   **Cleaning:** Use a very mild leather-specific cleaner or saddle soap. Avoid harsh detergents. Wipe with a damp cloth, do not saturate.
*   **Drying:** Air dry slowly and naturally. Stuff with newspaper to absorb internal moisture. Do not use direct heat; this can crack or shrink the leather.
*   **Conditioning:** After drying, apply a leather conditioner. This keeps the leather supple and prevents cracking.
*   **Odor Control:** Baking soda works well for leather interiors. Be careful with vinegar, as it can be drying if not diluted properly.

### Mesh and Fabric Sections

Many cleats have mesh or fabric panels for breathability. These areas can also trap dirt and odor.

*   **Cleaning:** Use a soft brush or old toothbrush with your mild soap solution. Gently scrub the mesh to remove dirt.
*   **Rinsing:** Rinse thoroughly but gently. Do not rub aggressively, which can damage the mesh.
*   **Drying:** These areas dry quickly. Ensure they are completely dry to prevent mildew.

These sections are often more delicate than the main body of the cleat. Treat them with care.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Cleaning Cleats

Cleaning smelly football cleats can go wrong if you make certain mistakes. These errors can damage your cleats or make the odor problem worse. I want to help you avoid these pitfalls. Knowing what not to do is as important as knowing what to do.

Avoid these common missteps:

*   **Using a Washing Machine:** Many people ask if they can machine wash cleats. The answer is usually no. The aggressive tumbling can damage the cleat's structure, studs, and glue. It can also cause materials to shrink or warp.
*   **Using Harsh Chemicals:** Bleach or strong chemical cleaners can strip color. They can also break down materials and cause irritation. Stick to mild soaps and natural deodorizers.
*   **Drying with High Heat:** As mentioned, direct heat sources like dryers or radiators are damaging. They can deform cleats, crack leather, and melt synthetic components. Air drying is always the safest option.
*   **Soaking Cleats for Too Long:** While some initial soaking helps loosen dirt, prolonged immersion can weaken adhesives and damage materials. Clean them efficiently and rinse quickly.
*   **Ignoring the Insoles:** Insoles absorb a lot of sweat and harbor bacteria. Leaving them in during cleaning or not cleaning them separately is a missed opportunity to eliminate odor.
*   **Putting Damp Cleats Away:** This is the biggest mistake. Damp cleats are a breeding ground for bacteria and mildew. Always ensure they are bone dry before storing them.
*   **Using Abrasive Scrubbers:** While you need a stiff brush for dirt, avoid steel wool or overly abrasive scrubbers. These can scratch or damage the cleat's surface, especially on leather or delicate mesh.

By avoiding these mistakes, you protect your investment. You also ensure your cleaning efforts are truly effective. You will have fresh, long-lasting cleats.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I put my football cleats in the washing machine?

No, I do not recommend putting football cleats in a washing machine. The harsh agitation and high spin cycles can damage the cleat's structure, studs, and adhesives. It can also cause materials to warp or shrink. Hand cleaning with mild soap and water is much safer and more effective.

### Q2: How often should I clean my football cleats to prevent smell?

You should aim to give your cleats a quick clean after every use. This means wiping off loose dirt and mud and letting them air dry properly. A deeper clean, targeting interior odors, should be done at least once a week or every few uses, especially if you play frequently.

### Q3: What is the best natural product to remove cleat odor?

Baking soda is a highly effective natural product for removing cleat odor. Its ability to absorb and neutralize smells makes it ideal. You can sprinkle it directly into the cleats and leave it overnight, then shake it out. White vinegar spray (diluted with water) also works well to kill odor-causing bacteria.

### Q4: How long does it take for cleats to dry completely?

Cleats typically take 24 to 48 hours to dry completely when air-dried. This timeframe can vary based on humidity, airflow, and how wet the cleats were initially. Using methods like stuffing with newspaper or placing them near a fan can help speed up the drying process significantly without using heat.

### Q5: Can I use a hair dryer to dry my cleats faster?

I advise against using a hair dryer or any direct heat source to dry your cleats. High heat can cause the cleat materials to crack, shrink, or become misshapen. It can also melt adhesives, weakening the shoe's structure. Always opt for air drying in a well-ventilated area.

### Q6: What should I do if my cleats still smell after cleaning?

If your cleats still smell after cleaning, try a stronger odor elimination method. Reapply baking soda for a longer period, or use a commercial athletic shoe deodorizer spray or powder. Ensure they are fully dry, as lingering moisture can trap odors. Consider using cedar shoe inserts for ongoing odor absorption.
