## Transitioning and Feeding Guidelines for Golden Retrievers

When you change your Golden Retriever's food, do it gradually. This helps prevent digestive upset. A sudden switch can cause stomach issues. My experience shows slow transitions are best. Your dog's system needs time to adjust to new ingredients.

Start by mixing a small amount of the new Royal Canin food with their old food. Over 7 to 10 days, slowly increase the new food's proportion. Decrease the old food's amount. By the end of this period, your dog should be eating only the new Royal Canin formula. This method helps their digestive system adapt smoothly.

Feeding guidelines are on the Royal Canin bag. These guidelines are a starting point. Your Golden Retriever's activity level affects how much food they need. Active dogs often need more calories. Less active dogs need fewer. Monitor your dog's weight and body condition. Adjust the food amount as needed.

Puppies typically eat more frequently than adults. An adult Golden Retriever usually eats two meals a day. Puppies might need three or four smaller meals. Always provide fresh water alongside their food. Water is essential for digestion and overall health. You can find more information about feeding frequency at [how many times a day should I feed my Royal Canin dog](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-times-a-day-should-i-feed-my-royal-canin-dog).

Consider feeding times as part of their routine. Consistent feeding times help establish good habits. This also helps with house training. Avoid free-feeding if your Golden Retriever tends to overeat. Measured meals help maintain a healthy weight.

Royal Canin provides detailed feeding charts. These charts consider your dog's age and anticipated adult weight. For example, a Golden Retriever puppy's chart will account for their growth phase. An adult chart focuses on maintaining an ideal body condition. Always weigh the food to ensure accuracy. Using a kitchen scale is more precise than scoops.

### Gradual Transition Steps

1.  **Days 1-2:** Mix 25% new food with 75% old food.
2.  **Days 3-4:** Mix 50% new food with 50% old food.
3.  **Days 5-6:** Mix 75% new food with 25% old food.
4.  **Days 7-10:** Feed 100% new Royal Canin food.

If your dog shows signs of digestive upset, slow down the transition. Extend each step by a few days. Consult your veterinarian if problems persist. They can offer personalized advice for your dog's dietary needs.
