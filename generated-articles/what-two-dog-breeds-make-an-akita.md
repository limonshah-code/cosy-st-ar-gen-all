## What Two Dog Breeds Make An Akita: Unraveling Its Origins

Have you ever looked at a majestic Akita and wondered about its powerful build and noble presence? Many people ask, "What two dog breeds make an Akita?" It is a common question. Perhaps you think the Akita is a mix of just two distinct breeds.

The Akita breed has a rich and complex history. It is not a simple combination of two parent breeds. Its lineage developed over centuries in Japan. Understanding its true origins helps us appreciate this unique dog even more. In this article, we will explore the real story behind the Akita. We will look at its ancient ancestors and how the breed came to be.

### What two dog breeds make an Akita?

The Akita is not formed from two specific modern dog breeds. It is an ancient Japanese breed. Its ancestors were indigenous Japanese hunting dogs. These dogs evolved over centuries in the Akita prefecture of Japan. Therefore, the Akita developed naturally from native Japanese spitz-type dogs.

## Unpacking the Akita's True Ancestry: Beyond Two Breeds

When we ask, "What two dog breeds make an Akita?", we often think of modern crossbreeds. Think of a Labradoodle, which combines a Labrador and a Poodle. The Akita's story is different. It is not a designer dog created from two established breeds.

Instead, the Akita is a native Japanese breed. Its origins are in the mountainous regions of Japan. It descends from large hunting dogs. These dogs were used for hunting bear, wild boar, and deer. They were known as "Matagi-inu," or "hunting dog." This makes the Akita an ancient breed, not a recent mix. Its history is tied deeply to Japanese culture and tradition.

The Matagi-inu were robust dogs. They had strong bodies and bold spirits. People used them for centuries in the harsh northern Japanese climate. These dogs adapted well to tracking large game in deep snow. Their loyalty and courage made them valuable companions for hunters. Understanding this background helps us see the Akita as a product of evolution and selection, not a simple blend.

## The Ancient Roots of the Akita Inu

The earliest ancestors of the Akita are the Matagi dogs. These were medium to large Japanese spitz-type dogs. They lived in the Akita prefecture in northern Japan. Their main role was to hunt large game. This included bears and wild boar.

The Matagi dogs were brave and resilient. They worked closely with hunters. Their job was to track, corner, and hold game until the hunter arrived. This requires immense strength, focus, and a keen sense of smell. These traits are still visible in the modern Akita. The breed developed naturally from these powerful hunting companions over many generations.

During the Edo period (1603-1868), dogs like the Akita were owned by samurai. They served as guardians and hunting partners. This gave them a high status. Records show these dogs were prized for their loyalty and bravery. These qualities are a direct inheritance from their ancient roots. The Matagi dog laid the fundamental groundwork for the Akita we know today.

## Evolution of the Japanese Akita: A Journey Through History

The history of the Akita breed is not static. It involves periods of purity, crossbreeding, and dedicated preservation. After the Edo period, dog fighting became popular in Japan. This led to crossbreeding the Matagi dog with other large, strong breeds. These included the Tosa Inu and even German Shepherds. The goal was to create bigger, fiercer fighting dogs.

This crossbreeding almost erased the pure Akita lineage. Pure Akita types became rare. They faced extinction. Then, in the early 20th century, efforts began to preserve the original Akita type. Japanese enthusiasts recognized the importance of their native dog. They wanted to restore its purity.

In 1931, the Akita was declared a natural monument of Japan. This led to strict breeding programs. Breeders focused on bringing back the original spitz-type features. They aimed for the distinct Akita look. These efforts continued through World War II. During the war, many dogs were killed for their fur or meat. Some were crossbred with German Shepherds to avoid being culled. Only a few pure Akita lines survived. These survivors formed the foundation for the modern Japanese Akita. This intense focus on purity defines the Japanese Akita today.

## The American Akita: A Separate Lineage Emerges

The Akita's story takes a different turn after World War II. American soldiers stationed in Japan admired the Akita. They brought many Akitas back to the United States. These dogs often came from the mixed lines. These lines included crosses with German Shepherds and Tosa Inu.

In America, these dogs continued to be bred. There was less emphasis on reverting to the strict pure Japanese type. American breeders developed their own standards. They preferred the larger, heavier-boned dogs. These often had a black mask, which is not typical for the Japanese Akita. This led to the establishment of the American Akita.

So, while the Japanese Akita was being selectively bred back to its traditional form, the American Akita developed differently. It incorporated the traits from the various crosses that had occurred in Japan during the war years. This created two distinct Akita types. Both share a common ancestral past. However, their development paths diverged significantly. Understanding this divergence is key to knowing the breed. This explains why an American Akita might look different from a Japanese Akita.

## Key Traits Inherited from Ancestral Lines

The Akita's ancient heritage deeply influences its characteristics. Their ancestors were powerful hunting dogs. They needed to be strong, courageous, and highly intelligent. These traits are evident in today's Akitas. Their physical strength is remarkable. They have a muscular build and a broad head. Their thick double coat protects them from harsh weather. This coat is a direct link to their origins in cold, snowy regions of Japan.

Akitas are known for their loyalty to family. They form deep bonds with their owners. This loyalty stems from their history as companions to hunters and samurai. They are also very protective. They can be wary of strangers. This protective nature comes from their role as guardians. They needed to guard homes and families.

Their intelligence is also a strong inherited trait. Matagi dogs had to think independently during hunts. This means Akitas can be stubborn or strong-willed. They need consistent training and early socialization. This helps them become well-behaved family members. Owners must understand these inherent qualities. It allows them to provide proper care and training for this majestic breed.

## Akita Breed Preservation Efforts: Safeguarding a National Treasure

The Akita breed faced severe challenges throughout its history. Dog fighting in the late 19th and early 20th centuries almost destroyed its purity. As I mentioned, breeders crossed native Akitas with breeds like Tosa Inu and German Shepherds. This made the breed larger and more aggressive for fighting. These crosses diluted the traditional Akita characteristics.

Concerned enthusiasts launched a movement to preserve the original Akita type. In 1931, the Japanese government declared the Akita a natural monument. This was a crucial step. It helped protect the breed. Preservation societies began strict breeding programs. They aimed to eliminate the foreign influences. They sought to restore the true Akita form. This included its unique curled tail, erect ears, and specific coat colors.

World War II presented another grave threat. Many dogs were culled for food or fur. Some Akitas survived by being hidden or by crossbreeding with German Shepherds. After the war, dedicated efforts continued to rebuild the breed. This led to the distinct Japanese Akita we see today. These efforts ensured the Akita's survival. They also highlight the dedication to maintaining its unique heritage.

## Akita Today: A Powerful and Loyal Companion

Today, the Akita stands as a testament to its powerful ancestry. It is a large, sturdy dog. Its noble appearance commands respect. The Japanese Akita and American Akita both trace back to those ancient hunting dogs. They share core characteristics. Both are known for their unwavering loyalty. They protect their families fiercely.

Living with an Akita requires understanding its heritage. They are independent thinkers. They need firm, consistent training. Early socialization is vital. It helps them interact well with others. They can be reserved around strangers. They may not get along with other dogs of the same sex. This is a trait linked to their strong hunting and guarding instincts.

The Akita's thick coat requires regular grooming. They shed heavily twice a year. Owners must be prepared for this. Despite their strong personality, Akitas are deeply affectionate with their family. They thrive on companionship. They make excellent guardians. They offer a unique blend of ancient strength and loving devotion. Their rich history shaped them into the impressive dogs they are today.
