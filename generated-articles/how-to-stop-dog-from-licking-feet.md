## Identifying Medical Issues Causing Foot Licking

Sometimes, excessive licking of feet is not just a behavioral quirk. It can signal an underlying medical problem. Dogs cannot tell us when they hurt or feel unwell. Licking is often their way of self-soothing or drawing attention to a discomfort. If your dog suddenly starts licking your feet more often, especially intensely, a vet visit is important.

Allergies are a common culprit. Dogs can suffer from environmental allergies, food allergies, or flea allergies. These allergies often cause itchy skin, which can include the paws and surrounding areas. When their own paws itch, they might transfer that licking habit to your feet. They seek relief and a familiar scent. Skin infections, like bacterial or yeast infections, can also cause intense itching. These infections can develop from allergies or moisture. Constant licking can also create hot spots or open sores, making the problem worse. This can lead to your dog licking their own paws or skin raw. If this happens, you should look into [how to stop my dog from licking himself raw](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-stop-my-dog-from-licking-himself-raw).

Pain or discomfort in their own paws can also trigger foot licking behavior. This could be due to an injury, a foreign object lodged between paw pads, or even arthritis. Dogs often lick to soothe pain. If they cannot reach the painful area on themselves, they might transfer the licking to you. Parasites, such as mites or fleas, can also cause severe itching. Regular parasite prevention is important for your dog's health.

My advice is to rule out medical issues first. Take your dog to the veterinarian. The vet can perform a physical exam and recommend tests. They might check for allergies, infections, or pain. Addressing any underlying health problems is crucial. It ensures your dog's comfort and helps resolve the licking behavior.

## Effective Training Strategies to Discourage Licking

Once you have ruled out medical issues, you can focus on training. Consistent training is key to stopping your dog from licking feet. It requires patience and positive reinforcement. The goal is to teach your dog what behavior is acceptable instead.

The first step is redirection. When your dog starts to lick your feet, gently interrupt the behavior. Do not scold or punish your dog. Punishment can create fear or confusion. Instead, immediately offer them an appropriate item to lick or chew. A puzzle toy filled with peanut butter or a durable chew bone works well. When they engage with the alternative, praise them calmly. This teaches them to redirect their oral fixation to approved items.

Another effective method is teaching a "leave it" or "no lick" command. Start with a treat in your hand. Tell your dog "leave it." When they ignore the treat, give them a different treat and praise. Practice this command in various situations. Once they understand "leave it" for objects, apply it to your feet. When they approach to lick, say "leave it" and redirect them to a toy. If they stop, reward them instantly. Consistency is very important here. Everyone in the household must use the same commands and responses.

Manage the environment to prevent opportunities for licking. If you know your dog licks your feet when you are watching TV, wear socks or cover your feet. This removes the temptation. You can also move away or stand up when the licking starts. This sends a clear signal that the behavior stops the interaction. Remember, dogs repeat behaviors that are rewarded. Avoid giving attention, even negative attention, when the licking occurs. Give attention only when they are calm and not licking. Over time, your dog will understand that licking feet does not get the desired outcome.

## When Professional Help Becomes Essential

While many licking behaviors can be managed at home, there are times when professional intervention is necessary. Knowing when to seek help ensures your dog gets the best care. It also helps you find a lasting solution to the problem. Do not hesitate to reach out to experts.

If you suspect a medical cause, a visit to the veterinarian is the first step. If your dog's licking started suddenly, intensified, or is accompanied by other symptoms like skin redness, hair loss, or lameness, a vet check is critical. They can diagnose underlying issues such as allergies, infections, pain, or internal conditions. A vet can prescribe medication or recommend specialized treatments. They rule out serious health concerns that need medical attention. My vet always stresses early detection.

If medical causes have been ruled out, and the licking persists, a certified professional dog trainer or a veterinary behaviorist can help. A dog trainer can provide guidance on effective training methods. They can help you implement redirection techniques and teach commands. They often offer group classes or private sessions. This provides tailored advice for your dog's specific needs.

A veterinary behaviorist is a veterinarian with specialized training in animal behavior. They are equipped to handle complex behavioral issues like severe anxiety, compulsive disorders, or aggression. They can assess your dog's emotional state. They can create a behavior modification plan. This plan might include medication alongside behavioral training. This is especially true if anxiety or obsessive-compulsive disorder contributes to the licking. If the licking is extreme, causing skin damage, or significantly impacting your dog's quality of life, a behaviorist is the right choice. They offer in-depth solutions. Investing in professional help can save you time and frustration. It also ensures your dog's well-being in the long run.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is dog licking feet dangerous for humans?
Dog licking feet is usually not dangerous for humans. Dog saliva contains bacteria. The risk of serious infection is low on intact skin. However, avoid letting your dog lick open wounds or sensitive areas. Always wash your feet after contact, especially if you have cuts. Good hygiene reduces any potential risk.

### Why do dogs like salty feet?
Dogs are attracted to the salty taste on human feet. Sweat contains salts and other minerals. Dogs have a keen sense of smell and taste. They can detect these substances. This makes feet an interesting and tasty target for licking. It is a simple, natural attraction for many dogs.

### Can anxiety make my dog lick my feet more?
Yes, anxiety can definitely make your dog lick your feet more. Licking is a self-soothing behavior for dogs. Anxious dogs often engage in repetitive actions to cope with stress. This includes licking. If your dog seems stressed, consider their environment and routine. Address their anxiety to reduce the licking.

### What scents do dogs dislike to stop licking?
Dogs often dislike bitter or strong smells. Bitter apple spray is a common commercial deterrent. Some dogs also dislike the smell of citrus fruits like lemon or orange. Others may dislike vinegar or certain essential oils (use extreme caution with essential oils, as many are toxic to dogs). These scents can discourage licking.

### How do I stop my dog from licking my feet at night?
To stop nighttime licking, address boredom and anxiety during the day. Ensure your dog gets plenty of exercise and mental stimulation before bed. Consider wearing socks to bed as a physical barrier. You can also spray a bitter deterrent on your feet. Redirect any licking attempts with a quiet chew toy.

### Could my dog be trying to communicate something by licking my feet?
Yes, your dog might be trying to communicate. Licking can be a sign of affection, a way to show submission, or even a plea for attention. They might also be "grooming" you. Watch for other body language cues. This helps you understand what your dog is trying to tell you through their licking behavior.
