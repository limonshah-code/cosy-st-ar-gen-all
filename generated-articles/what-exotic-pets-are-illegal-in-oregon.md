The most immediate concern with illegal exotic pets is public safety. Many prohibited animals are inherently dangerous. They possess natural instincts that can lead to aggression. A captive wild animal can revert to its natural behaviors unexpectedly. A lion's natural instinct is to hunt. A venomous snake's natural defense is its bite.

These animals can escape. An escaped exotic animal poses a direct threat to neighbors. It also endangers other pets and livestock. Imagine a large python loose in a residential neighborhood. Illegal exotic pets also pose a significant threat to Oregon's environment. If an exotic animal escapes, it can become an invasive species. Invasive species can outcompete native wildlife for food and habitat. They can also introduce new diseases. These diseases can devastate native animal populations.

For example, a non-native snake might eat native birds or rodents. An escaped exotic bird could spread avian diseases. These problems disrupt delicate ecosystems. They can cause irreversible damage. Oregon imposes serious penalties for owning illegal exotic pets. These penalties serve as a strong deterrent. They reflect the gravity of the risks involved. Ignorance of the law is not an excuse.
