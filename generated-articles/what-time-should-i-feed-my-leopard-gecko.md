# What Time Should I Feed My Leopard Gecko

Bringing a new leopard gecko into your home is an exciting event. You want to give them the best care. A common question I hear is, "What time should I feed my leopard gecko?" Knowing the right feeding schedule is vital for your gecko's health. It helps them digest food correctly and feel secure in their environment.

This article will help you understand your gecko's natural eating habits. We will look at the best times to offer food. We will also discuss factors that influence feeding, like age and health. You will learn about feeder insects and how to set up a successful feeding routine. Lastly, we will cover when to adjust or skip a meal. My goal is to equip you with the knowledge to feed your leopard gecko at the optimal time every day.

## Takeaway

*   Feed leopard geckos in the evening or at dusk.
*   Establish a consistent daily or routine schedule.
*   Adjust feeding frequency based on age and growth.
*   Offer appropriate feeder insects with proper supplements.
*   Monitor your gecko's appetite and remove uneaten food promptly.

## Clear Answer to the Main Query

You should feed your leopard gecko in the late evening, around dusk. This time aligns with their natural crepuscular activity. Feeding them consistently at this time helps digestion. It also reduces stress, allowing them to hunt and eat in comfort.

## Understanding Your Leopard Gecko's Natural Rhythm

Leopard geckos are fascinating creatures with unique daily habits. To know the best time to feed them, we must understand their natural cycle. Unlike many common pets, leopard geckos are not fully nocturnal. They are crepuscular. This means they are most active during dawn and dusk. This is when the light is low, but not completely dark.

In their natural habitats, leopard geckos come out during these times to hunt for insects. The low light offers them protection from predators. It also keeps them from overheating in the desert sun. When you feed your leopard gecko during these hours, you mimic their natural environment. This makes them feel more secure. It also encourages their natural hunting instincts.

Feeding your gecko during the day is less ideal. They are usually sleeping or resting in a hide. Offering food when they are inactive can cause stress. They might not eat well. A regular feeding time linked to their active hours creates a routine. This routine is good for their physical and mental well-being. I always try to observe my geckos. I notice they become more alert and ready to eat as evening approaches. It is truly their prime time for activity. This understanding is key to a happy, healthy gecko. A leopard gecko can go for some time without food, but a regular schedule helps keep them robust. ## The Best Time to Feed Leopard Geckos

The best time to feed your leopard gecko is late in the evening. Aim for the time when natural light fades, such as 30 minutes to an hour after sunset. For many households, this means between 7:00 PM and 10:00 PM. This period directly matches their crepuscular activity cycle. At this time, their metabolism is ready for food. Their instincts tell them it is time to hunt.

Consistency is very important when setting a feeding schedule. Feeding your gecko at the same time each evening helps them adapt. They learn when to expect food. This can reduce stress and improve their appetite. A predictable routine also aids their digestion. Their bodies prepare for nutrient absorption. If you feed them at random times, it can disrupt their internal clock. This may lead to digestive issues or a lack of interest in food.

Think of it like our own meal times. We generally eat around the same times each day. Our bodies expect it. Leopard geckos are similar. They thrive on routine. I find my geckos often start poking their heads out or waiting at the glass as their feeding time approaches. This shows they are ready and expecting their meal. Establishing this habit makes feeding sessions smoother for both of you. ## Factors Influencing Feeding Time and Frequency

While evening is generally best, various factors influence how often and exactly when you should feed your leopard gecko. Age plays a major role. Young, growing geckos need more frequent meals than adults. Health, growth rate, and environmental conditions also affect their eating needs.

### Age and Growth

*   **Hatchlings and Juveniles (0-6 months):** These young geckos grow quickly. They need a lot of protein and calcium to develop properly. Feed them daily. Offer small meals each evening.
*   **Sub-Adults (6-12 months):** Their growth slows down a bit. You can reduce feeding to every other day. Keep offering meals in the evening. Monitor their body condition closely.
*   **Adults (12+ months):** Adult geckos do not grow much more. They need less frequent feeding. Two to three times per week is usually enough. Always feed them in the evening. Sometimes, an adult gecko can even be fed once a week, depending on their health and diet. For more details on this, check out [can I feed my leopard gecko once a week](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-i-feed-my-leopard-gecko-once-a-week).

### Health Condition

A healthy gecko has a good appetite. If your gecko is sick, they might eat less or refuse food. Always consult a reptile vet if you notice a lasting change in eating habits. Brumation, a natural cooling period for some reptiles, also causes a drop in appetite. During this time, they will eat very little or nothing.

### Environmental Factors

The temperature in your gecko's enclosure directly affects their metabolism and appetite. Leopard geckos need a warm spot to digest food properly. If their tank is too cold, they will not digest food well. This can lead to impaction or a refusal to eat. Make sure your heat source provides a belly heat of 90-94°F (32-34°C). If the temperature drops too low for an extended period, it can be dangerous for your gecko. Learn more about how long they can last without heat: [how long can a leopard gecko go without heat](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-long-can-a-leopard-gecko-go-without-heat). Proper environmental conditions are as vital as the feeding time itself.

## Feeder Insects and Their Impact on Feeding Schedules

The type of feeder insects you offer also plays a role in your gecko's feeding schedule. Different insects have different nutritional values. They also vary in how quickly your gecko digests them. A varied diet is crucial for a leopard gecko's health. You should offer a mix of staple feeders and occasional treats.

### Staple Feeders

*   **Mealworms:** These are easy to keep and readily available. They are a good staple. Offer them in a shallow dish.
*   **Crabs:** Crickets are an excellent natural prey item. They move a lot, encouraging hunting behavior. Make sure to remove any uneaten crickets after feeding. They can stress your gecko.
*   **Dubia Roaches:** Dubia roaches are highly nutritious and easy to gut-load. They are a superior feeder insect. They are also quiet and do not smell much. If you are wondering [how many Dubia roaches to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-dubia-roaches-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko), it depends on the gecko's size and age.
*   **Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL):** These are packed with calcium and do not require dusting. They are a great staple.

### Occasional Treats

*   **Superworms:** Superworms are high in fat and protein. Offer them in moderation. They are larger than mealworms. Be aware of [how many Superworms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-superworms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) to prevent overfeeding.
*   **Waxworms:** Waxworms are very high in fat. They are like candy for geckos. Use them only as an occasional treat, for example, to entice a gecko to eat. Overfeeding waxworms leads to obesity. Knowing [how many wax worms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-wax-worms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) is crucial for their health.

### Gut-Loading and Dusting

Always gut-load your feeder insects. This means feeding the insects nutritious food before offering them to your gecko. This transfers vitamins and minerals to your gecko. Dusting insects with calcium and D3 powder is also vital. Do this at every feeding for juveniles. For adults, dust every other feeding. This prevents metabolic bone disease. Proper nutrition through gut-loading and dusting makes the feeding time more effective for your gecko's long-term health.

## Setting Up a Successful Feeding Routine

Creating a consistent feeding routine is one of the best things you can do for your leopard gecko. It provides stability and reduces stress. A clear schedule also allows you to monitor their eating habits better. This helps you spot any health issues quickly. I follow a simple process with my geckos, and they thrive on it.

### Choose Your Time and Stick to It

As discussed, choose a time in the late evening, around dusk. Stick to this time every day or every other day, depending on your gecko's age. For example, if you decide to feed at 8:00 PM, try to offer food at 8:00 PM consistently. Geckos learn routines fast. They will likely be waiting for you.

### Prepare the Food

Before feeding, make sure your feeder insects are gut-loaded. Dust them with calcium and D3 powder. Place insects in a feeding dish for mealworms or superworms. For crickets or roaches, release them into the enclosure one by one. This encourages hunting. I often use feeding tongs to offer individual insects. This makes sure my gecko sees each one.

### Observe During Feeding

Watch your gecko as they eat. See if they are interested in the food. Note how many insects they eat. This helps you learn their appetite. It also helps you adjust the amount you offer in the future. If your gecko seems to ignore the food or acts lethargic, it could be a sign something is wrong.

### Remove Uneaten Food

Remove any uneaten feeder insects after about 15-20 minutes. Crickets left in the tank can bite and stress your gecko. Mealworms and roaches can burrow and hide, becoming a nuisance. Removing uneaten food keeps the tank clean. It also prevents your gecko from overeating if you offer too much. A clean enclosure is important for their well-being.

### Ensure Water Access

Always ensure your gecko has fresh, clean water available in their tank. While this is not directly part of the feeding time, hydration is vital for digestion and overall health. Change the water daily. Proper setup with a water dish helps keep your gecko healthy.

## When to Adjust or Skip a Feeding

Even with a perfect schedule, there are times you need to adjust or skip a feeding. Recognizing these times helps keep your leopard gecko healthy and happy. My geckos have shown me that flexibility is key to good care.

### Refusal to Eat

Sometimes, your leopard gecko might just not be hungry. If they ignore their food, do not force them. Try again the next day or at their next scheduled feeding. A missed meal or two is usually fine. Young geckos might be pickier than adults. However, a prolonged refusal to eat can signal a problem. If your gecko goes several days without eating, and they are usually consistent, it is time to investigate. This could mean they are stressed, too cold, or ill. You might need to examine their habitat conditions or seek vet advice.

### Overt Feeding Signs

If your gecko has a very plump tail or looks round, you might be feeding them too much. An overweight gecko can face health issues. Reduce feeding frequency or the number of insects per meal. Adjust their diet until they reach a healthy weight. Their tail should be thick but not overly round.

### Shedding Cycle

Leopard geckos shed their skin regularly as they grow. During shedding, many geckos lose their appetite. They might hide more and not be interested in food. It is normal for them to skip meals during this time. Once they have shed, their appetite usually returns quickly. Offer food as usual after their shed is complete. Do not worry if they are not eating while shedding.

### Brumation

Some leopard geckos, especially adults, may enter a period called brumation. This is like a hibernation state. Their activity slows down significantly. Their appetite decreases, or they might stop eating completely. This is a natural process, often triggered by cooler temperatures or shorter daylight hours. If your gecko is brumating, offer food less often, if at all. Monitor their weight.

### Illness or Stress

Any illness or high-stress levels can cause a gecko to stop eating. If you notice other signs of illness, like lethargy, sunken eyes, or abnormal droppings, along with a lack of appetite, contact a reptile veterinarian. Moving to a new home or changes in their enclosure can also stress a gecko. Give them time to adjust before worrying too much about a missed meal or two. Always prioritize their comfort and health.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I feed my leopard gecko during the day?
No, it is not recommended to feed your leopard gecko during the day. Leopard geckos are crepuscular. They are most active and prefer to hunt during low light hours, specifically dusk and dawn. Feeding them during the day when they are typically resting can cause stress and may lead to them refusing to eat.

### Q2: How often should I feed an adult leopard gecko?
An adult leopard gecko (over 12 months old) should be fed two to three times per week. They do not need as much food as growing juveniles. Monitor their body condition to ensure they are not over or underfed. Adjust the frequency based on their weight and activity level.

### Q3: What if my leopard gecko is not eating at all?
If your leopard gecko is not eating, first check their enclosure temperatures. Ensure the warm spot is between 90-94°F (32-34°C). Stress from a new environment, shedding, or illness can also cause a lack of appetite. If they refuse food for more than a few days, or show other illness signs, consult a reptile veterinarian.

### Q4: How much food should I give my leopard gecko per feeding?
The amount of food depends on your gecko's age and size. For juveniles, offer 5-7 appropriately sized insects per feeding. For adults, 3-5 insects should be sufficient. An "appropriately sized" insect is no larger than the space between your gecko's eyes. Remove any uneaten insects after 15-20 minutes.

### Q5: Is it okay to use a feeding dish for all insects?
Using a feeding dish is great for mealworms, superworms, and black soldier fly larvae. These insects tend to burrow or hide. A dish keeps them contained and makes it easy for your gecko to find them. For crickets and Dubia roaches, you can release them into the enclosure one by one to encourage hunting.

### Q6: Does feeding time impact my gecko's digestion?
Yes, feeding time impacts digestion. Feeding your leopard gecko in the evening, during their active hours, aligns with their natural digestive cycle. They need warmth to digest food properly. A consistent schedule helps their body prepare for food intake and process nutrients efficiently.
