## How To Clean Paracord: A Comprehensive Guide

Your paracord works hard for you. Whether it is part of your survival kit, a pet leash, or a decorative bracelet, paracord collects dirt. Keeping your paracord clean helps it last longer. It also ensures it looks good and performs reliably when you need it most.

Cleaning paracord is a simple process. It needs only basic supplies. This guide will show you how to clean paracord using hand washing, machine washing, and spot cleaning methods. We will also cover proper drying and storage to keep your paracord in top condition. Let's make sure your paracord stays ready for its next adventure.

### Takeaway

*   **Assess Paracord Condition:** Check for dirt, grime, and specific stains before cleaning.
*   **Choose Cleaning Method:** Hand wash for delicate items, machine wash for durable ones.
*   **Use Mild Soap:** Always use a gentle, non-harsh cleaning agent.
*   **Rinse Thoroughly:** Remove all soap residue to prevent stiffness or damage.
*   **Air Dry Completely:** Hang paracord to dry away from direct heat or sunlight.
*   **Store Properly:** Keep paracord in a clean, dry place to prevent future grime buildup.

### How do you clean paracord effectively without causing damage?

To clean paracord effectively, you must use mild soap and water. Hand wash delicate items. Use a mesh bag for machine washing durable paracord. Always rinse the paracord thoroughly to remove all soap. Then, air dry it completely away from direct heat. This process helps keep the cord strong and looking new.

### Gathering Your Gear: Essential Tools for Cleaning Paracord

Before you start cleaning your paracord, gather the right tools. Having everything ready makes the process easy. You will need a few common household items. These items help you clean paracord without damaging it. Choosing the right tools ensures a thorough and safe cleaning.

### The Gentle Touch: Hand Washing Your Paracord Items

Hand washing is a great method for cleaning paracord. It is ideal for delicate items or small sections of cord. This method gives you control. It ensures a gentle but effective clean. I often choose hand washing for my paracord bracelets. This helps them keep their shape.

Follow these steps for hand washing your paracord:

1.  **Prepare the Cleaning Solution:** Fill a bucket or sink with warm water. Add a small amount of mild soap or detergent. Stir the water to mix the soap. You want a light sudsy solution.
2.  **Soak the Paracord:** Place your dirty paracord into the soapy water. Let it soak for 15-30 minutes. This helps loosen dirt and grime. For very dirty items, a longer soak might be helpful.
3.  **Gently Scrub:** After soaking, use your hands to agitate the paracord. Rub the cord against itself. Use a soft brush or toothbrush for stubborn spots. Scrub gently to avoid fraying the fibers.
4.  **Rinse Thoroughly:** Drain the soapy water. Rinse the paracord under cool, running water. Keep rinsing until all soap suds are gone. Soap residue can make paracord stiff. It can also attract more dirt. I always take my time rinsing. This ensures the paracord is truly clean.
5.  **Remove Excess Water:** Gently squeeze excess water from the paracord. Do not twist or wring it harshly. Use a clean towel to blot more water. This speeds up the drying process.

Hand washing is easy and effective. It keeps your paracord in great condition. I always feel good knowing my gear is clean.

### Machine Magic: Safely Cleaning Paracord in a Washer

Machine washing is a convenient way to clean larger amounts of paracord. It works well for durable items like lanyards or pet leashes. This method saves you time. However, you need to follow certain steps to protect the paracord. Improper machine washing can cause tangles or damage.

Here is how to safely machine wash your paracord:

1.  **Prepare the Paracord:** First, unravel any long sections of paracord. You can loosely coil them. Place the paracord inside a mesh laundry bag. This bag is very important. It prevents the cord from tangling. It also stops it from getting caught in the washing machine's agitator. I always use a mesh bag for my pet leashes. This prevents them from wrapping around other clothes.
2.  **Select Wash Cycle:** Choose a gentle cycle. Use cold or warm water, not hot. Hot water can shrink or damage nylon fibers. A delicate or hand wash setting is ideal.
3.  **Add Mild Detergent:** Use a small amount of mild laundry detergent. Avoid bleach or harsh cleaners. These can degrade the paracord's strength and color.
4.  **Wash and Rinse:** Start the washing machine. Let the cycle complete. Ensure the paracord gets a good rinse. Remove the paracord from the mesh bag after the wash. Check for any remaining soap residue. If you see any, you may need an extra rinse cycle.
5.  **Post-Wash Inspection:** Once the machine wash finishes, inspect the paracord. Look for any signs of damage or fraying. Machine washing is effective. Yet, it can be tougher on materials than hand washing. ### Tackling Tough Stains: Spot Cleaning and Deep Treatment

Sometimes paracord gets more than just surface dirt. Mud, grease, or sticky residues require special attention. Spot cleaning and deep treatment methods help remove these stubborn stains. These techniques target specific areas without needing to wash the entire cord. This saves time and effort. I often use spot cleaning for small, localized messes.

Here is how to tackle tough stains on your paracord:

*   **For Mud and Dirt:** Let the mud dry completely. Then, scrape off as much as you can with a dull knife or your fingernail. After removing the bulk, use a soft brush. Scrub the remaining dirt with a small amount of mild soap and water. Rinse the spot thoroughly with a damp cloth.
*   **For Grease or Oil:** Apply a small amount of grease-cutting dish soap directly to the stain. Let it sit for a few minutes. Gently rub the soap into the stain with your fingers or a soft brush. Rinse with warm water. Repeat this process if the stain remains. Dish soap is very effective against oil.
*   **For Sticky Residues (like sap or glue):** Try rubbing alcohol on a clean cloth. Dab the residue, do not rub. This helps dissolve it. You can also use a small amount of Goo Gone, but test it on a hidden spot first. Always rinse the area with water after using any solvents.
*   **For Odors:** If your paracord smells bad, a good soak can help. After cleaning, soak the paracord in a solution of warm water and a quarter cup of white vinegar. Let it sit for an hour. The vinegar neutralizes odors. Then, rinse it very well. Ensure all vinegar smell is gone. Air dry thoroughly.

These methods help keep your paracord ready for anything. I find targeted cleaning works wonders for specific issues. This saves me from doing a full wash every time. ### Drying Your Paracord: Crucial Steps for Longevity

Proper drying is as important as washing for your paracord. Incorrect drying can cause mildew, stiffness, or even damage to the fibers. You want your paracord to remain supple and strong. Air drying is the best method. It ensures the cord dries naturally without harsh heat. I always make sure my paracord is fully dry before storing it. This prevents any bad smells or mildew growth.

Follow these crucial steps for drying your paracord:

*   **Gentle Water Removal:** After washing, gently squeeze out excess water. Do not twist or wring the paracord. Hard wringing can stretch or damage the nylon. You can also roll the paracord in a clean, dry towel. Press down to absorb more moisture.
*   **Choose the Right Spot:** Hang your paracord to air dry. A clothesline, a drying rack, or even a hanger works well. Choose a spot with good air circulation. This helps the paracord dry evenly and quickly. Avoid direct sunlight. Direct sunlight can weaken nylon fibers over time. It can also cause colors to fade.
*   **Ensure Full Dryness:** Let the paracord dry completely. This might take several hours or even a full day. The drying time depends on humidity and cord thickness. Feel the paracord. It should be dry to the touch inside and out. Any dampness can lead to mildew. Mildew smells bad and can degrade the cord.
*   **Never Use High Heat:** Avoid using a clothes dryer or direct heat sources like a hairdryer. High heat can melt or damage the nylon fibers. It will make the paracord brittle and unusable. This applies to most synthetic ropes. Always stick to air drying for best results.

Proper drying keeps your paracord feeling new. It preserves its strength and flexibility. This extends its useful life significantly.

### Maintaining Cleanliness: Tips for Long-Term Paracord Care

Keeping your paracord clean does not end with washing and drying. Long-term care involves smart storage and preventative measures. These steps help reduce how often you need to clean your paracord. They also ensure it remains ready for use. I always practice good maintenance. This helps me rely on my paracord when I need it most.

Here are tips for long-term paracord care:

*   **Proper Storage:** Store your clean, dry paracord in a cool, dry place. Keep it away from direct sunlight. A gear bag, a drawer, or a storage box works well. Avoid storing it in damp environments. Dampness promotes mildew growth. Keep it coiled neatly. This prevents tangles and keeps it organized.
*   **Avoid Contaminants:** Try to keep your paracord away from harsh chemicals, oils, and strong solvents. These substances can weaken the nylon fibers. They can also cause permanent stains. If contact happens, clean the paracord immediately using the spot cleaning methods.
*   **Regular Inspection:** Periodically inspect your paracord. Look for signs of wear, fraying, or damage. Check for any dirt or grime accumulation. Addressing small issues early prevents bigger problems. This habit also reminds you to clean when needed.
*   **Use Protectors:** For frequently used items, consider using a protective sleeve or cover. This adds a barrier against dirt and abrasion. For instance, if you use paracord for tie-downs, a sleeve can prevent excessive mud buildup.
*   **Rotate Usage:** If you have multiple paracord items, rotate their use. This distributes wear and tear. It also gives you time to clean and maintain items between uses. ### FAQ Section

**Q1: Can I use bleach to clean paracord?**
No, you should not use bleach on paracord. Bleach is a harsh chemical. It can significantly weaken the nylon fibers. This makes the paracord unsafe to use. Bleach can also cause discoloration. Always use mild detergents or soaps instead.

**Q2: How often should I clean my paracord?**
The frequency depends on how often you use your paracord and how dirty it gets. For items used daily outdoors, like a dog leash, clean it every few weeks. For occasional use, clean it when it visibly looks dirty or feels stiff. Regular inspection helps you decide.

**Q3: Will washing paracord make it shrink?**
Nylon paracord generally does not shrink with proper washing. You must use cold or warm water. Avoid hot water, as high temperatures can potentially cause some shrinkage or damage to synthetic fibers. Always air dry your paracord to prevent heat-related issues.

**Q4: Can I put paracord in a dryer?**
No, do not put paracord in a dryer. The high heat from a clothes dryer can melt, shrink, or damage the nylon fibers. This makes the paracord brittle and compromises its strength. Always air dry your paracord completely in a well-ventilated area.

**Q5: What if my paracord still smells after cleaning?**
If your paracord still smells, it might need a deep odor treatment. After washing, soak the paracord in a solution of warm water and a quarter cup of white vinegar for an hour. Then, rinse it thoroughly and air dry it completely. Vinegar is excellent for neutralizing odors.

**Q6: How do I prevent my paracord from getting dirty quickly?**
Preventative measures help keep paracord clean longer. Store it in a clean, dry place. Avoid direct contact with mud, grease, and harsh chemicals when possible. You can also loosely coil it to prevent tangles that collect dirt. Regular gentle cleaning also helps prevent heavy buildup.
