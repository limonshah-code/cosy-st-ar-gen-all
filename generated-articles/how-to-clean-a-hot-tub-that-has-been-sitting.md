## How To Clean A Hot Tub That Has Been Sitting

Imagine the crisp evening air, a sky full of stars, and the inviting warmth of your hot tub. Then you remember: it has been sitting unused for months, or perhaps even years. A neglected hot tub can quickly turn into a murky, uninviting swamp, a far cry from the relaxing oasis it once was. You might be wondering if it is even possible to bring it back to life.

I understand that a hot tub that has been sitting can look like a daunting project. However, restoring it to a clean and safe condition is absolutely achievable with the right steps and a bit of effort. This guide will walk you through the entire process, from initial assessment to sparkling water, ensuring your hot tub is ready for enjoyment once more. We will cover everything from draining old water to deep cleaning the jets and filters, and finally, refilling and balancing the chemicals.

### Takeaway

Reviving a hot tub that has been sitting requires a thorough, multi-step cleaning process to ensure sanitation and functionality.
*   Start by inspecting the tub and gathering supplies.
*   Drain all old water completely.
*   Deep clean the shell, jets, and internal plumbing.
*   Clean or replace the filters.
*   Refill with fresh water and balance chemicals carefully.
*   Perform a shock treatment to sanitize the water.

To clean a hot tub that has been sitting, first drain all old water and physically remove any debris. Then, use a specialized hot tub flush product to clean the internal plumbing. Scrub the shell thoroughly with a hot tub-safe cleaner. Finally, clean or replace filters, refill with fresh water, and balance the chemicals, performing a strong shock treatment.

### Assessing the Situation: What's Been Sitting in Your Hot Tub?

Before you even think about cleaning, take a good look at your hot tub. Has it been sitting full of stagnant water, or was it drained but left uncovered? The condition of your neglected hot tub will dictate how much work is involved. A hot tub that has been sitting with old, green water will require a more intensive clean than one that was simply empty and uncovered.

Safety is your first priority. You should wear gloves and eye protection throughout the cleaning process, especially when dealing with chemicals. If the water is extremely foul, you might consider wearing a mask. Gather all your cleaning supplies beforehand to avoid interruptions. This includes a wet/dry vacuum, a garden hose, brushes, cleaning solutions, and test strips.

Look for visible signs of mold, mildew, algae, or even insect nests. Pay close attention to the waterline, jet openings, and corners where grime tends to accumulate. Documenting the initial state can also help you appreciate the transformation later. This initial assessment helps you prepare for the task ahead and ensures you have all the necessary tools at hand.

#### Pre-Cleaning Checklist

Before you dive in, make sure you have these items ready. Having everything accessible saves time and frustration. It also ensures you can complete each step thoroughly.

*   **Personal Protective Equipment:** Gloves, eye protection, and potentially a face mask.
*   **Drainage Tools:** Submersible pump (optional, but speeds up draining), garden hose.
*   **Cleaning Solutions:** Hot tub shell cleaner, specialized plumbing flush, filter cleaner. Do not use household cleaners, as they can cause foaming or damage components.
*   **Cleaning Tools:** Soft-bristle brush, scrub pad, wet/dry vacuum, sponge.
*   **Water Treatment:** Test strips, pH increaser/decreaser, alkalinity increaser, calcium hardness increaser, hot tub shock, fresh filters (or filter cleaning solution).
*   **Accessories:** Bucket, old towels.

Taking these precautions and preparing your tools makes the job much smoother. You are ready to tackle the physical cleaning steps once everything is assembled. It is important to avoid rushing this preparation stage.

### Draining and Initial Rinse: Clearing the Stagnant Water

The first major step in cleaning a hot tub that has been sitting is to completely drain all the old water. This water is likely full of contaminants, bacteria, and algae. You cannot effectively clean or sanitize the tub with this old water present. Find your hot tub's drain valve, usually located near the bottom of the tub or in the equipment compartment. Attach a garden hose to direct the water away from your home's foundation, landscaping, and storm drains if possible.

If the water is extremely dirty or green, or if the tub is very large, a submersible pump can significantly speed up the draining process. This avoids hours of waiting for gravity to do the job. Once the tub is empty, use a wet/dry vacuum to remove any remaining puddles or large debris that might have settled at the bottom. This ensures the tub is as dry as possible before the deep cleaning begins.

After draining, give the hot tub a preliminary rinse with fresh water from your garden hose. This helps to wash away loose debris, dirt, and any lingering biofilm from the surfaces. Focus on the walls, seats, and floor. Do not worry about getting it perfectly clean at this stage; this is just to clear the superficial grime. Repeat the rinse if necessary until most visible loose dirt is gone. This stage prepares the tub for serious scrubbing and internal cleaning. Properly handling your hot tub's drain system is important for overall maintenance. ### Deep Cleaning the Shell: Scrubbing Away Grime and Build-up

With the hot tub drained and rinsed, you can now focus on the shell's interior surfaces. This is where most visible grime, scum lines, and discoloration reside. Use a hot tub-specific shell cleaner. These cleaners are designed to be effective against hot tub grime without damaging the acrylic or fiberglass surface. Avoid harsh household cleaners like bleach or abrasive scrubbers, as these can permanently damage your tub.

Apply the cleaner according to the product instructions, usually by spraying it directly onto the surfaces. Let it sit for a few minutes to break down the dirt and oils. Then, use a soft-bristle brush or a non-abrasive scrub pad to thoroughly scrub all surfaces. Pay extra attention to the waterline, which often accumulates a stubborn "scum ring" from body oils and lotions. Also, scrub inside the cup holders and around the jets.

After scrubbing, rinse the entire interior of the hot tub thoroughly with fresh water. Ensure all cleaner residue is completely washed away. Any remaining cleaner can cause foaming when the tub is refilled. Use your wet/dry vacuum again to remove all rinse water from the bottom of the tub. A sparkling clean shell is a satisfying sight and indicates you are on the right track. Cleaning the tub surface thoroughly prevents future issues and contributes to overall hygiene. ### Flushing the Plumbing: The Invisible Battleground

The most overlooked and critical step when cleaning a hot tub that has been sitting is flushing the internal plumbing. Biofilm, a slimy layer of bacteria and other microorganisms, thrives in the warm, dark environment of hot tub pipes. Even if your tub looks clean, the pipes can harbor a significant amount of this unseen contamination. Simply draining and scrubbing the shell will not remove it.

You need a specialized hot tub plumbing cleaner or "jet flush" product. These products are designed to break down and dislodge biofilm from inside the pipes. Fill your hot tub with fresh water to just above the jets. Add the plumbing cleaner according to the manufacturer's instructions. Turn on the jets (without the air blowers) and let the water circulate for a recommended period, usually 30-60 minutes. You will likely see a significant amount of brown or black gunk being flushed out into the water. This is the biofilm leaving your pipes.

After the flush cycle, turn off the jets and let the water sit for another 30 minutes to an hour. This allows any remaining loosened debris to settle. Then, drain the hot tub completely again. You might need to rinse the tub shell one more time to wash away any remaining flushed-out debris. Skipping this step means you are refilling a tub with clean water that will immediately be contaminated by the biofilm lurking in the pipes. Ensuring your hot tub's drain is clear of blockages during this process is very important for effective cleaning. Similar to how one would want to avoid issues when [bathing a dog in a tub without clogging the drain](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-bathe-a-dog-in-a-tub-without-clogging-the-drain), ensuring a free-flowing drain is crucial here too.

### Filter Care and Replacement: The Hot Tub's Lungs

The filters are the hot tub's lungs, trapping debris, oils, and microscopic particles. If your hot tub has been sitting for a long time, the filters are probably heavily soiled, clogged with biofilm, or even degraded. You cannot have clean water without clean filters. Remove the existing filters from their housing.

First, give the filters a thorough rinse with a garden hose to remove loose debris. Spray water between each pleat to dislodge as much dirt as possible. For a deeper clean, soak the filters in a specialized filter cleaning solution overnight, or for the time recommended by the product. This solution dissolves oils, minerals, and other stubborn contaminants embedded deep within the filter material. After soaking, rinse the filters again very thoroughly to remove all the cleaning solution.

Inspect the filters carefully. Look for tears, rips, or brittle pleats. If the filter material is damaged, or if the filters are old (typically 1-2 years of regular use), it is best to replace them with new ones. New filters are relatively inexpensive compared to the cost of maintaining poor water quality. Always have at least two sets of filters so you can rotate them and always have a clean set ready. Proper filter maintenance significantly contributes to water clarity and overall hot tub health.

### Refilling and Shocking: Bringing Life Back to the Water

With the hot tub shell clean, pipes flushed, and filters ready (either clean or new), it is time to refill your hot tub with fresh water. Place your garden hose in the filter compartment or the main body of the tub to avoid airlocks in the plumbing lines. Fill the tub to the recommended water level, typically just above the highest jets. Once full, turn on the power to the hot tub and allow the pumps to run for a few minutes to purge any air from the lines.

Now, it is crucial to balance your water chemistry. Use reliable test strips or a liquid test kit to measure pH, alkalinity, and calcium hardness. Adjust these levels using appropriate hot tub chemicals.
*   **Alkalinity** (80-120 ppm): Adjust this first, as it buffers pH.
*   **pH** (7.4-7.6): Crucial for bather comfort and sanitizer effectiveness.
*   **Calcium Hardness** (150-250 ppm): Prevents equipment corrosion or scale build-up.
Always add chemicals slowly, one at a time, and allow for circulation before retesting.

Finally, "shock" the hot tub. This involves adding a large dose of sanitizer (usually chlorine or non-chlorine shock) to rapidly oxidize contaminants and kill any remaining bacteria or algae. Follow the product instructions for a "startup" or "heavy use" dose. Run the jets for at least 30 minutes with the cover off to allow the shock to work and dissipate. Retest the water after 24 hours. Your hot tub is now clean, sanitized, and ready for you to enjoy.

### Ongoing Maintenance: Keeping Your Revived Hot Tub Pristine

You have invested significant time and effort to clean a hot tub that has been sitting. Now, the goal is to keep it in pristine condition without needing another deep clean anytime soon. Consistent, simple maintenance will save you headaches and ensure your hot tub is always ready for use. Regular care prevents the buildup of grime and biofilm that made the initial cleaning so challenging.

Establish a routine for checking your water chemistry. Test the water at least 2-3 times per week, especially with frequent use. Adjust sanitizer levels (chlorine, bromine, or others) as needed to maintain a safe and clean environment. Monitor pH, alkalinity, and calcium hardness regularly and make adjustments to keep them within recommended ranges. Balanced water not only feels better on the skin but also protects your hot tub equipment from damage.

Regularly clean your filters. Rinse them with a hose at least once a month, and deep clean them with a filter solution every 3-4 months. Consider replacing them annually or as needed based on their condition. Wipe down the hot tub shell and waterline weekly to prevent scum buildup. Finally, always keep your hot tub covered when not in use. A good quality cover prevents debris from entering the water, reduces evaporation, and maintains heat efficiency. This diligent approach will extend the life of your hot tub and ensure endless hours of enjoyment.

### FAQ Section

#### How often should I drain and deep clean my hot tub?
You should drain and deep clean your hot tub every 3-4 months with regular use. If the hot tub has been sitting unused for an extended period, a full deep clean is always necessary before refilling. This frequency helps prevent biofilm buildup and keeps water fresh.

#### Can I use household bleach to clean my hot tub?
No, you should not use household bleach or other harsh household cleaners. Bleach can damage the hot tub's acrylic shell and internal components. It can also create excessive foam and be difficult to rinse completely. Always use hot tub-specific cleaning products designed for safe use.

#### What causes the scum line around my hot tub?
The scum line is typically caused by a combination of body oils, lotions, cosmetics, and other organic materials that accumulate at the water's surface. These substances can cling to the hot tub walls. Regular showering before using the hot tub and wiping down the waterline frequently can help prevent it.

#### How do I know if my hot tub filters need to be replaced?
Inspect your filters for tears, rips, or any sign of damage to the pleats. If the filter material is brittle, discolored even after cleaning, or if it no longer effectively clears the water, it is time for replacement. Most filters last 1-2 years with proper cleaning.

#### What is hot tub shocking and why is it important?
Hot tub shocking involves adding a concentrated dose of sanitizer (like chlorine or non-chlorine shock) to the water. This process oxidizes organic contaminants, breaks down chloramines (spent chlorine), and kills bacteria and algae. It is crucial for maintaining clear, clean, and safe water, especially after heavy use or when bringing a hot tub back from disuse.
