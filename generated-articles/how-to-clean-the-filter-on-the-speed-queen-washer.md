## Clean Your Speed Queen Washer Filter: A Complete Guide

Maintaining your laundry appliances is a smart habit. A clean Speed Queen washer filter helps your machine work its best. Ignoring this simple task can cause bigger problems. It ensures your clothes come out fresh and your washer lasts longer.

Many people overlook the filter, but it plays a big role. It catches lint, coins, and other small items. I found that a clean filter prevents drainage issues and unpleasant smells. This article will show you exactly how to clean the filter on your Speed Queen washer. We will cover locating the filter, gathering supplies, and a simple step-by-step cleaning process. ### Takeaway

*   **Locate the Filter:** Identify your washer's filter type and location. Speed Queen machines often have a drain pump filter accessible behind a lower panel.
*   **Prepare Tools:** Gather towels, a shallow pan, pliers, a brush, and a cleaning solution.
*   **Drain and Clean:** Turn off power, drain residual water, then remove and thoroughly clean the filter of all debris.
*   **Reassemble:** Securely reinsert the clean filter and panel.
*   **Regular Maintenance:** Clean the filter every 3-6 months for best performance.

To clean the filter on your Speed Queen washer, first, turn off the power and locate the drain pump filter, usually behind a bottom access panel. Place a shallow pan to catch water. Unscrew the filter by hand or with pliers, remove any lint and debris, then rinse it under warm water. Scrub any stubborn buildup with a brush. Reinstall the clean filter and close the panel.

## Locating the Filter on Your Speed Queen Washer

Finding the filter on your Speed Queen washer is the first step. Speed Queen models vary, but most share common filter locations. It is important to know which type of filter your machine uses. This helps you prepare for the cleaning task. I remember the first time I looked for it, I had to consult the manual.

Many modern Speed Queen washers, especially front-loaders, feature a drain pump filter. This filter sits at the bottom front of the machine. It is behind a small access panel. Always refer to your specific model's user manual. It provides the exact location and removal instructions.

### Drain Pump Filter Location

The drain pump filter is common in front-loading Speed Queen washers. It is designed to catch larger debris before it reaches the pump. This prevents clogs and damage to the pump. You will usually find a small square or rectangular panel. This panel is on the bottom front of your machine. It usually snaps or screws into place.

You might need a flathead screwdriver to gently pry it open. Be careful not to scratch the finish. Once open, you will see a cap or knob. This is the drain pump filter cap. It looks like a large plastic screw. Next to it, there might be a small hose. This hose allows you to drain excess water before removing the main filter. This design prevents a flood in your laundry room. It makes the cleaning process much neater.

If your top-loader has a removable lint filter, it usually pulls out. You then simply rinse it under water to clean it. If you are unsure if your top-loader has one, check your user manual. ## Step-by-Step Guide to Clean Your Speed Queen Washer's Drain Pump Filter

Cleaning your Speed Queen washer's drain pump filter is a straightforward process. You do not need to be a repair technician. I follow these steps every few months. It keeps my machine running smoothly. Let's walk through it together.

The first step is always safety. Disconnect your washer from the power outlet. This prevents accidental starts. Water and electricity do not mix. Next, prepare your workspace. Lay down old towels around the front base of the machine. Position your shallow pan directly under the access panel. This setup will catch any water that drains out. I always use a thick stack of towels for extra protection.

Now, locate the access panel. It is usually at the bottom front of the washer. Gently pry it open with a flathead screwdriver if needed. Be careful not to damage the machine's finish. Once the panel is open, you will see the filter cap and possibly a small drain hose. Use the drain hose first. Place its end into the shallow pan. Unscrew its cap to release any residual water. Let all the water drain out. This prevents a messy surprise when you remove the main filter.

### Removing and Cleaning the Filter

After draining the water, you are ready to remove the main drain pump filter.

1.  **Unscrew the Filter Cap:** Turn the large filter cap counter-clockwise. You might need pliers if it is tight. Be prepared for a small amount of water to still trickle out. Pull the filter straight out once it is loose.
2.  **Inspect and Clear Debris:** Look at the filter. It will likely be covered in lint, hair, coins, and other small items. Remove all visible debris by hand. I often find hair ties or buttons here. These things can ruin the pump if not caught.
3.  **Rinse Thoroughly:** Take the filter to a sink. Rinse it under warm, running water. Use your small brush or old toothbrush to scrub away any stuck-on grime or residue. Ensure the mesh is completely clear.
4.  **Soak (If Needed):** If the filter has stubborn buildup or smells bad, soak it. Use a solution of warm water and white vinegar or dish soap. Let it sit for 15-30 minutes. Then scrub and rinse again.
5.  **Clean the Housing:** While the filter is out, use your flashlight to inspect the filter housing. Wipe away any sludge or debris inside the opening. This prevents new blockages.

### Reassembling the Washer

Once the filter is sparkling clean, it is time to put everything back together.

1.  **Insert the Filter:** Carefully push the clean filter back into its housing. Turn it clockwise to tighten it. Make sure it is snug but do not overtighten it. An overtightened filter can be hard to remove next time. It also might damage the threads.
2.  **Secure the Drain Hose:** If your machine has a small drain hose, replace its cap. Secure the hose back in its clip or holder.
3.  **Close the Access Panel:** Snap or screw the access panel back into place. Ensure it is flush with the machine's surface.
4.  **Reconnect Power:** Plug your Speed Queen washer back into the power outlet.
5.  **Test for Leaks:** Run a short rinse or spin cycle without clothes. Check for any leaks around the filter area. If you see water, turn off the machine. Then recheck the filter's tightness and the panel's seal. I always do this to make sure I did not miss anything. If your Speed Queen top-loader does have a physical lint filter, it is usually easy to find. These are often located around the top rim of the wash tub. Preventive care goes beyond just cleaning the filter. It involves smart laundry practices. Always check pockets before washing clothes. This simple step stops many items from reaching the filter. ## Troubleshooting Common Filter Issues

Even with regular cleaning, sometimes problems arise. Your Speed Queen washer might still have issues. It is important to know how to troubleshoot them. This can save you a service call. I have faced a few issues myself. Knowing what to look for makes a big difference.

One common sign of a filter problem is slow drainage. You might see water lingering in the drum after a cycle. Or the cycle might take longer than usual to complete. Another indicator is an unpleasant odor. This smell often comes from stagnant water or trapped mildew. Loud noises during the drain cycle can also point to a filter issue. A small object might be rattling inside the pump area.

If you clean the filter and problems persist, do not panic. The issue might be elsewhere in the drain system. Or it could be a sign of a larger problem. Start by rechecking the filter installation. Make sure it is tight and correctly seated. Then, investigate other parts of the washer. A systematic approach helps pinpoint the real cause.

### Persistent Slow Drainage or No Drain

If your Speed Queen washer still drains slowly or not at all after cleaning the filter, check these points:

*   **Drain Hose Kink:** The drain hose might be kinked or crushed behind the machine. Pull the washer out slightly. Inspect the hose for any bends. Ensure it runs freely to the standpipe or sink.
*   **Drain Hose Clog:** The hose itself might have a clog further down. Sometimes, stubborn lint or hair accumulates. You might need to disconnect the hose (at the machine and the drain) to check. Carefully flush it with water.
*   **Household Drain Clog:** The problem might not be with the washer at all. Your household drain pipe might be clogged. Run water in a nearby sink. If it also drains slowly, the problem is with your home's plumbing. You would need to address that issue.
*   **Faulty Drain Pump:** If all else fails, the drain pump itself might be faulty. It might be worn out or damaged. This usually requires professional repair or replacement. However, filter cleaning often prevents this.

### Unpleasant Odors After Cleaning

You cleaned the filter, but the washer still smells bad. This is frustrating, but solvable.

*   **Mold/Mildew in Gasket (Front-Loaders):** For front-load Speed Queen washers, check the rubber door gasket. Mold and mildew often build up here. Wipe it thoroughly with a vinegar solution or bleach. Make sure to get into all the folds.
*   **Detergent Residue in Tub:** Sometimes, residue builds up inside the tub itself. Run a "clean washer" cycle. Use a washing machine cleaner or a cup of white vinegar. This helps sanitize the entire drum.
*   **Hard Water Scale:** Mineral deposits from hard water can contribute to odors. Use a descaling agent or vinegar regularly to combat this.
*   **Leave Door Ajar:** After each wash, leave the washer door slightly open. This allows air to circulate. It helps dry out the drum and prevents mold growth. I always do this.

Addressing these areas will typically eliminate lingering odors. Remember, a clean filter is one part of a fully clean machine. Keeping the entire system clean ensures fresh laundry every time. Regularly wiping down the interior and running cleaning cycles are good practices.
