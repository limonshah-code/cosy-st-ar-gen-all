## Differentiating Between Alligator Snapping Turtles and Common Snapping Turtles in Michigan

One of the most frequent reasons for confusion regarding Alligator Snapping Turtles in Michigan is their visual similarity to the native Common Snapping Turtle (**_Chelydra serpentina_**). While both species are large, powerful, and have a prehistoric appearance, there are distinct features that allow for accurate identification. Knowing these differences is critical for anyone who encounters a large turtle in Michigan's waters, preventing unnecessary alarm and ensuring proper reporting if a non-native species is suspected. I know many people mistake them, so let's clear up the confusion.

The most striking difference lies in their size and shell morphology. Alligator Snapping Turtles are significantly larger, with adults often weighing over 150 pounds and reaching shell lengths of up to 26 inches. Their shell is also distinct, featuring three prominent, ridged keels (raised lines) running from front to back, giving it a very rough, almost dinosaur-like appearance. The Common Snapping Turtle, while still large, is smaller, typically weighing 10-35 pounds with a shell length of 8-18 inches. Its shell is smoother and less ridged, though it may have some minor bumps, especially when young. If you're wondering how you can tell if it's a snapping turtle, these shell features are key. ### Key Identification Features to Look For

Here is a breakdown of the main features to help you distinguish between the two species:

*   **Size:**
    *   **Alligator Snapping Turtle:** Much larger, can exceed 200 pounds, with shell lengths over 2 feet.
    *   **Common Snapping Turtle:** Smaller, usually 10-35 pounds, shell length typically under 1.5 feet.
*   **Shell (Carapace):**
    *   **Alligator Snapping Turtle:** Three distinct, pronounced ridges (keels) running lengthwise, giving a very rugged, prehistoric look. The shell often appears more triangular or pointed at the back.
    *   **Common Snapping Turtle:** Smoother shell, often somewhat flattened or only slightly ridged, especially in older individuals.
*   **Head:**
    *   **Alligator Snapping Turtle:** Very large, triangular head with powerful jaws. The eyes are positioned on the sides of the head and face outwards.
    *   **Common Snapping Turtle:** Smaller, more rounded head. Eyes are positioned more forward.
*   **Tongue Lure:**
    *   **Alligator Snapping Turtle:** Possesses a unique, worm-like appendage on its tongue used to lure fish.
    *   **Common Snapping Turtle:** Does not have this tongue lure.
*   **Plastron (Underbelly):**
    *   **Alligator Snapping Turtle:** Small, cross-shaped plastron that does not allow the turtle to fully withdraw into its shell.
    *   **Common Snapping Turtle:** Larger plastron, though still small compared to other turtle species, offering slightly more coverage.

Understanding these differences is crucial. If you see a large turtle in Michigan, it is almost certainly a Common Snapping Turtle. However, if it exhibits the massive size and distinct ridged shell of an Alligator Snapping Turtle, it warrants further attention and reporting, as it is likely a non-native individual.
