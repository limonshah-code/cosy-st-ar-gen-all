## Clean Old Gas Out of Your Generator for Peak Performance

A generator provides crucial power during outages or for outdoor projects. However, a generator left with old gas can refuse to start or run poorly when you need it most. Old fuel degrades over time, causing serious problems for the engine. Learning how to clean old gas out of a generator is an essential skill for any generator owner.

This comprehensive guide shows you how to safely remove stale fuel and clean your generator's fuel system. We will cover draining the tank, cleaning components, and best practices for future fuel storage. You will restore your generator's reliability. Keep your power source ready for any situation.

### Takeaway

*   **Safety First:** Always use proper protective gear and work in a well-ventilated area.
*   **Drain Completely:** Remove all old, degraded fuel from the tank.
*   **Clean the Tank:** Flush out varnish and deposits from inside the fuel tank.
*   **Service Components:** Clean or replace the fuel filter and fuel lines.
*   **Carburetor Care:** Thoroughly clean the carburetor jets and bowl to remove clogs.
*   **Prevent Future Issues:** Use fuel stabilizer and proper storage methods.

To clean old gas out of a generator, you must first safely drain the stale fuel from the tank. Next, clean the fuel tank to remove varnish and sediment. Then, you should clean or replace the fuel filter and lines. Finally, you must thoroughly clean the carburetor to ensure proper fuel delivery.

### Why Old Gas Harms Your Generator

You might wonder why old gas causes problems. Gasoline does not last forever. Over time, its components break down and change. This degradation leads to specific issues inside your generator's fuel system.

Ethanol, commonly found in gasoline, attracts water. Water in the fuel tank causes phase separation. This means the water and ethanol mixture separates from the gasoline, forming a corrosive layer at the bottom of the tank. This layer can rust metal tanks and damage fuel system parts.

Gasoline also forms varnish and gum as it ages. These sticky residues cling to fuel lines, filters, and carburetor jets. They restrict fuel flow and can completely clog small passages. A clogged carburetor is a common reason generators fail to start or run rough.

When you use old gas, it burns inefficiently. This can lead to reduced power output and increased exhaust emissions. It also puts unnecessary strain on engine components. Regular fuel system maintenance, including removing old gas, helps prevent these expensive repairs.

### Safety First: Preparing for the Job

Working with gasoline requires serious safety precautions. Fuel is highly flammable, and its vapors can be dangerous. Always prioritize your safety before starting any generator maintenance. Proper preparation makes the cleaning process safer and more efficient.

Always work in a well-ventilated area. This helps disperse fuel vapors, reducing the risk of fire or inhaling harmful fumes. Never work near open flames, sparks, or any ignition sources. Turn off your generator completely and allow the engine to cool down before you begin.

Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE). This includes safety glasses to protect your eyes from splashes. You should also wear chemical-resistant gloves to protect your skin from gasoline. Keep a fire extinguisher close by as an added safety measure.

Have a suitable container ready for the old gasoline. This container should be approved for fuel storage. Do not use open buckets or containers not designed for gasoline. Dispose of old fuel responsibly at an authorized hazardous waste facility.

### Draining the Old Fuel Safely

Draining old fuel is the first critical step to restoring your generator. This process removes the degraded fuel that causes engine problems. Follow these steps carefully to ensure a safe and complete drain. Proper draining prevents contaminated fuel from entering the rest of your system.

First, ensure the generator is on a level surface. Place an approved fuel container directly under the fuel tank's drain point. Many generators have a petcock or a fuel line connection that can be disconnected. Refer to your generator's manual for its specific fuel drain location.

If your generator has a fuel shut-off valve, turn it to the "off" position. This prevents fuel from flowing into the carburetor while you prepare. Disconnect the fuel line leading from the tank to the carburetor. Be ready for fuel to start flowing once you disconnect it.

Open the fuel tank cap to allow air in, which helps the fuel flow out smoothly. Let all the old fuel drain into your collection container. You may need to tilt the generator slightly to get every last drop. Once empty, secure the fuel line and close any drain valves.

Remember to dispose of the old gasoline properly. Many local waste management centers accept used fuel. Do not pour it down drains or onto the ground. Responsible disposal protects the environment and prevents hazards.

### Cleaning the Fuel Tank

After draining the old fuel, cleaning the tank itself is vital. Old gas often leaves behind sticky varnish and sediment. These deposits can break loose and clog your fuel system later. A clean tank ensures that new fuel remains uncontaminated.

For plastic tanks, cleaning is relatively simple. You can use a mild degreaser or a dedicated fuel tank cleaner. Pour a small amount into the tank and slosh it around. This helps loosen any built-up residue. [Cleaning the inside of a plastic gas tank](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-inside-of-a-plastic-gas-tank) can remove these stubborn deposits.

For metal tanks, especially if rust is present, more aggressive methods may be needed. You can use specialized rust removers or even household items like vinegar. Vinegar is a gentle acid that helps dissolve rust. Fill the tank partially with vinegar and let it sit for several hours or overnight. Agitate the tank periodically to help scrub the rust. You can find detailed steps on [how to clean a rusty gas tank with vinegar](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-rusty-gas-tank-with-vinegar).

After using any cleaning solution, rinse the tank thoroughly with clean water. Ensure all cleaning agents are removed. Then, dry the tank completely. You can use compressed air or let it air dry in the sun. Any lingering moisture can cause new rust or contaminate fresh fuel. A clean tank helps extend the life of your entire fuel system.

### Servicing the Fuel System Components

Cleaning the fuel tank is only part of the job. You must also service the other components that handle fuel. These parts include the fuel lines and the fuel filter. Neglecting them means new fuel will still encounter old residue or blockages. This can quickly negate your tank cleaning efforts.

Start by inspecting the fuel lines. Look for any cracks, kinks, or signs of deterioration. Old fuel can make rubber lines stiff or brittle. If the lines look damaged, replace them. Clean fuel lines ensure a smooth flow of fuel to the engine.

Next, locate the fuel filter. Most generators have an inline fuel filter between the tank and the carburetor. This filter traps debris before it reaches the carburetor. Old fuel often leaves behind fine sediment that clogs these filters. A clogged filter restricts fuel flow, causing performance issues.

It is best practice to replace the fuel filter rather than trying to clean it. Fuel filters are inexpensive and easy to install. Simply disconnect the old filter and install a new one, ensuring the flow direction arrow points towards the engine. This simple replacement greatly improves fuel system efficiency. Replacing parts like these ensures your generator performs reliably. ### Carburetor Cleaning: A Detailed Look

The carburetor is the heart of your generator's fuel system. It mixes air and fuel in precise proportions for combustion. Old gas frequently clogs the tiny passages and jets inside the carburetor. A clogged carburetor is the most common reason a generator will not start or runs poorly. Thorough cleaning is essential.

First, you must remove the carburetor from the engine. Take pictures as you disassemble it. This helps with reassembly later. Carefully remove the float bowl, which often contains most of the gum and varnish deposits. You will likely see a sticky, discolored residue inside.

Disassemble the carburetor further, removing the float, pin, and main jet. Be very careful with these small parts. Use a specialized carburetor cleaner spray to clean all components. Spray into every small opening and passage. Pay close attention to the main jet and the idle jet. These are easily clogged due to their small size.

For stubborn clogs, a thin wire or a carburetor cleaning kit can help. Gently poke through the jets to ensure they are completely clear. Never use anything abrasive that could enlarge the holes. After cleaning, blow compressed air through all passages to remove cleaner residue and ensure clarity.

Inspect all gaskets and O-rings. Replace any that look worn or damaged. Reassemble the carburetor carefully, ensuring all parts are correctly seated. Then, reinstall the carburetor on the generator. This detailed cleaning process ensures optimal fuel delivery to your engine.

### Refueling and Testing Your Generator

After all the cleaning and reassembly, it is time to put fresh fuel into your generator. This step brings your generator back to life. Following proper refueling and testing procedures ensures a smooth start and reliable operation.

Before adding new fuel, make sure all fuel lines are reconnected and secure. Double-check any drain valves you opened earlier. Ensure the fuel filter is properly installed. Then, pour fresh, clean gasoline into the fuel tank. Use gasoline appropriate for your generator, often regular unleaded.

Add a high-quality fuel stabilizer to the new gas, especially if you plan to store the generator for more than a few weeks. Fuel stabilizer helps prevent the issues caused by old gas, keeping the fuel fresh for longer periods. This is a simple step that offers great long-term benefits.

Now, attempt to start the generator. You might need to pull the starter cord a few extra times to prime the system. The carburetor needs to fill with new fuel. Once it starts, let it run for a few minutes. Listen for smooth operation and check for any leaks.

If the generator runs rough, it might still have a tiny blockage or air in the fuel lines. Let it run for a bit, or try gently rocking it to help dislodge air bubbles. This final test confirms your hard work has paid off.

### Preventing Future Fuel Issues

Preventing fuel problems is far easier than fixing them. Proper storage and maintenance practices keep your generator ready for use. Taking a few proactive steps can save you time and money. This ensures your generator delivers reliable power every time you need it.

Always use fresh gasoline in your generator. Buy fuel in smaller quantities if you do not use your generator often. Gasoline starts to degrade within a few months. Using fresh fuel reduces the risk of varnish and gum buildup.

The most important preventative measure is using a fuel stabilizer. Add the recommended amount of stabilizer to your generator's fuel tank every time you fill it. Run the engine for 5-10 minutes after adding the stabilizer. This circulates the treated fuel throughout the entire fuel system, including the carburetor.

For long-term storage (over 30 days), consider completely draining the fuel tank and running the engine until it stops. This ensures no fuel is left in the carburetor. You can also run the generator dry after treating the fuel with stabilizer. Some people prefer using non-ethanol gasoline, which reduces water absorption issues.

Storing your generator in a cool, dry place also helps. Protect it from extreme temperatures and direct sunlight. Regular checks of fuel lines and filters prevent small issues from becoming big problems. These steps ensure your generator remains a dependable power source. ### FAQ Section

**How long can gas sit in a generator before it goes bad?**
Gasoline typically begins to degrade within 3-6 months. Its quality depends on factors like temperature and storage conditions. Ethanol-blended fuels can go bad even faster, especially when exposed to moisture. Always use fresh fuel for optimal generator performance.

**Can I mix old gas with new gas in my generator?**
Mixing old and new gas is not recommended. Old gas contains degraded components and potentially water, which can contaminate fresh fuel. Using only fresh, clean gasoline ensures proper engine function. Dispose of old fuel properly and start with a clean tank.

**What are the signs of bad gas in a generator?**
Signs of bad gas include a generator that will not start, runs rough, or lacks power. The fuel itself may appear dark, cloudy, or have a sour smell. You might also notice sticky deposits in the fuel tank or carburetor. These are clear indicators that the fuel has degraded.

**Is ethanol-free gas better for generators?**
Yes, ethanol-free gasoline is generally better for generators, especially small engines. Ethanol attracts water, leading to phase separation and corrosion over time. Ethanol-free fuel helps prevent these issues, extending the life of your fuel system components and keeping the fuel fresher for longer.

**How often should I clean my generator's fuel system?**
The frequency of cleaning depends on usage and storage. If you use your generator seasonally, clean the fuel system before storage and before the next season. If your generator sits for more than 3-6 months without fuel stabilizer, cleaning is often necessary before use. Regular use with stabilized fuel reduces the need for frequent deep cleaning.
