### Common Physical Differences

Many turtle species show sexual dimorphism. This means males and females have distinct physical features. Here are some common ways to differentiate them:

*   **Tail Length and Shape:** Male turtles often have longer, thicker tails than females. The cloaca (vent) might also be located further down the tail in males.
*   **Plastron Shape:** The plastron is the bottom shell. In many male turtles, the plastron is concave (curved inwards). This helps them fit better on a female's shell during mating. Females usually have a flat plastron.
*   **Claw Length:** Male aquatic turtles, like red-eared sliders, often have noticeably longer front claws. They use these claws during courtship displays.
*   **Size:** In some species, males are larger than females, while in others, females are larger. It varies greatly by species.
