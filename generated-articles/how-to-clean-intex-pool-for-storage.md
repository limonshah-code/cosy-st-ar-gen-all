## How To Clean Intex Pool For Storage

The swimming season has ended. You want to store your Intex pool until next year. Cleaning your Intex pool for storage correctly is important. It helps keep your pool in good shape. It also makes setting it up again much easier.

I remember my first time trying to store our pool. I thought a quick rinse was enough. Boy, was I wrong! Mold and mildew greeted me the next spring. This guide will help you avoid my mistakes. We will cover draining, cleaning, drying, and storing your pool. Proper care extends its life. It saves you money too. Let's make sure your Intex pool lasts for many seasons.

### Takeaway

To ensure your Intex pool remains pristine for next season, focus on these key steps:
*   **Complete Drainage:** Remove all water from the pool.
*   **Thorough Cleaning:** Scrub surfaces to remove dirt and algae.
*   **Absolute Drying:** Allow no moisture to remain before folding.
*   **Careful Storage:** Keep in a dry, cool, pest-free area.

Cleaning your Intex pool for storage involves completely draining it, thoroughly scrubbing all surfaces with mild soap, rinsing well, and ensuring the entire pool liner and frame parts are bone-dry to prevent mold and mildew before folding and storing in a cool, dry place.

### Why Proper Intex Pool Cleaning for Storage Matters

Closing your Intex pool for the season is more than just putting it away. It protects your investment. Proper cleaning prevents many problems. It ensures your pool is ready for fun next year. Skipping steps now creates big headaches later.

Think about mold and mildew. These grow fast in damp, dirty conditions. They can stain your pool liner permanently. They also create bad smells. A dirty pool left in storage can also attract pests. Rodents or insects might chew through the material. This causes leaks and damage.

Cleaning also extends the life of your pool. Dirt, chemicals, and sun exposure break down materials. Removing these elements protects the liner. It keeps the plastic flexible. This prevents cracks and tears. A clean pool means less work when you reopen it. You avoid scrubbing stubborn stains. You also start with fresh, clean water. I learned this the hard way with a stained liner once. Now, I always take the time to clean it right.

### Essential Tools and Supplies for Your Pool Cleaning Project

Getting ready is half the battle. You need the right tools for this job. Having everything organized makes the process smooth. It saves you trips back and forth. Gather your supplies before you start draining the pool.

Here is a list of what you will need:
*   **Garden Hose:** For rinsing the pool.
*   **Soft-Bristle Brush or Sponge:** To clean the liner without damage.
*   **Mild Soap or Pool Cleaner:** Choose a non-abrasive, non-toxic cleaner. Dish soap often works well.
*   **Bucket:** For mixing soap and water.
*   **Clean Towels or Mops:** For drying the pool thoroughly.
*   **Wet/Dry Shop Vacuum (Optional):** Helps remove stubborn water puddles.
*   **Broom or Leaf Blower (Optional):** Speeds up the drying process.
*   **Storage Bags/Containers:** For small parts and the pool itself.
*   **Gloves:** Protect your hands from cleaners.

I always make a checklist. This helps me ensure I do not forget anything important. Having everything nearby makes the job much faster. It reduces stress. You can even use a specific pool vacuum for more efficient cleaning during the season, but for storage cleaning, manual scrubbing is usually required after draining. ### Draining Your Intex Pool Safely and Completely

Draining your Intex pool is the first major step. You must remove all water before you can clean it properly. Safety is important during this process. Make sure to consider where the water will go. This prevents flooding or damage to your yard.

Intex pools have a drain plug. You will find it usually on the side or bottom. Attach your garden hose to this plug. Direct the other end of the hose to a safe drainage area. This might be a storm drain or a low-lying part of your yard. Avoid draining large amounts of chlorinated water onto plants or into natural bodies of water. The chlorine can harm them.

Gravity will do most of the work. As the water level drops, the pool sides will start to sag. This helps push out more water. Once the main drain stops, you will still have some water. This usually collects in low spots. I use a submersible pump for faster draining. For the last bits of water, a wet/dry shop vacuum works wonders. You can also use buckets or sponges. Ensure you get as much water out as possible. Every drop left means a longer drying time and risk of mold. If you've been wondering how to handle dirt at the bottom, once the water is mostly gone, it becomes easier to see and address any remaining debris, though for regular cleaning without draining, you might need different methods as described in [how to clean dirt from the bottom of the pool](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-dirt-from-the-bottom-of-the-pool).

### Thoroughly Cleaning Intex Pool Surfaces

After draining, it is time to scrub. This is where you remove all the dirt, grime, and algae. A clean pool liner is essential for long-term storage. Do not rush this part. Pay attention to every surface.

Start with the bottom and sides of the pool. Use your soft-bristle brush or sponge. Mix a small amount of mild soap with water in your bucket. Apply this solution to the pool surfaces. Scrub in small sections. Focus on any visible stains or slimy spots. Algae can stick firmly. You might need a little extra effort on these areas. Remember to be gentle to avoid scratching the liner.

Once you have scrubbed a section, rinse it immediately with your garden hose. This prevents the soap from drying on the liner. Keep rinsing until all soap residue is gone. Soap left behind can attract dirt later. It can also cause discoloration. Do not forget the top ring, if your Intex pool has an inflatable one. Clean the outer walls too. Any part that comes in contact with water needs cleaning. I find that working from the top down helps. Gravity assists in washing dirt away. ### Drying Your Intex Pool to Prevent Mold and Mildew

This is perhaps the most critical step for successful storage. Any remaining moisture leads to mold and mildew. These can ruin your pool liner. You need to get the pool completely, thoroughly dry. This means bone dry, inside and out. Do not skip this step or cut it short.

After rinsing, let the sun and air do most of the work. Leave the pool open and spread out. Allow it to air dry for several hours. A sunny, breezy day is perfect for this. Flip the liner periodically. This exposes all surfaces to the air. You want both sides of the liner to dry completely. I often check for damp spots with my hands.

For any stubborn wet spots or if the weather is not ideal, you have other options. Use clean towels to wipe down the liner. You can use a wet/dry shop vacuum to suck up any lingering puddles. A leaf blower can also help. It pushes air across the surface, speeding up evaporation. Pay special attention to seams and folds. Moisture tends to hide there. I usually wait an extra day just to be safe. It is much better to over-dry than under-dry. This prevents all future problems from dampness. This step helps prolong the life of your Intex pool.

### Disassembling and Organizing Intex Pool Components

Once your pool liner is perfectly dry, it is time to take it apart. This process is straightforward. Keep track of all the pieces. Losing a single part can make reassembly difficult. A systematic approach helps avoid frustration next spring.

Start by detaching any accessories. This includes the filter pump, skimmer, ladder, and hoses. Clean these items too. Wipe down the pump and hoses. Ensure they are also dry before storage. Disconnect the frame poles if your pool has them. Most Intex frame pools use a push-button locking system or simple slide-in connections. Make sure to consult your pool's manual for specific disassembly instructions. Some poles might be color-coded or numbered. This helps during reassembly.

Organize all the smaller components. Put bolts, pins, and caps into separate, labeled plastic bags. This is incredibly helpful. There is nothing worse than searching for a tiny screw when you are eager to set up the pool. I use clear zip-top bags and a permanent marker. It makes a huge difference. Then, carefully fold the dry pool liner. Avoid sharp creases that could damage the material. Lay it flat and fold it into a manageable size. If you have an inflatable ring, ensure it is fully deflated, similar to how you might [deflate an Intex air mattress](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-do-you-deflate-an-intex-air-mattress), to make folding easier and prevent damage.

### Optimal Storage Solutions for Your Intex Pool

Storing your Intex pool correctly protects it from the elements. It keeps pests away. A good storage location ensures your pool stays in great condition. Choose a spot that is dry, cool, and dark. Avoid extreme temperatures.

A shed, garage, or basement are ideal locations. Make sure the area is free from rodents and insects. These pests can chew through the liner or make nests. You might want to use airtight containers for smaller parts. This adds an extra layer of protection. Store the folded pool liner in a large storage container or a heavy-duty storage bag. This protects it from dust and accidental punctures. Avoid storing the pool directly on concrete floors. Concrete can draw moisture. Place it on a pallet or a piece of cardboard instead.

Keep the pool away from direct sunlight. UV rays can degrade the material even in storage. Also, avoid storing it near chemicals or sharp objects. These could damage the liner. Remember, proper storage is just as important as proper cleaning. It extends the life of your pool for many years of enjoyment. I always make sure my pool is off the floor and sealed tightly. This gives me peace of mind all winter.

### FAQs About Cleaning Your Intex Pool for Storage

**How often should I clean my Intex pool filter before storing the pump?**
You should clean your Intex pool filter thoroughly right before storing the pump. Remove the filter cartridge and rinse it with a hose until clean. For sand filters, perform a complete backwash and rinse cycle. Ensure the filter housing and all pump components are dry before storing. This prevents mold and mineral buildup during the off-season. For more regular filter maintenance, you can check [how often should you clean the pool filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-often-should-you-clean-the-pool-filter).

**Can I use bleach to clean my Intex pool liner before storage?**
No, I do not recommend using bleach directly on your Intex pool liner for cleaning. Bleach can be too harsh. It can potentially damage the PVC material. It may also fade the color. Instead, use a mild, non-abrasive soap or a cleaner specifically designed for pool liners. Always rinse thoroughly to remove all cleaning product residue.

**What if I cannot get my Intex pool completely dry?**
It is extremely important to get your Intex pool completely dry. Even a small amount of moisture can lead to mold, mildew, and liner degradation. If weather conditions prevent full air drying, use towels, a wet/dry shop vacuum, or even a leaf blower to assist. You can also bring the liner indoors to a warm, dry area for a day or two to ensure it is bone-dry before folding and storing.

**How do I prevent my Intex pool liner from cracking during winter storage?**
Proper cleaning and drying are key to preventing cracking. Ensure the liner is completely dry before folding. Fold it loosely rather than tightly to avoid sharp creases. Store the folded liner in a cool, dry place. Avoid extreme cold or heat. Extreme temperatures can make the PVC material brittle. Keeping it off concrete floors also helps prevent moisture absorption, which contributes to material degradation.

**Should I clean the outside of my Intex pool before storage?**
Yes, you should clean both the inside and outside of your Intex pool liner. The exterior can accumulate dirt, dust, and environmental grime. Cleaning the outside ensures a fully clean pool. It also helps prevent any growth of mold or mildew on those surfaces during storage. A quick wipe down with mild soap and water is usually sufficient for the exterior.

**What should I do with the pool chemicals before storing the pool?**
You should never store pool chemicals with your pool equipment. Store chemicals in their original containers. Keep them in a cool, dry, well-ventilated area. They should be away from direct sunlight and any heat sources. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for chemical storage. Dispose of any expired chemicals safely according to local regulations.
