## Restore Your Tackle: How To Clean Fishing Lures Effectively

Imagine you are on the water, the sun is out, and the fish are biting. You cast your favorite lure, but it does not perform as well as it used to. Perhaps it looks dull, or the hooks are a bit sticky. This happens when your fishing lures accumulate grime, debris, and even rust over time. Learning how to clean fishing lures is a crucial part of maintaining your fishing gear.

Proper lure maintenance keeps your tackle in top condition. Clean lures attract more fish and last longer. This article will show you simple steps to clean your lures. We will cover different lure types, tackle rust, and storage tips. Get ready to revitalize your fishing arsenal.

### Takeaway

*   **Boost Lure Performance:** Clean lures shine better and move naturally, making them more appealing to fish.
*   **Prevent Rust and Damage:** Regular cleaning stops rust from forming on hooks and metal parts, preserving the lure's integrity.
*   **Extend Lure Lifespan:** Proper care ensures your favorite lures stay functional and effective for many seasons.
*   **Maintain Sharp Hooks:** Cleaning allows you to inspect and sharpen hooks, ensuring better hook-sets.
*   **Save Money:** Extending the life of your lures reduces the need for frequent replacements.

To clean fishing lures effectively, gently scrub them with mild soap and warm water to remove grime. Inspect and sharpen hooks, and dry all parts completely before storage to prevent rust and maintain performance.

### Why Cleaning Fishing Lures Matters for Better Catches

Every cast puts your fishing lures into harsh environments. Water, dirt, algae, and fish slime cling to them. Over time, this buildup degrades your lure's appearance and action. A dirty lure might not flash correctly or swim true.

Clean fishing lures, on the other hand, look appealing to fish. They reflect light properly and move as designed. This makes a significant difference in attracting strikes. You want every advantage possible when fishing.

Beyond attracting fish, cleaning prevents damage. Rust is a major enemy of fishing lures, especially on hooks and split rings. Rust weakens metal, making hooks brittle and less effective. Regular cleaning stops rust before it starts, saving your valuable tackle. This simple maintenance step prolongs the life of your investment. It helps you keep your favorite lures in your tackle box for many years of successful fishing.

### Essential Tools and Materials for Lure Cleaning

Gathering the right tools makes the cleaning process much easier. Most items are common household supplies. You do not need specialized equipment to keep your fishing lures clean. Having everything ready before you start saves time.

Here are the basic items you will need:

*   **Mild Soap:** Dish soap works well. Avoid harsh detergents that can damage lure finishes. A gentle formula is key.
*   **Warm Water:** This helps dissolve grime and makes cleaning more effective. Cold water is less efficient.
*   **Soft Brushes/Sponges:** Old toothbrushes are excellent for scrubbing tight spots and textured surfaces. Soft sponges clean broad areas.
*   **Microfiber Cloths:** Use these for drying and polishing without scratching the lure surface. They are highly absorbent.
*   **Small Pliers:** These help with changing or removing hooks if necessary. Pliers also aid in bending wire components.
*   **Hook File or Sharpener:** Keep your hooks needle-sharp. A dull hook misses fish.
*   **Rust Remover (Optional):** For stubborn rust spots on hooks or metal components. Use sparingly and carefully.
*   **Lubricant (Optional):** A small amount of silicone spray can protect metal parts on some lures, like spinnerbait swivels.
*   **Rubbing Alcohol (Optional):** Effective for removing sticky residues or algae from plastic bodies. Use with caution on painted surfaces.

Having these tools at hand ensures a thorough and safe cleaning process. Preparing your workspace is also a good idea. Lay down an old towel or newspaper to protect surfaces. This setup makes cleaning efficient and straightforward.

### General Steps to Clean All Fishing Lures

Cleaning your fishing lures involves a few straightforward steps. These steps apply to most lure types. Following them ensures a thorough clean without causing damage. I find it helpful to set up an assembly line for my lures.

Here is a general guide to cleaning your fishing lures:

1.  **Rinse Off Loose Debris:** Start by rinsing each lure under warm tap water. This removes any loose dirt, mud, or fish scales. Use your fingers to gently rub off obvious grime. This initial rinse prevents scratching the lure during deeper cleaning.
2.  **Apply Mild Soap:** Put a few drops of mild dish soap into a bowl of warm water. Dip a soft brush or sponge into this soapy solution. Gently scrub the entire lure. Pay attention to cracks, crevices, and textured areas. The soft bristles of a toothbrush work great for this task.
3.  **Clean Hooks and Split Rings:** Do not forget the metal parts. Use the soapy brush to scrub hooks and split rings. Look for any signs of rust or buildup. This step is vital for hook integrity. If you are cleaning other fishing gear, remember to also maintain your [fishing reel](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-fishing-reel) and [fishing rod cork handles](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-cork-on-a-fishing-rod) for complete gear care.
4.  **Rinse Thoroughly:** Rinse the lure again under clean, warm water. Make sure all soap residue is gone. Soap left on a lure can attract dirt or leave a film. It is important that the lure is completely clean.
5.  **Dry Completely:** This is a critical step. Pat the lure dry with a clean microfiber cloth. Then, let it air dry in a well-ventilated area. Make sure there is no moisture left, especially on hooks and metal components. Any remaining water can lead to rust. You can use a fan to speed up the drying process.
6.  **Inspect and Sharpen Hooks:** Once dry, inspect each hook for sharpness. Use a hook file or sharpener to hone dull points. A sharp hook means more fish caught. Also, check for bent or damaged hooks and replace them if necessary. This general approach keeps your lures ready for action.

### Cleaning Hard Baits: Crankbaits, Jerkbaits, and Topwaters

Hard baits like crankbaits, jerkbaits, and topwater lures have durable bodies, often made of plastic or wood. Their hard surfaces make them relatively easy to clean. However, they often have intricate designs, lip systems, and multiple hooks that require attention. My personal collection of crankbaits benefits greatly from this specific care.

Here is how to clean these specific lure types:

*   **Initial Rinse and Soak:** Begin by rinsing the hard bait under warm water to remove loose debris. For stubborn grime, you can let the lure soak in a bowl of warm, soapy water for 5-10 minutes. This helps loosen stuck-on dirt and algae. Do not soak for too long, especially if the lure has clear coats or delicate paint.
*   **Gentle Scrubbing:** Use a soft toothbrush or sponge with mild dish soap to scrub the lure's body. Pay close attention to the diving lip, hook hangers, and any textured patterns. These areas tend to accumulate more grime. For deeper crevices, a cotton swab can reach where a brush cannot. Ensure you clean the entire surface thoroughly.
*   **Detailing the Eyes and Paint:** The eyes of hard baits are crucial attractors. Clean them carefully with a soft cloth or cotton swab. Be gentle around painted details and decals to avoid rubbing them off. If your lure has holographic finishes, be extra careful not to scratch them. Avoid abrasive cleaners at all costs.
*   **Hook and Hardware Inspection:** Carefully clean all treble hooks and split rings. Use your soapy toothbrush. If you find any rust, address it immediately. You can find detailed steps on [how to clean rusty fishing hooks](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-rusty-fishing-hooks) to ensure your lure's integrity. Also, check the integrity of the split rings and hook hangers. Replace any damaged components.
*   **Thorough Rinse and Dry:** Rinse the lure completely under clean, warm water. Ensure no soap residue remains. Then, dry it thoroughly with a microfiber cloth. Air dry completely before storing. Any moisture left behind, especially on the metal parts, will cause rust. This is a common issue with hard baits.

Following these steps keeps your hard baits looking new and performing their best. Regular maintenance ensures their vibrant colors and realistic actions continue to entice fish. This careful attention extends the life of your valuable hard baits.

### Caring for Soft Plastic Lures and Jigs

Soft plastic lures and jigs require a slightly different approach than hard baits. Soft plastics are more porous and can absorb scents and colors. Jigs often combine metal heads with soft skirts or plastic trailers. Cleaning these properly maintains their flexibility, scent, and overall effectiveness.

Here is how to clean soft plastic lures and jigs:

*   **Soft Plastic Lures:**
    *   **Separate and Inspect:** Remove soft plastics from your tackle box. Inspect each one for tears, holes, or significant damage. Discard heavily damaged ones.
    *   **Mild Soap Bath:** Fill a basin with warm, soapy water using mild dish soap. Place the soft plastics in the water and let them soak for 10-15 minutes. This helps loosen dirt, salt, and old scent residue.
    *   **Gentle Cleaning:** Gently rub each soft plastic lure with your fingers or a soft cloth. Do not scrub too hard, as this can damage the material. For stubborn spots, a very soft brush might work.
    *   **Rinse and Dry:** Rinse them thoroughly under clean, cool water. Make sure all soap is gone. Pat them dry with a clean towel. Air dry completely before storing. Store soft plastics in separate bags or compartments to prevent colors from bleeding together. Do not store them with hard baits, as this can cause warping.
*   **Jigs:**
    *   **Disassemble (If Possible):** If your jig has a removable skirt or trailer, take it apart. This allows for better cleaning of individual components.
    *   **Clean the Jig Head:** Use a soft brush with soapy water to clean the painted jig head. Check for chipped paint or corrosion. If you have a painted head, be gentle to avoid further damage.
    *   **Clean the Skirt:** Soak silicone or hair skirts in warm, soapy water. Gently swish them around. Rinse thoroughly and let them air dry completely. Ensure the skirt strands do not tangle.
    *   **Hooks and Weedguards:** Clean the jig hook meticulously. Use a hook sharpener to ensure it is razor sharp. Inspect the weedguard for bending or damage. A bent weedguard can affect the jig's action. If you need to clean other fishing gear components, remembering [how to clean fishing reel bearings](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-fishing-reel-bearings) is also helpful for a full maintenance routine.
    *   **Reassemble and Store:** Once all parts are dry, reassemble the jig. Store jigs neatly in tackle trays, ideally with compartments to prevent skirts from tangling or crushing.

Caring for soft plastics and jigs helps maintain their lifelike action and scent. This attention ensures they remain effective fish attractors trip after trip.

### Tackling Rust: Cleaning Rusty Hooks and Metal Components

Rust is the archenemy of fishing lures, especially for hooks and other metal components. It can weaken hooks, make them brittle, and dull their points. Addressing rust immediately saves your lures and ensures successful hook-sets. I often find myself dealing with rust after saltwater fishing trips.

Here is how to effectively clean rusty hooks and metal components:

*   **Assess the Rust:** First, inspect the extent of the rust. Light surface rust is easier to remove than deep, pitted corrosion. If a hook is severely rusted and compromised, it is often better to replace it entirely. Safety and performance are key.
*   **Mild Abrasives for Surface Rust:** For light rust, you can often use a mild abrasive. Steel wool (very fine grade) or a brass brush can gently scrub away surface rust. Apply light pressure to avoid scratching the underlying metal too much. A baking soda paste (baking soda mixed with a little water) also works as a gentle abrasive. Apply the paste, let it sit for a few minutes, then scrub.
*   **Specialized Rust Removers:** For more stubborn rust, consider using a commercial rust remover designed for metal. Always read the product instructions carefully. Apply it with a cotton swab or small brush only to the rusty areas. Keep it away from painted lure bodies or soft plastic parts, as it can cause damage. Ensure good ventilation when using chemical removers.
*   **Vinegar Soak (Natural Option):** White vinegar is a natural acid that can dissolve rust. You can soak rusty hooks or metal rings in a small bowl of white vinegar for 30 minutes to a few hours, depending on the rust severity. Check frequently. After soaking, scrub the rust away with a brush. Rinse thoroughly with water afterwards to neutralize the acid.
*   **Lubrication and Protection:** After removing rust and thoroughly drying the metal components, apply a light coat of silicone-based lubricant or a corrosion inhibitor. This adds a protective barrier against future rust. This is especially important for saltwater fishing lures.
*   **Hook Sharpening:** Once the rust is gone, always sharpen the hooks. Rust dulls points significantly. A sharp hook penetrates better, increasing your chances of landing fish. Replacing rusty hooks is often the best option if the rust is deep or structural. This is a direct approach to prolonging your lure's life. ### Drying and Storing Your Clean Fishing Lures

After cleaning your fishing lures, proper drying and storage are just as important as the cleaning itself. Incorrect drying or storage can undo all your hard work, leading to rust, tangles, or damage. I have learned the hard way that a few extra minutes here save hours later.

Here are the best practices for drying and storing your clean fishing lures:

*   **Thorough Drying is Key:** This is the most critical step after rinsing. After patting your lures dry with a microfiber cloth, let them air dry completely. Place them on a clean, dry surface like a towel or paper towels. Ensure good air circulation. A fan can help speed up the process. Do not rush this step. Even a small amount of residual moisture can lead to rust on hooks and metal components. Check all crevices and joints for water.
*   **Individual Air Drying:** Spread out your lures so they are not touching. This allows air to circulate around each one. Hanging them from a clothesline or a drying rack can work well for certain types of lures, allowing water to drip away freely.
*   **Organized Storage:** Once bone dry, store your lures in an organized manner. Tackle boxes with individual compartments are ideal. This prevents hooks from tangling together, which saves time on the water. It also stops lures from rubbing against each other, which can chip paint or damage finishes.
*   **Consider Desiccants:** For humid environments or long-term storage, placing small desiccant packets (like silica gel) in your tackle box can absorb moisture. This provides an extra layer of protection against rust.
*   **Protect Soft Plastics:** Store soft plastic lures separately from hard baits. The plastics can react with certain hard plastic tackle box materials, causing them to melt, warp, or bleed color. Keep them in their original packaging or in specialized soft plastic storage bags.
*   **Categorize Your Lures:** Organize lures by type, size, or color. This makes it easy to find the right lure when you are out fishing. Labeling compartments can be very helpful. Proper storage keeps your clean fishing lures in pristine condition. This ensures they are ready for your next fishing adventure.

### Special Considerations for Saltwater Fishing Lures

Saltwater environments are notoriously harsh on fishing gear. Salt is corrosive and accelerates rust formation like nothing else. If you regularly fish in saltwater, your lures require extra attention and specific cleaning protocols. I learned this quickly after my first few saltwater trips.

Here are special considerations for cleaning saltwater fishing lures:

*   **Immediate Rinse After Every Trip:** This is non-negotiable. As soon as you finish fishing in saltwater, rinse all your lures thoroughly with fresh, clean tap water. Do this before putting them back in your tackle box. This washes away corrosive salt crystals before they can bond to the metal.
*   **Soak in Warm Water:** For heavily used saltwater lures, a short soak (15-30 minutes) in a basin of warm, fresh water can help dissolve stubborn salt and grime. You can add a small amount of mild dish soap to this bath. Avoid hot water, as it can sometimes affect glue or plastic components on certain lures.
*   **Focus on Hooks and Hardware:** Pay extra attention to hooks, split rings, and swivels. These metal parts are most vulnerable to salt corrosion. Scrub them well with a soft brush during the cleaning process. Rust can form on these components very quickly in a saltwater environment.
*   **Rust Prevention is Key:** After cleaning and drying, consider applying a corrosion-inhibiting spray or a light coat of silicone lubricant to all metal components. Products specifically designed for saltwater use provide the best protection. This extra step forms a barrier against salt.
*   **Inspect Regularly:** Even with diligent cleaning, saltwater lures need regular, close inspection. Check for any signs of pitting, weakening, or structural damage on hooks and split rings. Replace any compromised components immediately. Corroded hooks can fail when you hook a big fish.
*   **Storage in Saltwater-Specific Boxes:** Some tackle boxes are designed with better seals to keep moisture out, or they are made from materials more resistant to saltwater effects. Storing saltwater lures separately from freshwater lures can also be a good practice to prevent cross-contamination of salt. Proper care for saltwater lures ensures their longevity and performance. This also helps you land those big game fish when the opportunity arises.

### Maintaining Lure Action: Inspection and Minor Repairs

Cleaning your fishing lures is also an opportunity to perform crucial inspections and minor repairs. A lure’s action is vital for attracting fish. Any small issue can affect how it swims, flashes, or vibrates. Regular checks keep your lures performing their best. I make this a part of my post-fishing routine.

Here is how to maintain lure action through inspection and minor repairs:

*   **Check Diving Lips and Bill:** For crankbaits and jerkbaits, the diving lip or bill is critical for its swimming action. Inspect it for cracks, chips, or bends. A damaged lip will cause the lure to run crooked or lose its depth. Small nicks can sometimes be smoothed with fine sandpaper, but a severely damaged lip usually means replacing the lure.
*   **Examine Body Integrity:** Look for cracks, punctures, or signs of water intrusion, especially in hard baits. Water inside a hard bait can ruin its balance and action. If you find small cracks, a bit of waterproof epoxy or super glue can seal them. This prevents further damage.
*   **Test Swivels and Snaps:** On spinnerbaits, buzzbaits, and some jig designs, swivels and snaps allow components to move freely. Test these to ensure they are not stiff or corroded. A drop of light oil can sometimes free a stiff swivel. Replace any that are rusted or compromised.
*   **Inspect Wire Components:** Check the wire frames on spinnerbaits and buzzbaits for bends or fatigue. A bent wire can cause the blade to spin improperly or the lure to swim off-kilter. Gently bend wires back into shape with pliers if needed. Look for any signs of metal fatigue that could lead to breakage.
*   **Evaluate Blade Movement:** On lures with blades (spinnerbaits, inline spinners), ensure the blades spin freely and without obstruction. Clean any gunk or debris that might be impeding their rotation. A dull blade might not flash as effectively.
*   **Hook and Split Ring Assessment:** Beyond rust, check hooks for proper alignment. Bent hook points or shanks can be carefully straightened with pliers. Ensure split rings are not opening up or showing signs of weakness. Replace any damaged hooks or rings to maintain strong hook-sets. This proactive approach to cleaning and repair ensures your fishing lures remain highly effective. It extends their life and keeps them ready for your next fishing adventure.

### Frequently Asked Questions About Cleaning Fishing Lures

#### How often should I clean my fishing lures?
Clean your fishing lures after every few trips, especially if fishing in saltwater. A quick rinse after each use is always a good idea. For a deep clean, once a month during active fishing season or before long-term storage works well. This routine prevents buildup and rust.

#### Can I use household cleaners on my fishing lures?
Stick to mild dish soap and warm water. Avoid harsh household cleaners, as they can strip paint, damage plastic, or leave residues. Chemicals can also deter fish. Always test a small, inconspicuous area first if you are unsure about a cleaner.

#### What if my lures have a strong fish smell?
A mild soap and water wash usually removes fish odors. For stubborn smells, a short soak in a dilute solution of baking soda and water can help. Rinse very thoroughly afterwards. Some anglers believe a residual fish smell can attract more fish, so this is often a personal preference.

#### How do I remove algae or slime from my lures?
For algae and slime, a warm water rinse with mild dish soap and a soft brush is usually effective. Rubbing alcohol can also be used for tougher spots, but use it sparingly and avoid painted areas. Ensure a complete rinse to remove all residue.

#### Is it okay to leave soft plastics on hooks when storing?
It is generally best to remove soft plastics from hooks before storage. The plastic can react with the metal hook, causing discoloration or even corrosion over time. Storing them separately also helps prevent the plastic from warping or losing its shape.

#### How should I store my fishing lures to prevent damage?
Store lures in tackle boxes with individual compartments to prevent tangles and paint chips. Ensure they are completely dry before storage. For extra protection, especially in humid climates, place desiccant packets in your tackle box. Store soft plastics separately to prevent reactions.
