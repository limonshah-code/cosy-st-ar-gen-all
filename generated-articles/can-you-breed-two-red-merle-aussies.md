## Breeding Two Red Merle Aussies: Is It Safe?

Red Merle Australian Shepherds are beautiful dogs. Their striking patterned coats and often captivating blue eyes draw many admirers. They are popular family pets and working dogs. Because of their unique look, people often wonder about breeding them. A common question arises: "Can you breed two Red Merle Aussies?" ### Takeaway

Breeding two Red Merle Australian Shepherds is strongly discouraged due to severe health risks for offspring.
*   **High Risk of Double Merle Puppies:** This pairing has a 25% chance of producing puppies with two copies of the merle gene (MM).
*   **Severe Health Issues:** Double merle dogs often suffer from significant vision impairment (blindness) and hearing loss (deafness).
*   **Ethical Responsibility:** Responsible breeders avoid merle-to-merle breedings to prevent suffering.
*   **Safe Pairings Exist:** Always breed a merle dog with a non-merle dog to ensure puppy health.

**Can you breed two Red Merle Aussies?**

No, you should not breed two Red Merle Aussies together. This pairing creates a high risk of producing "double merle" puppies. These puppies often suffer from severe health defects. They can have significant vision and hearing problems. Responsible breeders strictly avoid such pairings.

### Understanding the Merle Gene in Australian Shepherds

The merle gene is fascinating. It creates the unique mottled coat patterns seen in Australian Shepherds. This gene is dominant. A dog needs only one copy of the merle gene to display the merle pattern. We label the merle gene as 'M'. The non-merle gene is 'm'.

A Red Merle Australian Shepherd has one copy of the merle gene (Mm). This means the dog displays the merle pattern. Their coat is reddish-brown with lighter patches. Their eyes can be blue or partly blue. This single merle gene contributes to their striking appearance. It is important to know how this gene works.

The merle gene affects eumelanin. Eumelanin is the black or brown pigment. In merle dogs, the gene dilutes random patches of this pigment. This creates the mottled effect. Red merles have a base coat of red or liver. The merle gene then lightens parts of it. This creates the lighter, marbled areas.

A dog with an 'Mm' genotype is a standard merle. This includes Red Merles and Blue Merles. These dogs are generally healthy. They have typical vision and hearing. The problem arises when two 'Mm' dogs are bred together. This can lead to a 'MM' genotype.

It's crucial to understand merle genetics for breeding. Knowing the genetic makeup prevents serious health problems. Breeders use genetic testing. This helps them identify the exact genes of their dogs. This information guides responsible breeding decisions. It protects the health of puppies.

### The Perils of Merle-to-Merle Breeding

Breeding two Red Merle Aussies is a high-risk decision. Both parent dogs carry one copy of the merle gene (Mm). When these two dogs breed, there are specific genetic outcomes. Each puppy inherits one gene from each parent. There are three possible combinations.

Let us look at the probabilities. There is a 25% chance of a puppy inheriting 'MM'. This means the puppy gets two copies of the merle gene. This dog is called a "double merle." There is a 50% chance of a puppy inheriting 'Mm'. This puppy will be a normal merle, like its parents. There is also a 25% chance of a puppy inheriting 'mm'. This puppy will be a solid-colored dog. It will not have the merle pattern.

The main concern is the "MM" genotype. Double merle dogs often have severe health issues. These issues affect their eyes and ears. These conditions are not curable. They cause a lifetime of challenges for the dog. They also cause heartbreak for owners.

Responsible breeders never intentionally create double merles. They understand the genetic risks. They prioritize puppy health over coat color. Avoiding merle-to-merle pairings is a fundamental rule in ethical breeding. This rule applies whether the merles are red or blue. All merle dogs carry the same gene responsible for these risks. It is not a matter of trying to get more merle puppies. It is a matter of avoiding severe disabilities. Breeding involves moral responsibility. This responsibility extends to the health of every single puppy.

### Health Risks for Double Merle Aussies

Double merle Australian Shepherds face severe health challenges. These issues stem from having two copies of the merle gene (MM). The merle gene plays a role in pigment development. When duplicated, it disrupts normal development. This disruption affects specific areas of the body.

The most common problems are vision and hearing defects.
*   **Vision Impairment:** Double merle dogs often have severe eye abnormalities.
    *   **Microphthalmia:** Their eyes may be abnormally small.
    *   **Colobomas:** Parts of the eye structure, like the iris or retina, may be missing.
    *   **Abnormal pupils:** Pupils can be oddly shaped or non-functional.
    *   **Cataracts:** Lenses can become cloudy.
    *   **Blindness:** Many double merle dogs are partially or completely blind. They may be born blind. Or their vision can deteriorate over time. Even if they have blue eyes, as some [Red Merle Aussies do](https://cosyhomecreation.com/do-all-red-merle-aussies-have-blue-eyes), it does not mean their vision is healthy if they are double merle.

*   **Hearing Loss:** Auditory problems are also common.
    *   **Partial Deafness:** Some dogs can hear only certain sounds or frequencies.
    *   **Complete Deafness:** Many double merle dogs are completely deaf. This means they cannot hear at all. The merle gene can affect the pigment cells in the inner ear. These cells are essential for hearing. Without them, sound cannot be properly transmitted.

These conditions significantly impact a dog's quality of life. A deaf and blind dog needs constant supervision. They require specialized training. They face many daily struggles. They cannot navigate their environment easily. They can startle easily. They are more prone to accidents. These dogs need a lot of patience and care.

Some people refer to double merles as "lethal whites." This term is misleading. Double merle dogs are not truly lethal. They often live long lives. However, they live with severe disabilities. This term highlights the severity of their health issues. It emphasizes the ethical problem of breeding them. Breeders must consider the lifelong implications for these puppies. They must prevent this outcome. It is a serious issue that responsible breeders strive to avoid. ### Responsible Breeding Practices for Red Merle Aussies

Responsible breeding is critical for the health of Australian Shepherds. It involves careful planning. It means making ethical choices. The primary goal is to produce healthy puppies. This includes avoiding genetic conditions like those seen in double merles.

When breeding a Red Merle Australian Shepherd, you must select the right mate. The key is to avoid breeding two merle dogs together. This applies to all merle colors, red or blue. The safest and most recommended pairings involve a merle dog with a non-merle dog.

Here are the recommended pairings for a Red Merle Aussie:

*   **Red Merle (Mm) to Solid Red (mm):** This pairing produces healthy puppies. All puppies will inherit one merle gene (Mm) or no merle gene (mm). There is no risk of producing double merles (MM). The puppies will be either merle or solid colored.
*   **Red Merle (Mm) to Solid Black (mm):** Similar to solid red, this is a safe pairing. Puppies will be either merle or solid. They will be healthy.
*   **Red Merle (Mm) to Bi-Color or Tri-Color (mm):** Dogs with bi-color (two colors) or tri-color (three colors) coats are non-merle. They carry no merle gene. This is a safe and common pairing. The offspring will be healthy. Some puppies will be merle. Some will be non-merle.

Before breeding, genetic testing is essential. A simple DNA test can confirm a dog's merle status. This test identifies if a dog is 'Mm' (merle), 'mm' (non-merle), or 'MM' (double merle). This information guides the breeding process. It removes any guesswork. Reputable breeders always test their dogs.

Responsible breeders also screen for other common health issues. They check for hip and elbow dysplasia. They check for eye conditions like cataracts. They use genetic tests for various inherited diseases. These tests ensure the parents are healthy. This reduces the risk of passing on problems to puppies.

Understanding the proper pairing options is crucial. It ensures the health and well-being of the next generation of Australian Shepherds. ### FAQ Section

#### Q1: What is a "double merle" dog?

A double merle dog inherits two copies of the merle gene (MM), one from each parent. This happens when two merle dogs are bred together. Double merles often appear largely white. They usually have severe vision and hearing impairments. These can range from partial loss to complete blindness and deafness.

#### Q2: Can a Red Merle Aussie carry the "hidden" merle gene?

No, a "hidden" merle gene is not possible in the sense of a non-merle dog secretly carrying merle. The merle gene is dominant. If a dog has one copy (Mm), it will show the merle pattern. However, some merle patterns can be very light or "cryptic." Genetic testing is needed to confirm if a seemingly solid dog is actually cryptic merle.

#### Q3: Are all double merle Aussies deaf and blind?

Not all double merle Aussies are completely deaf and blind, but a very high percentage suffer from some degree of impairment. Many are born completely deaf and/or blind. Others may have partial hearing or vision, or develop issues later. The severity varies, but significant sensory deficits are extremely common and expected outcomes.

#### Q4: What are safe breeding partners for a Red Merle Australian Shepherd?

A Red Merle Australian Shepherd (Mm) should only be bred with a non-merle dog (mm). Safe partners include solid-colored Aussies (red, black, blue, or tri-color) that do not carry the merle gene. This pairing ensures no puppies inherit two copies of the merle gene, preventing double merle health issues.

#### Q5: Is it legal to breed two merle Aussies?

While it may not be explicitly illegal in all jurisdictions, breeding two merle Aussies is considered highly unethical by reputable breeders and breed clubs worldwide. It is a practice that knowingly produces puppies with severe genetic defects. Many breed registries will not register double merle puppies or will register them with restrictions.

#### Q6: What if I accidentally bred two Red Merles?

If you accidentally bred two Red Merles, it's important to be prepared for the potential outcome. Seek veterinary guidance immediately. Have the puppies checked for vision and hearing problems by an experienced vet. If double merle puppies are born, they will require specialized care and finding them homes will be more challenging. Always test parents before future breedings.
