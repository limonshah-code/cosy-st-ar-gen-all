# How To Clean A Submersible Water Pump

Ever wonder what keeps your submersible water pump running smoothly? It works hard, often out of sight, deep in water. A dirty pump can quickly lose power. It might even stop working entirely. Keeping your submersible pump clean is very important for your water system.

This article gives you clear steps to clean a submersible water pump. We will talk about safety, the tools you need, and how to properly clean each part. You will learn to spot common issues and make your pump last longer. Let's make sure your water pump stays efficient and reliable.

## Takeaway

*   **Prioritize Safety:** Always disconnect power before touching your pump.
*   **Gather Tools:** Collect brushes, mild soap, and a descaling solution.
*   **Inspect Thoroughly:** Check for wear or damage during cleaning.
*   **Clean Components:** Remove debris from the intake screen and impeller.
*   **Rinse Well:** Ensure no cleaning agents remain on pump parts.
*   **Reassemble Correctly:** Put the pump back together with care.

## Main Query Answer

To clean a submersible water pump, first disconnect power and carefully remove it from the water. Disassemble accessible parts like the intake screen and impeller. Scrub away sediment and debris using a brush and mild detergent or a descaling solution for mineral deposits. Rinse all components thoroughly and reassemble the pump carefully.

## Why Cleaning Your Submersible Water Pump Matters

Your submersible water pump is a vital part of your water system. It sits in water and moves liquid where it needs to go. Over time, dirt, sand, and minerals build up on pump parts. This buildup affects how well the pump works.

A dirty pump has to work much harder. This extra effort uses more electricity. It also causes parts to wear out faster. You might notice weaker water flow or a pump that runs more often. Regular cleaning helps your pump deliver water efficiently. It keeps your energy bills lower.

If you do not clean your pump, it can break down completely. Sediment can clog the impeller, which is the spinning part that pushes water. Mineral deposits can harden on surfaces. This prevents the pump from moving water. A clean pump means a reliable water supply for your home or garden.

## Essential Safety Precautions Before You Start

Safety is the first thing you must consider when working with a submersible water pump. These pumps run on electricity and handle water. Mixing water and electricity is very dangerous. Never begin any work on the pump without taking safety steps.

Always disconnect the power supply to the pump. Find the circuit breaker that controls the pump and turn it off. If it is a plug-in pump, simply unplug it from the wall. This step prevents accidental electrocution. It keeps you safe while you work.

Wear protective gear to shield yourself from grime and chemicals. Gloves protect your hands from dirt and cleaning solutions. Safety glasses protect your eyes from splashes or flying debris. Some pumps can be heavy, so lift with your legs, not your back, to prevent injury. Refer to your pump's owner's manual for specific safety instructions.

## Tools and Materials You'll Need for Cleaning

Gathering the right tools before you start makes the cleaning job easier. You do not want to stop halfway through to find a missing item. Having everything ready saves time and effort. Most of these items you might already have around your home.

You will need a few types of brushes. A stiff brush helps remove tough dirt and algae from the pump's exterior. A smaller, softer brush, like an old toothbrush, works well for detailed areas. Buckets are useful for rinsing parts and catching dirty water. A mild dish soap mixed with water can create an effective cleaning solution.

For mineral deposits, white vinegar or a specific descaling solution is helpful. Always check if the descaling solution is safe for your pump's materials. Old towels or rags are great for wiping parts dry. Sometimes, you may need basic hand tools like a screwdriver or pliers for minor disassembly. A garden hose provides water for rinsing.

## Preparing and Removing Your Submersible Pump

Getting your submersible pump ready for cleaning involves careful steps. Proper preparation ensures both your safety and the pump's integrity. It starts with making sure the pump is completely powered down. This is the most important step before you touch anything.

First, disconnect the pump from its power source. Unplug it if it has a cord, or turn off the circuit breaker. This removes any electrical hazard. Next, you need to remove the pump from its location. Whether it is in a well, a pond, or a sump pit, carefully lift it out. Be aware that the pump will be heavy and possibly wet.

As you lift the pump, avoid pulling it by the power cord. Always handle the pump by its body or designated lifting points. Let any water drain from the pump as you bring it out. Once out, place the pump on a protective surface, like an old tarp or cardboard. Give it an initial rinse with a garden hose to remove loose dirt and debris. This pre-rinse makes the detailed cleaning much easier.

## Detailed Cleaning of Your Submersible Pump's Components

Now that your pump is out and rinsed, we can begin the detailed cleaning. This step involves taking apart the accessible sections of the pump. We will clean each part individually. This ensures all dirt and mineral buildup are removed.

Start by examining the pump's exterior. Look for the intake screen or filter basket. This is the first line of defense against debris. Remove the intake screen. It might be held by clips or a few screws. Scrub the screen thoroughly with your stiff brush and soapy water. You want to clear all trapped leaves, sand, and other particles. This step is similar to [how to clean the pool pump basket](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-pool-pump-basket), ensuring free water flow.

Next, focus on the impeller area. This is where the pump moves water. Some pumps allow easy access to the impeller, while others require more disassembly. If you can access it, use your small brush to clean around the impeller blades. Remove any string, hair, or stubborn debris. These items can wrap around the impeller and prevent it from spinning. If the impeller is stuck, check for hard mineral deposits. For general water system maintenance, knowing [how to clean a water filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-water-filter) can also help prevent debris from reaching your pump.

For mineral deposits, use white vinegar. Soak affected parts in vinegar for a few hours. This helps dissolve calcium and lime buildup. You can also use a commercial descaling solution, but read the instructions carefully. After soaking, scrub the areas again. Rinse all components thoroughly with clean water. Make sure no cleaning solution remains on the parts. Before reassembling, check all seals and O-rings. Replace any that look cracked or damaged. Finally, put the pump back together in reverse order of disassembly. Ensure all connections are secure.

## Common Issues and Troubleshooting During Cleaning

You might encounter a few issues while cleaning your submersible pump. Knowing what to look for helps you address them effectively. One common problem is a stuck impeller. Sediment or string can bind it tightly. Gently try to dislodge the obstruction with a thin tool. Do not force it, as you might break the blades.

Another issue is excessive scale buildup. Hard water can leave thick mineral deposits. If vinegar does not work, try a stronger, pump-safe descaling product. Always follow product instructions carefully. Ensure proper ventilation. These chemicals can be strong.

Sometimes, you might find damaged parts during inspection. Look for cracks in the housing or impeller. Check the power cord for fraying or cuts. Inspect seals and O-rings for wear. If a major component is broken, cleaning alone will not fix it. You might need to order replacement parts or consider professional repair. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I clean my submersible pump?
The cleaning frequency depends on its use and water quality. For dirty water applications, clean every 3-6 months. For cleaner well water or sump pump use, cleaning once a year is often enough. Always observe your pump's performance; decreased flow is a sign it needs attention.

### Can I use harsh chemicals to clean my pump?
No, avoid harsh, abrasive chemicals. These can damage the pump's seals, plastic parts, or motor windings. Stick to mild dish soap, white vinegar, or descaling solutions specifically designed for pumps or water systems. Always rinse thoroughly after using any cleaning agent.

### What if my pump doesn't work after cleaning?
First, recheck all electrical connections and ensure power is restored. Confirm that all parts are reassembled correctly and securely. The impeller must spin freely. If it still does not work, check for clogs you might have missed or look for any damaged components identified during cleaning. You might need to consult the manufacturer's troubleshooting guide.

### How do I know if my pump needs cleaning?
Common signs include reduced water flow or pressure. You might notice the pump running more frequently than usual. Strange noises, such as grinding or rattling, can also signal a dirty or obstructed pump. A visual inspection might show visible sediment or algae buildup on the intake screen.

### Is cleaning a submersible well pump different?
The basic cleaning steps for a submersible well pump are similar. However, removing and reinstalling a well pump can be more challenging. Well pumps are often deeper and connected to piping. You might need specialized lifting equipment or professional help to safely extract and lower a well pump.
