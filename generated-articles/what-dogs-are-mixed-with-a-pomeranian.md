## Discovering What Dogs Are Mixed With A Pomeranian

Have you ever wondered about the fluffy, spirited little dogs that seem to combine the best traits of different breeds? Many of these delightful companions are the result of mixing a Pomeranian with other dogs. The Pomeranian, known for its vibrant personality and striking foxy face, makes an excellent parent breed for creating designer dogs. I find these mixes fascinating because they offer a unique blend of characteristics, often resulting in truly one-of-a-kind pets. This article will explore the diverse range of breeds that are commonly mixed with Pomeranians. We will look at their typical traits, appearance, and what makes each mix special, helping you understand the unique world of Pomeranian hybrid breeds.

### Takeaway

When considering a Pomeranian mix, remember these key points:

*   **Diverse Traits:** Each Pomeranian mix combines the Pomeranian's small size and lively spirit with distinct characteristics from the other parent breed.
*   **Unpredictable Outcomes:** Hybrid breeds can inherit any combination of traits from either parent, making their exact appearance and temperament unpredictable.
*   **Variety of Needs:** Research specific mix needs, including grooming, exercise, and training, as these vary greatly depending on the mixed breed.
*   **Health Considerations:** Be aware of potential health issues from both parent breeds.
*   **Reputable Breeders:** Always seek reputable breeders who prioritize health and temperament for any designer dog.

Pomeranians are commonly mixed with various small to medium-sized dog breeds to create designer dogs. These mixes often combine the Pomeranian's fluffy coat, lively personality, and small stature with traits from breeds like the Husky, Chihuahua, Poodle, or French Bulldog, resulting in unique and beloved companions.

## The Popular Pomsky: Pomeranian and Siberian Husky Mix

The Pomsky is perhaps one of the most famous Pomeranian mixes, capturing hearts worldwide with its wolf-like appearance in a compact size. This mix results from breeding a Pomeranian with a Siberian Husky. Breeders typically use a female Husky and a male Pomeranian to ensure a safe pregnancy due to the significant size difference between the parent breeds. The Pomsky is a designer dog known for its captivating looks, often featuring the Husky's striking blue eyes and facial markings, combined with the Pomeranian's fluffy coat and smaller stature.

I find that the appeal of the Pomsky lies in its miniature Husky resemblance. Their coat can be thick and plush, ranging in colors like black, white, gray, brown, and red, often with the distinctive Husky mask. Their size varies considerably, usually falling between 10 to 40 pounds, depending on which parent's genetics are more dominant. Temperament-wise, Pomskies are intelligent and energetic. They inherit the Husky's playful and sometimes stubborn nature, alongside the Pomeranian's bold and confident personality. This means they need consistent training and early socialization to ensure they grow into well-behaved adults.

Pomskies thrive on activity and mental stimulation. They are not content being couch potatoes. Daily walks, playtime, and puzzle toys are essential to keep them happy and prevent destructive behaviors. Their intelligence means they learn quickly, but their stubborn streak can make training a challenge for first-time dog owners. I always recommend positive reinforcement methods. Grooming is also a significant commitment; their double coat sheds considerably, especially during seasonal changes, requiring regular brushing to prevent mats and control shedding. Potential owners must be ready for this level of care.

### Pomsky Health and Care

Like all breeds, Pomskies can inherit health issues from their parent breeds. Common concerns include patellar luxation, eye problems (like cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy), allergies, and dental issues. Responsible breeders will conduct health screenings on parent dogs to minimize these risks. I believe it's important for prospective owners to ask for health clearances.

Their dietary needs are similar to other active small-to-medium dogs. A high-quality dog food suitable for their age, size, and activity level is best. Portion control is important to prevent obesity. Regular veterinary check-ups, vaccinations, and parasite control are vital for a long, healthy life. With proper care, a Pomsky can live for 12 to 15 years. They make wonderful companions for active families who can dedicate time to their training, exercise, and grooming needs.

## The Lively Pomchi: Pomeranian and Chihuahua Mix

The Pomchi is a popular and adorable mix resulting from breeding a Pomeranian with a Chihuahua. This hybrid combines the feisty spirit of the Chihuahua with the fluffy charm of the Pomeranian, creating a small dog with a big personality. I often see Pomchis as delightful companions for those seeking a lap dog that also enjoys playful antics. They are known for their portability and devotion to their owners.

Pomchis typically weigh between 4 to 10 pounds, making them quite small. Their appearance can vary widely, inheriting features from either parent. They might have the longer, fluffy coat of a Pomeranian or the shorter, smoother coat of a Chihuahua, or even a mix of both. Their ears can be erect like a Chihuahua's or slightly floppier. Coat colors are diverse, including black, brown, cream, white, and various combinations. Each Pomchi truly looks unique.

In terms of temperament, Pomchis are often alert, intelligent, and confident. They can be very affectionate and loyal to their family members. However, they may also inherit the "small dog syndrome" from both parents, meaning they can be prone to barking and can be assertive if not properly socialized. Early socialization is crucial for a Pomchi to ensure they are comfortable around strangers and other animals. I recommend exposing them to various sights and sounds from a young age.

### Caring for Your Pomchi Companion

Pomchis, despite their small size, are quite active and enjoy playtime. Short daily walks and indoor games are usually sufficient to meet their exercise needs. Mental stimulation is also important; they enjoy puzzle toys and learning new tricks. Their intelligence makes them eager to please during training sessions, but consistency is key. Housetraining can sometimes be a challenge for small breeds, so patience is a must.

Grooming depends on their coat type. A longer-haired Pomchi will require regular brushing to prevent tangles and reduce shedding, while a short-haired one needs less frequent brushing. ## The Adorable Pomapoo: Pomeranian and Poodle Mix

The Pomapoo is a charming and intelligent designer dog created by mixing a Pomeranian with a Poodle, typically a Toy or Miniature Poodle. This mix aims to combine the Poodle's intelligence and low-shedding coat with the Pomeranian's fluffy appearance and playful nature. I often hear that people choose Pomapoos for their hypoallergenic potential, making them a good option for allergy sufferers. They are also known for their affectionate and adaptable personalities.

Pomapoos are generally small dogs, weighing between 5 to 15 pounds, depending on the size of the Poodle parent. Their coats can vary from the Poodle's curly, low-shedding texture to the Pomeranian's longer, fluffier double coat, or a combination of both. Many Pomapoos have a soft, wavy coat that falls somewhere in between. Colors are diverse, reflecting the wide range of Poodle and Pomeranian hues, including black, white, brown, cream, red, and parti-colors. Their expressive eyes and often button noses contribute to their appealing look.

These dogs typically inherit the Poodle's intelligence and eagerness to please, combined with the Pomeranian's lively and sometimes mischievous streak. Pomapoos are often quick learners and excel in obedience training. They are affectionate and enjoy spending time with their families, often forming strong bonds. They can be prone to barking if not properly trained, a trait inherited from both vocal parent breeds. I believe consistent training and early socialization are key to developing a well-behaved Pomapoo.

### Nurturing Your Pomapoo's Well-being

Pomapoos are moderately active dogs that enjoy daily walks and indoor play. Their exercise needs are manageable for apartment living, provided they get sufficient mental stimulation. They thrive on interaction and can become bored if left alone for too long, which might lead to destructive behaviors. Puzzle toys and training sessions are excellent ways to engage their intelligent minds.

Grooming requirements for a Pomapoo depend heavily on their coat type. Those with a curlier, Poodle-like coat will need regular brushing to prevent mats and professional grooming every 6-8 weeks. Dogs with a fluffier, Pomeranian-like coat will also need frequent brushing to maintain coat health and manage shedding. Dental care is crucial, as small dogs are prone to dental issues.

Pomapoos typically have a lifespan of 12 to 15 years. They can be susceptible to health conditions common in both Poodles and Pomeranians, such as patellar luxation, tracheal collapse, dental disease, and eye conditions. Responsible breeders will screen their dogs for these issues. With attentive care, including a balanced diet, regular exercise, and routine veterinary check-ups, a Pomapoo can be a healthy and delightful companion, bringing joy and lively energy to any home.

## The Unique French Pom: Pomeranian and French Bulldog Mix

The French Pom, also known as a Frenchie Pom, is an intriguing and relatively uncommon mix of the playful Pomeranian and the charming French Bulldog. This hybrid brings together two breeds with very distinct appearances and temperaments, resulting in a dog that is truly unique. I find that this mix can be quite unpredictable in terms of appearance, making each French Pom a delightful surprise. Owners are often drawn to their adorable combination of traits.

French Poms vary significantly in appearance due to the dramatic differences between their parent breeds. They typically weigh between 8 to 25 pounds, with some leaning more towards the larger French Bulldog size and others remaining smaller like the Pomeranian. Their coats can range from the fluffy, long fur of a Pomeranian to the short, smooth coat of a French Bulldog. Some may even have a medium-length coat. They might inherit the French Bulldog's bat ears or the Pomeranian's erect, foxy ears. Their facial structure can be a blend, often resulting in a slightly flattened nose with a more defined muzzle. Temperamentally, the French Pom can inherit traits from both sides. French Bulldogs are known for being affectionate, mellow, and clownish, while Pomeranians are spirited, bold, and energetic. This mix often results in a dog that is loving and playful, but can also have a stubborn streak. They tend to be good companions, eager to be part of family activities. Early socialization is particularly important for this mix to ensure they are well-adjusted to different environments and people. I recommend consistent, positive reinforcement training from a young age.

### Essential Care for Your French Pom

French Poms have moderate exercise needs. They enjoy short walks and playtime, but owners must be mindful of potential brachycephalic (short-nosed) issues if they inherit more French Bulldog characteristics. Overheating can be a concern for those with flatter faces. Their exercise routines should be tailored to their individual respiratory capabilities. Mental stimulation through toys and training helps keep them engaged.

Grooming depends on their coat type. A longer coat will require more frequent brushing to prevent mats, while a shorter coat is easier to maintain. Regardless of coat length, regular ear cleaning and nail trims are important. Due to the French Bulldog lineage, monitoring for skin folds is necessary to prevent infections. These include respiratory problems common in French Bulldogs (like Brachycephalic Obstructive Airway Syndrome), patellar luxation, eye problems, and skin allergies. Their lifespan generally ranges from 10 to 14 years. Choosing a reputable breeder who performs health screenings is crucial. Despite the potential challenges, a French Pom can be a delightful, affectionate, and charming addition to the right home, offering a unique blend of two beloved breeds.

## The Athletic Pomaussie: Pomeranian and Australian Shepherd Mix

The Pomaussie is a less common but increasingly recognized mix that combines the spirited Pomeranian with the intelligent and energetic Australian Shepherd. This pairing creates a dog with a fascinating blend of characteristics, often resulting in a highly trainable and active companion in a smaller package. I find this mix particularly interesting because it brings together a working breed with a companion breed, often yielding a versatile dog.

Pomaussies vary significantly in size, generally weighing between 10 to 30 pounds, depending on which parent breed they favor. Their appearance is highly variable. They often inherit the Australian Shepherd's striking merle coat patterns and eye colors (blue, amber, or heterochromia), combined with the Pomeranian's fluffy double coat and foxy face. Their ears might be erect like a Pom's or semi-prick like an Aussie's. Each Pomaussie is truly unique, showcasing a blend of their parents' distinctive looks.

In terms of temperament, Pomaussies are typically intelligent, energetic, and loyal. They inherit the Aussie's strong work ethic and trainability, alongside the Pomeranian's bold and sometimes mischievous personality. This makes them quick learners but also requires consistent mental and physical stimulation. They thrive on having a "job" to do, whether it's learning tricks, participating in dog sports, or simply being an active companion. Early socialization is vital to ensure they are well-adjusted, especially given the Aussie's herding instincts.

### Keeping Your Pomaussie Engaged and Healthy

Pomaussies have moderate to high energy levels. They need daily exercise, including walks, playtime, and opportunities for mental challenges. Their intelligence means they can become bored and destructive if not adequately stimulated. I always suggest activities like agility, obedience training, or puzzle toys to keep their minds sharp. They are not suited for sedentary lifestyles.

Grooming is a significant commitment for Pomaussies due to their double coats. Regular brushing, at least a few times a week, is necessary to prevent mats, reduce shedding, and keep their fur healthy. During shedding seasons, daily brushing may be required. Professional grooming might also be beneficial for some coat types. Pomaussies can be prone to health issues found in both parent breeds, such as hip and elbow dysplasia, eye conditions (like Collie Eye Anomaly), patellar luxation, and drug sensitivities (due to the MDR1 gene in Australian Shepherds). A Pomaussie is an excellent choice for active individuals or families who can provide ample exercise, mental stimulation, and consistent training, rewarding them with a devoted and intelligent companion.

## The Sweet Pom-Shih: Pomeranian and Shih Tzu Mix

The Pom-Shih is a delightful blend of the playful Pomeranian and the affectionate Shih Tzu. This mix creates a charming lap dog known for its sweet disposition and adorable appearance. I find that the Pom-Shih often combines the best qualities of both breeds: the Shih Tzu's calm, loving nature and the Pomeranian's vibrant, lively spirit. They are gaining popularity as ideal companions for many households.

Pom-Shihs are small dogs, typically weighing between 4 to 12 pounds. Their appearance can vary significantly, often displaying a charming combination of features. They usually have a long, soft, flowing coat, which can be straight or slightly wavy, reflecting the dense fur of both parent breeds. Their colors are diverse, including black, white, brown, cream, and various patterns. They might have the Shih Tzu's underbite or the Pomeranian's foxy face, and their ears can be either erect or floppy. Each Pom-Shih has a unique look, which I think adds to their appeal.

In terms of temperament, Pom-Shihs are generally affectionate, friendly, and outgoing. They enjoy being close to their owners and are often excellent cuddle buddies. They inherit the Shih Tzu's gentle and sociable nature, combined with the Pomeranian's confident and sometimes vocal personality. They are good with children and other pets, especially if socialized from a young age. While they can be spirited, they typically do not require extensive exercise, making them suitable for apartment living or less active individuals.

### Caring for Your Fluffy Pom-Shih

Pom-Shihs have moderate exercise needs. Short daily walks and indoor play sessions are usually sufficient to keep them happy and healthy. They thrive on attention and interaction, so regular playtime and cuddle sessions are a must. Training a Pom-Shih is generally straightforward as they are intelligent and eager to please, though they can have a stubborn streak like both parent breeds. Positive reinforcement methods work best for them.

Grooming is a significant aspect of owning a Pom-Shih due to their luxurious double coat. Daily brushing is essential to prevent mats and tangles, as well as to reduce shedding. Many owners opt for professional grooming every few weeks to keep their coats trimmed and manageable. Regular dental care is also crucial, as small breeds are prone to dental issues. I recommend daily tooth brushing.

Pom-Shihs typically live for 10 to 16 years. They can be prone to health conditions common in both parent breeds, such as patellar luxation, eye problems (like cataracts and dry eye), respiratory issues (especially if they inherit a flatter face from the Shih Tzu), and dental disease. Responsible breeders will perform health screenings to minimize these risks. A Pom-Shih is a wonderful choice for those seeking a loving, low-to-moderate energy companion who will thrive on affection and bring much joy to their home.

## The Affectionate Pugapom: Pomeranian and Pug Mix

The Pugapom is a distinctive mix, bringing together the outgoing Pomeranian with the charmingly wrinkled Pug. This hybrid results in a dog that is often playful, affectionate, and full of character. I find the Pugapom to be a fascinating combination of two very different small breeds, creating a companion that often inherits the best of both worlds in terms of personality. They are known for their loving nature and their ability to bring laughter to any household.

Pugapoms are small-to-medium sized dogs, typically weighing between 7 to 18 pounds. Their appearance can be quite variable, depending on which parent's genes are more dominant. They might inherit the Pug's characteristic flat face and wrinkled forehead, or the Pomeranian's more foxy muzzle. Their coat can be short and smooth like a Pug's, long and fluffy like a Pomeranian's, or a medium blend. Colors are diverse, including black, fawn, cream, or a combination. Often, they possess the Pug's curled tail and the Pomeranian's expressive eyes.

Temperamentally, Pugapoms are generally affectionate, friendly, and sociable. They often inherit the Pug's clownish and devoted nature, combined with the Pomeranian's spirited and confident personality. This results in a dog that loves attention, enjoys playtime, and thrives on being part of the family. They tend to be good with children and other pets, provided they are properly socialized from a young age. While they can be charming, they may also exhibit a stubborn streak, so consistent training is essential.

### Providing the Best Care for Your Pugapom

Pugapoms have moderate exercise needs. Short daily walks and indoor play sessions are usually sufficient. Owners must be particularly mindful of their breathing, especially if they inherit the Pug's brachycephalic (flat-faced) features, as they can be prone to overheating. Avoid strenuous exercise in hot weather. Mental stimulation is also important; they enjoy puzzle toys and learning new tricks.

Grooming depends on their coat type. A Pugapom with a shorter, Pug-like coat will require less brushing but may shed more. Those with a longer, Pomeranian-like coat will need regular brushing to prevent mats and tangles. Regardless of coat length, regular cleaning of any facial wrinkles (if present) is crucial to prevent skin infections. Dental hygiene is important, as small breeds are prone to dental issues.

Pugapoms typically have a lifespan of 10 to 14 years. They can be prone to health conditions common in both parent breeds, such as respiratory problems (Brachycephalic Obstructive Airway Syndrome), patellar luxation, eye problems, and skin fold dermatitis. Responsible breeders will screen their parent dogs for these conditions. A Pugapom can be a charming, loving, and entertaining companion for those who can provide a safe and caring environment, paying attention to their unique health and grooming needs.

## The Bold Pom-Beagle: Pomeranian and Beagle Mix

The Pom-Beagle, often called a Pomeagle, is an intriguing mix that combines the vocal and energetic Beagle with the confident and spirited Pomeranian. This hybrid creates a dog with a unique personality, often inheriting the Beagle's keen sense of smell and the Pomeranian's fluffy charm. I find that this mix can be quite lively and intelligent, making them suitable for active individuals or families who enjoy outdoor activities.

Pomeagles typically weigh between 10 to 25 pounds, displaying a variable appearance. They might inherit the Beagle's droopy ears or the Pomeranian's erect, foxy ears. Their coat can range from the Beagle's short, dense fur to the Pomeranian's longer, fluffier double coat, or a combination. Common colors include the tri-color (black, white, tan) patterns of a Beagle, as well as various solid colors seen in Pomeranians. Their size and build can vary significantly, leaning towards either parent.

In terms of temperament, Pomeagles are generally intelligent, curious, and energetic. They often inherit the Beagle's strong sense of smell and tendency to follow scents, combined with the Pomeranian's bold and sometimes vocal nature. This makes them alert and spirited companions. They can be very affectionate and loyal to their families but may also be prone to independent thinking and a stubborn streak, especially when a scent captures their attention. Early socialization and consistent training are paramount to managing their vocalizations and potential wanderlust.

### Managing Your Energetic Pomeagle

Pomeagles have moderate to high energy levels and a strong need for mental stimulation. Daily vigorous walks, playtime in a securely fenced yard, and opportunities for scent-based games are essential to keep them happy and well-behaved. Their Beagle heritage means they might be prone to following their nose, so a leash is crucial during walks. Their intelligence makes them capable learners, but their independent nature means training requires patience and consistency. I always recommend positive reinforcement training.

Grooming depends on their coat type. A Pomeagle with a shorter coat will need less brushing but may shed seasonally. Those with a longer, fluffier coat will require regular brushing to prevent mats and manage shedding. All Pomeagles need regular ear checks and cleaning, especially if they have floppy Beagle-like ears, to prevent infections. Dental care is also important.

Pomeagles generally have a lifespan of 12 to 15 years. They can be prone to health conditions found in both parent breeds, such as hip and elbow dysplasia, eye conditions (like glaucoma), patellar luxation, and epilepsy. Responsible breeders will conduct health screenings on parent dogs. A Pomeagle is an excellent choice for active owners who can provide consistent training, ample exercise, and engaging mental challenges, rewarding them with a devoted and spirited companion who loves to explore the world.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Pomeranian Mixes

### What is the most common Pomeranian mix?
The Pomsky (Pomeranian and Siberian Husky mix) is widely considered the most common and sought-after Pomeranian mix. People often desire their miniature wolf-like appearance. The Pomchi (Pomeranian and Chihuahua mix) is also extremely popular due to its small size and endearing qualities, often making it another top choice among designer dog enthusiasts.

### Do Pomeranian mixes shed a lot?
Shedding in Pomeranian mixes varies significantly depending on the other parent breed. If mixed with a heavy-shedding dog like a Husky, they will likely shed a lot. If mixed with a low-shedding breed like a Poodle, they might shed less, but rarely are they completely non-shedding. I always advise expecting some level of shedding and preparing for regular grooming.

### Are Pomeranian mixes good with kids?
Many Pomeranian mixes can be good with kids, but it largely depends on the individual dog's temperament, proper socialization, and the age and behavior of the children. Smaller mixes like Pomchis may be fragile for very young children. I recommend supervision and teaching children how to interact gently with any dog.

### What is the average lifespan of a Pomeranian mix?
The average lifespan of a Pomeranian mix typically ranges from 12 to 16 years, depending on the health and longevity of the other parent breed. Small breeds generally live longer than larger breeds. Proper nutrition, regular exercise, and consistent veterinary care contribute significantly to a longer, healthier life.

### How much do Pomeranian mixes cost?
The cost of Pomeranian mixes varies widely, depending on the specific mix, breeder reputation, location, and demand. Popular mixes like Pomskies can cost anywhere from $1,500 to $5,000 or more. Other mixes might range from $500 to $2,000. I always encourage adopting from rescues or carefully vetting reputable breeders.
