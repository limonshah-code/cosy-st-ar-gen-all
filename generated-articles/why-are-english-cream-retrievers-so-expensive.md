## Health Clearances: A Non-Negotiable Investment

One of the primary reasons English Cream Retrievers are so expensive is the extensive health testing. Responsible breeders spend thousands of dollars on health clearances for their breeding dogs. This investment protects the future health of puppies. It also provides peace of mind for new owners.

These tests screen for genetic conditions common in Golden Retrievers. They help prevent the transmission of debilitating diseases to the next generation. This proactive approach saves owners from costly veterinary bills later. It also ensures a longer, healthier life for the dog.

### Common Health Screenings

Parent dogs undergo several critical health screenings. These screenings address common issues in Golden Retrievers. Hip and elbow dysplasia are significant concerns. Breeders get certified evaluations of their dogs' joints. This minimizes the risk of painful orthopedic problems.

Eye examinations are also crucial. Veterinary ophthalmologists perform yearly eye clearances. This screens for conditions like progressive retinal atrophy. Heart evaluations are another vital test. Cardiologists check for subvalvular aortic stenosis (SAS). SAS is a serious inherited heart condition.

Beyond these structural tests, genetic DNA panels are increasingly common. These tests screen for various genetic disorders. They include Ichthyosis, PRCD-PRA, and Degenerative Myelopathy. Knowing the genetic status of parents allows breeders to make informed breeding decisions. They avoid pairing carriers of recessive genes.

### The Cost of Prevention

Each of these health clearances costs money. Hip and elbow evaluations can be hundreds of dollars per dog. Eye certifications are typically over one hundred dollars annually. Heart evaluations also carry a significant fee. Genetic DNA panels can add several hundred dollars more per dog.

Many breeders have multiple breeding dogs, so these costs add up quickly. This financial investment is continuous throughout the breeding dog's life. It is not a one-time expense. Breeders perform these tests year after year. This commitment ensures the highest health standards.

These costs are a direct reflection of responsible breeding. They are passed on to the puppy buyer in the form of a higher purchase price. This initial investment pays off by reducing the likelihood of expensive veterinary treatments later.
