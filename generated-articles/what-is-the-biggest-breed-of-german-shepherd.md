### What is the biggest German Shepherd breed?

There is no "biggest breed" *of* German Shepherd. The German Shepherd is one distinct breed with a defined size standard. However, some larger dog breeds, like the King Shepherd and the Shiloh Shepherd, were developed using German Shepherd lineage. These related breeds are often larger than a standard German Shepherd.

## Understanding The Standard German Shepherd Size

The German Shepherd Dog is a well-known breed worldwide. It has specific size standards set by kennel clubs. These standards help keep the breed consistent. A male German Shepherd typically stands 24 to 26 inches tall at the shoulder. Females are slightly smaller, usually 22 to 24 inches tall.

Regarding weight, a male German Shepherd often weighs between 65 and 90 pounds. A female weighs 50 to 70 pounds. These ranges ensure the dog is agile and fit for its working roles. This breed is known for its balanced proportions and athletic build. They are not meant to be overly heavy or bulky.

Breeders aim for dogs that fit these guidelines. Dogs that are too far outside these ranges are not ideal for showing. However, some individual dogs might grow a bit larger or smaller due to genetics. It is important to remember that size is just one trait of this versatile breed. ### The German Shepherd's Purpose and Build

The German Shepherd was bred for herding sheep. This job required strength, intelligence, and endurance. Their size and build support these tasks. They needed to be strong enough to control livestock but also agile enough to move quickly. The breed's creator, Max von Stephanitz, focused on utility.

Their strong bones and muscular bodies allow for powerful movement. A German Shepherd's body shape is slightly longer than it is tall. This gives them a balanced appearance. This structural design helps them maintain stamina over long periods. It also helps them perform tasks like running, jumping, and quickly changing direction.

Beyond herding, German Shepherds excel in many other roles. They are often police dogs, service dogs, and search-and-rescue dogs. Their physical attributes are well-suited for these demanding jobs. The standard size ensures they can perform their duties effectively without extra strain on their joints. Their defined size contributes to their overall health and longevity.

## The King Shepherd: A True Giant Among Shepherds

When people ask about the biggest breed of German Shepherd, they often think of the King Shepherd. This breed is a cross between the German Shepherd, Alaskan Malamute, and Great Pyrenees. The goal was to create a larger, calmer Shepherd type dog. King Shepherds are much bigger than standard German Shepherds.

Male King Shepherds stand 27 to 31 inches tall at the shoulder. They can weigh between 110 and 150 pounds, or even more. Females are also large, standing 25 to 29 inches tall. Their weight ranges from 90 to 120 pounds. Their size makes them one of the most impressive Shepherd-type dogs.

King Shepherds have a thick, dense coat that protects them from cold weather. They often have a regal appearance. Their calm nature makes them good family pets, despite their size. They are gentle giants who bond closely with their families. This breed is known for its impressive size and mild temperament.

### Origin and Development of the King Shepherd

The King Shepherd breed began in the 1990s in the United States. Shelley Watts-Cross and David Turk created the breed. They wanted to fix some health issues seen in German Shepherds, like hip problems. They also aimed for a dog with a calmer temperament and a larger size. The mixing of breeds brought in new genetic traits.

The inclusion of the Great Pyrenees adds to their calm nature. The Alaskan Malamute contributes to their large size and strong build. This careful breeding created a dog that looks like a very large German Shepherd but with a distinct lineage. The King Shepherd is not a recognized breed by all major kennel clubs yet. However, it has its own dedicated breed club and registry. This helps maintain the breed's standards and health. People who own them love their gentle nature and imposing presence.

## The Shiloh Shepherd: Another Large German Shepherd Relative

The Shiloh Shepherd is another large dog breed often confused with the biggest breed of German Shepherd. This breed was developed in the 1970s by Tina Barber in New York. Her goal was to create a German Shepherd with better health, a larger size, and a softer temperament. Shiloh Shepherds are known for their gentle disposition and impressive stature. They are a distinct breed, not just a variant of the German Shepherd.

Male Shiloh Shepherds typically stand 28 to 30 inches tall at the shoulder. They weigh between 100 and 140 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, usually 26 to 28 inches tall. Their weight ranges from 80 to 120 pounds. They have a more substantial bone structure than standard German Shepherds. Their coats can be smooth or long-haired. This breed has a calm and intelligent expression.

Shiloh Shepherds are often good with children and other pets. They make excellent family companions. Their gentle nature means they are less likely to be overly protective or aggressive. They need proper socialization and training from a young age. Their large size makes early training essential.

### How Shiloh Shepherds Were Developed

Tina Barber started the Shiloh Shepherd breed using German Shepherd lines. She carefully selected dogs for health, temperament, and size. She also used a few carefully chosen large breed crosses in her foundational stock. These crosses were done to enhance specific traits. The goal was to eliminate common health issues like hip and elbow dysplasia, which can affect traditional German Shepherds.

The Shiloh Shepherd breed has its own registry and breeding standards. This helps ensure breeders follow strict guidelines. They aim to produce healthy, stable, and large dogs. The breed emphasizes good temperament, making them suitable for families. While they share a strong resemblance to German Shepherds, their unique development path makes them a separate breed. They are recognized by various rare breed clubs. The Shiloh Shepherd represents a successful effort to create a German Shepherd type dog with specific desired qualities. Many people find them to be a wonderful alternative for those seeking a gentle giant.

## Health and Care Considerations for Large Shepherd Breeds

Bigger dogs often come with their own unique health and care needs. This is true for King Shepherds and Shiloh Shepherds. Their large size puts different stresses on their bodies. They are more prone to certain conditions common in giant breeds. These include hip and elbow dysplasia. These conditions affect their joints and can cause pain. Responsible breeders screen their dogs for these issues. They use health clearances to reduce the risk in puppies.

Another concern for large dogs is bloat, or gastric torsion. This is a life-threatening condition where the stomach twists. Owners of large breeds should learn the symptoms of bloat. Feeding smaller, more frequent meals can help reduce the risk. Avoid vigorous exercise right after eating. You should also choose dog food designed for large breeds. These foods have a balanced nutrient profile that supports proper growth rates.

Training and socialization are crucial for these big dogs. Because of their size, they need early training to ensure they are well-behaved. A large, untrained dog can be hard to manage. Regular exercise is also important to keep them fit and prevent obesity. However, exercise should be gentle during puppyhood to protect growing joints. ## Training and Temperament of Larger Shepherds

The temperament of King Shepherds and Shiloh Shepherds generally differs from a standard German Shepherd. While standard German Shepherds are known for their high drive and intense focus, the larger varieties often have a calmer, more laid-back nature. This was a deliberate breeding goal for both the King and Shiloh Shepherds. They tend to be less reactive and more tolerant in various situations. This calm disposition makes them excellent family dogs. They often get along well with children and other pets, provided they are properly socialized from a young age.

Despite their calm nature, these dogs are still very intelligent. They are eager to please their owners, which makes them highly trainable. Positive reinforcement methods work best with these sensitive dogs. Early socialization is key to ensure they grow into well-adjusted adults. Expose them to different people, places, and sounds when they are puppies. Puppy classes are a great way to start. Consistency in training is vital, as they learn best with clear expectations.

Their large size means training cannot be overlooked. A large dog that pulls on the leash or jumps on guests is hard to control. Basic obedience commands like "sit," "stay," "come," and "heel" are a must. Leash training should start early to teach them to walk politely by your side. These dogs enjoy having a job to do, even if it is just learning new tricks or going for long walks. Their intelligence means they need mental engagement, not just physical exercise.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Is the King Shepherd a recognized breed by major kennel clubs?
The King Shepherd is not currently recognized by the American Kennel Club (AKC). However, it is recognized by other breed registries, like the American Rare Breed Association (ARBA). These clubs help maintain the breed's standards and ensure responsible breeding practices. Dedicated clubs support the breed's growth.

### Q2: Are King Shepherds or Shiloh Shepherds good for first-time dog owners?
Due to their large size and strength, King Shepherds and Shiloh Shepherds may be a handful for first-time owners. They require consistent training and early socialization. Owners need to be prepared for their significant needs regarding exercise, grooming, and potential health issues. Experience with large breeds helps.

### Q3: What is the typical lifespan of a King Shepherd or Shiloh Shepherd?
The typical lifespan for King Shepherds and Shiloh Shepherds is similar to many large dog breeds. They generally live between 10 to 12 years. Proper nutrition, regular exercise, and consistent veterinary care can help maximize their lifespan. Genetic health can also play a role.

### Q4: Do these larger Shepherd breeds shed a lot?
Yes, both King Shepherds and Shiloh Shepherds have thick, dense coats and are moderate to heavy shedder. They shed year-round, with heavier shedding periods during seasonal changes. Regular brushing, at least a few times a week, is essential to manage loose hair and keep their coats healthy.

### Q5: What are common health concerns for King and Shiloh Shepherds?
Common health concerns for King and Shiloh Shepherds include hip and elbow dysplasia, which are joint conditions. They can also be prone to bloat, a serious stomach condition. Responsible breeders screen for these issues. Owners should be aware of symptoms and preventive measures.

### Q6: Can these dogs live in apartments?
Living in an apartment with a King Shepherd or Shiloh Shepherd is challenging due to their large size. They need ample space to move around. While daily exercise is crucial, a large private yard is generally preferred. Apartment living is possible but requires a dedicated owner who can provide extensive daily outdoor activity and mental stimulation.
