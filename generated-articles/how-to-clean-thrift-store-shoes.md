## Revive Your Finds: How To Clean Thrift Store Shoes

Finding great shoes at a thrift store feels like hitting the jackpot. You get unique styles and save money. But these pre-loved treasures often need a good cleaning before wearing. Understanding **how to clean thrift store shoes** is essential for hygiene and extending their life. It helps remove dirt, odors, and any unseen germs.

This guide will show you simple, effective methods. We cover everything from initial inspection to deep cleaning different materials. You will learn to sanitize the insides, tackle stubborn smells, and bring your secondhand shoes back to life. Get ready to transform your thrift store finds into fresh, wearable footwear.

### Takeaway:

*   **Assess Condition:** Check shoes for material type and damage before cleaning.
*   **Remove Debris:** Brush off loose dirt and clean soles thoroughly.
*   **Sanitize Inside:** Use baking soda, disinfectant spray, or rubbing alcohol.
*   **Clean Exterior by Material:** Use specific methods for fabric, leather, or suede.
*   **Dry Properly:** Air dry completely to prevent mold and odors.

To clean thrift store shoes, first remove loose dirt and debris. Then, sanitize the insides with a disinfectant spray or baking soda. Next, clean the exterior based on its material type, using gentle cleaners. Finally, air dry them completely in a well-ventilated area to prevent mildew and odors.

## Essential First Steps: Inspecting Your Thrift Store Shoes

Before you begin any deep cleaning, a thorough inspection of your thrift store shoes is crucial. This step helps you understand what kind of cleaning approach is needed. It also lets you spot any damage that might affect cleaning methods. Take your time with this initial assessment.

Start by looking at the overall condition of the shoes. Check for visible dirt, stains, or scuffs on the outside. Look closely at the soles for gum, tar, or other stuck-on grime. Understanding the material is also key. Shoes can be made of leather, suede, canvas, synthetic fabric, or a mix of these. Each material requires different cleaning products and techniques.

Next, remove any insoles or laces. Inspect the insoles for significant wear or strong odors. Sometimes, it is best to replace old insoles entirely. Examine the interior of the shoe for signs of mildew, excessive dirt, or pet hair. This early check guides your cleaning process. It prevents you from damaging the shoes and ensures you use the right tools.

*   **Initial Visual Scan:** Check for general dirt, scuffs, and obvious damage.
*   **Material Identification:** Determine if the shoes are leather, suede, canvas, or synthetic.
*   **Sole Inspection:** Look for embedded dirt, gum, or other residues on the bottom.
*   **Insole Removal:** Take out insoles and laces for separate cleaning or replacement.
*   **Interior Assessment:** Check for dirt, hair, or mildew inside the shoe.

This careful inspection sets the stage for effective cleaning. It helps you decide if a simple wipe-down is enough or if a more intensive cleaning is necessary. Knowing your shoe's material is especially important, as improper cleaning can cause irreversible damage.

## Deep Cleaning the Soles and Exteriors

Cleaning the soles and exteriors of your thrift store shoes removes visible grime. This step makes the biggest visual impact. It also prepares the shoes for deeper sanitation. We will focus on effective methods for both the bottom and the main outer surfaces.

Start with the soles. These often carry the most dirt and germs. Use a stiff brush or an old toothbrush to remove loose dirt, small stones, and mud from the treads. For tougher grime, mix a small amount of dish soap with warm water. Dip your brush into the soapy water and scrub the entire bottom of the sole. Rinse the brush frequently. You can also use a damp cloth to wipe away the loosened dirt. This process ensures the base of your shoes is clean and fresh. For more specific advice on cleaning shoe bottoms, you can refer to our guide on [how to clean the bottom of shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bottom-of-shoes).

For the shoe uppers, the cleaning method depends on the material. For canvas or synthetic shoes, a mix of mild soap and water works well. Gently scrub with a soft brush or cloth. Wipe away excess soap with a clean, damp cloth. For leather shoes, use a specialized leather cleaner or a very mild soap solution. Apply it with a soft cloth, then buff dry. Avoid oversaturating leather. Suede shoes require a specific approach, using a suede brush to remove dry dirt and a suede eraser for stains. Always test any cleaner on an inconspicuous area first.

*   **For Soles:**
    *   Brush off loose debris with a stiff brush.
    *   Scrub with a solution of mild dish soap and warm water.
    *   Wipe clean with a damp cloth.
*   **For Canvas/Synthetics:**
    *   Mix mild laundry detergent or dish soap with water.
    *   Scrub gently with a soft brush or sponge.
    *   Wipe clean with a damp cloth.
*   **For Leather:**
    *   Use a dedicated leather cleaner or a very diluted soap solution.
    *   Apply with a soft cloth and wipe gently.
    *   Buff dry with a clean cloth. For specific leather care, insights from cleaning items like a [leather purse from a thrift store](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-leather-purse-from-a-thrift-store) can be helpful.
*   **For Suede:**
    *   Use a dry suede brush to remove dirt.
    *   Address scuffs with a suede eraser.
    *   Do not use water or wet cleaners. Our guide on [how to clean suede bottom shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-suede-bottom-shoes) offers more detailed steps.

Paying attention to these details will ensure a thorough cleaning of the outside of your shoes. This prepares them for internal sanitation.

## Sanitizing the Interiors and Removing Odors

Cleaning the outside of thrift store shoes is only half the battle. Sanitizing the interiors and eliminating odors is equally important for hygiene and comfort. Used shoes can harbor bacteria and unpleasant smells. Addressing these makes the shoes truly ready to wear.

Start by removing any insoles. If the insoles are washable (like foam or fabric), you can hand wash them with mild soap and water. Scrub them gently, rinse thoroughly, and let them air dry completely. If the insoles are heavily soiled or excessively worn, consider replacing them. New insoles provide better support and freshness.

For the shoe's interior, a multi-pronged approach works best. First, liberally sprinkle baking soda inside each shoe. Baking soda is a natural odor absorber. Let it sit for at least 24 hours, or even longer for strong odors. After the time passes, shake out the baking soda thoroughly. You can also use a small vacuum cleaner hose to remove all traces. Next, spray the interior with a disinfectant spray. Look for sprays designed for fabric or shoes. Ensure the spray reaches all areas inside. Alternatively, a cotton ball with rubbing alcohol can wipe down non-fabric interiors, killing germs. For a comprehensive guide on this crucial step, read our article on [how to clean the inside of used shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-inside-of-used-shoes).

For very stubborn odors, a vinegar solution can help. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Lightly mist the inside of the shoes. The vinegar smell will dissipate as it dries, taking the bad odors with it. Ensure the shoes dry completely after any wet treatment to prevent new mildew. Placing activated charcoal pouches inside can also absorb lingering smells over time.

*   **Clean/Replace Insoles:**
    *   Wash washable insoles with soap and water, then air dry.
    *   Replace heavily soiled or worn-out insoles.
*   **Baking Soda Treatment:**
    *   Sprinkle baking soda generously inside shoes.
    *   Leave for 24+ hours to absorb odors.
    *   Vacuum or shake out thoroughly.
*   **Disinfectant Spray:**
    *   Spray a shoe-safe disinfectant inside the shoes.
    *   Allow to air dry completely.
*   **Rubbing Alcohol (for non-fabric):**
    *   Wipe down interior with rubbing alcohol on a cotton ball.
*   **Vinegar Solution (for stubborn odors):**
    *   Lightly mist with equal parts white vinegar and water.
    *   Air dry completely.
*   **Activated Charcoal:**
    *   Place charcoal pouches inside for ongoing odor absorption.

Sanitizing the interior is a critical step for making your thrift store shoes feel truly yours and hygienic.

## Specific Material Care: Leather, Suede, and Fabric

Different shoe materials require specific cleaning methods. Using the wrong products can damage your thrift store finds. Understanding these differences helps maintain the shoe's integrity and appearance. Let's look at how to approach leather, suede, and fabric shoes.

**For Leather Shoes:** Leather is durable but needs gentle care. Start by wiping off dust with a soft, dry cloth. For dirt and scuffs, mix a small amount of mild soap (like dish soap) with water. Dip a clean, soft cloth into the solution, wring it out well, and gently wipe the leather surface. Do not saturate the leather. Wipe away any soap residue with a separate damp cloth. After cleaning, let the shoes air dry completely. Once dry, apply a leather conditioner to keep the material supple and prevent cracking. This also helps restore its shine.

**For Suede Shoes:** Suede is delicate and reacts poorly to water. Begin by using a dry suede brush. Brush in one direction to remove loose dirt and dust. For scuffs or dry stains, use a suede eraser. Gently rub the eraser over the mark. If the suede looks matted, use the suede brush to restore the nap. Avoid using any wet cleaners on suede, as they can cause water stains. If you have any oily stains, cornstarch can absorb it if applied immediately. Let it sit, then brush off. For more in-depth care, you can read our guide on [how to clean suede bottom shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-suede-bottom-shoes).

**For Fabric/Canvas Shoes:** Canvas and other fabric shoes are generally easier to clean. Remove laces and insoles first. For light dirt, use a soft brush and a solution of mild laundry detergent or dish soap mixed with warm water. Gently scrub the entire fabric surface. For tougher stains, you can sometimes pre-treat with a stain remover safe for fabrics. For white fabric shoes, a paste of baking soda and a small amount of water can lift stains. Apply, let sit, then scrub and rinse. After scrubbing, wipe down the shoes with a clean, damp cloth to remove all soap residue. Do not submerge the shoes unless they are specifically designed for machine washing, which is rare for thrifted items.

*   **Leather Care:**
    *   Wipe with a soft, dry cloth.
    *   Clean with a damp cloth and mild soap solution.
    *   Apply leather conditioner after drying.
*   **Suede Care:**
    *   Brush with a dry suede brush.
    *   Use a suede eraser for scuffs.
    *   Avoid water; use cornstarch for oil stains.
*   **Fabric/Canvas Care:**
    *   Remove laces and insoles.
    *   Scrub with mild detergent and water solution.
    *   Use baking soda paste for white fabric stains.
    *   Wipe clean with a damp cloth.

Knowing the right technique for each material ensures your thrift store shoes look their best and last longer. Always remember to test any cleaning product on a hidden area first.

## Tackling Stubborn Stains and Unwanted Scents

Sometimes, thrift store shoes come with more than just surface dirt. Stubborn stains and persistent odors require extra attention. Do not give up on a good pair of shoes just because of a challenge. With the right approach, you can often remove even tough marks and smells.

For difficult stains on fabric shoes, consider a targeted stain remover. Always choose one safe for the shoe's specific material. Apply the remover directly to the stain, following product instructions. Let it sit for the recommended time before gently scrubbing with a soft brush or cloth. For mud or dirt stains on fabric, let the mud dry completely first. Then, brush off the dry mud before applying any cleaner. For general cleaning of heavily soiled fabric shoes, a gentle laundry detergent mixed with warm water and a good scrubbing brush can work wonders. Remember to rinse thoroughly with a damp cloth.

Eliminating strong odors might take more than just baking soda. If odors persist after the baking soda treatment, try activated charcoal inserts. Place these inside the shoes overnight or when not in use. They are excellent at absorbing lingering smells. Another option is a shoe deodorizer spray that contains odor-neutralizing agents, not just fragrances. For truly potent smells, consider putting the shoes in a sealed bag with crumpled newspaper or coffee grounds for a few days. These also absorb odors effectively. For very strong, unusual smells like tar, specialized cleaning methods may be required, similar to those discussed in our article on [how to clean tar off shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-tar-off-shoes).

For scuffs on leather or synthetic materials, a magic eraser can often work wonders. Lightly dampen the magic eraser and gently rub it over the scuff mark. Be careful not to rub too hard, especially on colored surfaces, as it can remove finish. For deeper scuffs on leather, a leather repair kit or shoe polish matching the shoe's color might be necessary. Always test any strong cleaner or polish on an inconspicuous area first to prevent damage.

*   **For Fabric Stains:**
    *   Pre-treat with a fabric-safe stain remover.
    *   Brush off dry mud before cleaning.
    *   Use a baking soda paste for white shoe stains.
*   **For Persistent Odors:**
    *   Use activated charcoal inserts.
    *   Employ shoe deodorizer sprays.
    *   Try newspaper or coffee grounds in a sealed bag.
*   **For Scuffs:**
    *   Use a magic eraser on leather or synthetics.
    *   Consider shoe polish or a leather repair kit for deep leather scuffs.

Patience and the right tools are key to making these challenges disappear. Your thrifted shoes will thank you.

## Drying Your Shoes and Final Touches

Proper drying is just as important as cleaning. Incorrect drying can lead to new problems like mildew, shrinking, or damage to the shoe's material. Once your thrift store shoes are clean, follow these steps to ensure they dry safely and thoroughly.

Never put shoes in a clothes dryer. The high heat can shrink materials, warp soles, and melt adhesives. Instead, air drying is the best method. Find a well-ventilated area away from direct sunlight or extreme heat. Direct sunlight can fade colors and crack materials like leather. If possible, place the shoes outdoors on a dry, sunny day, but not in intense, direct sun for too long. If drying indoors, a fan can help circulate air and speed up the process.

To help shoes maintain their shape during drying, stuff them with newspaper or paper towels. This absorbs excess moisture from the inside and prevents the shoes from collapsing or wrinkling. Change the paper every few hours, especially if the shoes were very wet, until no more moisture is absorbed. Make sure to leave the shoes untied and tongues pulled forward to allow maximum airflow. It can take 24 to 48 hours for shoes to dry completely, depending on their material and how wet they got. Patience is key to preventing mildew and odor.

Once completely dry, reinsert clean insoles and re-lace the shoes. Now is also a good time for any final cosmetic touches. For leather shoes, apply a final coat of leather conditioner or polish. This restores shine and protects the material. For suede, a final brush with a suede brush helps restore the nap. For fabric shoes, a fabric protector spray can help repel future stains. Take a moment to inspect your hard work. Your thrift store shoes should now look, feel, and smell fresh and ready to wear.

*   **Air Dry Only:**
    *   Avoid clothes dryers and direct heat sources.
    *   Choose a well-ventilated area.
    *   Keep away from direct, intense sunlight.
*   **Maintain Shape and Absorb Moisture:**
    *   Stuff with newspaper or paper towels.
    *   Change paper frequently.
    *   Leave laces loose and tongues open.
*   **Allow Ample Drying Time:**
    *   Expect 24-48 hours for full drying.
*   **Final Touches:**
    *   Reinsert clean insoles and laces.
    *   Apply leather conditioner/polish (for leather).
    *   Brush suede (for suede).
    *   Consider fabric protector spray (for fabric).

These steps ensure your thrift store shoes are perfectly clean and ready for their next adventure with you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you get the smell out of used shoes?
To remove smells from used shoes, first sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda inside each shoe. Let it sit overnight or for 24 hours to absorb odors. You can also use activated charcoal pouches or a disinfectant shoe spray. Ensure the shoes are completely dry, as moisture can cause odors.

### Is it safe to buy used shoes?
Yes, it is generally safe to buy used shoes, especially if you clean and sanitize them properly. Thorough cleaning helps remove dirt, bacteria, and odors. Always inspect shoes for major damage or deep-seated issues before purchase. Cleaning ensures they are hygienic and ready for wear.

### Can you put thrift store shoes in the washing machine?
Most thrift store shoes should not go into a washing machine. The harsh agitation and heat can damage adhesives, cause shrinking, or warp materials. Canvas or certain synthetic sneakers might be machine washable, but always check the manufacturer's guidelines if available, or opt for hand cleaning.

### How do you disinfect shoe insoles?
To disinfect shoe insoles, first remove them from the shoes. If they are washable, hand wash them with mild soap and water, then air dry completely. For non-washable insoles, spray them with a shoe disinfectant spray. You can also sprinkle baking soda on them to absorb odors and moisture.

### How do you clean the white soles of used shoes?
To clean white soles on used shoes, mix a paste of baking soda and a small amount of water. Apply the paste to the soles and scrub with an old toothbrush. For stubborn scuffs, a magic eraser works well. Rinse or wipe the soles thoroughly with a damp cloth afterward.

### What should I look for when buying thrift store shoes?
When buying thrift store shoes, check for overall condition, especially the soles and heels for wear. Look for structural damage, tears, or excessive creasing. Inspect the interior for heavy staining or strong odors. Identify the material to ensure it can be cleaned.
