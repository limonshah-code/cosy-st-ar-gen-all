## Feeding Your Crested Gecko: A Complete Diet Guide

Bringing a crested gecko into your home is an exciting experience. These charming creatures make wonderful pets, but their unique dietary needs often puzzle new owners. One of the most common questions I hear is, "What should I feed my crested gecko?" Providing the correct crested gecko food is crucial for their health and longevity. A balanced diet ensures your gecko gets all necessary nutrients. This article provides a complete guide to feeding your crested gecko properly. We will cover commercial meal replacement powders, live insects, and appropriate fruit supplements. You will learn about feeding schedules, portion sizes, and foods to avoid.

### Takeaway

*   **Primary Food Source:** Use high-quality commercial crested gecko diets (CGD) as the main food.
*   **Essential Protein:** Offer live, gut-loaded feeder insects regularly.
*   **Fruit Supplements:** Provide ripe fruits as occasional treats, never as a staple.
*   **Calcium and Vitamins:** Dust insects with calcium and vitamin supplements.
*   **Fresh Water:** Always provide a clean water source.

Crested geckos need a varied and balanced diet. Their main food source should be a high-quality commercial powdered diet. You must supplement this with live, gut-loaded insects. Occasional small servings of ripe fruit can also add variety to their menu.

### The Foundation: Commercial Crested Gecko Diets (CGD)

Commercial crested gecko diets form the backbone of your pet's nutrition. These powdered formulas are specially formulated to meet most of a crested gecko's dietary requirements. They contain a balanced blend of protein, vitamins, minerals, and fruit sugars. Major brands like Pangea and Repashy are widely trusted by gecko enthusiasts.

You prepare these diets by mixing the powder with water to create a smoothie-like paste. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for mixing ratios. The consistency should be similar to ketchup or a thick pancake batter. Do not make it too watery or too thick. I find mixing a small amount daily works best for freshness.

These diets are vital because they are complete. They eliminate much of the guesswork from providing a balanced meal. Young geckos and adults both thrive on these specialized foods. Always offer fresh CGD every other day. Remove any uneaten food after 24 hours to prevent mold and bacteria growth.

Using CGD ensures your gecko receives essential nutrients. It provides the right balance of fats, carbohydrates, and protein. This diet prevents many common nutritional deficiencies. Always buy from reputable sources to ensure product quality.

### Beyond CGD: Essential Live Feeder Insects

While commercial crested gecko diets are complete, live feeder insects are also a critical part of a healthy crested gecko diet. Geckos are natural insectivores and benefit greatly from hunting live prey. This activity also provides mental stimulation and physical exercise. Insects offer additional protein and chitin, which aids digestion.

Crickets are a popular choice for crested geckos. They are readily available and provide good nutrition when gut-loaded. Ensure the crickets are appropriately sized for your gecko; their length should not exceed the distance between your gecko's eyes. You might also consider offering dubia roaches. They are a highly nutritious option and easier to keep than crickets for many owners.

Other suitable insects include small mealworms and black soldier fly larvae (BSFL). Always gut-load insects before feeding them to your gecko. Gut-loading means feeding the insects a nutritious diet for at least 24-48 hours before offering them to your gecko. This process transfers vital nutrients from the insect's gut to your gecko. Offer insects 1-2 times per week. My geckos always get excited when they see crickets in their enclosure. You can find more information on how many crickets to feed a crested gecko to ensure proper portions.

Remember to dust all feeder insects with a calcium supplement before feeding. A calcium powder with D3 is often recommended for geckos not exposed to UVB light. This prevents metabolic bone disease. Some keepers also use a multi-vitamin supplement periodically.

### Supplemental Nutrition: Fruits and Calcium Dust

Crested geckos enjoy fruit as an occasional treat. However, fruit should never form the main part of their diet. It lacks the balanced nutrition found in commercial gecko diets and insects. Fruit is high in sugar and low in protein and calcium, which can lead to nutritional imbalances if overfed. Always choose ripe, non-citrus fruits.

Good fruit options include mango, papaya, berries (strawberries, raspberries, blueberries), and peaches. Avoid citrus fruits like oranges and lemons, as their high acidity can upset your gecko's stomach. Also, do not offer avocado; it is toxic to many animals. I usually mash a small piece of fruit or offer it in tiny, digestible chunks.

Serve fruit sparingly, perhaps once every week or two. It should be a small portion, no larger than a pea for a juvenile gecko or a blueberry for an adult. Think of it as a dessert for your gecko. Always wash fruits thoroughly before offering them to remove any pesticides.

Calcium and vitamin supplements are vital. Even with a good CGD, dusting insects is often necessary, especially if your gecko does not receive UVB lighting. Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD) is a serious condition caused by calcium deficiency. It can deform bones and be fatal. I always use a reptile-specific calcium powder with D3 for dusting insects. A multi-vitamin supplement can be used less frequently, maybe once every two weeks. This ensures your crested gecko receives all essential vitamins and minerals for strong bones and overall health.

### Feeding Schedules and Portion Sizes

Establishing a consistent feeding schedule is important for your crested gecko's well-being. Young crested geckos, typically under 6 months old or still growing rapidly, need food more frequently. They grow quickly and use a lot of energy. For juveniles, offer commercial crested gecko diet (CGD) every day or every other day. Adults, once they reach full size, can be fed CGD every other day or every third day.

When it comes to portion sizes, a good rule of thumb is to offer an amount that your gecko can consume within 12-24 hours. For CGD, a small amount in a shallow dish is sufficient. For juveniles, a teaspoon-sized dollop is usually enough. Adult geckos might need a slightly larger amount, perhaps a tablespoon. My geckos often leave a little bit behind, which helps me gauge if I'm offering too much.

Live insects, such as crickets or roaches, should be offered 1-2 times per week. The number of insects depends on their size and your gecko's appetite. Offer 2-4 appropriately sized insects per feeding session. Observe your gecko; if they eat all insects quickly and look for more, you can offer one or two more. If they leave some, reduce the quantity next time. Providing a consistent feeding routine helps your gecko feel secure. It also allows you to monitor their appetite and weight. Knowing how long crested geckos can go without food is also useful in emergencies, but consistency is best.

Remember, every gecko is an individual. Some may eat more than others. Adjust feeding frequency and portions based on your gecko's age, activity level, and body condition. A healthy crested gecko will have a plump tail base and a good weight without being overweight.

### Hydration and Water Needs

Proper hydration is just as important as the right food for your crested gecko. These geckos absorb water through their skin and by licking water droplets. They are native to humid environments. Maintaining proper humidity in their enclosure is crucial for their overall health and hydration.

You should mist the enclosure once or twice daily. This creates water droplets on leaves and tank surfaces, which your gecko will drink. Use a spray bottle with clean, dechlorinated water for misting. The enclosure should dry out partially between misting sessions to prevent mold growth, but not completely dry out. Maintaining the right humidity levels is part of how to set up a crested gecko tank properly.

While misting is essential, always provide a shallow water dish with fresh, clean water. Although many geckos prefer to drink from misted surfaces, a water dish offers a constant water source. Change the water daily to keep it fresh and clean. Use a heavy, shallow dish that cannot be easily tipped over. My geckos sometimes soak in their water dish, especially during shedding.

Signs of dehydration include sunken eyes, wrinkled skin, and lethargy. If you notice these signs, increase misting frequency and ensure the water dish is always full. A well-hydrated gecko will have plump skin and clear eyes. Proper hydration aids in digestion and overall bodily functions.

### Foods to Absolutely Avoid

Just as it is crucial to know what to feed your crested gecko, it is equally important to understand what foods to avoid. Feeding inappropriate items can lead to severe health issues, including nutritional deficiencies, organ damage, or even death. Some foods are outright toxic, while others are simply unbalanced.

**Harmful Foods:**
*   **Citrus Fruits:** Oranges, lemons, limes, and grapefruits are too acidic for crested geckos. They can cause digestive upset and irritation.
*   **Avocado:** This fruit contains persin, which is toxic to many animals, including reptiles. Never feed avocado to your gecko.
*   **Processed Human Foods:** Anything cooked, seasoned, sugary, or high in fat is unsuitable. This includes bread, dairy products, chocolate, chips, and cooked meats. Their digestive systems are not equipped to handle such complex ingredients.
*   **Wild-Caught Insects:** Do not feed insects caught from your garden or outdoors. They may carry parasites, diseases, or have traces of pesticides. Always use feeder insects from reputable pet stores or breeders.
*   **Fireflies (Lightning Bugs):** These insects contain chemicals that are highly toxic and often fatal to reptiles. Keep your gecko away from them.
*   **Large or Hard-Shelled Insects:** Insects that are too large for your gecko can cause impaction. Very hard-shelled insects can also be difficult to digest. Avoid beetles or adult superworms for crested geckos.
*   **Spinach and Rhubarb:** These contain oxalates, which bind with calcium and prevent its absorption. This can lead to calcium deficiency over time.

Sticking to commercial diets, gut-loaded feeder insects, and safe fruits ensures your gecko remains healthy. Always prioritize their specific dietary needs over what might seem convenient. When in doubt about a specific food item, it is always best to avoid it.

### Troubleshooting Common Feeding Issues

Sometimes, despite your best efforts, your crested gecko might refuse to eat. This can be worrying for owners. Understanding common feeding issues helps you address them effectively. Many factors can influence a crested gecko's appetite.

**Reasons for Refusal to Eat:**
*   **Stress:** A new environment, changes in tank setup, or excessive handling can stress a gecko. This often leads to a temporary loss of appetite. Allow your gecko time to acclimate to its surroundings. Ensure their enclosure is set up correctly with proper hides and decor. You can learn more about how to set up a crested gecko tank to reduce stress.
*   **Temperature or Humidity:** Incorrect temperature or humidity levels can make a gecko uncomfortable and affect digestion. Ensure your thermometer and hygrometer are accurate. Keep temperatures between 72-78°F (22-26°C) and humidity around 60-80%.
*   **Shedding:** Geckos often lose their appetite a few days before and during shedding. This is normal. Their energy focuses on skin regeneration. Appetite usually returns to normal after shedding is complete.
*   **Impaction:** If your gecko ingests substrate or other indigestible items, it can lead to impaction, blocking their digestive tract. This is why loose substrates are not always recommended for young geckos. Symptoms include lack of appetite, lethargy, and lack of bowel movements. A vet visit is necessary for impaction.
*   **Illness:** A persistent lack of appetite could signal an underlying health issue. Other symptoms like lethargy, weight loss, or abnormal droppings warrant a vet visit.
*   **Food Preferences:** Some geckos are picky. They might prefer one flavor of CGD over another, or a specific type of insect. Try offering different flavors or insect varieties.
*   **Overfeeding:** If you offer too much food, your gecko might not eat as frequently. Ensure you are providing appropriate portion sizes.

If your gecko is consistently refusing food for more than a few days, especially if they are losing weight, consult a reptile veterinarian. They can diagnose any underlying issues and provide guidance. Patience and observation are key in troubleshooting feeding problems.

### FAQ Section

**How often should I feed my crested gecko?**
Feed juvenile crested geckos (under 6 months) commercial crested gecko diet (CGD) every day or every other day. Adults can have CGD every other day or every third day. Offer live, gut-loaded insects 1-2 times per week. Adjust frequency based on your gecko's age, growth rate, and activity level.

**Can crested geckos eat baby food?**
No, you should not feed crested geckos baby food. While it might seem convenient, human baby food lacks the balanced nutritional profile crested geckos require. It often contains too much sugar and not enough protein, calcium, or essential vitamins. Stick to specially formulated commercial gecko diets.

**What fruits are safe for crested geckos?**
Safe fruits for crested geckos include mango, papaya, berries (strawberries, raspberries, blueberries), and peaches. Always offer ripe, non-citrus fruits. Feed fruit sparingly as an occasional treat, not a main diet. Avoid citrus fruits and avocado.

**Do crested geckos need live insects?**
Yes, live insects are an important part of a crested gecko's diet. They provide essential protein, chitin, and enrichment through hunting behavior. While commercial diets are complete, insects offer additional nutrients and stimulate natural instincts. Always gut-load and dust insects with calcium.

**How much calcium do crested geckos need?**
Crested geckos need a significant amount of calcium to prevent metabolic bone disease. Dust all feeder insects with a high-quality calcium powder containing D3 for geckos not exposed to UVB. Some commercial gecko diets already contain adequate calcium, but insect dusting remains vital for insect-fed meals.

**What if my crested gecko is not eating?**
A crested gecko refusing food can be due to stress, shedding, incorrect temperatures/humidity, or illness. First, check your tank's conditions. If your gecko is new, allow time to acclimate. If refusal persists for several days, especially with weight loss, consult a reptile veterinarian to rule out health issues.

### Conclusion

Understanding what to feed your crested gecko is fundamental to their health and happiness. By providing a balanced diet of high-quality commercial crested gecko food, gut-loaded live insects, and occasional fruit treats, you set your pet up for a long, healthy life. Remember to supplement with calcium and vitamins, maintain proper hydration, and always avoid harmful foods.

I believe that a well-fed gecko is a happy gecko. Observing your crested gecko's eating habits and adjusting their diet as needed will help them thrive. Do not hesitate to consult a reptile veterinarian if you have persistent feeding concerns or notice signs of illness. Your dedication to their diet directly impacts their overall well-being. Start their journey to good health today by optimizing their meals!
