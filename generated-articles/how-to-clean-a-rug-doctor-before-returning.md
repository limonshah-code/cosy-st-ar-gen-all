## How To Clean Your Rug Doctor Before Returning It

Returning a rental item often comes with unspoken rules. When it comes to a Rug Doctor carpet cleaner, leaving it spotless goes beyond basic courtesy. I know you just finished a big cleaning project, and the last thing you want to do is clean another machine. However, cleaning a Rug Doctor before returning it is essential. This practice helps you avoid potential extra charges from the rental company. It also shows respect for the next person who will use the machine. A dirty machine can harbor odors, bacteria, and leftover grime. This guide will walk you through every step. We will ensure your Rug Doctor rental is pristine. You will learn the best way to clean all parts of the machine, from tanks to brushes.

### Takeaway

*   **Avoid Fees:** Thorough cleaning prevents extra charges.
*   **Ensure Hygiene:** Remove dirt, grime, and bacteria.
*   **Follow Steps:** Empty tanks, clean exterior, scrub brushes, disinfect.
*   **Allow Drying:** Prevent mold and mildew odors.
*   **Be Prepared:** Gather tools like gloves, brushes, and mild detergent.

### How To Clean a Rug Doctor Before Returning: Quick Answer

To clean a Rug Doctor before returning, first empty and rinse both solution and recovery tanks. Then, wipe down the machine's exterior and clean the brushes and nozzle thoroughly. Disinfect key components, allow all parts to dry completely, and perform a final inspection to ensure it is spotless.

### Step-by-Step: Emptying and Rinsing the Tanks

The tanks are arguably the dirtiest parts of a Rug Doctor. This is where all the grime from your carpets collects. Therefore, properly emptying and rinsing them is the first critical step. It is key to clean a Rug Doctor before returning it. I always start here because it removes the bulk of the mess.

#### Emptying the Dirty Water Tank

First, locate the dirty water recovery tank. This is usually the bottom tank on the Rug Doctor machine. Carefully unlatch it and lift it off the base. Be careful, as it can be heavy if full. Carry it to a utility sink or a toilet. Then, pour out all the dirty water. You will likely see a murky, dark liquid filled with hair and debris. It is always surprising to see how much dirt comes out! Rinse the inside of this tank thoroughly with clean, warm water. Swish the water around to dislodge any remaining dirt or particles. Empty it again. You may need to repeat this rinse a few times until the water runs clear. Use your brush to scrub any stubborn spots inside the tank. Pay attention to corners and edges.

#### Rinsing the Solution Tank

Next, turn your attention to the clean water/solution tank. This is typically the upper tank. Even if you emptied it during your cleaning job, it is good practice to rinse it. Remove the tank and open its lid. Rinse the inside with clean, warm water. This removes any leftover detergent residue. These residues can become sticky and attract dirt over time. I usually fill it halfway with clean water, shake it, and then pour it out. Do this a couple of times. Make sure both tanks are completely empty before moving on. Thorough rinsing ensures no old dirty water or soap remains. This prevents odors and mildew. It also prepares the machine for the next user. ### Cleaning the Machine's Exterior and Brushes

Once the tanks are clean, focus on the outside of the Rug Doctor. The exterior and brushes also collect a lot of dirt and hair. Giving them attention is vital to clean a Rug Doctor before returning it. This step ensures the entire machine looks presentable and works well. I often find this part very satisfying, as the machine truly transforms.

#### Wiping Down the Exterior

Start by grabbing a clean microfiber cloth. Dampen it with warm water and a small amount of mild dish soap. Carefully wipe down all exterior surfaces of the Rug Doctor. This includes the handle, body, and any exposed plastic parts. Pay extra attention to areas where you hold the machine. These spots tend to accumulate more hand grime. Use your brush or an old toothbrush for hard-to-reach crevices. This might include around the wheels or under the handle. Make sure to remove any visible dirt, dust, or sticky residues. After wiping with soap, use a fresh, damp cloth to wipe away any soap residue. Finally, use a dry cloth to thoroughly dry the exterior. This prevents water spots and ensures no moisture is left behind.

#### Addressing the Brushes and Nozzle

The vibrating brush bar and suction nozzle are crucial parts of the Rug Doctor. They are also prone to getting tangled with hair and packed with dirt. This is a key area to clean a Rug Doctor before returning it. First, carefully inspect the brushes. Use scissors or a seam ripper to cut away any hair or fibers wrapped around the brush roll. This takes patience, but it is important for the machine's performance. Once the hair is removed, use your stiff brush and soapy water to scrub the bristles. Get rid of any embedded dirt. Rinse them thoroughly.

Next, examine the suction nozzle or "squeegee" at the front of the machine. This part pulls the dirty water into the recovery tank. It can get clogged with debris. Use your brush to clean out any blockages in the nozzle opening. Ensure it is completely clear. You might need to detach some parts if the manual allows for easier cleaning. Always refer to the Rug Doctor's specific instructions if you are unsure. Proper cleaning of these components guarantees the machine works efficiently for the next user. It also extends the life of the machine. ### Disinfecting and Deodorizing Key Components

Cleaning is not just about removing visible dirt. It is also about eliminating unseen germs and odors. Disinfecting and deodorizing are critical steps to clean a Rug Doctor before returning it. This ensures the machine is not only clean but also fresh and hygienic. I make sure to include this step, especially after cleaning tough stains.

First, focus on the interior of the tanks. Even after rinsing, residual bacteria can linger. A simple and effective disinfectant is a diluted white vinegar solution. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Spray the inside surfaces of both the dirty water tank and the clean solution tank. Let it sit for about 5-10 minutes. Vinegar naturally kills many types of bacteria and neutralizes odors. After soaking, rinse the tanks again thoroughly with clean water. This removes any vinegar smell.
