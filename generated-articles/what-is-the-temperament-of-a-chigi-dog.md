## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Chigi dogs good for first-time owners?
Yes, Chigi dogs can be good for first-time owners. They are intelligent and trainable, but they do require consistent effort. Their potential for stubbornness and vocal nature means owners must commit to early socialization and ongoing training. If you are ready for a dog that needs attention and consistent boundaries, a Chigi can be a rewarding choice.

### Do Chigis bark a lot?
Chigis can be prone to barking, a trait inherited from both Chihuahuas and Corgis. They often bark to alert their owners to strangers or sounds. While this makes them good watchdogs, excessive barking can be managed with early training. Teach them a "quiet" command and reward calm behavior.

### How much exercise does a Chigi need?
A Chigi needs moderate daily exercise. Aim for 30-60 minutes of activity, such as walks, playtime in a securely fenced yard, or indoor games. They are not high-energy like a Border Collie, but they are also not couch potatoes. Regular physical activity helps manage their energy and prevents boredom.

### Are Chigi dogs good with children?
Chigi dogs can be good with children, especially older, respectful ones. Their small size makes them vulnerable to accidental injury from very young children. Supervision is always important. Teach children how to interact gently with the dog. Early socialization helps them feel comfortable around kids.

### Are Chigi dogs aggressive?
Chigi dogs are not inherently aggressive. However, without proper socialization and training, they can become fearful or defensive, leading to nipping or growling. This is especially true if they feel threatened or overwhelmed. Proper early exposure and consistent boundaries help them grow into confident, well-adjusted dogs.

### Can Chigis be left alone for long periods?
Chigis generally do not do well when left alone for long periods. They form strong bonds with their families and can develop separation anxiety. This might manifest as destructive behavior or excessive barking. If you must leave them, consider dog walkers or doggy daycare. Provide plenty of toys and mental stimulation.
