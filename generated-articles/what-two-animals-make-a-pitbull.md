### What Two Animals Make A Pitbull: The Clear Answer

A Pitbull is not a mix of two different animal species. Instead, the term "Pitbull" refers to a *type* of dog, encompassing several distinct dog breeds. These breeds, primarily the American Pit Bull Terrier and Staffordshire Bull Terrier, originated from crosses between Old English Bulldogs and terriers in 19th-century England. They are, unequivocally, dogs.

## The True Ancestry: Bulldog and Terrier Roots

To understand Pitbulls, we must look at their true historical roots. The ancestors of modern Pitbull-type dogs emerged in 19th-century England. Breeders created them by crossing specific types of dogs. They combined the strength and tenacity of Old English Bulldogs with the agility and gameness of various terriers. This combination was deliberate and served a specific purpose.

Old English Bulldogs were robust dogs, originally bred for bull-baiting. This brutal sport involved dogs fighting bulls. Terriers, on the other hand, were quick and courageous, used for hunting rats and other vermin. Their combined traits created a powerful and athletic canine. These early crosses were known as "Bull-and-Terrier" dogs. They possessed the powerful jaws of the Bulldog and the spirited nature of the terrier. This unique blend formed the foundation of the Pitbull lineage.

These dogs were initially bred for blood sports, particularly dog fighting. This dark chapter in their history unfortunately contributed to their enduring reputation. However, their physical prowess and determination were also highly valued. Early breeders sought specific traits for these activities. We now understand that these historical uses do not define the individual dog. Many countries later outlawed these cruel sports. This led to a shift in how people viewed and bred these dogs. Understanding these origins is key to appreciating their modern form. ### The Role of Old English Bulldogs

The Old English Bulldog played a crucial role in Pitbull ancestry. These dogs were quite different from the modern English Bulldog we know today. They were taller, more athletic, and possessed immense strength. Their primary purpose was to grip and hold large animals, like bulls, during baiting sports. This required extreme power and determination.

Breeders selected dogs for their courage and physical build. Their powerful jaws and stocky, muscular bodies were highly desired. They passed these traits down through generations. These characteristics are still evident in Pitbull-type dogs today. The Bulldog contributed much of the physical strength we associate with Pitbulls. Their strong build helped them endure physically demanding tasks.

### The Influence of Terriers

Terriers brought different, but equally important, traits to the mix. These dogs were known for their relentless drive and quick reflexes. They excelled at hunting small, fast-moving prey. Their "gameness" – a term referring to their courage and unwillingness to give up – was a key factor. This gameness, while desirable in hunting, also made them formidable in fighting.

When crossed with Bulldogs, terriers added agility and a high prey drive. This combination created a dog that was both strong and incredibly quick. They were able to engage and hold with surprising speed. This blend created a dog that was tenacious and powerful. The terrier influence helped shape the energetic and alert nature common in Pitbulls.

## Key Breeds in the Pitbull Family

The term "Pitbull" is an umbrella term, encompassing several distinct dog breeds. These breeds share common ancestry and physical characteristics. Understanding these individual breeds helps clarify what a "Pitbull" truly is. This distinction is important for breed identification and understanding specific traits. Let's look at the primary breeds often referred to as Pitbulls.

### American Pit Bull Terrier (APBT)

The American Pit Bull Terrier is arguably the most well-known of the Pitbull-type dogs. It is recognized by the United Kennel Club (UKC) and the American Dog Breeders Association (ADBA). However, it is not recognized by the American Kennel Club (AKC). This lack of AKC recognition often causes confusion. The APBT is known for its muscular build, broad head, and strong jaws. Their coats are short and come in many colors.

Historically, APBTs were bred from the early Bull-and-Terrier crosses. They were valued for their strength, agility, and powerful physique. Beyond fighting, they also became working farm dogs. Many owners appreciated their loyalty and versatility. They helped with tasks like herding cattle and protecting livestock. These dogs show great affection towards people, especially their families. They are often intelligent and eager to please.

### American Staffordshire Terrier (AmStaff)

The American Staffordshire Terrier (AmStaff) shares a common ancestry with the APBT. However, the AKC recognizes the AmStaff as a distinct breed. Breeders developed the AmStaff for conformation shows. This meant selecting for specific physical standards and temperaments. Their appearance is similar to the APBT, but generally a bit heavier and more substantial. They have a proud carriage and a confident demeanor.

AmStaffs are known for their intelligence and courage. They make excellent family companions when properly trained and socialized. These dogs are generally good with children. They require consistent training and a firm, gentle hand. They need plenty of exercise to stay happy and healthy. Many people consider the AmStaff a well-rounded and devoted companion. ### Staffordshire Bull Terrier (Staffy)

The Staffordshire Bull Terrier, often called a "Staffy," is another important breed in the Pitbull family. This breed originated in England and is considerably smaller than its American counterparts. The AKC recognizes the Staffordshire Bull Terrier. They have a muscular build, broad head, and a distinctive "grin." Staffies are known for their affectionate nature. They often form strong bonds with their human families.

Staffies earned the nickname "nanny dog" for their perceived good nature with children. While no dog should be left unsupervised with kids, this highlights their gentle side. They are energetic and playful dogs. Staffies need regular exercise and mental stimulation. Their compact size makes them suitable for various living situations. They are loyal and brave dogs.

### Myth: Pitbulls Have "Locking Jaws"

A common myth claims that Pitbulls have "locking jaws." This idea suggests their jaws can somehow lock onto something and not release. This is scientifically untrue. No dog breed possesses a biological "locking mechanism" in its jaws. Their jaw anatomy is similar to other dogs. Pitbulls do have powerful jaws and strong bite muscles, as do many other breeds. Their tenacity in holding on is due to their determination, not a unique physiological feature. This myth often creates undue fear.

### Myth: Pitbulls Are Inherently Aggressive

Another pervasive myth is that Pitbulls are inherently aggressive. This suggests they are born with a predisposition to attack. This is also false. Aggression in dogs is complex. It results from a combination of genetics, environment, training, and socialization. Any dog, regardless of breed, can become aggressive under certain circumstances. A Pitbull raised with love, proper training, and socialization is no more likely to be aggressive than any other breed. Their historical use in fighting led to this stereotype, but it does not define the breed's natural temperament. Many Pitbulls are gentle and friendly.

### Myth: Pitbulls Are Not Good with Children

Some people believe Pitbulls are not suitable for families with children. This is a damaging myth. As mentioned, some Pitbull-type breeds earned the nickname "nanny dog." While individual temperament varies, many Pitbulls are excellent with children. They can be patient, tolerant, and protective playmates. Adult supervision is always necessary when dogs and children interact. This rule applies to all dog breeds, not just Pitbulls.
