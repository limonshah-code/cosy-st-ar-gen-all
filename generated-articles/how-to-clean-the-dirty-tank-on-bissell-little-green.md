## Clean Your Bissell Little Green's Dirty Tank Easily

Your Bissell Little Green spot cleaner is a powerful ally against spills and stains. It works hard to keep your carpets and upholstery looking their best. However, this hard work means its dirty water tank collects a lot of grime. A neglected dirty tank can lead to unpleasant odors and reduced machine performance. I know how frustrating it is when a cleaning tool starts to smell worse than the mess it cleaned.

Keeping your Bissell Little Green in top shape requires regular care. This care includes properly cleaning its dirty water tank. A clean tank ensures your machine operates efficiently. It also prevents the spread of bacteria or mold. This article will guide you through every step of cleaning the dirty tank on your Bissell Little Green. You will learn how to empty, rinse, and deep clean the tank. We will also cover essential maintenance tips to keep your spot cleaner fresh and ready for action.

### Takeaway

*   Regularly empty and rinse the dirty water tank after each use.

To clean the dirty tank on your Bissell Little Green, empty its contents, rinse it thoroughly with warm water, and scrub away any residue. Disinfect with a vinegar solution or mild cleaner, then let it air dry completely before returning it to the machine. This keeps your spot cleaner fresh and ready.

## Understanding Your Bissell Little Green's Tanks

The Bissell Little Green machine has two main tanks. Each tank serves a distinct purpose in the cleaning process. Knowing the role of each tank helps you maintain your machine correctly. I often explain this to friends who are new to portable spot cleaners.

The first tank is the clean water tank. This tank holds your cleaning solution. You fill it with water and your chosen Bissell cleaning formula. This mixture is sprayed onto the stained area. This tank is usually clear or has a fill line.

The second tank is the dirty water collection tank. This tank collects all the dirty water. After you spray and scrub, the machine suctions up the liquid. This liquid, now mixed with dirt and grime, goes into this dirty tank. This tank is often darker or opaque to hide the collected filth. It is also the tank that most commonly develops odors and residue. Keeping both tanks clean is part of good household practices, similar to how you would [clean a dirty house](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-dirty-house) overall.

When you finish cleaning, the dirty tank will contain a mix of water, cleaning solution, and the grime it pulled from your surfaces. It is crucial to empty this tank promptly. Letting dirty water sit in the tank can lead to significant problems. These problems include mold growth and foul smells. Proper care ensures your Bissell Little Green continues to deliver powerful cleaning results.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Emptying and Rinsing the Dirty Tank

Emptying and rinsing the dirty tank immediately after each use is the most important step. This prevents grime from drying and sticking. It also stops odors from developing. This is the routine I follow every time I use my Little Green.

### 1. Detach the Dirty Water Tank

First, turn off and unplug your Bissell Little Green machine. Locate the dirty water tank on your machine. It usually has a handle or a latch. Carefully unlatch it and lift it off the main unit. This tank should come off easily.

### 2. Empty the Contents

Carry the detached tank to a sink or toilet. Pour out all the dirty water and debris. Be prepared for how murky or gritty the water might be. Rinse the sink or toilet thoroughly afterward. It is a good practice to dispose of this water responsibly.

### 3. Rinse the Tank Thoroughly

Now, fill the dirty tank with warm, clean water. Swish the water around vigorously inside the tank. Pay attention to all corners and the lid. Pour out this rinse water. Repeat this step several times until the water you pour out runs clear. This removes loose dirt and residue.

### 4. Clean the Lid and Filter

The lid of the dirty tank often collects grime too. Remove any removable parts from the lid, such as a small filter or float. Rinse these under warm running water. Use a small brush, like an old toothbrush, to scrub away any stuck-on debris. Ensure the float mechanism moves freely if your model has one. ## Deep Cleaning the Dirty Water Tank: Tackling Stubborn Residue

Sometimes a simple rinse is not enough. Stubborn residue, mineral buildup, or lingering odors require a deeper clean. This is especially true if you forgot to empty the tank right away. I perform a deep clean monthly or whenever I notice a persistent smell.

### 1. Prepare a Cleaning Solution

After rinsing, fill the dirty tank about halfway with warm water. Add a few drops of mild dish soap. You can also add a quarter cup of white vinegar for extra deodorizing and disinfectant power. Vinegar helps break down mineral deposits and kills bacteria.

### 2. Scrub the Interior

Close the tank and shake it vigorously for about 30 seconds. This helps the cleaning solution reach all internal surfaces. Then, use your small brush or bottle brush to scrub the inside walls. Pay special attention to corners, crevices, and the suction inlet. This physical scrubbing removes stubborn gunk.

### 3. Disinfect and Deodorize

For persistent odors, let the vinegar and water solution sit in the tank for 15-30 minutes. The acetic acid in vinegar works as a natural disinfectant. It neutralizes odor-causing bacteria. If you do not have vinegar, a small amount of a diluted, pet-safe disinfectant can work. Just make sure it is safe for plastics. The importance of disinfection is high, as a dirty tank can indeed pose health risks, much like wondering if [can a dirty turtle tank make you sick](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-a-dirty-turtle-tank-make-you-sick).

### 4. Rinse Thoroughly and Dry

After scrubbing and soaking, pour out the cleaning solution. Rinse the tank multiple times with fresh, warm water until no soap bubbles remain. Ensure all vinegar smell is gone if you used it. Finally, and most importantly, let the tank air dry completely. Place it upside down on a drying rack or paper towels. Leave it disassembled until it is bone dry. This prevents mold and mildew from growing.

## Maintaining Your Bissell Little Green: Beyond the Dirty Tank

Proper maintenance extends beyond just cleaning the dirty water tank. A comprehensive approach ensures your entire Bissell Little Green machine stays in top condition. I always encourage a holistic view of appliance care.

### 1. Clean the Solution Tank

Even the clean solution tank needs attention. Over time, residue from cleaning formulas can build up. After emptying the dirty tank, empty any leftover solution from the clean tank. Rinse it with warm water and let it air dry. This prevents clogs in the spray nozzle.

### 2. Check and Clean the Nozzle/Brush Head

The nozzle and brush head are where all the cleaning action happens. Hair, fibers, and dried residue can accumulate here. After each use, detach the cleaning tool from the hose. Rinse it under warm water. Use a small brush to dislodge any trapped debris from the spray nozzle and suction path. This ensures proper spray and suction on your next clean.

### 3. Wipe Down the Machine's Exterior

The main body of your Bissell Little Green can also get dirty. Use a damp cloth to wipe down the exterior. Remove any dust, spills, or fingerprints. Keeping the outside clean reflects good care of the inside. This simple step makes your machine look newer for longer.

### 4. Store Properly

Once all parts are clean and completely dry, reassemble your Bissell Little Green. Store it in a cool, dry place. Do not store it with water or solution in either tank. Leaving liquid in the tanks promotes mold growth and can damage the machine. Proper storage protects your investment and keeps it ready for the next mess. ## Troubleshooting Common Dirty Tank Issues and Odors

Even with regular cleaning, you might encounter some common issues. These issues often relate to the dirty water tank. Knowing how to troubleshoot them helps you quickly resolve problems. I have dealt with these issues myself.

### Persistent Odors

If your dirty tank still smells even after deep cleaning, residual bacteria might be the cause. Try a stronger vinegar soak. Mix equal parts white vinegar and warm water. Let this solution sit in the tank for several hours or overnight. Rinse thoroughly afterward. You can also use a specialized enzyme cleaner designed for pet odors. These cleaners break down organic matter. Remember to let it dry completely. A clean tank helps prevent such issues, similar to understanding [why does my turtle tank get dirty so fast](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-does-my-turtle-tank-get-dirty-so-fast) and taking proactive steps.

### Slime or Mold Buildup

Visible slime or mold means moisture was trapped for too long. Scrub the affected areas aggressively with a brush and a bleach-free bathroom cleaner. Rinse multiple times. For prevention, ensure the tank is *always* completely dry before storage. You can even leave the lid off the tank for a day or two to guarantee air circulation. Humidity is mold's best friend.

### Reduced Suction

If your machine has reduced suction, the problem might be more than just a dirty tank. Check the dirty tank's float valve. This small mechanism often stops suction when the tank is full. Ensure it moves freely and is not stuck with debris. Also, check the hose and nozzle for clogs. A blockage anywhere in the suction path reduces performance. Disconnect the hose and rinse it thoroughly from both ends if possible.

### Leaking Tank

A leaking dirty tank indicates either improper reassembly or a damaged seal. First, make sure the tank is securely latched onto the machine. If it continues to leak, inspect the rubber gasket or seal around the tank opening. If it looks cracked or worn, you might need to contact Bissell for a replacement part. Do not overtighten; gentle pressure is usually enough.

## FAQ Section

### How often should I clean the dirty tank on my Bissell Little Green?

You should empty and rinse the dirty tank after every single use. This prevents odors and residue buildup. Perform a deeper clean with soap and a brush at least once a month, or more often if you use the machine frequently or notice any smells. Prompt cleaning maintains hygiene and machine efficiency.

### Can I use bleach to clean the Bissell Little Green tank?

No, you should avoid using bleach. Bleach can be too harsh for the plastic components of your Bissell Little Green. It can degrade the plastic over time, leading to cracks or leaks. Instead, use mild dish soap, white vinegar, or cleaners specifically designed for Bissell machines. These options are safer and effective.

### What causes bad odors in my Bissell Little Green dirty tank?

Bad odors usually come from bacteria growing on collected dirt, pet dander, and old cleaning solution left in the tank. If the tank is not emptied and dried properly after use, this organic matter decomposes. The decomposition process releases foul-smelling gases. Regular, thorough cleaning and complete drying prevent these odors.

### Is it okay to store the Little Green with water in the tanks?

No, it is not okay to store your Bissell Little Green with water or cleaning solution in either tank. Storing liquid encourages mold, mildew, and bacterial growth. This leads to unpleasant smells and can damage the machine's internal parts. Always empty both tanks and allow them to air dry completely before storing your machine.

### How do I prevent mold in my Bissell Little Green tanks?

Preventing mold involves two key steps: always empty both tanks immediately after use, and ensure they are completely air dried before storage. Leave the tank lids off or place them upside down for several hours. This allows air to circulate. A final wipe with a dry cloth can also help remove any last traces of moisture.
