## The Rules and Preparation for Competitive Eating

Competitive eating, especially in the Nathan's Hot Dog Contest, follows strict rules. The competition lasts for ten minutes. Competitors must eat as many hot dogs and buns as possible during this time. Judges sit next to each eater. They count every hot dog and bun swallowed. Each "hot dog" includes both the frankfurter and the bun. Partial consumption might not count.

The most critical rule is "reversal of fortune." This means any regurgitation results in disqualification. Competitors must keep all consumed food down. This rule adds a layer of difficulty and pressure. It tests both physical and mental endurance. Eaters train to avoid this outcome.

Preparation for competitive eaters is intense. They focus on stomach capacity and jaw strength. Many practice eating large volumes of food. They often drink large amounts of water to stretch their stomachs. Some also chew gum to strengthen their jaw muscles. This dedicated training builds the necessary physical attributes.

Eaters also develop specific techniques. These include "dunking" the buns in water. This makes the bread easier to swallow. They might separate the hot dog from the bun. They chew the hot dog quickly. They then put the wet bun in their mouth. This maximizes speed. Mental preparation is also key. Eaters must stay focused and ignore discomfort. They push their bodies to extreme limits for the duration of the contest. The entire contest usually lasts for a short period of intense action.
