## Simple Steps to Create Your Easy Pillow Cover (Envelope Style)

Making an **easy pillow cover** using the envelope style is perfect for beginners. This method avoids zippers or buttons, making it very straightforward. I find this style quick and satisfying. It gives a clean look and allows for easy removal of the pillow form for washing. Let's walk through each step together.

### Measuring and Cutting Your Fabric

First, measure your existing pillow form. An 18x18 inch pillow is a common size. For the width of your fabric, add 1 inch to the width of your pillow form for seam allowances. So, an 18-inch wide pillow needs a 19-inch wide fabric piece. For the length, multiply the pillow form's length by two, then add 6 inches. For an 18-inch long pillow, this means (18 x 2) + 6 = 42 inches. You will cut one long piece of fabric based on these measurements. This single piece will wrap around your pillow.

Cut your fabric piece according to these measurements. Use your fabric scissors and a ruler for straight lines. Accuracy here helps the final product look professional. A straight cut makes subsequent steps much easier.

### Hemming the Edges

Take the two shorter ends of your long fabric piece. Fold each raw edge over by 1/2 inch towards the wrong side of the fabric. Press it flat with your iron. Fold it over another 1/2 inch and press again. This creates a clean, double-hemmed edge. Stitch these hems in place using your sewing machine. A simple straight stitch close to the inner folded edge works best. This prevents fraying and gives a tidy finish to the opening of your pillow cover.

### Assembling the Cover

Lay your large fabric piece wrong side up on your work surface. Take one hemmed end and fold it towards the center of the fabric. The fold should cover roughly two-thirds of your pillow form length. For an 18-inch pillow, this means folding it in about 12-13 inches. Now, take the other hemmed end and fold it over the first one. The two hemmed ends should overlap in the middle, creating the "envelope" opening. This overlap is crucial; it keeps the pillow form securely inside. The total length of the overlapped fabric should be the length of your pillow form. Pin along the top and bottom raw edges of this assembly. You are essentially forming a tube.

### Finishing Touches

Sew along the pinned top and bottom edges using a 1/2-inch seam allowance. Use a straight stitch. Reinforce the beginning and end of each seam with a backstitch. This prevents the stitches from unraveling. Trim any excess fabric close to your seam. You can also zig-zag stitch or serge these raw edges to prevent fraying. Turn your **easy pillow cover** right side out. Push out the corners gently to get a crisp shape. Insert your pillow form through the envelope opening. Your new pillow cover is complete! This simple method is great for quickly changing your decor. ## Choosing the Right Fabric for Your Easy Pillow Cover

Selecting the right fabric is a key step in making an **easy pillow cover**. The fabric choice affects the pillow's look, feel, and durability. It also impacts how easy it is to sew. I always consider the end use of the pillow before buying fabric. A decorative pillow for a sofa might need a different fabric than a pillow for a child's bed.

Cotton fabrics are an excellent choice for beginners. They are easy to cut, sew, and press. Quilting cottons come in thousands of patterns and colors. They offer a smooth finish and are machine washable. Canvas or duck cloth are more durable options. They are heavier and perfect for outdoor pillows or high-traffic areas. These fabrics hold their shape well.

Linen provides a natural, rustic look. It softens with age and washing, giving a casual elegance. However, linen can wrinkle easily. Velvet or faux fur add a luxurious touch. These can be trickier to sew due to their texture and pile. They require careful handling and often a specialized foot on your sewing machine. I recommend these for more experienced sewers.

Consider the fabric's weight and drape. A stiffer fabric will give a more structured pillow. A softer, lighter fabric will result in a more relaxed look. Always check the care instructions on the fabric bolt. You want a fabric that is easy to clean and maintain. Pre-washing your fabric is always a good idea to prevent shrinkage after your pillow cover is sewn. This simple step ensures your finished cover fits your pillow form perfectly, even after its first wash.

## Troubleshooting Common Easy Pillow Cover Challenges

Even with an **easy pillow cover** project, you might encounter small issues. Do not get discouraged. Every sewer faces challenges. I have learned that fixing mistakes is part of the learning process. Knowing how to troubleshoot helps you create a better finished product.

A common problem is crooked seams. This often happens if you pull the fabric while sewing. Instead, let the sewing machine feed the fabric through evenly. Guide it gently. Using plenty of pins also helps keep your fabric layers aligned. Pin every few inches along the seam line. This prevents shifting. If a seam looks too wavy, use your seam ripper and sew it again. Straight seams make a huge difference in the final appearance.

Uneven hems can also be frustrating. This usually comes from inaccurate measuring or pressing. Always measure twice before cutting. Use a ruler or a hem gauge to ensure consistent fold lines. Pressing your hems firmly with an iron before stitching helps them stay straight. If your hem is still uneven, unpick it and re-fold. Take your time with pressing.

Fabric fraying is another challenge, especially with loose-weave fabrics. To prevent this, finish your raw edges. After sewing your main seams, use a zig-zag stitch or an overcast stitch on your sewing machine. You can also use a serger if you have one. These stitches secure the fabric fibers and stop them from unraveling. This creates a much more durable **easy pillow cover**.

## Caring for Your Handmade Easy Pillow Cover

Proper care ensures your **easy pillow cover** stays beautiful for a long time. The care instructions depend largely on the fabric you chose. Always refer to your fabric's original care label if you have it. If not, a gentle approach is usually best for handmade items. I always test a small, hidden spot if I am unsure about cleaning.

Most cotton pillow covers are machine washable. Use cold water and a gentle cycle. Hot water can cause shrinkage or color fading, especially with vibrant prints. I recommend turning the pillow cover inside out before washing. This protects the outer surface and any embellishments. Wash similar colors together to prevent dye transfer.

For delicate fabrics like velvet, silk, or heavily embellished covers, hand washing or dry cleaning might be necessary. Hand wash in cool water with a mild detergent. Gently squeeze out excess water; do not wring. Air dry flat to help maintain the fabric's shape. Direct sunlight can fade some fabrics, so dry indoors or in a shady spot.

Ironing helps keep your pillow cover looking crisp. Use the appropriate iron setting for your fabric type. For many fabrics, ironing while slightly damp produces the best results. If your cover has embellishments, iron around them or use a pressing cloth. Store pillow covers in a clean, dry place when not in use. This protects them from dust and damage. Regular care will keep your handmade **easy pillow cover** looking fresh and vibrant for many years.

## FAQ Section

### How much fabric do I need for an easy pillow cover?

For an 18x18 inch pillow form, you need a fabric piece that is 19 inches wide and 42 inches long. This calculation includes seam allowances and the necessary overlap for the envelope closure. Always measure your specific pillow form and add 1 inch to the width, and (2x length) + 6 inches to the length for accurate fabric needs.

### Can I make an easy pillow cover without a sewing machine?

Yes, you can make a no-sew pillow cover. Popular methods include using fabric glue, fusible web, or even tying knots with fringed fabric. These methods are simple and require no sewing skills. They offer a quick way to update decor. A sewing machine, however, provides a more durable and professional finish.

### What is the easiest type of pillow cover to make?

The envelope style pillow cover is widely considered the easiest. It uses a single piece of fabric folded to create an overlapping back opening. This eliminates the need for zippers, buttons, or complex closures. It is perfect for beginners and yields a clean, functional result.

### How do I wash my handmade pillow cover?

Most handmade cotton pillow covers are machine washable on a gentle cycle with cold water. Turn the cover inside out to protect the fabric. For delicate materials or those with embellishments, hand washing or dry cleaning might be safer. Always check your fabric's specific care instructions.

### Can I use old clothes for an easy pillow cover?

Absolutely! Repurposing old clothes like denim jeans, flannel shirts, or durable cotton dresses is a sustainable and creative way to make an **easy pillow cover**. Ensure the fabric is clean and in good condition. Consider cutting out interesting patterns or textures from the clothing to make your cover unique.
