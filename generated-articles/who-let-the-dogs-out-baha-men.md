## The Baha Men's Journey Beyond One Hit

The Baha Men became famous for "Who Let The Dogs Out." Many people consider them a one-hit wonder. However, their musical journey began long before this global hit. The group formed in the Bahamas in 1977. They started as High Voltage. Their focus was playing traditional Bahamian junkanoo music.

This unique genre involves drums, cowbells, and whistles. It is often played during parades and celebrations. The Baha Men brought this vibrant sound to an international audience. They had released several albums prior to 2000. These albums showcased their talent and dedication to their craft. "Who Let The Dogs Out Baha Men" was just one chapter in their extensive career.

### From High Voltage to Baha Men

The group High Voltage officially changed their name to Baha Men in 1991. This name change marked a new phase in their career. They aimed to broaden their appeal. They started incorporating more contemporary pop and R&B influences into their music. This evolution helped them sign with major record labels.

Their earlier albums, like "Junkanoo" (1992) and "Kalik" (1994), showed their growth. They developed a unique sound blending Bahamian roots with global appeal. Their live performances were legendary for their energy. The band toured extensively, building a loyal fan base. They were more than just a studio band; they were performers.

### The Baha Men's Discography and Achievements

Before "Who Let The Dogs Out," the Baha Men released several albums. These included "I Like to Move It" (1997) and "Doong Spank" (1998). While these albums did not achieve global chart success, they were well-received. They demonstrated the band's versatility and commitment to their art. Their music was popular within the Caribbean region.

After "Who Let The Dogs Out," the Baha Men released more music. They tried to capitalize on their newfound fame. Albums like "Move It Like This" (2002) featured similar upbeat tracks. While none reached the same level of success, they continued to record and perform. They received a Grammy Award in 2001 for Best Dance Recording for "Who Let The Dogs Out."
