### Understanding Biliverdin: The Green Pigment

The culprit behind the green hue is a pigment called biliverdin. Biliverdin is a byproduct of hemoglobin breakdown. Hemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. When red blood cells break down, they release heme. Heme then converts into biliverdin. This pigment is naturally green. It is responsible for the greenish color sometimes seen in bruises.

In humans and animals, biliverdin typically converts into bilirubin. Bilirubin is a yellowish pigment. This conversion mostly happens in the liver. However, under specific conditions within the womb, biliverdin can interact with the puppy's fur. This causes the temporary green staining. It is a natural process that usually poses no risk to the newborn pup.

### Hemoglobin Breakdown and Biliverdin Formation

Our bodies constantly break down and produce red blood cells. When old red blood cells are removed, their hemoglobin is processed. The heme component of hemoglobin is a porphyrin ring. This ring undergoes enzymatic degradation. One of the first products of this degradation is biliverdin. This is a green pigment.

Biliverdin is then usually reduced to bilirubin, which is yellow. This entire pathway is crucial for waste removal. It contributes to bile production, essential for digestion. In the case of a green puppy, the biliverdin phase is key. It means that somewhere in the fetal environment, this green pigment becomes accessible to the puppy's developing fur.

### The Placental Connection: How Pigment Reaches the Puppy

For a puppy's fur to turn green, biliverdin must be present in the amniotic fluid. The amniotic fluid surrounds and protects the fetus in the womb. If the placenta is stressed or slightly damaged, it can release substances into this fluid. These substances can include biliverdin. Sometimes, a small amount of maternal blood can leak into the amniotic fluid. The breakdown of this blood's hemoglobin can then lead to biliverdin accumulation.

The puppy's light fur absorbs this pigment from the fluid. It acts like a dye. The staining occurs directly on the developing hair shafts. This is why the color is external. It does not affect the puppy's internal organs or genetic makeup. The placenta acts as a filter, but sometimes, these pigments can bypass it. This temporary exposure leads to the unique green tint.
