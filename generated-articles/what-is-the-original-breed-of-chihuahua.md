These small dogs quickly captivated visitors. Their unique appearance and portability made them popular souvenirs.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: What is the earliest evidence of the Chihuahua's existence?
The earliest evidence points to the Techichi, believed to be the Chihuahua's ancestor. Archaeological findings in Mexico, including ancient pottery and dog remains, date back to civilizations like the Toltecs (around 900-1200 AD) and later the Aztecs. These artifacts depict small dogs resembling the Chihuahua's ancient form.

### Q2: Did the Techichi look exactly like a modern Chihuahua?
No, the Techichi likely did not look exactly like a modern Chihuahua. Evidence suggests the Techichi was slightly larger and sturdier than today's tiny Chihuahua. Artistic depictions indicate a flatter head and potentially different ear sets. Modern Chihuahuas have been selectively bred for their very small size and distinctive "apple head" shape.

### Q3: Why is the breed named Chihuahua?
The breed is named after the Mexican state of Chihuahua. American travelers rediscovered these small dogs in significant numbers in this region during the mid-19th century. As the dogs were brought to the United States and gained popularity, they became associated with their place of origin, hence the name "Chihuahua."

### Q4: Were Chihuahuas always companion dogs?
The ancient Techichi served multiple roles. They were companions, but also held significant ceremonial and spiritual importance in Toltec and Aztec cultures. They were believed to guide souls and protect against evil. Modern Chihuahuas are almost exclusively bred and kept as companion animals and lapdogs.

### Q5: Are there any other breeds related to the Techichi?
While the Techichi is primarily linked to the Chihuahua, the exact genetic tree of ancient Mesoamerican dogs is complex. Some theories suggest a broader group of small, native Mexican dogs, but the Techichi remains the most prominent and widely accepted ancestor for the modern Chihuahua.

### Q6: How did the Chihuahua become so small?
The Chihuahua's extreme small size is largely due to selective breeding. Over generations, breeders favored smaller individuals, gradually miniaturizing the breed from its slightly larger Techichi ancestors. This process began in earnest after the breed gained popularity in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
