Wipe the points clean with a lint-free cloth and contact cleaner, then reassemble and adjust the point gap for proper engine timing.

## Understanding Distributor Points: The Heart of Your Ignition

Distributor points, also known as contact breaker points, are a crucial component in older vehicle ignition systems. They act like an electrical switch. Their main job is to interrupt the low-voltage current flowing to the ignition coil. This interruption creates a high-voltage spark.

When the points open, the magnetic field in the coil collapses. This collapse induces a powerful voltage in the coil's secondary winding. This high voltage then travels through the distributor rotor and cap to the correct spark plug. The spark ignites the air-fuel mixture in the engine cylinder. For the engine to run smoothly, this process must happen at a precise moment for each cylinder.

Over time, these points can become corroded or pitted. Each time the points open, a tiny spark occurs. This spark slowly wears away the metal. It creates a rough surface or builds up carbon deposits. Such buildup hinders the electrical current flow. This causes a weaker spark at the spark plug.

A weak spark leads to various engine problems. You might experience misfires, reduced power, or poor fuel economy. The engine may also become difficult to start. Regularly inspecting and cleaning distributor points helps maintain their efficiency. It ensures your engine gets the strong, consistent spark it needs. This simple task can significantly improve your vehicle's overall performance.

## Accessing Distributor Points: Step-by-Step Guide

The first step in learning how to clean points on a distributor is gaining access to them. The distributor is usually located on the engine block. It typically drives off the camshaft. Always consult your vehicle's service manual for its exact location. The manual will also show specific removal instructions for your model.

Here is how you typically access the distributor points:

1.  **Locate the Distributor:** Find the distributor cap, which is a round, often black, plastic cap. Several spark plug wires connect to it. A larger wire from the ignition coil also connects to the center.
2.  **Mark the Rotor Position (Crucial!):** Before removing anything, it is critical to mark the rotor's position. Use a piece of chalk or a marker to draw a line on the distributor housing and the engine block. This ensures you reinstall the distributor in the exact same orientation. Incorrect reinstallation can severely affect engine timing.
3.  **Remove the Distributor Cap:** The cap is usually held in place by two spring clips or small screws. Release these clips or unscrew them. Carefully lift the cap straight up. If it is stubborn, a gentle wiggle can help. You will see the rotor underneath.
4.  **Remove the Rotor:** The rotor sits on top of the distributor shaft. It usually pulls straight off or is held by a small screw. Note its orientation before removing it. It often has a keyway to prevent incorrect reinstallation.
5.  **Locate the Points and Condenser:** With the cap and rotor off, you will see the points assembly. This consists of two contact points, one fixed and one movable, mounted on a base plate. The condenser, a small cylindrical component, is typically mounted nearby. Its wire connects to the points.

6.  **Examine the Points:** Look closely at the contact surfaces of the points. They should be clean and smooth. You will likely see pitting, corrosion, or a bluish-black coating. This indicates they need cleaning.

Once you have exposed the points, you are ready for the cleaning process. Take your time with these steps. Precision here ensures easier reassembly and proper engine function.

## The Cleaning Process: How to Clean Distributor Points Effectively

Now that you have accessed the points, it is time for the cleaning. This step directly addresses how to clean points on a distributor. The goal is to remove any oxidation, pitting, or carbon buildup. This ensures a strong, consistent electrical connection.

Follow these steps for effective cleaning:

1.  **Separate the Points:** Carefully open the points as wide as they can go. Some points have a small lever you can press. Others may require you to gently pry them apart with a non-metallic tool. Be gentle to avoid bending or damaging the arm.
2.  **Clean the Contact Surfaces:**
    *   **Using a Point File:** A dedicated point file is the best tool for this job. It has fine teeth and is designed to create a flat, smooth surface. Slide the file between the contact points. Gently rub it back and forth a few times. The file removes the pitted or oxidized material. It creates a fresh metal surface. Do this until both contact surfaces look clean and smooth.
    *   **Using Fine-Grit Sandpaper:** If you do not have a point file, you can use very fine-grit sandpaper (around 400-600 grit). Fold a small piece of sandpaper. Insert it between the points with the abrasive side facing the contacts. Gently rub the sandpaper back and forth. Be careful not to round off the edges of the points. Sandpaper might leave small abrasive particles, so a thorough cleaning afterward is crucial.
3.  **Wipe Away Debris:** After filing or sanding, use a lint-free cloth or a cotton swab. Dip it in contact cleaner or rubbing alcohol. Carefully wipe both contact surfaces. This removes any metal dust, carbon particles, or residue. Make sure no fibers are left behind. Clean points are essential for good electrical conductivity.
4.  **Inspect the Points Again:** Once cleaned, look at the points closely. They should appear bright and smooth. There should be no visible pitting, corrosion, or black spots. If you still see imperfections, repeat the cleaning process. Ensure both surfaces meet flush when closed.

Proper cleaning restores the electrical integrity of your distributor points. This improves spark quality. It brings your ignition system closer to optimal performance.

## Inspecting and Adjusting Distributor Points

Cleaning the distributor points is only part of the job. After cleaning, you must inspect them thoroughly. Then, you must adjust the point gap to the correct specification. This step is critical for proper engine timing and efficient ignition.

Here is how to inspect and adjust your distributor points:

1.  **Inspect the Point Surfaces:** After cleaning, the contact surfaces should be flat, smooth, and free of pitting or oxidation. If the points are severely worn, burned, or if the moving arm is weak, cleaning alone may not be enough. In such cases, replacement is the better option. A healthy point surface ensures good electrical transfer.
2.  **Set the Engine to Top Dead Center (TDC) or Point Opening Position:** You need to rotate the engine manually. This positions the distributor cam lobe so the points are fully open.
    *   **Method 1: Bump the Engine:** Briefly turn the ignition key to "start" in short bursts. Watch the distributor shaft. Stop when the heel of the moving point arm is on the highest part of a cam lobe. This is where the points are widest open.
    *   **Method 2: Rotate Crankshaft by Hand:** If your car allows, use a socket wrench on the crankshaft pulley bolt. Slowly turn the engine until a cam lobe fully opens the points.
3.  **Adjust the Point Gap:**
    *   **Use a Feeler Gauge:** Select the correct feeler gauge blade for your vehicle's point gap specification. This information is in your service manual.
    *   **Insert the Gauge:** Slide the feeler gauge blade between the two contact points. It should slide in with a slight drag. It should not be too loose or too tight.
    *   **Loosen and Adjust:** If the gap is incorrect, loosen the small screw that holds the fixed point plate. Use a screwdriver or small pry bar in the adjustment notch to move the fixed point plate. Adjust it until the feeler gauge slides with the correct drag.
    *   **Tighten and Recheck:** Tighten the retaining screw securely. Recheck the gap with the feeler gauge. Sometimes tightening can slightly change the gap.
4.  **Check Dwell Angle (Optional but Recommended):** After setting the point gap, a dwell meter provides a more precise adjustment. Connect the dwell meter according to its instructions. Start the engine. The dwell meter measures how long the points remain closed. Adjust the point gap slightly until the dwell angle matches your vehicle's specification. An accurate dwell angle ensures optimal coil saturation and spark energy.

Correct point gap and dwell angle are fundamental for precise ignition timing. This leads to efficient combustion and better engine performance.

## Reassembly and Final Checks

1.  **Install the Rotor:** Carefully place the rotor back onto the distributor shaft. Ensure it aligns correctly with any keyways or alignment marks. The rotor should sit firmly in place. Remember its original orientation.
2.  **Install the Distributor Cap:** Position the distributor cap over the rotor and distributor housing. Align it with the original marks you made earlier. Ensure all spark plug wires are routed correctly and securely seated. Fasten the cap with its spring clips or screws. Make sure the cap is fully seated and does not wobble.

## When to Replace Distributor Points

Cleaning distributor points is a good temporary fix. However, there comes a time when cleaning is no longer enough. Knowing when to replace your distributor points is crucial for maintaining engine health. Ignoring badly worn points can lead to more serious ignition problems.

Here are the signs and situations that indicate it is time for a replacement:

*   **Severe Pitting and Erosion:** If the contact surfaces are deeply pitted or significantly eroded, cleaning will not restore them. The metal is simply too worn away. This prevents a flat, clean contact. A small amount of pitting is normal, but deep craters mean replacement.
*   **Burned or Carbonized Points:** Points that are consistently burned black or covered in heavy carbon deposits often indicate a failing condenser. However, if the points themselves show severe burning even after cleaning or condenser replacement, they need to be replaced.
*   **Weak or Bent Moving Arm:** The spring arm that holds the moving contact point can lose its tension or become bent. This prevents the points from opening and closing properly. A weak arm might cause erratic point opening. This leads to inconsistent spark timing.
*   **Difficulty Setting Point Gap:** If you find it impossible to set the point gap accurately, the points might be too worn. The retaining screw might not hold the adjustment. This signals that the entire assembly needs replacement.
*   **Consistent Misfires or Rough Idling:** If you have cleaned and adjusted the points, but your engine still experiences persistent misfires, rough idling, or poor performance, the points themselves might be the underlying issue. A new set can often resolve these problems.
*   **Routine Maintenance Interval:** Many manufacturers recommend replacing points as part of a regular tune-up. This is typically every 10,000 to 20,000 miles, depending on the vehicle. Even if they look okay, replacing them as preventative maintenance can prevent future breakdowns.

### FAQ Section

#### Q1: How often should I clean distributor points?
**A:** You should typically inspect and clean distributor points every 6,000 to 12,000 miles or during a regular tune-up. Driving conditions and vehicle age can influence this frequency. If you notice engine performance issues, check them sooner. Regular maintenance helps prevent severe wear and maintains engine efficiency.

#### Q2: What causes distributor points to get dirty or pitted?
**A:** Distributor points get dirty and pitted primarily due to electrical arcing. Each time the points open, a small spark forms. This spark erodes the metal surfaces. Over time, this leads to carbon buildup, oxidation, and physical pitting. A failing condenser can also accelerate point wear.

#### Q3: Can I use regular sandpaper to clean distributor points?
**A:** It is best to use a dedicated point file or very fine-grit sandpaper (400-600 grit) to clean distributor points. Regular, coarser sandpaper can remove too much material. It can also create an uneven surface. This might worsen electrical contact. Always wipe away any abrasive residue thoroughly afterward.

#### Q4: What happens if I do not clean my distributor points?
**A:** If you do not clean your distributor points, your engine's performance will suffer. Dirty or pitted points cause a weak or inconsistent spark. This leads to misfires, rough idling, poor acceleration, and reduced fuel economy. Eventually, the engine may become difficult to start or fail to run entirely.

#### Q5: Is cleaning distributor points difficult for a DIY mechanic?
**A:** Cleaning distributor points is a relatively simple DIY task. It requires basic tools and patience. The most critical steps are accurately setting the point gap and ensuring correct timing. First-time DIYers should follow instructions closely and refer to their vehicle's service manual.

#### Q6: Do all cars have distributor points?
**A:** No, not all cars have distributor points. Distributor points were common in older vehicles, typically those manufactured before the mid-1970s. Modern cars use electronic ignition systems. These systems provide more reliable, maintenance-free ignition. They do not have physical contact points that wear out.
