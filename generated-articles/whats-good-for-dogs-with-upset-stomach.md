## What's Good For Dogs With Upset Stomach: Soothe Their Tummy

Watching your dog suffer from an upset stomach is stressful. My heart sinks every time I see my dog uncomfortable. Their usual playful energy disappears. You want to know **what's good for dogs with upset stomach**. Many things can cause a dog's stomach to feel bad. These include eating something bad, stress, or even an infection. Knowing how to help your dog is important. This guide will help you understand common causes. We will also explore safe foods and home remedies. You will learn when to seek veterinary help.

### Takeaway

When your dog has an upset stomach, focus on these key actions:

*   **Withhold food:** Allow the digestive system to rest for 12-24 hours.
*   **Offer small amounts of water:** Prevent dehydration with fresh, clean water.
*   **Introduce a bland diet:** Begin with boiled chicken and plain white rice.
*   **Monitor symptoms:** Watch for signs of worsening conditions or new issues.
*   **Consult your vet:** Seek professional advice for persistent or severe symptoms.

### Main Query Answer

For dogs with an upset stomach, a short period of food withholding is often good. After this, introduce small, frequent servings of a bland diet. This diet usually involves plain boiled chicken and white rice. Ensure your dog drinks plenty of water to prevent dehydration.

## Understanding Your Dog's Upset Stomach

A dog's upset stomach can show in many ways. It might involve vomiting or diarrhea. Sometimes, a dog just seems to have low energy. I always watch my dog closely for any changes. Common causes range from mild to serious. It is important to know what might be happening.

### Common Causes of Digestive Issues

Many things can make a dog's stomach feel bad. Dietary indiscretion is a main culprit. This means eating garbage or human food they should not have. Stress can also affect a dog's gut. Changes in routine or environment may cause this. Parasites like worms are another cause. They live in the intestines and irritate them. Viral or bacterial infections can also lead to stomach upset. These infections often cause quick and severe symptoms.

*   **Dietary Indiscretion:** Eating spoiled food, fatty table scraps, or non-food items.
*   **Stress and Anxiety:** New environments, loud noises, or separation anxiety.
*   **Parasites:** Internal worms or protozoa like Giardia.
*   **Infections:** Viral (e.g., parvovirus) or bacterial (e.g., salmonella) infections.
*   **Food Allergies or Sensitivities:** Reaction to specific ingredients in their food.
*   **Sudden Diet Changes:** Switching dog food too quickly.
*   **Medication Side Effects:** Some medicines can irritate the stomach.

Knowing the cause helps you decide the best action. Sometimes, the issue resolves on its own. Other times, it needs medical attention. If you are wondering about the initial steps, read more on [what to do for dog with upset stomach](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-for-dog-with-upset-stomach).

### Recognizing the Symptoms

Symptoms of an upset stomach vary. Vomiting is a common sign. Diarrhea is also very typical. My dog might also show lethargy. This means they have less energy than normal. Loss of appetite is another key sign. They may refuse their favorite food. Sometimes, you see excessive drooling. This can mean nausea. Abdominal pain might appear as hunching or whining. Dogs might also lick their lips often. These signs tell you something is wrong. Pay close attention to how your dog behaves. Early detection helps. You can then provide quicker comfort.

*   **Vomiting:** Expelling stomach contents.
*   **Diarrhea:** Loose or watery stools.
*   **Lethargy:** Reduced energy and activity.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or eating less.
*   **Excessive Drooling:** A sign of nausea.
*   **Abdominal Pain:** Hunching, stretching, or vocalizing discomfort.
*   **Repeated Swallowing:** Also a sign of nausea.

Monitor symptom severity. Note how often vomiting or diarrhea occurs. This information helps your vet. It guides your home care decisions.

## The Go-To Bland Diet: Boiled Chicken and Rice

When my dog has an upset stomach, a bland diet is the first step. This approach gives their digestive system a break. It provides nutrients without causing further irritation. The most common and effective bland diet involves boiled chicken and white rice. These ingredients are easy to digest. They are gentle on a sensitive stomach.

### Preparing Boiled Chicken

Boiled chicken is a staple for an upset dog. Use plain, boneless, skinless chicken breast. Avoid any seasonings, oils, or butter. These additions can further irritate your dog's stomach. I simply boil the chicken until it is cooked through. Then, I shred it into small pieces. This makes it easier for my dog to eat and digest. A meat thermometer ensures it is fully cooked. Cooked chicken provides needed protein. It also helps your dog regain strength.

*   **Choose:** Boneless, skinless chicken breast.
*   **Cook:** Boil in plain water until fully cooked.
*   **Prepare:** Shred into small, easily digestible pieces.
*   **Avoid:** Seasonings, oils, butter, or chicken skin.

Remember, the goal is plainness. Any extra ingredients can do more harm than good. You can also explore more on [what to feed a dog with upset stomach](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-feed-a-dog-with-upset-stomach).

### Preparing Plain White Rice

Plain white rice complements the chicken perfectly. It is a simple carbohydrate. It adds bulk to stools, which can help with diarrhea. Cook white rice in water until it is very soft. Do not add any salt or butter. Brown rice is not good for an upset stomach. It has too much fiber. The fiber is harder to digest. White rice absorbs water in the gut. This helps firm up loose stools.

*   **Choose:** Plain white rice.
*   **Cook:** Boil in water until very soft.
*   **Avoid:** Salt, butter, or brown rice.

Mix the shredded chicken and rice. A good ratio is one part chicken to two parts rice. Start with small amounts. Offer a tablespoon or two every few hours. Gradually increase the amount as your dog improves. The aim is to feed small, frequent meals. This prevents overloading the sensitive digestive system.

### Introducing the Bland Diet Gradually

Introduce the bland diet slowly. Do not offer a large meal right away. After a period of fasting (usually 12-24 hours), give a very small amount. A tablespoon is a good starting point for a small dog. Two tablespoons for a larger dog. Wait an hour. If no vomiting occurs, offer another small portion. Continue this throughout the day. If your dog keeps the food down, gradually increase the amount and frequency. Do this over the next 2-3 days.

*   **Initial Step:** Fast your dog for 12-24 hours, if appropriate and advised by a vet.
*   **Small Portions:** Begin with 1-2 tablespoons every 2-3 hours.
*   **Monitor:** Watch for any return of symptoms.
*   **Gradual Increase:** Slowly increase portion size and reduce frequency over several days.

Transition back to their regular food slowly. Mix a small amount of their regular food with the bland diet. Do this for a few days. Then, increase the normal food and decrease the bland diet. This gradual change prevents another upset. It gives their stomach time to adjust. ## Other Soothing Foods for Canine Digestion

While chicken and rice are primary, other foods can also help. These options offer different nutrients. They can soothe a sensitive digestive system. Always introduce new foods slowly. Monitor your dog for any adverse reactions. The goal is to provide gentle nourishment.

### Canned Pumpkin (Plain)

Plain canned pumpkin is excellent for digestion. It is rich in soluble fiber. This fiber absorbs excess water in the gut. This helps firm up loose stools. It also helps add bulk if your dog is constipated. Make sure to use 100% plain canned pumpkin. Do not use pumpkin pie filling. Pie filling has sugar and spices. These ingredients are bad for dogs. Start with a small amount. A teaspoon for small dogs. A tablespoon for large dogs. Add it to their bland diet or offer it alone.

*   **Type:** 100% plain canned pumpkin (not pie filling).
*   **Benefit:** High in soluble fiber, helps with both diarrhea and constipation.
*   **Dosage:** Start with small amounts (1 tsp - 1 tbsp) mixed with food.

Pumpkin is a natural remedy. It helps many dogs feel better. Many sources recommend plain pumpkin as something [what can you give a dog for upset stomach](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-can-you-give-a-dog-for-upset-stomach).

### Boiled Sweet Potato

Boiled sweet potato is another great option. Like pumpkin, it is rich in fiber. It provides vitamins and minerals. These include Vitamin A and C. Sweet potatoes are easy to digest. Boil the sweet potato until it is very soft. Then mash it. Do not add any seasonings. Feed it plain. A small portion mixed with chicken can be very soothing. It adds variety to the bland diet.

*   **Preparation:** Boil until soft, then mash.
*   **Benefit:** Good source of fiber, vitamins, and minerals.
*   **Serving:** Offer plain, unseasoned, in small amounts.

### Bone Broth (Plain)

Bone broth is a comforting liquid. It is highly digestible. It contains amino acids. These amino acids support gut healing. It also helps with hydration. Make sure to use plain, unsalted bone broth. Avoid broths with onions or garlic. These are toxic to dogs. You can buy pre-made dog-specific bone broth. Or make your own at home. Offer it warm. It encourages drinking. This is very good if your dog is not drinking enough water.

*   **Type:** Plain, unsalted bone broth (dog-specific or homemade without onions/garlic).
*   **Benefit:** Aids gut healing, provides hydration, easy to digest.
*   **Serving:** Offer warm, in small amounts.

Bone broth can be a great way to introduce liquids and nutrients. It is gentle on a sensitive stomach.

## Hydration is Key: Preventing Dehydration

Dehydration is a serious risk for dogs with an upset stomach. Vomiting and diarrhea cause fluid loss. My primary concern when my dog has an upset tummy is always making sure they drink enough. Preventing dehydration is vital for recovery. It helps all body functions work correctly.

### Offering Water Regularly

Provide fresh, clean water at all times. Change the water bowl often. This ensures the water is appealing. Some dogs may not want to drink much. Offer small amounts of water frequently. An ice cube can sometimes encourage drinking. My dog loves to lick ice cubes. It can be a fun way to get fluids into them. Do not let your dog gulp down a large amount of water at once. This can trigger more vomiting.

*   **Availability:** Always provide fresh, clean water.
*   **Frequency:** Offer small amounts often.
*   **Encouragement:** Use ice cubes or a little plain bone broth in water.
*   **Caution:** Prevent rapid, large intake to avoid further vomiting.

Keep an eye on their water intake. If they are not drinking, this is a concern.

### Electrolyte Solutions (Vet-Approved)

For severe fluid loss, plain water might not be enough. Electrolyte solutions help restore mineral balance. These minerals are vital for body function. They are lost during vomiting or diarrhea. Do not give human electrolyte drinks. These often contain too much sugar or artificial sweeteners. Some sweeteners like Xylitol are toxic to dogs. Always use a veterinarian-approved electrolyte solution. Your vet can tell you the right product. They can also advise on dosage.

*   **Purpose:** Restore lost minerals and hydration.
*   **Source:** Use only vet-approved dog-specific solutions.
*   **Caution:** Avoid human electrolyte drinks; they may contain harmful ingredients.

Your vet might suggest oral rehydration solutions. These are specially made for pets. They contain the right balance of electrolytes. This helps your dog absorb fluids better. Keeping your dog hydrated is one of the most important things you can do. It directly impacts their recovery.

## Natural Remedies and Supplements for Gut Health

Beyond food, certain natural remedies and supplements can support gut health. They can help soothe an upset stomach. They may also aid long-term digestive balance. I consider these options when my dog needs a little extra help. Always discuss these with your vet first.

### Probiotics

Probiotics are beneficial bacteria. They help maintain a healthy gut. An upset stomach can disrupt the balance of gut flora. Probiotics replenish good bacteria. This supports digestion. It also boosts the immune system. Choose dog-specific probiotics. Human probiotics may not have the right strains for dogs. Give probiotics after the acute upset phase. They work best for long-term gut health.

*   **Function:** Restore healthy gut bacteria balance.
*   **Source:** Use dog-specific probiotic supplements.
*   **Timing:** Introduce after initial stomach upset calms.

You can often find probiotics in powder or chewable forms. They are easy to add to your dog's food. ### Ginger

Ginger is known for its anti-nausea properties. It has been used for centuries to calm upset stomachs. A small amount of fresh ginger can help. You can steep a tiny piece of fresh ginger in hot water. Let it cool completely. Offer a teaspoon of the cooled "ginger tea" to your dog. Or, finely mince a tiny piece. Mix it with their bland food. Start with very small amounts. Too much ginger can cause stomach upset.

*   **Benefit:** Known for anti-nausea effects.
*   **Preparation:** Make a weak ginger tea or mince tiny amount.
*   **Dosage:** Start with very small amounts, less than a quarter teaspoon.

Always use fresh ginger. Avoid powdered ginger. It can be more potent.

### Slippery Elm Bark

Slippery elm bark is a natural demulcent. It forms a protective coating on mucous membranes. This includes the lining of the stomach and intestines. It can soothe irritation. It may help with both diarrhea and vomiting. Buy slippery elm powder. Mix it with cool water to form a thin slurry. Give it orally with a syringe. Or mix it with bland food. Follow dosage instructions carefully. Consult your vet before using.

*   **Benefit:** Soothes irritated digestive tract lining.
*   **Form:** Use slippery elm bark powder.
*   **Preparation:** Mix with cool water to form a slurry.

Slippery elm can sometimes affect medication absorption. Give it at least an hour apart from any other medications.

## When to Call the Vet: Red Flags and Serious Symptoms

While home care is often helpful, some situations require immediate vet attention. My rule of thumb is: when in doubt, call the vet. It is always better to be safe. Recognizing warning signs can save your dog's life.

### Recognizing Emergency Situations

Certain symptoms point to a more serious issue. Severe vomiting or diarrhea, especially if bloody, needs urgent care. Constant retching without producing anything can be a sign of bloat. This is a life-threatening condition. Extreme lethargy or weakness also means trouble. If your dog collapses, seek emergency help. Pale gums are a sign of shock or anemia. Any swelling of the abdomen should be alarming. These signs mean something much more serious is happening.

*   **Severe or Persistent Vomiting:** More than two episodes in a few hours, or projectile vomiting.
*   **Bloody Vomit or Diarrhea:** Bright red blood or dark, tarry stools.
*   **Constant Retching:** Trying to vomit but nothing comes up (a sign of bloat).
*   **Extreme Lethargy or Weakness:** Unable to stand or unresponsive.
*   **Pale Gums:** Indicates poor circulation or anemia.
*   **Abdominal Swelling or Pain:** A hard, distended belly, or vocalizing pain.
*   **Fever:** Body temperature above 102.5°F (39.2°C).

If you see any of these, do not wait. Get to a vet right away. For advice on what to give dogs if they are also throwing up, you can read [what to give dogs for upset stomach and throwing up](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-give-dogs-for-upset-stomach-and-throwing-up).

### Chronic or Worsening Symptoms

An upset stomach should improve within a day or two with home care. If symptoms last longer, consult your vet. If they get worse, also call the vet. This includes continued vomiting, diarrhea, or loss of appetite. Dehydration can worsen quickly in small dogs or puppies. They are more vulnerable. Chronic digestive issues also need a vet's diagnosis. This could point to underlying conditions. Examples include inflammatory bowel disease or organ problems.

*   **Symptoms lasting over 24-48 hours:** Diarrhea or vomiting that does not resolve.
*   **No improvement with bland diet:** Dog still refuses food or vomits.
*   **Increased frequency or severity:** Symptoms are getting worse over time.
*   **Signs of Dehydration:** Sunken eyes, sticky gums, loss of skin elasticity.
*   **Weight Loss:** Unexplained weight loss with digestive issues.
*   **Recurring episodes:** Frequent bouts of upset stomach.

Your vet can perform tests. They can find the cause of the problem. They can then suggest the right treatment. This might involve medication or a specific diet plan. It is important to work with your vet. They are your best resource for your dog's health. ## Preventing Future Upset Stomachs: Proactive Care

Preventing an upset stomach is always better than treating one. Good proactive care helps keep your dog's digestive system healthy. I follow these steps to reduce my dog's risk of tummy troubles. A consistent routine and smart choices make a big difference.

### Consistent Diet and Slow Transitions

Feeding a consistent, high-quality diet is fundamental. Avoid frequent changes in dog food. Your dog's digestive system adapts to what they eat. Sudden changes can disrupt this balance. If you must change your dog's food, do it slowly. Mix the new food with the old food. Start with mostly old food and a small amount of new. Gradually increase the new food over 7-10 days. This gives their stomach time to adjust. It helps prevent digestive upset.

*   **High-Quality Food:** Choose a consistent, reputable brand of dog food.
*   **Avoid Frequent Changes:** Stick to one food type for extended periods.
*   **Gradual Transition:** Mix new food with old food over 7-10 days.

This approach helps maintain a stable gut environment. It reduces stress on the digestive system.

### Limiting Table Scraps and Unsafe Foods

Human food, especially fatty or spicy items, can quickly upset a dog's stomach. Many human foods are simply not good for dogs. Some are even toxic. Things like onions, garlic, chocolate, and grapes are dangerous. Avoid giving your dog table scraps. It is tempting to share, but it is not good for them. Stick to dog-specific treats. Supervise your dog closely when outdoors. Prevent them from eating unknown items. This includes trash or plants.

*   **No Table Scraps:** Avoid giving human food, especially fatty or spicy items.
*   **Identify Toxic Foods:** Learn which foods are dangerous for dogs (e.g., chocolate, grapes, onions).
*   **Supervise Outdoors:** Prevent eating trash, unknown plants, or animal waste.

This proactive measure greatly reduces the risk of dietary indiscretion. It protects your dog from harmful substances.

### Regular Vet Check-ups and Parasite Control

Regular vet visits are crucial for overall health. Your vet can catch potential issues early. They can advise on proper nutrition for your dog's specific needs. Routine parasite control is also very important. Worms and other parasites cause many digestive problems. Deworming as recommended by your vet helps prevent these issues. My vet always checks for parasites during annual visits. This gives me peace of mind.

*   **Routine Exams:** Schedule regular check-ups with your veterinarian.
*   **Parasite Prevention:** Follow your vet's recommendations for deworming and parasite control.
*   **Discuss Diet:** Talk to your vet about your dog's diet and any digestive concerns.

These steps help maintain a healthy digestive system. They minimize the chances of future upset stomachs. Being proactive helps your dog live a happier, healthier life. ## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I give my dog human medicine for an upset stomach?

No, do not give your dog human medicine. Many human medicines are toxic to dogs. Even common over-the-counter drugs can be dangerous. Always consult your veterinarian before giving any medication. They will prescribe safe, dog-specific treatments if needed. A vet can properly diagnose the problem.

### Q2: How long should I fast my dog before offering food again?

A fasting period of 12-24 hours is often recommended for adult dogs. This allows their digestive system to rest and recover. Never fast puppies or very small dogs for long periods. They are prone to low blood sugar. Always provide access to fresh water during fasting. Consult your vet for advice specific to your dog.

### Q3: What are common signs of dehydration in dogs?

Signs of dehydration include sunken eyes, sticky or dry gums, and reduced skin elasticity. You can check skin elasticity by gently pulling up the skin between their shoulder blades. If it does not snap back quickly, your dog may be dehydrated. Lethargy and weakness are also common. Severe dehydration needs immediate vet attention.

### Q4: When should I be worried about my dog's diarrhea?

You should worry if diarrhea is severe, frequent, or bloody. If it lasts longer than 24-48 hours, see a vet. Other red flags include extreme lethargy, vomiting, or signs of pain. Puppies are more vulnerable to dehydration. Any dog with persistent diarrhea should see a vet promptly.

### Q5: Can stress cause a dog's upset stomach?

Yes, stress and anxiety can certainly cause an upset stomach in dogs. Just like humans, dogs can experience digestive issues from stress. Changes in routine, new environments, or loud noises can trigger it. Address the source of stress if possible. Provide a calm, stable environment for your dog.

### Q6: Is plain yogurt good for a dog's upset stomach?

Plain, unsweetened yogurt can sometimes be good due to its probiotics. However, many dogs are lactose intolerant. This means dairy can actually worsen their stomach upset. It is generally safer to use dog-specific probiotic supplements. Always choose plain, unsweetened varieties if you do offer it. Start with a very small amount to test tolerance.
