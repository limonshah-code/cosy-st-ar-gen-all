### The Science Behind Carprofen's Action

Rimadyl's active ingredient is carprofen. Carprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug. It works by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes. These enzymes are key players in the body's inflammatory response. There are two main types: COX-1 and COX-2.

COX-1 enzymes protect the stomach lining and maintain kidney function. COX-2 enzymes primarily trigger inflammation and pain. Carprofen preferentially inhibits COX-2 enzymes. This means it reduces inflammation and pain with fewer side effects on the stomach and kidneys compared to some older NSAIDs. This selective action is a significant advantage. ### Proper Dosing Guidelines

Rimadyl comes in various forms, including chewable tablets and injectable solutions. The typical oral dosage for dogs is 2 mg per pound of body weight per day. This can be given as a single daily dose or divided into two smaller doses every 12 hours. Your vet will specify the exact regimen.

It is important to give the medication with food. This can help reduce the risk of gastrointestinal upset. Always ensure your dog consumes the entire dose. If you miss a dose, give it as soon as you remember. However, do not double dose. Consistent administration is key to maintaining stable pain relief for your dog.

### Common and Serious Adverse Reactions

Most dogs tolerate Rimadyl well. However, some dogs may experience side effects. Common side effects usually involve the gastrointestinal system. These include:

*   **Vomiting:** Your dog might throw up after taking the medication.
*   **Diarrhea:** Loose stools can occur.
*   **Decreased Appetite:** Your dog might show less interest in food.
*   **Lethargy:** Your dog may seem unusually tired or less active.

More serious, though less common, side effects can affect the liver, kidneys, or digestive tract. Signs of these more severe reactions include:

*   **Black, tarry stools or blood in vomit:** These indicate gastrointestinal bleeding.
*   **Jaundice:** Yellowing of gums or whites of the eyes, suggesting liver problems.
*   **Increased thirst or urination:** Possible signs of kidney issues.
*   **Behavioral changes:** Changes in mood or activity levels.

If you observe any of these serious signs, stop giving Rimadyl immediately and contact your veterinarian.
