## Cleaning Your Bissell Little Green Head for Optimal Use

You rely on your Bissell Little Green machine for quick cleanups. Its compact design makes it perfect for tackling spills and stains. However, the cleaning head, often called the upholstery tool or stain tool, gets dirty over time. Learning how to clean Bissell Little Green head components ensures your spot cleaner works efficiently every time. I want you to keep your machine in top shape. This guide offers straightforward steps for thorough cleaning.

This article provides a complete guide for cleaning the Bissell Little Green head. We will cover daily rinsing, deep cleaning, and important maintenance tips. You will learn how to disassemble parts, remove stubborn grime, and ensure proper drying. I will also share how to prevent future clogs and maintain your machine's power. Let's make your cleaning tool sparkle.

### Takeaway

*   **Rinse After Every Use:** Flush the cleaning head with warm water immediately after each cleaning session. This prevents immediate build-up.
*   **Disassemble for Deep Cleaning:** Separate the brush, nozzle, and clear cover for a more thorough cleaning process. This allows access to hidden grime.
*   **Soak Stubborn Residue:** Use a warm, soapy water solution or a vinegar solution to break down dried-on dirt and sticky residues. This helps lift embedded grime.
*   **Clear Clogs with Tools:** Use a small brush or toothpick to dislodge trapped fibers and debris from the nozzle and brush bristles. This restores suction and spray.
*   **Dry Completely Before Storage:** Allow all parts to air dry fully to prevent mildew and odors. This ensures a fresh machine for your next use.

To clean the Bissell Little Green head, detach the handheld tool, rinse its nozzle and brush under warm water to remove loose debris, and use a small brush to scrub away any stuck-on dirt or hair. For a deeper clean, disassemble the clear cover and soak the parts in warm, soapy water before rinsing and air-drying completely. This process keeps the tool ready for effective stain removal.

### Immediate Rinse After Every Use

Rinsing the Bissell Little Green head right after each use is a simple, effective habit. This step prevents dirt and cleaning solution from drying and hardening inside the tool. Dried residue is much harder to remove later. A quick rinse saves you from needing a deep clean every time. I always do this while the machine is still out.

First, disconnect the cleaning head from the hose. Hold it under a warm water faucet. Let the water run through the nozzle and over the brush. You will see dirt and loose fibers wash away. Make sure water flows freely from both the spray opening and the suction channel. This flushing action is important.

You can also use the machine itself for a quick flush. Fill the clean water tank with warm water, without any cleaning solution. Reattach the tool. Spray clean water onto a clean cloth or into a sink, then suction it up. Repeat this a few times until the water being suctioned appears clear. This method cleans the internal pathways of the tool.

Do not forget to gently wipe the exterior of the tool. Use a damp cloth to remove any surface grime. Pay attention to the edges and crevices. This quick wipe keeps the plastic clear and prevents discoloration. I often use a microfiber cloth for this step.

Allow the tool to air dry completely after rinsing. You can stand it upright or lay it on a clean towel. This prevents mold and mildew from growing inside. A dry tool also ensures no unpleasant odors linger. Proper drying is a crucial part of simple maintenance. ### Deep Cleaning the Handheld Cleaning Tool

Sometimes, a quick rinse is not enough. For a truly thorough clean, you need to deep clean the handheld tool of your Bissell Little Green. This involves disassembling the tool to reach hidden grime and buildup. A deep clean restores full suction and spray power. I usually do this once a month, depending on how often I use it.

Start by detaching the cleaning head from the hose. Most Bissell Little Green models allow you to remove the clear cover from the brush and nozzle assembly. Look for tabs or clips that hold it in place. Gently separate these parts. If you are unsure, refer to your machine's manual.

Once disassembled, you will see the brush, the suction nozzle, and the clear cover as separate pieces. Each part needs attention. Take a small brush, like an old toothbrush or a specialized cleaning brush, and scrub the bristles of the main brush. Remove any trapped hair, lint, or fabric fibers. I find a stiff brush works best here. For a more focused approach on just the brush head, you can refer to our guide on [cleaning the Bissell Little Green brush head](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bissell-little-green-brush-head).

Next, focus on the suction nozzle and the clear cover. These areas can accumulate sticky residue from cleaning solutions and dirty water. Rinse them thoroughly under warm water. If there is stubborn buildup, fill a basin with warm water and a few drops of dish soap or a mild all-purpose cleaner. Soak the parts for 15-30 minutes. This helps loosen the grime.

After soaking, scrub all surfaces with your small brush. Pay close attention to the small opening of the suction channel and the spray nozzle. Make sure no blockages remain. You might need a toothpick or a paperclip to dislodge anything stuck in very tiny holes. Rinse all parts again with clean, warm water until no soap residue remains.

### Clearing Clogs and Restoring Suction

Clogs are common problems for the Bissell Little Green head, especially in the suction nozzle. They happen when hair, lint, or dried solution blocks the pathway for dirty water. A clogged head means poor suction, leaving your carpets wetter and less clean. This issue can be frustrating when you are trying to remove a tough stain.

If your machine's suction feels weak, the first place to check is the nozzle of the cleaning head. Disconnect the tool and look directly into the suction opening. You might see a visible wad of hair or debris. Use tweezers or a small, stiff wire to carefully pull out any obstructions. Be gentle to avoid damaging the plastic.

Sometimes, the clog is not visible but deep inside the nozzle or hose connector. For this, a flushing method helps. Fill the clean water tank with hot water and no cleaning solution. Attach the tool. Spray the hot water into a sink or bucket, then immediately suction it up. The hot water can help dissolve stubborn, dried-on residue. Repeat this process several times.

You can also try a vinegar solution for stubborn clogs. Mix equal parts white vinegar and warm water in the clean water tank. Run this solution through the machine as described above. Vinegar is excellent for breaking down mineral deposits and soap scum. Follow with a plain water rinse to clear the vinegar smell. This helps clean the internal paths of the main machine head too. For very persistent clogs, you may need to use a small, flexible brush, like a pipe cleaner or a specialized cleaning brush designed for small tubes. Carefully push it through the suction channel from both ends if possible. This helps dislodge any stuck debris. Always rinse thoroughly after clearing a clog to ensure all loosened particles are gone.

### Troubleshooting Common Head Issues

Even with regular cleaning, you might encounter issues with your Bissell Little Green head. Knowing how to troubleshoot these problems can save you time and frustration. I have run into these issues myself, and a systematic approach always helps.

**Issue 1: Weak or No Suction.**
If your machine has weak suction, the first suspect is a clog in the cleaning head. Disconnect the head and visually inspect the suction nozzle for hair or debris. Use tweezers or a small wire to remove any blockages. Also, check the hose itself for kinks or blockages. Sometimes, a clog can be further down the hose. Flush the head and hose with hot water as described in the "Clearing Clogs" section. Ensure the dirty water tank is not overfilled or improperly seated. A full tank or loose tank can also cause suction loss.

**Issue 2: No or Uneven Spray.**
If the cleaning solution is not spraying, or the spray is weak or uneven, the spray nozzle on the head is likely clogged. This tiny hole can get blocked by dried solution or mineral deposits. Use a very fine needle or a thin wire to gently clear the opening. Do not force anything or try to widen the hole. Also, ensure the clean water tank has enough solution and is properly seated. Check if the solution filter (if your model has one) is clean. A quick flush of the system with hot water can sometimes clear minor blockages.

**Issue 3: Leaking from the Head.**
Leaking usually indicates a problem with the seals or the assembly of the cleaning head. First, check that the clear cover is securely snapped into place. If it's loose, tighten it. Inspect the rubber gaskets or seals around the suction nozzle and where the head connects to the hose. If these are cracked, worn, or missing, they need replacement. Sometimes, overfilling the dirty water tank can cause leaks if water backs up. Ensure all connections are snug and dry before use.

**Issue 4: Unpleasant Odors from the Head.**
Odors typically come from mold, mildew, or dried dirty water left inside the head. This means your cleaning and drying process might not be thorough enough. Perform a deep clean of the head, ensuring all parts are disassembled, scrubbed, and rinsed. Soak parts in a solution of warm water and white vinegar to kill odor-causing bacteria. Crucially, ensure all parts are completely air-dried before reassembly and storage. Store the machine in a well-ventilated area. Also, ensure the dirty water tank is always emptied and rinsed.

Addressing these common issues quickly helps you maintain optimal performance from your Bissell Little Green. My goal is always to get back to cleaning quickly.

### FAQ Section

**How often should I clean the Bissell Little Green head?**
You should rinse the Bissell Little Green head immediately after every use to prevent daily build-up. A deep clean, involving disassembly and scrubbing, is recommended at least once a month, or more often if you use the machine frequently or clean particularly dirty messes. Regular maintenance prevents clogs and keeps your machine working well.

**Can I put my Bissell Little Green head in the dishwasher?**
No, you should not put your Bissell Little Green head or its components in the dishwasher. High heat from a dishwasher can warp the plastic parts, rendering them unusable. Always hand wash the cleaning head with warm water and mild soap. Air drying is the safest and most effective method.

**What cleaning solution is best for removing tough grime from the head?**
For tough grime and dried-on residue, a solution of warm water with a few drops of dish soap works well. For mineral deposits or soap scum, a solution of equal parts white vinegar and warm water is very effective. Remember to rinse thoroughly with plain water after using any cleaning solution.

**Why is my Bissell Little Green not spraying solution from the head?**
If your Bissell Little Green head is not spraying, the spray nozzle is likely clogged. Use a very fine needle or thin wire to carefully clear the tiny opening. Also, check that the clean water tank has enough solution and is properly seated. Ensure the solution filter, if present, is clean and not blocked.

**How do I prevent mold and mildew in my Bissell Little Green head?**
To prevent mold and mildew, always ensure all parts of the Bissell Little Green head are completely dry before storage. After cleaning, disassemble the head and allow all pieces to air dry thoroughly, ideally for several hours, in a well-ventilated area. Prompt rinsing after use also helps remove moisture.

**Can I use rubbing alcohol to clean the plastic parts of the head?**
It's generally best to avoid harsh chemicals like rubbing alcohol on the plastic parts of your Bissell Little Green head. Warm water with mild dish soap or a diluted vinegar solution is safer and usually sufficient. Harsh chemicals can potentially damage or discolor the plastic over time.
