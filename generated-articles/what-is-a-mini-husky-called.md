## The Primary Contender: Alaskan Klee Kai

When people ask, "what is a mini husky called," the Alaskan Klee Kai is usually the breed they are thinking of. This dog is not a mix. It is a purebred dog, specifically developed in the United States. Its creation began in the 1970s. Linda S. Spurlin, along with her family, created the breed. They wanted a companion-sized dog that looked like a husky.

The Alaskan Klee Kai literally means "small dog" in an Inuit dialect. This name perfectly describes their size. They possess the striking facial mask, erect ears, and plumed tail of their larger husky relatives. Their appearance is captivating. These dogs come in three size varieties: Toy, Miniature, and Standard. Toy Klee Kai are up to 13 inches tall. Miniature Klee Kai stand between 13 and 15 inches. Standard Klee Kai are over 15 inches up to 17 inches. All weights are proportional to height. This makes them significantly smaller than a Siberian Husky.

Alaskan Klee Kai are known for their intelligence and agility. They are also very active. Despite their small size, they have a lot of energy. These dogs thrive on consistent training and mental stimulation. They can be reserved with strangers. This makes early socialization important. They bond strongly with their families. ## The Miniature Siberian Husky – A Breeder's Creation

Another answer to "what is a mini husky called" involves the Miniature Siberian Husky. This is not a separate breed like the Alaskan Klee Kai. Instead, it is a smaller version of the standard Siberian Husky. Breeders achieve this smaller size through specific breeding practices. They select the smallest dogs from purebred Siberian Husky litters. Then, they breed these smaller individuals together. This process aims to consistently produce smaller offspring.

The goal is to maintain all the characteristics of a Siberian Husky. This includes their coat, eye color, and temperament. The only difference is their size. Standard Siberian Huskies typically weigh 35-60 pounds. They stand 20-23.5 inches tall. Miniature Siberian Huskies aim for weights under 35 pounds and heights often below 20 inches. This size reduction makes them more manageable for some owners.

However, potential owners should consider the methods used. Reputable breeders focus on health first. They ensure the smaller size does not compromise the dog's well-being. Unethical breeders might use unhealthy runts or breed dogs with dwarfism. This can lead to serious health issues. Always ask about the lineage and health clearances of the parents.
