## Fix Your Little Green Machine Not Suctioning Today!

Nothing stops a cleaning session faster than a Little Green Machine not suctioning. I know this frustration well. You fill the clean water tank, spray the solution, and then nothing. The dirty water just sits there. This common problem affects many users of the popular Bissell Little Green carpet cleaner.

Understanding why your machine loses suction helps you fix it fast. I will guide you through common causes, from simple clogs to maintenance steps. This article covers everything you need to know. We will explore troubleshooting steps and offer practical solutions. You will soon have your Little Green Machine working perfectly again, pulling up dirt and stains with ease. Let's get your cleaner back in action.

### Takeaway

If your Little Green Machine is not suctioning, perform these checks:
*   Empty and secure the dirty water tank.
*   Clean the nozzle and brush head area for clogs.
*   Inspect the suction hose for blockages.
*   Ensure the clean water tank is seated correctly.
*   Clean the formula and water tanks thoroughly.

A Little Green Machine not suctioning often signals a clog, a full dirty water tank, or a loose tank seal. Checking and cleaning the tanks, hose, nozzle, and brush head will typically restore proper suction power.

## Why Your Little Green Machine Stops Suctioning

When your Little Green Machine is not suctioning, it usually means something obstructs the airflow or the vacuum seal breaks. The machine relies on a strong vacuum to pull dirty water from carpets. Any interruption to this system causes a loss of suction. It is like trying to drink from a straw with a hole in it. The machine cannot create the necessary negative pressure.

Many things can cause this problem. Sometimes, the fix is very simple. Other times, it requires a closer look at the machine's components. Understanding the design of your Bissell Little Green helps. It pulls solution from one tank, sprays it, and then suctions dirty water into another tank. If any part of this process fails, suction stops. I always start with the easiest checks.

### Full Dirty Water Tank or Loose Seal

A primary reason for a Little Green Machine not suctioning is a full dirty water tank. The machine stops suctioning when this tank is completely full. It is a safety feature to prevent overflow. I always check this first. The tank has a float valve that rises when full, blocking the suction path.

Make sure to empty the dirty water tank regularly. After emptying, ensure the tank is clean and replace it properly. A loose or improperly seated dirty water tank breaks the vacuum seal. The machine cannot create suction without a tight seal. Push down firmly on the tank until it clicks into place. Sometimes, I hear a small air leak sound if it is not sealed right.

### Clogged Suction Nozzle or Brush Head

The suction nozzle is where the machine lifts the dirty water. If this area is clogged, your Little Green Machine will not suction properly. Hair, carpet fibers, or dried cleaning solution can block the nozzle. This is a very common issue, especially after cleaning heavily soiled areas.

I often find clumps of debris here. Regularly check the nozzle and [clean the Little Green Machine head](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-little-green-machine-head). If your model has a rotating brush, the brush head can also accumulate hair and dirt. This reduces its effectiveness and can indirectly affect suction by preventing proper contact with the carpet. [Cleaning the Little Green Machine brush head](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bissell-little-green-brush-head) ensures optimal performance. Use a pair of scissors to cut away any tangled hair.

## Inspecting Hoses and Components for Blockages

Beyond the tanks and nozzle, internal hoses and various components can cause your Little Green Machine not to suction. The path from the suction nozzle to the dirty water tank must be clear. Any blockage along this path reduces or stops suction completely. These blockages often build up slowly over time.

I have found that small bits of debris can cause big problems. It is worth taking a moment to check these areas carefully. Patience during this step saves you future headaches. Disconnecting hoses for a visual check is often the best approach. Remember to reassemble everything securely afterwards.

### Clogged Suction Hose

A clogged suction hose is a frequent culprit when your Little Green Machine is not suctioning. Dirty water, especially with dissolved grime, can leave residue inside the hose. This residue builds up, narrowing the hose and blocking suction. Sometimes, larger debris like pet hair or carpet pieces get sucked in and stuck.

To check for a clog, carefully remove the clear suction hose from the machine. I usually hold it up to a light source. You can often see obstructions. If you spot a clog, try flushing the hose with warm water. A long, flexible brush, like a bottle brush, can also help dislodge stubborn blockages. Make sure water flows freely through the entire hose after cleaning.

### Dirty Water Tank Gasket and Filter

The gasket on the dirty water tank ensures a tight seal. If this gasket is dirty, damaged, or out of place, it creates an air leak. This air leak means the machine cannot create a strong vacuum. Your Little Green Machine will not suction efficiently. Always check the gasket for debris or tears.

Some Little Green models also have a small filter inside the dirty water tank opening. This filter prevents larger debris from entering the motor area. This filter can become caked with dirt and grime, blocking airflow. I make sure to clean this filter thoroughly every time I empty the tank. [Cleaning the dirty tank on your Bissell Little Green](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-dirty-tank-on-bissell-little-green) is a vital maintenance step.

## Addressing Tank and Lid Issues

Tank issues are often straightforward fixes for a Little Green Machine not suctioning. The clean water tank and its lid play a role in proper operation. While the clean water tank primarily delivers solution, its correct placement indirectly supports the suction process. A poorly seated tank or a faulty lid can cause issues.

I have learned that even minor misalignments can disrupt the system. Always handle your tanks with care to prevent damage. They are critical to the machine's function. Proper assembly ensures that both the cleaning solution flows correctly and the dirty water is extracted.

### Clean Water Tank Not Seated Correctly

The clean water tank supplies the cleaning solution. While it does not directly cause a suction loss, a poorly seated tank can affect overall machine performance. Sometimes, the machine relies on all parts being in their correct positions. A misaligned tank might indirectly prevent the proper formation of a vacuum seal.

Ensure the clean water tank sits firmly in its designated spot. It should click into place without wobbling. I press down gently to confirm it is secure. If the tank is not seated right, the solution might not dispense properly. This makes the cleaning process less effective, even if some suction remains.

### Loose or Damaged Tank Lids

Both the clean and dirty water tanks have lids. These lids need to be secure and undamaged. A loose lid on the dirty water tank lets air into the suction system. This breaks the vacuum seal. If your Little Green Machine is not suctioning, check this lid. Make sure it snaps shut tightly.

The lid on the clean water tank also needs to be tight. If it leaks, solution can spill. While not directly stopping suction, it indicates an issue with tank integrity. Always check for cracks or wear on both lids. Replace them if they show signs of damage. Proper lids are essential for the machine's efficiency.

The spray nozzle delivers the cleaning solution onto your carpet. If this nozzle clogs, solution might not come out, or it might spray unevenly. While this does not directly stop suction, an inability to apply solution makes cleaning impossible. Dirty water suction then becomes irrelevant.

Check the small holes on the spray nozzle for blockages. Use a small pin or needle to clear any dried solution or debris. Follow the tubing that connects the clean water tank to the spray nozzle. Look for kinks or blockages. Ensuring a clear spray path ensures you can actually use the suction feature effectively. ## When to Consider Professional Help or Parts Replacement

Sometimes, despite your best efforts, your Little Green Machine is not suctioning. At this point, the problem might be more serious. It could involve internal components that are difficult to access. Or, a part might be broken and needs replacement. Do not feel bad if you reach this stage. Some issues are beyond simple DIY fixes.

### Motor or Pump Failure

If you have checked all the common issues and your Little Green Machine still has no suction, the motor or pump might be failing. The pump creates the spray, and the motor creates the vacuum for suction. If either of these components malfunctions, the machine cannot perform its primary function. You might hear the motor running but feel no suction.

Diagnosing motor or pump failure usually requires disassembling the machine. This is a more complex task. I advise caution here. Unless you are comfortable with electronics and machine repair, this is a job for a professional. They have the specialized tools and knowledge to fix these internal components.

### Damaged Hoses or Internal Components

External hoses are easy to check, but internal hoses can also kink or crack. Over time, plastic components can become brittle. A small crack in an internal hose or seal can cause an air leak. This makes your Little Green Machine not suction effectively. These issues are often hidden from view.

If you suspect internal damage, a careful inspection is needed. Sometimes, you can find replacement parts online. ## FAQs About Little Green Machine Suction

### Why does my Bissell Little Green spray but not suction?

If your Bissell Little Green sprays solution but does not suction, the dirty water recovery system has a problem. This usually means a clog in the suction path. Check the dirty water tank for fullness or a loose seal. Inspect the suction nozzle, brush head, and the main suction hose for blockages like hair or debris. A damaged tank gasket can also prevent a proper vacuum seal.

### How do I unclog the Little Green Machine hose?

To unclog the Little Green Machine hose, first disconnect it from the machine. Hold the hose up to a light to see the obstruction. Flush warm water through the hose from both ends. For stubborn clogs, use a long, flexible brush, like a bottle brush, to push the blockage through. Ensure water flows freely before reattaching.

### Can a dirty tank cause no suction?

Yes, a dirty tank can definitely cause no suction. If the dirty water tank is full, a float mechanism stops suction to prevent overflow. If the tank's filter or internal components are caked with grime, it blocks airflow. Also, a dirty or improperly seated tank gasket can create an air leak, breaking the vacuum seal needed for suction.

### How often should I clean my Little Green Machine to prevent suction issues?

You should rinse your Little Green Machine's tanks and nozzle after every use. Perform a more thorough cleaning of the brush head, suction hose, and internal components every few uses or once a month. This regular maintenance prevents the buildup of dirt, hair, and cleaning solution residue. It keeps the machine working efficiently and avoids suction problems.

### Is it worth replacing parts if my Little Green Machine is not suctioning?

Replacing parts is often worth it if the machine is otherwise in good condition and the problematic part is easily replaceable. Minor parts like gaskets, hoses, or nozzles are usually affordable. For major components like the motor or pump, compare the repair cost with buying a new machine. Many users find replacing a few key parts extends their machine's life significantly.
