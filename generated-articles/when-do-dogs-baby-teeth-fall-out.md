### When Do Dogs Baby Teeth Fall Out?

Dogs usually start losing their baby teeth, also known as deciduous teeth, around 3 to 4 months of age. The process continues until they are approximately 6 to 7 months old. By this time, most puppies have a full set of 42 permanent adult teeth.

## The Puppy Teething Timeline: When Do Dog Baby Teeth Fall Out?

Puppies are born without teeth. Their first set of baby teeth emerges quickly. These tiny, needle-sharp teeth are called deciduous teeth. They are also known as milk teeth. Most puppies have 28 deciduous teeth by 6 to 8 weeks of age. This initial set of teeth helps them transition from nursing to solid food.

The process of losing these baby teeth begins a few months later. Permanent teeth begin to push through the gums. This pressure helps loosen the baby teeth. The roots of the baby teeth resorb, meaning they dissolve. This allows the baby teeth to fall out easily. Most puppies swallow their baby teeth, which is completely normal.

The timeline for when do dogs baby teeth fall out is quite consistent across breeds. However, smaller breeds might start and finish a little later. For example, you might wonder [what age do Frenchies teeth fall out](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-age-do-frenchies-teeth-fall-out). Their timeline is generally similar to other dogs, but small differences can exist.

Here is a general breakdown of when different types of puppy teeth typically fall out:

*   **Incisors (front teeth):** These are usually the first to go. They fall out between 3 and 5 months of age.
*   **Canines (fang teeth):** These long, pointed teeth typically fall out between 4 and 6 months of age.
*   **Premolars (teeth behind canines):** These emerge between 4 and 6 months of age. Puppies do not have molars in their baby tooth set.
*   **Molars (back teeth):** Adult molars start to erupt between 5 and 7 months of age. They have no deciduous predecessors.

By the time a puppy is 6 to 7 months old, they should have a full set of 42 adult teeth. This includes 20 upper teeth and 22 lower teeth. ## Recognizing the Signs of Teething in Puppies

Teething can be a challenging time for puppies and their owners. Knowing the signs helps you offer comfort. Puppies experience discomfort as their new teeth push through the gums. This discomfort often leads to noticeable changes in their behavior. Understanding these signs makes you a more prepared pet parent.

One of the most obvious signs is increased chewing. Puppies chew on everything during teething. They do this to relieve the pressure and discomfort in their gums. They might chew on furniture, shoes, or even your hands. Providing appropriate chew toys is crucial during this stage. This protects your belongings and keeps your puppy safe.

Other common signs of teething include:

*   **Drooling:** You might notice more saliva around your puppy's mouth. This is a natural response to gum irritation.
*   **Red or swollen gums:** Inspect your puppy's gums gently. They may appear redder and more swollen than usual.
*   **Small spots of blood:** You might find tiny specks of blood on their chew toys or bedding. This happens when a baby tooth falls out. It is usually not a cause for concern.
*   **Decreased appetite or difficulty eating:** Some puppies might eat less. Chewing can be painful. Offering softer foods might help if they show a strong reluctance to eat their regular kibble.
*   **Fussiness or irritability:** Your usually playful puppy might seem more irritable. They might cry more or seek more comfort.
*   **Finding tiny teeth:** You might occasionally find a small baby tooth on the floor or in their bed. Most puppies swallow them, but finding one is a clear sign of teething.
*   **Bad breath:** Teething can sometimes cause a temporary increase in bad breath. This is due to inflammation and potential minor bleeding in the gums.

Always monitor your puppy closely during this period. If discomfort seems severe, or if you notice unusual symptoms, contact your veterinarian. They can offer advice or check for any issues. Never give your dog human pain relievers without vet approval. ## Essential Teething Toys and Chews for Your Puppy

When selecting teething toys, prioritize durability and material. The toy must withstand vigorous chewing without breaking into small pieces. Small pieces can become choking hazards. Also, ensure the material is non-toxic. Look for toys specifically designed for puppies or for strong chewers.

Here are some types of effective teething toys:

*   **Rubber or Silicone Chew Toys:** These are often very durable. Many have textured surfaces. The textures help massage gums and clean teeth. Some can be filled with treats like peanut butter or yogurt, making them more appealing. You can also freeze these toys. The cold helps numb sore gums.
*   **Rope Toys:** These provide a good texture for chewing. They also offer an opportunity for interactive play. Be careful with rope toys if your puppy tends to shred them. Ingested strings can cause digestive issues. Supervise play with rope toys.
*   **Edible Chews:** Choose edible chews specifically formulated for puppies. Look for products that are easily digestible and do not splinter. Rawhide can be problematic for some dogs due to digestibility and choking risks. Consider vet-recommended dental chews. Always supervise your puppy with edible chews.
*   **Frozen Items:** Items like frozen carrots (supervised), ice cubes, or a frozen wet washcloth can offer immense relief. The cold helps soothe inflamed gums. Ensure the items are appropriately sized to prevent choking. A frozen Kong toy stuffed with a safe treat is a popular choice.

Always supervise your puppy with any new chew toy. Replace toys as soon as they show signs of wear or damage. This prevents your puppy from swallowing broken pieces. ## Managing Puppy Discomfort During Teething

*   **Frozen chew toys:** Many rubber or silicone toys are freezer-safe. The cold helps to reduce inflammation and pain.
*   **Ice cubes:** Offer small ice cubes for your puppy to chew on. This provides a gentle cooling sensation.

Gentle gum massage can also provide relief. Use a clean finger or a soft, puppy-specific toothbrush. Gently rub your puppy's gums for a few minutes. This can help stimulate blood flow and ease pressure. Start slowly and ensure your puppy is comfortable with this contact. This practice also helps accustom your puppy to having their mouth handled. *   **Proteins and Fats:** These provide energy and building blocks for all tissues, including gums and tooth ligaments.
*   **Antioxidants:** Vitamins E and C, and other antioxidants, support gum health. They help reduce inflammation and protect cells from damage.

Avoid feeding your puppy excessive sugary treats. Sugars contribute to plaque and tartar buildup on teeth. This can lead to dental disease later in life. ## Common Teething Problems and When to Seek Veterinary Care

While the process of when do dogs baby teeth fall out is natural, sometimes complications arise. Being aware of these potential issues helps you know when to seek professional help. Early intervention can prevent more serious dental problems down the line. I always recommend caution when it comes to puppy health.

One of the most common issues is **retained deciduous teeth**. This happens when a baby tooth does not fall out as the permanent tooth erupts. The new adult tooth then grows in alongside or behind the baby tooth. This is particularly common with canine teeth. Retained teeth create several problems:

*   **Crowding:** They can cause teeth to become too close together. This makes them difficult to clean. Food particles and plaque can get trapped.
*   **Misalignment (Malocclusion):** The permanent tooth might be forced to grow in an abnormal position. This can lead to an improper bite. An incorrect bite can cause pain, difficulty eating, and wear on other teeth.
*   **Gum disease:** Food and bacteria accumulation around retained teeth increases the risk of gum inflammation and periodontal disease.

Your vet will check for retained teeth during puppy wellness exams. If a baby tooth is retained, your vet will likely recommend its extraction. This usually happens during your puppy's spay or neuter surgery. [How much does it cost to remove a dog's teeth](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-remove-a-dogs-teeth) can vary, but timely removal is often crucial to prevent long-term issues.

Other less common but serious teething problems include:

*   **Fractured baby teeth:** Although rare, a puppy can break a baby tooth. This might expose the pulp, leading to pain and infection.
*   **Oral infections:** Sometimes, the gums can become infected around erupting teeth. Signs include severe swelling, pus, or a foul odor.
*   **Impacted teeth:** Rarely, a permanent tooth might fail to erupt properly. It can remain under the gum line. This requires veterinary attention.
*   **Pain that prevents eating:** If your puppy refuses to eat for an extended period due to pain, it needs veterinary assessment.

If you notice any of these signs, or if your puppy's teething process seems unusually painful or prolonged, contact your veterinarian. They can perform an oral examination. They can then recommend the best course of action. Regular veterinary check-ups are essential during your puppy's growth period. This ensures their dental development is progressing normally.

## FAQ Section

### Is it normal for puppies to swallow their baby teeth?

Yes, it is perfectly normal for puppies to swallow their baby teeth. You might occasionally find a tiny tooth, but most puppies ingest them. These small teeth are easily digested and pass through their system without any issue. There is no need for concern if you do not find any teeth.

### What if a puppy's baby tooth does not fall out?

If a puppy's baby tooth does not fall out and the permanent tooth starts to erupt next to it, it is called a retained deciduous tooth. This is a common problem, especially with canine teeth. You should contact your veterinarian. Retained teeth can cause crowding, misalignment, and increase the risk of gum disease. Your vet will likely recommend extraction.

### When do puppies stop teething completely?

Puppies generally finish teething by 6 to 7 months of age. By this time, they should have their full set of 42 permanent adult teeth. While the main period of discomfort and tooth loss ends, some chewing behavior might continue. This is part of exploration or boredom, not necessarily due to teething pain.

### Can I help my puppy's baby teeth fall out?

You should not try to pull out your puppy's baby teeth yourself. This can cause pain, breakage, or damage to the underlying permanent tooth. The best way to help is to provide plenty of safe chew toys. Chewing helps loosen the baby teeth naturally. If a tooth seems stuck or causes significant discomfort, consult your veterinarian.

### What are the signs of a serious problem during puppy teething?

While some discomfort is normal, severe signs warrant veterinary attention. Look for excessive bleeding, swelling, or pus around the gums. Refusal to eat for an extended period, severe lethargy, or very bad breath could indicate an infection or other serious issue. Retained baby teeth also require veterinary intervention to prevent future problems.
