## Clean a Drained Hot Tub: A Complete Guide

Maintaining a clean hot tub ensures a relaxing experience every time you use it. When it is time to refresh your spa, knowing how to clean a drained hot tub properly is important. A thorough cleaning helps prevent issues like algae growth and cloudy water later. This guide will walk you through each step for a sparkling clean tub, ready for fresh water.

**Takeaway:**
*   Prepare your hot tub by draining and gathering tools.
*   Scrub the shell, focusing on the waterline and stains.
*   Clean filters and jets to remove buildup.
*   Inspect and maintain the hot tub cover.
*   Rinse, refill, and balance your water for safe use.

When you clean a drained hot tub, you remove old water deposits and prevent future issues. Start by rinsing the empty tub. Then, scrub all surfaces and clean internal components like filters and jets. This full process makes your hot tub look new again and ensures healthy water.

### 1. Preparing Your Drained Hot Tub for Cleaning

Before you start scrubbing, you need to prepare your hot tub. This step ensures safety and makes the cleaning process much easier. I always gather all my supplies first, so I do not have to stop midway. Make sure your hot tub is completely empty before you begin.

First, turn off the power to your hot tub at the breaker. This is a critical safety step. You do not want any electricity running to the tub while you work inside it. Next, ensure the hot tub is fully drained. Sometimes a small amount of water remains in the footwell. You can use a wet/dry vacuum to remove this last bit of water.

Gather your cleaning tools. You will need a soft-bristle brush, a non-abrasive sponge, and a garden hose. For cleaners, I suggest a hot tub-specific shell cleaner. You might also need white vinegar or a diluted bleach solution for tougher spots, but always check your manufacturer's recommendations first. Never use household cleaners not designed for hot tubs, as they can damage surfaces or create foam. Having everything ready saves time and frustration. It allows you to focus on the cleaning itself.

*   **Turn Off Power:** Flip the circuit breaker for safety.
*   **Drain Completely:** Remove all water, use a wet/dry vac for any remaining puddles.
*   **Gather Supplies:** Soft brush, sponge, hose, hot tub shell cleaner, bucket.
*   **Safety First:** Wear gloves and eye protection when using cleaners.

Having a prepared workspace makes the job go smoothly. I always find that taking these initial steps correctly sets the stage for a great clean. This careful preparation truly helps when you need to [clean a hot tub that has been sitting](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-that-has-been-sitting) for a while, making the main cleaning task simpler.

### 2. Deep Cleaning the Hot Tub Shell

Now that your hot tub is drained and ready, it is time to focus on the shell. This is where most of the dirt and grime accumulate. A thorough cleaning here makes a big difference in your hot tub's appearance. I recommend working in small sections to keep the shell wet and prevent cleaners from drying.

Start by spraying down the entire interior of the hot tub with a garden hose. This helps loosen any loose debris or light grime. Then, apply your hot tub shell cleaner. Follow the product instructions for application and dwell time. I typically spray a section, let it sit for a few minutes, then start scrubbing. Use your soft-bristle brush or non-abrasive sponge. Scrub the walls, floor, and seats. Pay special attention to the waterline, as this area often collects a scum line. For tough areas, you might need a little extra elbow grease.

I often encounter situations where a hot tub has developed a stubborn ring around the waterline. This [scum on the hot tub](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-scum-off-the-hot-tub) is usually a mix of body oils, lotions, and chemical residues. A dedicated scum remover or a paste made from baking soda and water can work wonders. Apply the paste, let it sit, then scrub gently. After scrubbing, rinse the entire shell thoroughly with fresh water. Make sure to rinse from the top down, pushing all the dirty water towards the drain. You want to remove all traces of cleaner before refilling. A clean shell means a clean start for your new water.

*   **Rinse the Shell:** Use a hose to wet all surfaces.
*   **Apply Cleaner:** Use a hot tub specific cleaner, following directions.
*   **Scrub Thoroughly:** Use a soft brush or sponge on walls, floor, and seats.
*   **Target Scum Lines:** Use a specialized cleaner or baking soda paste for tough rings.
*   **Rinse Completely:** Flush all cleaner residue and dirty water out.

This deep cleaning of the shell is vital for overall hot tub hygiene. Without it, new water quickly becomes contaminated, reducing your enjoyment. This is also the perfect time to address any specific issues, like if you ever need to [clean sand from a hot tub](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-sand-from-a-hot-tub) after a beach trip, by ensuring all grit is removed from the shell and drain area.

### 3. Tackling Stubborn Stains and Biofilm

Sometimes, regular scrubbing is not enough for everything you find in a drained hot tub. You might see lingering stains or a slimy film that needs special attention. Identifying these issues early helps you address them effectively. I always look for discolored patches or a slick feeling on the tub surfaces.

Stubborn stains often come from minerals in your water or organic matter. For mineral stains, a diluted white vinegar solution can be effective. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water, apply it to the stain, and let it sit for 15-20 minutes before scrubbing. For organic stains, a mild bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) can work, but always test on a small, hidden area first and rinse thoroughly. Bleach can affect certain hot tub surfaces if used improperly. My experience shows careful application yields the best results.

Biofilm is another common problem. This is a slimy layer of bacteria that can cling to surfaces, especially in the plumbing. It often feels slick and can be hard to remove with just scrubbing. If you see or feel biofilm, you need a specialized hot tub plumbing cleaner. These products circulate through the jets and pipes, breaking down the biofilm from the inside out. You add these cleaners to a partially filled hot tub, run the jets for a period, then drain and rinse again. [Cleaning biofilm from the hot tub](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-biofilm-from-the-hot-tub) is essential for maintaining water clarity and sanitation. Skipping this step can lead to recurring water quality problems even after refilling.

*   **Address Mineral Stains:** Use a diluted white vinegar solution.
*   **Handle Organic Stains:** Try a mild bleach solution, with caution.
*   **Identify Biofilm:** Look for slimy, slick areas on surfaces or within plumbing.
*   **Use a Plumbing Cleaner:** For biofilm, run a specialized cleaner through the jets.
*   **Rinse Thoroughly:** Flush out all cleaners and dissolved contaminants.

A hot tub with a clean shell and clear plumbing prevents many common water issues. This thorough approach makes your spa enjoyable for a longer time. Dealing with issues like [green hot tub water](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-green-hot-tub-water) often starts with a deep clean like this.

### 4. Cleaning and Reinstalling Hot Tub Filters

Your hot tub filters are the workhorses of your water system. They catch all the debris and contaminants, so cleaning them is non-negotiable when you clean a drained hot tub. Ignoring them means dirty water, even with a clean shell. I always make sure to clean my filters every time I drain the tub.

First, remove your hot tub filters from their housing. You will likely find them covered in dirt, hair, and other particles. Start by rinsing them thoroughly with a garden hose. Use a strong spray to get into each pleat. Many people use a special filter cleaning wand for this. It helps direct water effectively between the pleats. This initial rinse removes the loose debris.

For a deeper clean, soak your filters in a specialized hot tub filter cleaner. You can find these cleaners at most spa supply stores. Follow the product instructions, but typically you mix the cleaner with water in a large bucket and let the filters soak overnight. This process breaks down oils, lotions, and other embedded grime that plain water cannot remove. Another excellent option for a natural deep clean is using a vinegar solution. [Cleaning hot tub filters with vinegar](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-hot-tub-filters-with-vinegar) can effectively remove calcium buildup and organic matter. After soaking, rinse the filters again thoroughly with clean water until no suds or cleaner residue remains. Let them air dry completely before reinstalling them. Having spare, clean filters is a smart idea. You can rotate them, always ensuring you have a clean set ready to go.

*   **Remove Filters:** Take them out of the hot tub.
*   **Rinse Thoroughly:** Use a hose or filter wand to remove loose debris.
*   **Soak in Cleaner:** Use a specialized filter cleaner or vinegar solution.
*   **Rinse Again:** Flush out all cleaning solutions.
*   **Air Dry:** Allow filters to dry completely before putting them back.

Clean filters are essential for clear, healthy hot tub water. They ensure your pump does not have to work too hard and extend the life of your equipment. Without clean filters, even perfectly balanced water will quickly turn dirty.

### 5. Sanitizing Hot Tub Jets and Plumbing

When you clean a drained hot tub, you cannot forget the jets and internal plumbing. These areas can harbor bacteria and biofilm, even after using a shell cleaner. A comprehensive cleaning includes flushing these hidden parts. I always take a few extra minutes to make sure my jets are clear.

First, inspect each jet for any visible debris or mineral buildup. Sometimes, a small amount of grit or even hair can get lodged in the jet nozzles. You can often carefully remove this with a toothpick or a small brush. If you have adjustable jets, rotate them to ensure they move freely. If you used a plumbing cleaner in a previous step, great. This would have helped clean the internal lines. If not, consider a targeted approach now.

Even after using a plumbing cleaner, a quick manual scrub around the jet faceplates helps. Use your soft brush and shell cleaner to clean the visible parts of the jets. Rinse these areas well. For a truly sanitized system, especially if you had significant biofilm, I recommend performing a "purge" before refilling. This involves partially filling the tub, adding a specific hot tub purge product, and running the jets with the air blowers on for a short period. This action dislodges any remaining hidden debris or contaminants. Then, you drain the tub again and rinse thoroughly.

*   **Inspect Jets:** Look for blockages or buildup in each nozzle.
*   **Remove Debris:** Use small tools like a toothpick if needed.
*   **Clean Jet Faces:** Scrub visible parts with a soft brush and shell cleaner.
*   **Consider a Purge:** Use a specialized hot tub purge product if biofilm was an issue.
*   **Flush Plumbing:** Ensure all lines are clear before refilling.

Properly sanitized jets and plumbing are crucial for maintaining clean water. They prevent the quick return of issues like cloudy water or algae growth. A clean system from the inside out makes a big difference in [keeping your hot tub water crystal clear](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-keep-the-hot-tub-water-crystal-clear).

### 6. Inspecting and Caring for Your Hot Tub Cover

Your hot tub cover plays a vital role in keeping your water clean and warm. While you clean a drained hot tub, do not forget to give the cover some attention. A well-maintained cover prevents heat loss and keeps debris out. I always inspect my cover for damage during the cleaning process.

First, remove the cover from the hot tub and place it on a clean, flat surface. I usually lay it on a tarp or a clean patch of grass. Start by rinsing the top and bottom surfaces with a garden hose. This removes loose dirt, leaves, and other debris. For the top surface, use a soft brush or sponge and a mild cleaner designed for hot tub covers. Never use abrasive cleaners or harsh chemicals, as they can damage the vinyl. Scrub gently, focusing on any dirty spots.

The underside of the cover often accumulates mold, mildew, or a slimy film due to condensation. [Cleaning mold off a hot tub cover](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-mold-off-hot-tub-cover) is important for air quality and sanitation. I use a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) or a specialized cover cleaner on the underside. Scrub carefully, then rinse thoroughly. Ensure all cleaning solution is removed. After cleaning, apply a UV protectant to the top surface. This helps protect the vinyl from sun damage, extending the cover's life. Check for any tears, cracks, or waterlogged sections. Repair minor tears with a vinyl repair kit. If your cover is heavy or waterlogged, it might be time for a replacement.

*   **Remove and Rinse:** Clean both sides of the cover with a hose.
*   **Clean Top:** Use a mild cover cleaner and a soft brush.
*   **Clean Underside:** Address mold and mildew with a diluted bleach solution or specialized cleaner.
*   **Rinse Well:** Ensure no cleaner residue remains.
*   **Protect and Inspect:** Apply UV protectant and check for damage.

A clean, intact cover saves you money on heating and keeps your hot tub water cleaner between uses. This care extends the life of your entire hot tub system. This is especially important when you are getting ready to [clean a hot tub in the winter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-hot-tub-in-the-winter) and need maximum insulation.

### 7. Rinsing and Refilling Your Hot Tub

After all the scrubbing and cleaning, you are almost ready to enjoy your hot tub again. The rinsing and refilling steps are crucial to ensure you have fresh, clean water. Do not rush these final stages. I take my time here to make sure everything is perfect.

First, give the entire hot tub shell one last thorough rinse. Use your garden hose to spray down all surfaces, pushing any remaining cleaner residue or loosened debris towards the drain. Make sure the drain is open until the rinse water runs clear. This final rinse removes any lingering chemicals or contaminants that could affect your new water chemistry. Once you are satisfied that the tub is completely clean, close the drain plug.

Now, you are ready to refill your hot tub. Place your garden hose in the filter compartment or the access panel for the heater. This method helps to "air lock" the system, preventing air bubbles from getting trapped in the plumbing. Fill the hot tub to the recommended waterline, which is usually just above the highest jets. Once filled, you can turn the power back on at the breaker. Run the jets for 10-15 minutes with the air off to ensure all air is purged from the lines. This is a good time to double-check for any leaks around the plumbing.

*   **Final Rinse:** Flush the shell one last time until water is clear.
*   **Close Drain:** Secure the drain plug properly.
*   **Refill Correctly:** Place hose in the filter compartment and fill to the waterline.
*   **Purge Air:** Turn power on and run jets with air off to remove air from lines.
*   **Check for Leaks:** Inspect plumbing connections during the purge.

A properly rinsed and refilled hot tub sets the stage for accurate water balancing. This careful refilling helps prevent air locks and ensures your system runs smoothly from the start.

### 8. Initial Water Balance and Ongoing Maintenance

With your hot tub filled, the final steps involve balancing the water and setting up a routine for ongoing maintenance. This ensures safe and comfortable use. This is where I start adding chemicals to make the water perfect.

Before adding any chemicals, test your water. Use test strips or a liquid test kit to measure pH, alkalinity, and calcium hardness. These parameters are fundamental to hot tub health. Adjust them one at a time, following the instructions on your water balance chemicals. For example, if your pH is low, you would add a pH increaser. Always add chemicals slowly, one at a time, and allow the water to circulate before retesting. My goal is always to get the water within the ideal ranges as quickly as possible.

Once your water is balanced, add your sanitizer. This could be chlorine, bromine, or a mineral sanitizer. Follow the product's recommended dosage for initial startup. Run your jets for a cycle to distribute the sanitizer throughout the water. From this point forward, regular testing and chemical adjustments will be part of your hot tub routine.
