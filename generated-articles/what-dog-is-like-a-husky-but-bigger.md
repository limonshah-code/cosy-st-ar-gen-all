## The Alaskan Malamute: The Husky's Larger Cousin

If you are asking "what dog is like a Husky but bigger," the Alaskan Malamute is likely the first breed that comes to mind. They possess a striking resemblance to their Siberian Husky cousins. However, Malamutes are undeniably larger and more robust. They are powerful dogs with a strong build.

Alaskan Malamutes are one of the oldest Arctic sled dog breeds. They were originally bred by the Mahlemut Inuit tribe in Alaska. Their primary purpose was to haul heavy freight over long distances. This history explains their impressive strength and endurance. Their size and muscular frame reflect their working heritage.

### Malamute Size and Appearance

Alaskan Malamutes are substantially larger than Siberian Huskies. Male Malamutes typically weigh 75 to 90 pounds and stand 23 to 25 inches tall at the shoulder. Females are slightly smaller, weighing 65 to 80 pounds and standing 22 to 24 inches tall. In contrast, male Siberian Huskies usually weigh 45 to 60 pounds and are 21 to 23.5 inches tall. Female Huskies weigh 35 to 50 pounds and are 20 to 22 inches tall. This size difference is quite noticeable.

Malamutes have a broad, deep chest and a strong, compact body. Their heads are broader, and their muzzles are less tapered than a Husky's. They share the characteristic thick, double coat that provides excellent insulation in cold climates. Coat colors include various shades of gray, black, sable, red, and white. Their eyes are always brown; blue eyes are a disqualifying fault in the breed standard. This helps differentiate them from many Huskies. I find their sturdy build truly impressive. Many people often wonder [which is bigger, a Husky or a Malamute](https://cosyhomecreation.com/which-is-bigger-a-husky-or-a-malamute), and the answer is consistently the Malamute.

### Temperament and Needs

Alaskan Malamutes are affectionate and loyal companions. They form strong bonds with their families. They are generally good with children when properly socialized. However, their strong prey drive means they might not be suitable for homes with small pets. Their independent nature makes training a challenge. You need consistency and patience.

Malamutes require significant exercise. They need daily long walks, runs, or hikes to stay happy and healthy. Their double coat needs regular brushing, especially during shedding seasons. They are heavy shedders. Potential owners should know about this. They thrive in colder climates and need protection from heat. Understanding [how to know if your dog is a Husky or Malamute](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-do-i-know-if-my-dog-is-a-husky-or-malamute) involves looking at these size and physical differences. The distinctions are clear once you know what to look for.

## Samoyeds: Gentle Giants with Husky-Like Charm

Beyond the Malamute, the Samoyed is another wonderful answer to "what dog is like a Husky but bigger." These dogs are known for their stunning white coats and their perpetual "Samoyed smile." While they do not have the same sharp, wolf-like features as a Husky, they share the thick double coat and an unmistakable Nordic beauty. I am always charmed by their happy expression. They embody a gentle spirit.

Samoyeds originated in Siberia with the Samoyed people. They were bred to herd reindeer, hunt, and pull sleds. Their history as working dogs means they possess strength and endurance. They are true all-around utility dogs. Their friendly disposition made them integral to family life.

### Samoyed Characteristics

Samoyeds are mid-to-large-sized dogs. Males typically stand 21 to 23.5 inches tall and weigh 45 to 65 pounds. Females are 19 to 21 inches tall and weigh 35 to 50 pounds. This places them generally on the larger end of the Husky size range, with some individuals being noticeably bigger. Their build is sturdy and muscular. They are built for endurance and strength rather than speed.

Their most distinctive feature is their dense, pure white, cream, or biscuit double coat. This coat is incredibly thick and soft. It provides excellent insulation in cold temperatures. Their dark, intelligent eyes and upturned mouth corners give them their famous smiling expression. Unlike Huskies, blue eyes are not seen in Samoyeds. Their tails curl over their backs. These dogs truly stand out in a crowd.

### Living with a Samoyed

Samoyeds possess a friendly and outgoing temperament. They are often described as good-natured and gentle. This makes them excellent family pets. They thrive on human companionship and do not do well when left alone for long periods. They are intelligent but can be stubborn. Consistent positive reinforcement training works best for them.

Like Huskies, Samoyeds are energetic and require regular exercise. Daily walks, playtime, or even a good run will keep them happy. Their thick coats demand significant grooming. Daily brushing is necessary to prevent matting and manage shedding. They are heavy shedders, especially during seasonal blowouts. This breed is better suited for cooler climates. I often compare them to a fluffy cloud, but a very active cloud!

## Canadian Eskimo Dog: A Rare, Powerful Husky Alternative

For those still wondering what dog is like a Husky but bigger, and seeking a truly powerful and ancient breed, consider the Canadian Eskimo Dog. Also known as the Qimmiq, this is one of North America's oldest native dog breeds. They share a profound ancestral connection with the Siberian Husky and Alaskan Malamute. These dogs are impressive specimens of Arctic resilience. I find their history fascinating.

The Canadian Eskimo Dog was crucial for the survival of the Inuit people for thousands of years. They served as primary transportation, hunting partners, and protectors. Their numbers significantly declined due to snowmobiles and intentional culling. They are now critically endangered. Efforts are in place to preserve this magnificent breed.

### History and Traits

Canadian Eskimo Dogs are large and powerfully built. Males stand 23 to 27 inches tall and weigh 66 to 88 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, 19 to 24 inches tall and 39 to 71 pounds. This places them firmly in the "bigger than a Husky" category. Their appearance is rugged and muscular. They have a deep chest and a strong neck.

They possess a thick, dense double coat. This coat allows them to withstand extreme cold. Their fur comes in various colors, including white, black, brown, grey, and combinations. Their ears are erect and triangular. Their eyes are typically dark and almond-shaped. They carry their tails curled over their backs, much like a Husky. Their overall look is more primitive and robust compared to the Siberian Husky. They exude raw power.

### Care and Temperament

Canadian Eskimo Dogs are known for their loyalty and bravery. They are very intelligent but also independent and stubborn. Early and consistent training is essential. They bond strongly with their families but can be reserved with strangers. Their high prey drive means they should not be trusted with smaller animals. They require an experienced owner.

These dogs have immense energy and stamina. They need extensive daily exercise. They thrive in environments where they can run, pull, or participate in dog sports. Living in a cold climate is ideal for them. Their thick coat requires regular grooming, especially during seasonal shedding. This is not a dog for apartment living. They need a job or purpose to truly flourish. Finding one of these dogs is rare, but a dedicated search could connect you with one.

## Beyond the Sled: Other Large Dogs with Husky Traits

When considering "what dog is like a Husky but bigger," we often focus on Nordic breeds. It is important to remember that "Husky-like" can mean different things to different people. For some, it is the wolf-like face. For others, it is the fluffy double coat. And for some, it is the independent spirit. These breeds offer variations on those themes.

### Great Pyrenees

The Great Pyrenees is a majestic giant. They are known for their impressive size and beautiful white coat. Males stand 27 to 32 inches tall and weigh over 100 pounds. Females are 25 to 29 inches tall and weigh over 85 pounds. This makes them significantly larger than any Husky. Their thick, weather-resistant double coat is typically white, sometimes with badger, gray, or tan markings.

While they do not have the pointed ears or upright tail of a Husky, their sheer size and fluffiness can be appealing. Great Pyrenees were bred as livestock guardians. They are independent thinkers and can be stubborn. They are very protective of their families. Their exercise needs are moderate, much less intense than a Husky's. However, they need a large, secure yard. Their guarding instincts mean they can be vocal. Their grooming needs are substantial due to their dense coat. They are certainly a "big dog with a lot of fur."

### Bernese Mountain Dog

The Bernese Mountain Dog is another large, fluffy breed that could come to mind. They possess a striking tri-color coat (black, rust, and white). Males stand 25 to 27.5 inches tall and weigh 80 to 115 pounds. Females are 23 to 26 inches tall and weigh 70 to 95 pounds. They are undeniably larger than Huskies. Their build is sturdy and powerful. They were historically used for herding and drafting in the Swiss Alps.

Bernese Mountain Dogs have a generally sweet and affectionate temperament. They are often described as gentle giants. They are very devoted to their families and typically good with children. Unlike Huskies, they are not typically independent or prone to wandering. Their exercise needs are moderate. They enjoy walks and outdoor activities but are not high-energy athletes. Their long, thick coat requires regular brushing to prevent matting and manage shedding. While they do not have the "wolfy" look, their size and cuddly nature appeal to many looking for a large, soft companion. They are certainly bigger than a Husky. You might even wonder [what dog is bigger than a Bernese Mountain Dog](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-dog-is-bigger-than-a-bernese-mountain-dog).

### Tamaskan Dog

The Tamaskan Dog is a newer breed specifically developed to look like a wolf. They strongly resemble a Husky in appearance but are often larger. Males stand 25 to 28 inches tall and weigh 66 to 99 pounds. Females are 24 to 26 inches tall and weigh 55 to 84 pounds. This places them in the "bigger than a Husky" category. Their coat colors mimic those of a wolf. They have a thick double coat, erect ears, and a brush tail.

Tamaskan Dogs are intelligent and generally friendly. They require significant socialization and training. They are active dogs that need a lot of exercise and mental stimulation. They are not as independent as a Husky but still need a confident owner. Their appearance is very striking, truly embodying the wolf-like look that many admire in Huskies. They are a good option for someone looking for that specific aesthetic but with a larger frame.

## FAQ Section

### What is the biggest dog that looks like a Husky?
The Alaskan Malamute is generally considered the biggest dog that looks like a Husky. They are substantially larger and more powerfully built than Siberian Huskies, weighing between 75 to 90 pounds for males. They share the Husky's thick coat and similar facial features. Their robust frame is designed for hauling heavy loads, making them significantly more imposing.

### Are Alaskan Malamutes harder to train than Huskies?
Alaskan Malamutes can be just as challenging to train as Huskies, if not more so, due to their independent and strong-willed nature. They are highly intelligent but can be stubborn and easily bored by repetitive tasks. Consistency, patience, and positive reinforcement methods are crucial for successful training. Early socialization is also vital.

### Do Samoyeds shed as much as Huskies?
Yes, Samoyeds shed profusely, often as much as or even more than Huskies, especially during their seasonal "blowouts" when they shed their entire undercoat. Their thick, double coat requires daily brushing to prevent matting and manage the shedding. Regular grooming is essential to keep their coat healthy and minimize loose fur in your home.

### Are there any Husky mixes that are bigger than a purebred Husky?
Yes, certain Husky mixes can be bigger than a purebred Husky, especially when crossed with larger breeds. For example, a Husky-Malamute mix (often called an Alusky) or a Husky-German Shepherd mix could easily exceed the size of a purebred Husky. The size depends on the genetics inherited from both parent breeds.

### What is the typical lifespan of these larger Husky-like breeds?
The lifespan of larger Husky-like breeds varies. Alaskan Malamutes typically live for 10 to 14 years. Samoyeds have a similar lifespan, ranging from 12 to 14 years. Canadian Eskimo Dogs also generally live 10 to 15 years. These lifespans are comparable to or slightly shorter than the average Siberian Husky lifespan of 12 to 15 years.

### Do these larger breeds need more exercise than a Husky?
While a Husky is very high energy, these larger breeds often need a different *type* of exercise. Malamutes and Canadian Eskimo Dogs need vigorous exercise to satisfy their working dog instincts, often involving endurance over speed. Samoyeds also need regular activity. While not always needing more *intensity* than a Husky, they often require more consistent physical and mental stimulation.
