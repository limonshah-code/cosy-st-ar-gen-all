## What Is A Black Golden Retriever Called? Unraveling the Mystery

Have you ever seen a dog that looks exactly like a Golden Retriever but has a sleek, black coat instead of the familiar golden one? Many people wonder, "What is a black Golden Retriever called?" This is a common question. Golden Retrievers are famous for their sunny dispositions and their beautiful golden, cream, or light red coats. A true purebred Golden Retriever, according to breed standards, does not come in black. Their genetics simply do not allow for a black coat.

So, if you see a dog that appears to be a black Golden Retriever, it is likely one of two things. It could be a mixed-breed dog, or it might be a dog from another retriever breed that shares similar characteristics. This article explores the truth behind black Golden Retrievers. We will look at Golden Retriever genetics. We will also discuss common black-coated mixes that might resemble the beloved Golden Retriever. *   Purebred Golden Retrievers do not have black coats due to genetic limitations.

### A Black Golden Retriever is a Misnomer: Understanding the True Identity

A "black Golden Retriever" is not a recognized purebred Golden Retriever. The Golden Retriever breed standard, established by kennel clubs worldwide, specifies a range of golden, cream, or light red coat colors. A dog described as a black Golden Retriever is actually a mixed-breed dog. It often has a Golden Retriever parent and a parent from a black-coated breed. Alternatively, it might be a different breed of retriever altogether. This dog might also share some physical traits with a Golden Retriever.

## The Genetic Code: Why Purebred Golden Retrievers Are Not Black

Understanding dog coat color genetics helps explain why purebred Golden Retrievers do not come in black. Coat color in dogs is controlled by specific genes. The "E locus" and "B locus" genes play a significant role in determining whether a dog can express black pigment. I find this genetic information quite fascinating.

### The E Locus and B Locus Genes

The E locus gene controls the production of eumelanin, which is black pigment, and pheomelanin, which is red pigment. Golden Retrievers possess two recessive "e" alleles at the E locus (ee). This genetic combination prevents the production of black pigment in their hair. Instead, it allows only the red pigment to be expressed, resulting in their characteristic golden coat. Even if a Golden Retriever carries the genes for black pigment at the B locus, the "ee" genotype overrides it for hair color. This means they cannot display a black coat.

The B locus gene affects the intensity of black or brown pigment. A dog with at least one dominant "B" allele will produce black pigment. A dog with two recessive "b" alleles will produce brown pigment. While Golden Retrievers can carry "B" or "b" alleles, their "ee" genotype at the E locus prevents any black or brown pigment from showing in their fur. This genetic setup is strict. It defines the Golden Retriever's famous color. This is why you will never see a true purebred Golden Retriever with a black coat. You might be surprised to learn that it is genetically impossible for a purebred Golden Retriever to have black fur. ## Common Mixes That Resemble a Black Golden Retriever

When people see a dog that looks like a Golden Retriever but is black, it's usually a mix. Several breeds are commonly crossed with Golden Retrievers. These crosses can produce offspring with Golden Retriever-like traits and a black coat. These dogs often inherit the Golden Retriever's friendly temperament. They might also have similar body shapes or fur textures.

One of the most common mixes that fits the description is a Golden Retriever and a [Black Labrador Retriever mix](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-a-golden-retriever-and-black-lab-mix-called). This cross, often called a "Goldador," is popular. Labradors come in black, yellow, and chocolate. When a Golden Retriever is bred with a black Labrador, some puppies will inherit the black coat gene from the Labrador parent. These Goldadors can have the build of a Golden Retriever with a black coat. They are usually friendly, intelligent, and energetic. Their appearance can vary greatly. Some might look more like a Lab, others more like a Golden, and some a true blend. These dogs make wonderful family pets due to their gentle nature.

### Golden Retriever and Flat-Coated Retriever Mix

Another mix that often results in a black-coated dog is a cross between a Golden Retriever and a Flat-Coated Retriever. Flat-Coated Retrievers are inherently black or liver-colored. They share many physical and temperamental similarities with Golden Retrievers. Both breeds are known for their retrieving abilities and friendly dispositions. A mix between these two breeds can produce a dog with a black coat. This dog might also have the Golden Retriever's characteristic feathered tail and cheerful expression. The resulting offspring, while not a purebred Golden Retriever, can be an excellent companion dog. They often combine the best traits of both parent breeds. ### Other Black-Coated Retriever Mixes

Many other breeds can contribute a black coat to a Golden Retriever mix. These mixed breeds are often referred to by portmanteau names. Examples include "Goldador" (Golden Retriever + Labrador) or "Goldenshepherd" (Golden Retriever + German Shepherd). Understanding the parent breeds helps identify these dogs more accurately. It also clarifies why they possess certain traits. When you see a beautiful black dog with the spirit of a Golden Retriever, it is likely one of these unique and beloved mixed breeds.

## The Flat-Coated Retriever: A Distinct Black Retriever Breed

When people see a black dog with a Golden Retriever-like appearance, they might be looking at a Flat-Coated Retriever. This breed is often mistaken for a black Golden Retriever. It is important to know the differences. The Flat-Coated Retriever is a distinct breed. It has its own unique characteristics and history.

### Origins and Appearance

The Flat-Coated Retriever originated in the United Kingdom. It was developed to be a versatile gun dog. They excel at retrieving game on both land and water. Unlike Golden Retrievers, their primary coat colors are black or liver. They have a dense, flat-lying coat. It has moderate feathering on the legs, tail, and chest. Their build is sleek and athletic, a bit less bulky than a Golden Retriever. They often have a characteristic "one-piece" head. This means their skull and muzzle blend smoothly. Their eyes are usually dark brown and have an intelligent, cheerful expression.

### Temperament and Distinctions

Flat-Coated Retrievers are known for their perpetual puppy-like enthusiasm. They are often described as the "Peter Pan" of the retriever world. They are outgoing, playful, and affectionate. While sharing the Golden Retriever's friendliness, they can be more boisterous and require consistent training. Their energy levels are quite high. They thrive on activity and companionship.

Key differences from Golden Retrievers include:

*   **Coat Color:** Flat-Coats are primarily black or liver. Golden Retrievers are only golden, cream, or light red.
*   **Head Shape:** Flat-Coats have a longer, more refined head. Golden Retrievers have a broader skull and a more pronounced stop.
*   **Build:** Flat-Coats are often lankier and more streamlined. Golden Retrievers tend to have a more muscular, slightly heavier build.

Despite these differences, their shared retrieving instincts and loving temperaments make them both excellent family pets. For anyone considering a black retriever, the Flat-Coated Retriever is a fantastic, recognized breed. It embodies many desirable qualities.

## How Golden Retriever Genetics Influence Puppy Litters

Understanding the genetics behind a Golden Retriever's coat color helps explain what might happen in a litter of puppies. Purebred Golden Retrievers, as we've established, are genetically programmed to be golden. Their "ee" genotype at the E locus prevents black pigment. But what happens if a Golden Retriever mates with a black-coated dog? This changes the genetic equation significantly.

### When a Golden Retriever Mates with a Black-Coated Dog

If a Golden Retriever (genotype 'ee' at the E locus) mates with a dog that carries the dominant black gene ('E' at the E locus and 'B' at the B locus, for black pigment), their puppies can inherit the black coat. For example, a Black Labrador Retriever carries the 'E' allele. When a Golden Retriever (ee) breeds with a Black Labrador (EE or Ee), some of the puppies will inherit at least one 'E' allele from the Labrador parent. These puppies will then be able to express black pigment, assuming they also inherit a 'B' allele from at least one parent.

The resulting puppies' coat colors will depend on the specific genetic makeup of both parents. Some puppies might be golden, inheriting 'e' from both parents. Others might be black, inheriting 'E' from the Labrador and 'e' from the Golden, along with 'B' from the Labrador. This is why mixed litters can have a surprising variety of colors.
