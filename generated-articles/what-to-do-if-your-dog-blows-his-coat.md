## Manage Dog Shedding: What To Do If Your Dog Blows His Coat

Suddenly, you find dog hair everywhere – on your furniture, clothes, and even in your coffee cup. This sudden explosion of fur can be alarming if you are not prepared. Your dog is likely "blowing his coat," a natural and important process for many breeds. Knowing what to do if your dog blows his coat will help you keep your home cleaner and your pet more comfortable. This guide will help you understand why this happens, how to manage the extra hair, and when to seek help. We will cover grooming techniques, home cleaning tips, and the role of nutrition.

### Takeaway

When your dog blows his coat, you need to act. Here are the key steps:
*   Understand this natural shedding phase.
*   Brush your dog often with the right tools.
*   Clean your home more frequently.
*   Give your dog good food and plenty of water.
*   Know when to talk to your vet about unusual shedding.

When your dog blows his coat, he is undergoing a natural, heavy shedding cycle, typically seen in double-coated breeds. The best approach involves frequent, thorough grooming to remove loose undercoat, alongside consistent home cleaning and proper nutritional support.

### Understanding Why Your Dog Blows His Coat

Your dog’s coat is more than just fur; it is a complex system that protects him from the elements. When a dog "blows his coat," it means he is shedding a large amount of old fur, particularly his undercoat. This is a normal part of how many dogs regulate their body temperature. It prepares them for the upcoming season.

This phenomenon is most common in double-coated dog breeds. Breeds like Siberian Huskies, German Shepherds, Labrador Retrievers, Golden Retrievers, Australian Shepherds, and Bernese Mountain Dogs have two layers of fur. The outer layer, or topcoat, is made of coarser, guard hairs. These hairs repel water and dirt. The inner layer, or undercoat, is soft and dense. It provides insulation against heat and cold. When the seasons change, the dog sheds this insulating undercoat. This helps them adjust to new temperatures.

The process of blowing coat usually happens twice a year. It occurs in spring as winter temperatures rise and in fall as winter approaches. In spring, dogs shed their thick winter undercoat. This allows for better airflow and helps them stay cool in warm weather. In fall, they shed their lighter summer coat. This makes way for a denser winter coat. Indoor lighting and consistent temperatures can sometimes disrupt these natural cycles. Some indoor dogs might shed more consistently year-round, but still have heavier seasonal "blows."

#### The Double Coat Advantage

A double coat offers many benefits to your dog. It keeps them warm in cold weather and cool in hot weather. The undercoat traps air, creating an insulating layer. This layer prevents body heat from escaping in winter. In summer, the same trapped air creates a barrier against external heat. It also protects the skin from sunburn and insect bites. Understanding [what are the benefits of a double coat dog](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-are-the-benefits-of-a-double-coat-dog) helps explain why this shedding is so vital. It is a natural system for their health and comfort.

#### Seasonal Shedding Patterns

Most double-coated dogs follow a predictable shedding pattern. Spring shedding is often the heaviest. It prepares the dog for warmer months. Fall shedding prepares them for winter. However, every dog is an individual. Factors like diet, health, and living environment can affect shedding intensity. Indoor dogs might shed less dramatically or more often due to consistent indoor temperatures. A dog living outside will typically follow a stricter seasonal shedding schedule. This is due to direct exposure to temperature and light changes. Observing your dog's specific pattern helps you prepare for the shedding periods.

### Essential Grooming Strategies for a Blowing Coat

When your dog is blowing his coat, regular and effective grooming becomes crucial. This helps remove the massive amount of loose hair. It also prevents matting, which can cause skin irritation and discomfort. Dedicate more time to grooming during these heavy shedding periods. Daily brushing is often necessary.

The right grooming tools make a big difference. An undercoat rake or a deshedding tool is essential. These tools reach deep into the coat to pull out loose undercoat without damaging the topcoat. A slicker brush or pin brush can help with general detangling and smoothing the outer coat. Start brushing from the head and work your way back. Use gentle strokes. Always brush in the direction of hair growth. This prevents skin irritation.

Bathing can also help loosen dead hair. Use a dog-specific shampoo and conditioner. A deshedding shampoo can be particularly effective. After bathing, thoroughly rinse your dog. Use a powerful dog dryer if you have one. The air from the dryer helps push out loose hair. If you do not have a dog dryer, towel dry and then brush as the coat dries. This prevents moisture from getting trapped. It also helps remove more loose hair before it spreads around your home.

#### Choosing the Right Tools

Selecting the correct grooming tools is the first step to successful deshedding.
*   **Undercoat Rake:** This tool has long, widely spaced teeth. It reaches deep into the undercoat. It pulls out loose fur without cutting the healthy topcoat. This is essential for double-coated breeds during a coat blow.
*   **Deshedding Tool (e.g., Furminator):** These tools have fine, comb-like teeth. They grab and remove large amounts of loose undercoat. Use these carefully to avoid skin irritation. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.
*   **Slicker Brush:** A slicker brush has fine, wire bristles. It is good for general brushing and removing surface tangles. It also helps distribute natural oils.
*   **Pin Brush:** A pin brush has metal pins with rounded ends. It works well for dogs with longer hair. It can remove loose hair and detangle without pulling.

Having a variety of tools allows you to tackle different parts of the grooming process. Each tool serves a specific purpose in managing your dog’s shedding.

#### Effective Brushing Techniques

Proper brushing technique maximizes hair removal and keeps your dog comfortable.
1.  **Brush Daily:** During a coat blow, brush your dog every day. Short, frequent sessions are more effective than long, infrequent ones.
2.  **Section the Coat:** For large or thick-coated dogs, brush in sections. Use one hand to hold up the top layer of fur. Use the other hand to brush the undercoat.
3.  **Brush Down to the Skin:** Ensure your brush reaches the undercoat. Gently work through the hair. Avoid harsh pulling or scraping the skin.
4.  **Use Short, Gentle Strokes:** Do not press too hard. Let the tool do the work. Focus on removing the dead hair, not irritating the skin.
5.  **Be Patient:** Shedding can take time. Praise your dog throughout the grooming session. Offer treats to create a positive experience.
6.  **Outdoor Brushing:** If possible, brush your dog outside. This helps contain the mess. It also prevents loose hair from spreading inside your home.
These techniques will help you manage the large volume of hair. They also ensure your dog remains comfortable during this natural process.

### Managing Loose Hair Around Your Home

When your dog blows his coat, hair will spread throughout your living space. A proactive cleaning routine is essential. This helps maintain a comfortable and hygienic environment for everyone. It also reduces potential allergens. Consistent effort makes a big difference.

Start by increasing your vacuuming frequency. Invest in a good quality vacuum cleaner designed for pet hair. Look for models with strong suction and specialized attachments. These attachments can reach into crevices and upholstery. Vacuum carpets, rugs, and hard floors daily or every other day. Focus on areas where your dog spends most of his time. This includes his bed, favorite napping spots, and entryways. Regular vacuuming prevents hair from accumulating and becoming embedded in fabrics.

In addition to vacuuming, use lint rollers on furniture, clothing, and pet beds. Keep several lint rollers handy in different rooms. Damp cloths or microfiber mops work well on hard surfaces. They pick up loose hair effectively without just pushing it around. Wipe down surfaces like tables, shelves, and baseboards frequently. Dog hair settles on these surfaces quickly.

Protective covers for furniture are another practical solution. Use washable slipcovers on sofas and chairs. These covers can be easily removed and laundered. This protects your furniture from excessive hair and dirt. Consider placing washable blankets or towels on your dog's favorite resting spots. These are easy to toss in the wash.

Air filters and purifiers can also help. Change your HVAC air filters more often during shedding season. This traps airborne pet dander and hair. An air purifier with a HEPA filter can remove fine particles from the air. This improves overall air quality. Remember to clean or replace your dog's bed regularly. His bed can collect a surprising amount of hair.

#### Daily Cleanup Habits

Integrating small cleaning habits into your daily routine can significantly reduce hair buildup.
*   **Quick Sweeps:** Spend 5-10 minutes each morning or evening doing a quick sweep of main living areas. A broom or dust mop works well for quick pickups.
*   **Lint Roller Duty:** Keep a lint roller by the door. Use it on your clothes before leaving the house. Encourage family members to do the same.
*   **Wipe Down Surfaces:** A quick wipe of dusty surfaces each day helps control hair. Hair often settles with dust.
*   **Shake Out Pet Beds:** Take your dog's bed outside and give it a good shake. This dislodges loose hair that accumulates in the bedding.
These small, consistent efforts prevent hair from overwhelming your home. They make the heavy shedding period more manageable.

#### Protecting Your Furniture

Dog hair can stick to furniture like glue. Protecting your furniture saves cleaning time and preserves your items.
*   **Washable Covers:** Use slipcovers on couches and chairs. Choose materials that are easy to clean, such as cotton or blends.
*   **Designated Dog Spots:** Create specific, comfortable spots for your dog. Use a dog bed or a special blanket on a particular part of the sofa. This directs most of the hair to one area.
*   **Rubber Gloves:** For quick hair removal from upholstery, wear a damp rubber glove. Rub your hand over the fabric. The hair will clump together, making it easy to pick up.
*   **Fabric Sprays:** Some sprays claim to loosen pet hair from fabric. Test a small, hidden area first to ensure it does not stain.
These methods protect your furniture from excessive shedding. They also make cleanup much simpler.

### Nutritional Support for a Healthy Coat

A dog’s diet plays a vital role in the health and quality of his coat. While nutrition won't stop a natural coat blow, it can support healthy hair growth and minimize excessive or unhealthy shedding. Feeding your dog a high-quality, balanced diet is fundamental. It provides the building blocks for strong, healthy fur.

Look for dog foods with real meat as the first ingredient. This ensures a good source of protein, which is crucial for hair structure. Hair is primarily made of protein. A deficiency can lead to dull, brittle fur and increased shedding. High-quality fats are also important. These include omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids. These fats keep the skin healthy and the coat shiny. They reduce dryness and irritation, which can contribute to shedding.

Many owners add supplements to their dog’s diet for coat health. Fish oil, rich in omega-3 fatty acids like EPA and DHA, is a popular choice. It supports skin hydration and reduces inflammation. Salmon oil is another excellent source of these beneficial fats. Always consult your vet before adding any new supplements to your dog's diet. They can recommend the right dosage for your specific dog.

Hydration is also key. Ensure your dog always has access to fresh, clean water. Proper hydration helps maintain skin elasticity and overall health. A dehydrated dog might have dry skin, which can lead to more shedding and discomfort. A well-hydrated dog will generally have healthier skin and a more resilient coat.

#### Key Nutrients for Coat Health

Certain nutrients are particularly beneficial for maintaining a strong and healthy coat.
*   **Protein:** As the main component of hair, sufficient protein is vital. Choose dog foods with high-quality, digestible animal protein sources.
*   **Omega-3 Fatty Acids:** Found in fish oil, flaxseed oil, and certain meats, these fats reduce inflammation and promote healthy skin and shiny fur.
*   **Omega-6 Fatty Acids:** Found in vegetable oils and poultry fat, these are also essential for skin barrier function. A proper balance between omega-3 and omega-6 is important.
*   **Vitamins (A, E, B-complex):** Vitamin A supports skin cell production. Vitamin E is an antioxidant that protects skin cells. B vitamins, especially biotin, are critical for hair growth.
*   **Minerals (Zinc, Copper):** Zinc helps with wound healing and cell growth. Copper plays a role in hair pigmentation and strength.
Ensuring your dog gets these nutrients can significantly improve coat health. It reduces unnecessary shedding.

#### The Importance of Hydration

Water is often overlooked but is fundamental for skin and coat health.
*   **Skin Elasticity:** Good hydration keeps your dog's skin supple and elastic. Dry skin can be itchy and lead to increased scratching and shedding.
*   **Nutrient Transport:** Water helps transport nutrients to skin cells and hair follicles. This supports healthy hair growth from within.
*   **Detoxification:** Proper hydration aids in the body's natural detoxification processes. This helps maintain overall cellular health.
*   **Overall Health:** Dehydration affects every system in the body, including skin and coat. Ensure your dog always has access to a fresh bowl of water. Consider adding a pet water fountain to encourage more drinking.
Proper hydration ensures that your dog's body can function optimally. This includes supporting a healthy, resilient coat.

### Common Concerns and When to Seek Veterinary Advice

While blowing a coat is a natural process, it's important to distinguish normal heavy shedding from excessive hair loss caused by health issues. Monitoring your dog's coat and skin during these periods is crucial. You should know when to contact your veterinarian.

Normal shedding means the hair comes out evenly across the body. The skin underneath looks healthy, with no redness, sores, or bald patches. The new hair growing in should look normal. Excessive hair loss, on the other hand, might show as thinning patches, bald spots, or clumps of hair coming out with roots attached. You might also notice skin irritation.

Watch for signs of underlying skin issues. These include excessive scratching, licking, or biting at the skin. Redness, inflammation, scabs, or a foul odor from the skin are also red flags. Hot spots, which are localized areas of moist, inflamed skin, often appear during shedding due to irritation or trapped moisture. If you see any of these signs, your dog needs a vet visit.

Allergies are a common cause of excessive shedding and skin problems. Dogs can be allergic to food ingredients, environmental allergens (like pollen or dust mites), or even flea bites. Parasites like fleas, mites, or ticks can also cause severe itching and hair loss. Your vet can perform tests to identify the cause.

Hormonal imbalances can also affect coat health. Conditions like hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) or Cushing's disease (excess cortisol) can lead to dull coats, thinning hair, and increased shedding. Nutritional deficiencies, though less common with good quality dog food, can also be a factor. If your dog's shedding seems abnormal, or if it's accompanied by other symptoms like lethargy, changes in appetite, or weight loss, contact your vet immediately. Early diagnosis and treatment can prevent more serious issues.

#### Recognizing Abnormal Shedding

Understanding what is normal versus abnormal shedding can help you decide when to seek professional help.
*   **Pattern of Loss:** Normal shedding is even across the body. Abnormal shedding often appears as patches of hair loss, bald spots, or uneven thinning.
*   **Skin Condition:** Healthy skin under shedding hair is clear, supple, and free of irritation. Abnormal shedding often reveals red, inflamed, flaky, or scabbed skin.
*   **Accompanying Symptoms:** Normal shedding typically has no other symptoms beyond hair loss. Abnormal shedding might come with itching, excessive licking, odor, lethargy, or changes in appetite.
*   **Hair Quality:** The shed hair in a normal blow coat is loose undercoat. Abnormally shed hair might look brittle, broken, or have visible roots.
If you observe any of these abnormal signs, consult your vet promptly.

#### Signs of Skin Problems

Skin issues can complicate shedding and cause discomfort for your dog.
*   **Redness and Inflammation:** Look for any areas of red, swollen skin. This indicates irritation or infection.
*   **Excessive Scratching or Licking:** If your dog constantly scratches, licks, or chews at his skin, it suggests itchiness. This can be due to allergies, parasites, or dry skin.
*   **Scabs or Sores:** Open sores, scabs, or crusts on the skin are signs of a problem. They might be from scratching or an infection.
*   **Hot Spots:** These are moist, red, painful skin lesions that appear quickly. They are often caused by continuous licking or chewing in one area. They require immediate vet attention.
*   **Odor:** An unusual or strong odor from the skin can indicate a bacterial or yeast infection.
These signs point to underlying skin conditions that need a vet's diagnosis and treatment.

### Making the Process Easier for You and Your Dog

Living with a dog who is blowing his coat can be challenging for both of you. It is a messy time, but your patience and positive attitude make a big difference for your dog. You can make the shedding process easier and even turn grooming into a bonding experience.

First, patience is key. Your dog is not shedding to annoy you. It is a natural process. Getting frustrated or angry will only stress your dog. This can make grooming more difficult. Approach grooming sessions calmly. Use a soft, encouraging voice. Keep sessions positive and rewarding.

Establish a consistent grooming routine. Daily brushing during the shedding season is ideal. Even short, 10-15 minute sessions can remove a lot of loose hair. Consistency helps your dog get used to the routine. It also prevents matting, which can be painful. Choose a specific time each day for grooming. This helps both you and your dog anticipate the activity.

Minimize stress for your dog during grooming. Some dogs do not enjoy being brushed. Start with very short sessions. Use gentle strokes. If your dog shows signs of stress, stop for a moment. Give a treat and praise. Gradually increase the session length as your dog becomes more comfortable. Create a calm environment for grooming. Turn off loud noises. Ensure you have plenty of space to work.

Making grooming a bonding experience strengthens your relationship. Use grooming time as an opportunity for connection. Pet your dog gently. Talk to him. Offer praise and small, healthy treats. This helps your dog associate grooming with positive feelings. It also builds trust between you. Think of it as a massage session for your dog. This positive association makes future grooming easier for everyone.

Embrace the shedding season as a natural part of dog ownership. With the right tools, consistent effort, and a patient attitude, you can manage your dog’s coat blow effectively. You will keep your home cleaner and ensure your dog remains comfortable and happy. Remember, this phase passes. Your efforts lead to a healthy, beautiful coat and a stronger bond with your furry friend.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: How long does it take for a dog to blow his coat?**
The duration of a coat blow varies by breed and individual dog. Typically, a heavy shedding cycle can last anywhere from 2 to 6 weeks. Factors like climate, diet, and individual genetics play a role. Consistent grooming helps speed up the process by removing loose hair more quickly.

**Q2: Can I shave my double-coated dog to prevent shedding?**
No, you should not shave a double-coated dog. Shaving can damage the natural insulating properties of their coat. It can lead to improper regrowth, skin irritation, and increased risk of sunburn. Regular brushing is a safer and more effective way to manage shedding.

**Q3: Is it normal for my dog to shed year-round?**
Some dogs, especially those living indoors with artificial light and consistent temperatures, can shed moderately year-round. However, they will still have heavier shedding periods, or "coat blows," during seasonal changes. If shedding seems excessive or patchy year-round, consult your vet.
