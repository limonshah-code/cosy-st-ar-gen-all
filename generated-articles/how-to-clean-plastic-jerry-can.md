## Master the Art of Cleaning Your Plastic Jerry Can

A trusty plastic jerry can keeps you prepared for adventures or everyday tasks. It holds vital liquids like water, fuel, or other essentials. But over time, these containers can develop odors, build up grime, or even harbor mold. Learning **how to clean plastic jerry can** properly ensures your supplies stay pure and your equipment lasts longer.

Keeping your jerry can clean is not just about hygiene; it is also about safety. Residue from previous contents can contaminate new liquids, or lingering fuel vapors can pose hazards. This guide provides clear, easy-to-follow steps for cleaning various types of plastic jerry cans. We will cover everything from basic rinses to specialized techniques for stubborn stains and odors.

### Takeaway

*   Identify your jerry can's previous contents before cleaning.
*   Prioritize safety, especially with fuel cans, ensuring ventilation.
*   Use mild soap and water for general cleaning of water or utility cans.
*   Employ specific methods like degreasers or detergents for fuel residue.
*   Address odors and mold with vinegar, baking soda, or diluted bleach.
*   Dry your jerry can completely to prevent mold and odors.
*   Store it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.

To clean a plastic jerry can, first empty all contents safely. Rinse thoroughly with warm water. For water or utility cans, scrub the inside with dish soap and a brush, then rinse well. For fuel cans, use a specialized fuel tank cleaner or a strong detergent, ensuring good ventilation. Always dry the can completely upside down before storage.

### Understanding Your Plastic Jerry Can and Its Contents

Before you start cleaning, it is important to know what your plastic jerry can previously held. Jerry cans come in different designs and are made for specific uses. A can used for water will need a different cleaning approach than one that carried gasoline. Knowing the history helps you choose the correct cleaning agents and safety measures.

Ignoring the can's past contents can lead to problems. Residual fuel can contaminate drinking water, making it unsafe. Similarly, mold from old water can spoil new water. Proper identification prevents cross-contamination and ensures the safety of future contents. This initial step makes the cleaning process more effective and safer for everyone involved.

#### Identifying Jerry Can Type

Plastic jerry cans are often color-coded for their intended contents. Red cans typically hold gasoline, yellow cans are for diesel, and blue or clear cans are for water. Always check any labels on your specific can. These indicators help you understand the risks involved and the type of cleaning needed.

If your can does not have clear labels, think about its past use. Was it used for camping water or for fueling your lawnmower? This knowledge guides your cleaning choices. Never assume a can is clean enough for water if it has ever held fuel. You should treat all fuel cans with extreme caution.

#### Pre-Cleaning Safety Measures

Safety is crucial when cleaning any jerry can, especially those that held fuel. Fuel vapors are flammable and can be dangerous. Always work in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors. Wear protective gear like gloves and safety glasses.

Avoid open flames or sparks near fuel cans. Keep children and pets away from the cleaning area. ### Basic Steps for Cleaning a Water or Utility Plastic Jerry Can

Cleaning a jerry can used for water or other non-hazardous liquids is straightforward. These cans often only need basic cleaning to remove slime, dirt, or light odors. Regular cleaning keeps your water fresh and prevents bacterial growth. It also extends the life of your can.

You can use common household items for this cleaning. This makes the process simple and accessible. Always aim for a thorough clean to ensure the can is safe for its next use. A clean jerry can provides peace of mind, whether you are on a trip or at home.

#### Simple Dish Soap Method

For a basic clean, dish soap works well. It cuts through grime and helps remove mild odors. This method is safe for cans used for potable water. I find it to be my go-to for light cleaning.

Here are the steps:

1.  **Empty the can:** Pour out any remaining liquid.
2.  **Rinse:** Fill the can with warm water and swirl it around. Pour it out. Repeat this step several times until the water runs clear.
3.  **Add soap and water:** Pour about a quarter cup of liquid dish soap into the can. Fill the can halfway with warm water.
4.  **Shake and scrub:** Secure the lid and shake the can vigorously for several minutes. If you have a long-handled brush, use it to scrub the inside surfaces.
5.  **Rinse thoroughly:** Empty the soapy water. Fill the can completely with clean, warm water. Swirl and pour out. Repeat this rinsing process many times until no soap suds remain and the water runs perfectly clear. You want to ensure no soap residue stays behind, especially for water cans.
6.  **Dry:** Allow the can to air dry completely, upside down.

This method is effective for maintaining cleanliness and preventing accumulation of residue. For similar challenges with other plastic items, you might also find tips on [how to clean plastic water tank](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-plastic-water-tank) useful.

#### Baking Soda Solution for Odors

Sometimes, even after cleaning, a water jerry can might retain a stale smell. This is common if water sat in the can for a long time. Baking soda is excellent for absorbing and neutralizing odors naturally. It is a safe and effective choice for food-grade plastic containers.

To use baking soda:

1.  **Clean first:** Perform the basic dish soap method outlined above.
2.  **Add baking soda:** Pour about half a cup of baking soda into the empty, rinsed can.
3.  **Fill with warm water:** Fill the can with warm water, shaking gently to dissolve the baking soda.
4.  **Let it sit:** Leave the baking soda solution in the can for several hours, or even overnight. For strong odors, let it sit for 24-48 hours.
5.  **Rinse completely:** Empty the solution. Rinse the can repeatedly with fresh water until no baking soda residue remains.
6.  **Dry:** Air dry thoroughly.

This baking soda treatment leaves your jerry can smelling fresh and ready for new use.

### Specialized Cleaning for Fuel-Contaminated Plastic Jerry Cans

Cleaning a plastic jerry can that has held fuel requires extra care and specific products. Fuel leaves behind a greasy residue and strong, lingering odors. These residues are flammable and harmful if ingested. It is essential to remove them completely, especially if you plan to repurpose the can for non-fuel use, though it is generally best to dedicate fuel cans solely to fuel.

Working with fuel cans demands good ventilation and protective gear. You must eliminate all traces of fuel vapors before considering the can truly clean. This process protects you from health risks and prevents fire hazards. Treating a fuel can means understanding the properties of gasoline or diesel.

#### Removing Old Fuel and Fumes

The first step is to safely dispose of any old fuel. Never pour fuel down drains or onto the ground. Take it to a designated hazardous waste facility. After emptying, you must vent the can to allow remaining fumes to dissipate.

*   **Empty completely:** Tilt the can to drain every drop of old fuel.
*   **Initial rinse (caution!):** Fill the can with a small amount of warm water and a strong degreaser (like Simple Green or specialized fuel tank cleaner). Swirl vigorously, then dispose of this wash water properly at a waste facility. Do not rinse with just water, as this can make fuel residue stick to the plastic.
*   **Air out:** Place the can outdoors in a well-ventilated area, away from ignition sources. Leave the cap off for several days. This allows most of the residual fumes to escape. Sunlight and fresh air help with this process.
*   **Use a cleaner:** Consider using a commercial plastic fuel tank cleaner designed to break down fuel residue. These products are formulated for this exact purpose. For more detailed instructions on cleaning out old gasoline, see our guide on [how to clean old gas out of the plastic tank](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-old-gas-out-of-the-plastic-tank).

#### Degreasing and Deodorizing Steps

After the initial fume dissipation, you need to tackle the greasy residue and stubborn fuel smell. This is where strong cleaning agents come in. Dish soap is generally not strong enough on its own for heavy fuel residue.

Here is a method I use:

1.  **Heavy-duty detergent:** Pour about half a cup of a robust degreaser or heavy-duty laundry detergent into the can. For specific advice on similar tasks, our article on [how to clean a plastic fuel tank](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-plastic-fuel-tank) offers more context.
2.  **Hot water fill:** Fill the can with hot (not boiling) water. Hot water helps dissolve grease.
3.  **Shake vigorously:** Secure the lid tightly and shake the can for at least 5-10 minutes. The agitation helps the detergent penetrate and lift the fuel residue.
4.  **Soak:** Let the solution sit in the can for several hours, or even overnight, for very stubborn smells.
5.  **Rinse repeatedly:** Empty the detergent solution. Rinse the can thoroughly with hot water, then cold water, multiple times. Continue rinsing until no suds appear and the water runs completely clear and odor-free. This can take many rinses.
6.  **Air dry:** Allow the can to air dry completely outdoors for several days with the cap off. The sun helps with residual odors. ### Tackling Stubborn Stains, Mold, and Odors in Plastic Jerry Cans

Sometimes, a simple wash is not enough. Plastic jerry cans can develop persistent stains, grow mold, or hold onto powerful odors. These issues require more aggressive, yet safe, cleaning strategies. Mold, in particular, can be a health hazard and must be eliminated completely. Stubborn odors, often from old contents, can make a can unusable.

These problems often arise from improper drying or storage. Addressing them ensures your jerry can is truly clean and safe for future use. I have found certain common household items to be very effective in these situations. Always remember to ventilate properly and wear protective gear, especially when dealing with mold or strong chemicals.

#### Eliminating Mold and Mildew

Mold and mildew love dark, damp environments. A jerry can with residual moisture can quickly become a breeding ground. They appear as black, green, or fuzzy spots inside the can. You must remove them completely to prevent contamination.

*   **Vinegar solution:** Vinegar is a natural fungicide and deodorizer.
    1.  Pour equal parts white vinegar and warm water into the can.
    2.  Secure the lid and shake vigorously for several minutes.
    3.  Let the solution sit for several hours or overnight.
    4.  Empty and rinse thoroughly with clean water.
*   **Bleach solution (use with extreme caution):** Bleach is a powerful disinfectant, but it should only be used for water cans and rinsed *very* thoroughly. Never mix bleach with other cleaners, especially ammonia.
    1.  Mix one tablespoon of household bleach with one gallon of water.
    2.  Pour the diluted bleach solution into the can.
    3.  Shake for a minute and let it sit for 30-60 minutes.
    4.  **Rinse repeatedly and thoroughly.** This is crucial to remove all bleach residue. I rinse at least 5-7 times.
*   **Denture tablets:** These fizzing tablets release cleaning agents and oxygen, which can break down mold.
    1.  Drop 3-5 denture tablets into the can.
    2.  Fill the can with warm water.
    3.  Let it sit for several hours or overnight until the fizzing stops and the solution has worked.
    4.  Empty and rinse thoroughly.

#### Neutralizing Persistent Smells

Some odors, especially from certain chemicals or stagnant water, can really cling to plastic. Beyond baking soda, you have other options to try. These methods aim to absorb or neutralize the odor molecules trapped in the plastic.

*   **Activated charcoal:** Activated charcoal is a fantastic odor absorber.
    1.  After cleaning and rinsing the can, place several pieces of activated charcoal (available at pet stores or garden centers) inside the dry can.
    2.  Seal the lid loosely and let it sit for several days or a week.
    3.  Remove the charcoal before using the can.
*   **Coffee grounds:** Used coffee grounds can also absorb odors, leaving a subtle coffee smell.
    1.  After cleaning, place a few scoops of *dry*, used coffee grounds inside the can.
    2.  Close the lid and leave for a few days.
    3.  Empty the grounds and rinse the can.
*   **Crushed ice and salt:** For a more abrasive, mechanical approach to stubborn films that hold odors.
    1.  Pour about a cup of crushed ice and half a cup of coarse salt into the can.
    2.  Add a small amount of lemon juice or vinegar for extra cleaning power.
    3.  Secure the lid and shake vigorously. The ice and salt scour the interior.
    4.  Empty the mixture and rinse thoroughly.

### Drying and Storing Your Plastic Jerry Can Properly

Proper drying is just as important as cleaning. Any moisture left inside a jerry can creates an ideal environment for mold, mildew, and bacteria to grow. This can quickly undo all your hard work and lead to new odors. A completely dry can also prevents material degradation over time.

Correct storage further protects your jerry can from damage and contamination. It ensures the can is ready for immediate use whenever you need it. Ignoring these steps can shorten the lifespan of your jerry can and compromise its contents.

#### Ensuring Complete Dryness

Achieving complete dryness can take time, but it is a critical step. Do not rush this process.

*   **Air drying:** After rinsing, turn the jerry can upside down at an angle. This allows water to drain out and air to circulate inside. You can prop it up against a wall or use a sturdy drying rack.
*   **Sunlight:** Direct sunlight can help accelerate drying and also has some sanitizing properties. Place the inverted can in the sun for several hours or a full day. Ensure the opening is facing downwards to prevent dust or debris from entering.
*   **Fan assistance:** If drying indoors, place a small fan near the opening of the inverted can. The airflow helps evaporate moisture more quickly.
*   **Desiccants:** For very humid conditions or if you need to dry it faster, you can place a packet of silica gel or a small bag of rice inside the dry-ish can. These absorb residual moisture. Remember to remove them before using the can.
*   **Inspect:** Before putting the cap back on, visually inspect the inside of the can with a flashlight. Look for any remaining water droplets or condensation.

#### Best Storage Practices

Once your jerry can is thoroughly dry, proper storage helps maintain its cleanliness and integrity.

*   **Cap off or loose:** Store the can with its cap off or loosely placed on top. This maintains airflow and prevents moisture buildup from condensation.
*   **Cool, dry place:** Store jerry cans in a cool, dry area away from direct sunlight. Sunlight can degrade plastic over time, making it brittle.
*   **Elevated storage:** If possible, store cans off the ground on shelves or pallets. This protects them from moisture, pests, and temperature fluctuations on the floor.
*   **Dedicated storage:** Keep fuel cans separate from water cans to prevent any accidental cross-contamination. Label them clearly. ### DIY Cleaning Solutions and Eco-Friendly Alternatives for Jerry Cans

You do not always need harsh chemicals to clean your plastic jerry can effectively. Many common household items offer powerful cleaning properties. These DIY solutions are often more eco-friendly, safer for you, and gentler on your can's plastic. They are also usually more cost-effective.

Using natural alternatives reduces your exposure to synthetic chemicals. It also minimizes environmental impact. These methods are excellent for routine cleaning or for handling common issues like light grime and mild odors. I always prefer to start with these gentle options before moving to stronger cleaners.

#### Natural Cleaners for Everyday Use

Several natural ingredients can tackle most jerry can cleaning needs. They are safe for plastic and leave no harmful residues.

*   **White Vinegar:** As mentioned, white vinegar is fantastic for disinfection, deodorizing, and breaking down mineral deposits.
    *   Mix equal parts white vinegar and water.
    *   Fill the can with this solution, shake, and let it sit for several hours.
    *   Rinse thoroughly afterward.
*   **Lemon Juice:** Lemon juice contains citric acid, which is a natural degreaser and deodorizer. It also leaves a fresh scent.
    *   Squeeze several lemons into the can, or use bottled lemon juice.
    *   Add warm water and a tablespoon of salt for an abrasive boost.
    *   Shake well and let it sit before rinsing.
*   **Baking Soda and Vinegar Paste:** For caked-on residue that is hard to reach, a paste can work wonders.
    *   Mix baking soda with a little vinegar to create a thick paste.
    *   Apply the paste to the dirty areas using a brush or sponge, if accessible.
    *   Let it sit for 15-30 minutes, then scrub and rinse.

#### Using Abrasives Safely

Sometimes, you need a little friction to dislodge stubborn grime or film. Natural abrasives can do the job without scratching the plastic. These techniques are often called "shake and scrub" methods.

*   **Rice and Soap:** This is a classic method for cleaning bottles with narrow openings.
    *   Add a quarter cup of uncooked rice (or small gravel/sand for tougher jobs) to the can.
    *   Pour in a few drops of dish soap and about two cups of warm water.
    *   Secure the lid and shake vigorously for several minutes. The rice acts as a gentle scrubber, dislodging grime.
    *   Empty the rice and dirty water, then rinse the can thoroughly. Ensure all rice grains are removed.
*   **Coarse Salt and Ice:** Another effective abrasive technique, particularly good for internal films.
    *   Pour about half a cup of coarse salt (like kosher or sea salt) and a cup of crushed ice into the can.
    *   Add a splash of water or lemon juice.
    *   Shake the can vigorously, allowing the salt and ice to rub against the interior surfaces.
    *   The ice melts, and the salt dissolves, carrying away grime. Empty and rinse completely.

### Maintaining Your Plastic Jerry Can for Long-Term Use

A clean jerry can is a well-maintained jerry can. Regular care not only extends the life of your container but also ensures its reliability and safety. Neglecting maintenance can lead to accelerated wear, contamination, and the need for costly replacements. I have learned that a little effort upfront saves a lot of trouble later.

Implementing a simple maintenance routine prevents minor issues from becoming major problems. This includes not just cleaning, but also inspecting your can for any signs of damage. Being proactive helps your jerry can serve you effectively for many years, whether for emergency fuel, camping water, or backyard tasks.

#### Routine Cleaning Schedule

Establishing a routine cleaning schedule depends on how often and for what purpose you use your jerry can.

*   **After every use (water/utility cans):** For water or utility cans, especially those holding potable water, I recommend a quick rinse and air dry after *every* use. This prevents mold and staleness. If it has held something sticky or dirty, a full wash with dish soap is best.
*   **Quarterly or Bi-annually (storage cans):** For cans stored long-term, even if empty, a quarterly or bi-annual clean helps prevent dust buildup and general degradation. Check for any condensation or strange odors during these inspections.
*   **After emptying fuel:** Always clean fuel cans thoroughly as soon as they are empty. Fuel residues can harden and become much harder to remove over time. Prioritize safe disposal of residues and proper ventilation.

Consistent cleaning minimizes the chance of stubborn issues developing. It also ensures the interior remains pristine for its next contents.

#### Inspection and Replacement Guidelines

Beyond cleaning, regularly inspect your plastic jerry can for any signs of wear or damage. Plastic can degrade over time due to UV exposure, extreme temperatures, or chemical contact.

*   **Check for cracks:** Look closely for any hairline cracks, especially around the seams, handle, and cap area. Cracks can lead to leaks, which are dangerous for fuel and messy for water.
*   **Inspect the cap and gasket:** Ensure the cap still seals tightly. The rubber gasket inside the cap can dry out or crack, compromising the seal. Replace the gasket if it shows signs of wear. A loose cap is a common source of leaks.
*   **Look for discoloration or brittleness:** If the plastic appears discolored, hazy, or feels brittle to the touch, it indicates degradation. This means the plastic is losing its integrity and strength.
*   **Check for swelling or distortion:** If a can, especially a fuel can, shows signs of swelling or distortion, it means pressure buildup has occurred. This can be very dangerous.
*   **Odor persistence:** If, after repeated cleaning attempts, a strong odor persists in a fuel can, it may be beyond safe use. Residual odors often indicate deeply embedded fuel particles.

**When to Replace:**

*   **Visible cracks or leaks:** Any sign of a leak means immediate replacement, especially for fuel.
*   **Brittle plastic:** If the can feels rigid and easily breakable, it is a hazard.
*   **Deformed shape:** Significant bulging or distortion.
*   **Persistent fuel odor (in a repurposed can):** If you cannot get rid of the fuel smell, do not use it for water or food. It is safer to replace it or designate it *only* for fuel.
*   **Aging:** Most plastic jerry cans have a lifespan. While not always explicitly stated, a general rule is to consider replacing fuel cans every 5-10 years, and water cans every 10-15 years, depending on use and exposure.

Following these guidelines ensures your jerry can remains a safe and reliable tool for all your liquid storage needs.
