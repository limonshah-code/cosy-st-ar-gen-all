Judges meticulously examine the dog's bone structure, proportions, and overall balance. A slight deviation can make a difference in such a high-level competition. This foundation of proper conformation is crucial for success.

Second, conditioning plays a vital role. Champion show dogs are in peak physical condition. They receive a special diet and exercise routine. ## The Journey to Best in Show: A Breed's Path

The path to Best in Show at Westminster is a long and challenging one. It begins with individual breeds competing within their groups. Dogs first win their breed, then their group, before reaching the final Best in Show ring. Understanding this structure helps explain why certain breeds consistently make it to the top.

There are seven distinct breed groups at Westminster: Hound, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, Herding, Sporting, and Working. Each Best of Breed winner competes against others in its group. Only one dog from each group advances to the Best in Show ring. This means that to become the "winningest dog breed at Westminster," a breed must consistently produce individual champions capable of winning not just their breed, but their group.

The final Best in Show competition pits the seven group winners against each other. Here, the judge evaluates each dog one last time. They look for the dog that best represents its breed standard among the champions. This is where presence, gait, and overall impression become critical. The judge must select one dog that stands out above all others.

Some breeds seem to have a natural advantage. Their physical traits might be easier to maintain to standard. Or their temperament might lend itself well to the show ring. However, every win is a testament to careful breeding and exceptional individual dogs. The journey to the top is a testament to years of effort by breeders, owners, and handlers.
