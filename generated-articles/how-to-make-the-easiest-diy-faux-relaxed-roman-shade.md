## Understanding Faux Relaxed Roman Shades

A faux relaxed Roman shade offers the stylish appearance of a traditional Roman shade without the need for intricate cords or mechanisms. Unlike working shades that lift and lower, a faux shade remains stationary. It provides decorative flair and a touch of privacy. The "relaxed" part means the bottom edge gently curves or sags in the center, creating a softer, more organic look compared to crisp, straight-edged Roman shades. This style brings a sense of effortless elegance to any room.

Choosing a faux shade is perfect for windows where you do not need to open and close the covering regularly. Think of it for windows with blinds already in place, or for adding visual interest above a kitchen sink or in a guest bathroom. The simplicity of its function directly translates to the ease of its creation. You avoid complex hardware and the frustrations that come with it. This DIY approach focuses on aesthetics and simple construction, making it an ideal project for beginners.

## Precise Measurement and Fabric Cutting

Accurate measurement is critical for any window treatment project. It ensures your finished faux relaxed Roman shade fits your window perfectly. Taking your time during this step prevents frustrating mistakes later on. Remember the adage: measure twice, cut once. This holds especially true when working with fabric, as errors can be costly.

Before you even touch your fabric, decide if you want an inside mount or an outside mount. An inside mount means the shade fits snugly within the window frame. An outside mount means the shade is installed on the wall above the window opening, extending slightly beyond the frame. Each method requires different measurements and offers a distinct look. I generally prefer an outside mount for faux shades because it allows the fabric to hang more freely and creates a more substantial visual presence.

### Measuring for Inside Mount Shades

For an inside mount, measure the width of your window frame in three places: top, middle, and bottom. Use the narrowest measurement to ensure the shade fits without bunching.
Then, measure the height of your window frame in three places: left, center, and right. Use the longest measurement for the height.

*   **Width:** Window frame width - 1/4 inch (to allow for clearance).
*   **Length:** Window frame height + 2 inches for a top hem + 2 inches for a bottom hem + extra for the relaxed fold. A good rule of thumb for the relaxed fold is to add 6-8 inches for a standard window. This provides enough fabric for a pleasing curve.

An inside mount gives a very clean, integrated look. It works best when your window frame is perfectly square. Minor imperfections in the frame can make an inside mount challenging.

### Measuring for Outside Mount Shades

An outside mount offers more flexibility and can make a window appear larger. It is often simpler to install.

*   **Width:** Measure the width of your window opening. Add 4-6 inches to this measurement (2-3 inches on each side) to ensure the shade extends beyond the frame. This helps block light more effectively and creates a fuller appearance.
*   **Length:** Measure the height of your window opening. Add 6-8 inches to this measurement (2-3 inches above the frame, 1 inch below the sill, plus the fabric needed for the relaxed fold). Again, add 6-8 inches for the relaxed curve. Also, account for 2 inches for the top hem and 2 inches for the bottom hem.

I personally find outside mounts less stressful for DIYers. You have more room for error, and the shade often looks more substantial. This approach lets you truly show off your chosen fabric.

### Cutting Your Fabric Accurately

Once you have your final width and length measurements, it is time to cut your fabric. Lay your pre-washed and ironed fabric flat on your large work surface. Use your measuring tape and a pencil or fabric marker to carefully mark your cutting lines.

*   **Marking:** Draw a straight line for your desired width. Then, measure down from this line to mark your total length. Ensure your corners are square. A large T-square or carpenter's square can be very helpful here.
*   **Cutting:** Use sharp fabric scissors to cut along your marked lines. Cut slowly and steadily to ensure straight edges. A clean cut prevents fraying and makes hemming easier. Remember, precision in cutting simplifies all subsequent steps in making your easiest DIY faux relaxed Roman shade.

## Step-by-Step No-Sew Assembly

Now that your fabric is perfectly cut, it is time to assemble your easiest DIY faux relaxed Roman shade. The no-sew method makes this stage incredibly straightforward. You will use fabric adhesive or iron-on hemming tape to create clean edges and shape the signature relaxed curve. Patience and careful pressing are key here. I find that working section by section helps keep everything neat and organized.

This assembly process avoids the intimidation of a sewing machine. You simply apply the adhesive and secure the fabric. The result is a professional-looking shade achieved with minimal skill. It is a fantastic way to customize your home decor without needing special equipment or extensive training.

### Creating Clean Side and Bottom Hems

First, we will create crisp hems on the sides and bottom of your fabric panel. This gives your shade a finished, polished appearance.

1.  **Side Hems:**
    *   Lay your fabric right side down.
    *   Fold each long side edge over by 1 inch towards the wrong side of the fabric. Press firmly with your iron.
    *   Apply a thin line of fabric glue along the inside of the folded edge, or place iron-on hemming tape within the fold.
    *   Fold the edge over again by another 1 inch. Press firmly to create a double-folded hem. If using iron-on tape, hold the iron in place according to the tape instructions to activate the adhesive. If using glue, hold in place with clips until dry. This double fold hides raw edges and adds durability.
    *   Repeat for the other side.

2.  **Bottom Hem:**
    *   Now, focus on the bottom edge of your fabric.
    *   Fold the bottom edge up by 1 inch towards the wrong side. Press firmly.
    *   Apply glue or tape.
    *   Fold it up by another 1 inch. Press firmly. This forms a strong, neat bottom hem. This hem will be visible, so make it perfect.

### Forming the Relaxed Curve

The relaxed curve is what gives this shade its unique charm. This is where your careful measurements for extra length come into play.

1.  **Marking the Curve:**
    *   Lay your fabric, right side down, on your work surface.
    *   Measure up from the bottom finished hem 6-8 inches (or your chosen relaxed fold length). Mark this point on each side of the shade.
    *   Find the center of your shade's width. From the bottom finished hem, measure up 4-5 inches (this measurement should be less than your side marks to create the curve). Mark this point.
    *   Using a flexible ruler or a string, gently draw a smooth, shallow curve connecting your side marks and dipping down to your center mark. This creates the guideline for your relaxed fold.

2.  **Creating the Fold:**
    *   Carefully fold the fabric along your drawn curve line. Ensure the fold is smooth and even.
    *   Press the fold lightly with your iron. This helps set the curve. Do not press too hard, as this is a soft fold.
    *   You can use a small amount of fabric glue along the inside of this fold to hold it in place. Apply only tiny dots or a very thin line, just enough to secure the curve. You want the fabric to maintain its soft drape.

### Preparing the Top for Mounting

The top of your shade needs to be ready for attachment to your chosen mounting method.

1.  **Top Hem:**
    *   Fold the top raw edge of your fabric over by 1 inch towards the wrong side. Press.
    *   Apply glue or tape, then fold it over again by another 1 inch. Press firmly. This creates a sturdy channel for a tension rod or a strong surface for Velcro.

2.  **Attaching Velcro (Optional, but Recommended for Outside Mount):**
    *   Cut a piece of the "loop" (soft) side of the self-adhesive Velcro tape the same width as your shade.
    *   Peel off the backing and carefully press the Velcro onto the wrong side of your top hem. Ensure it is perfectly straight and centered. Press firmly for a good bond. This Velcro will later attach to the "hook" side installed on your window frame or mounting board. This makes it easy to take down your shade for cleaning. For general fabric care, you might want to review tips like [how to clean fabric Roman blinds](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-fabric-roman-blinds), as the principles often apply.

Following these no-sew steps will allow you to create a beautiful, durable, and truly easiest DIY faux relaxed Roman shade. The simplicity of this assembly makes the project fun and rewarding for anyone.

## Mounting Your Faux Relaxed Roman Shade

After carefully assembling your easiest DIY faux relaxed Roman shade, the next exciting step is to mount it. Proper mounting ensures your shade hangs beautifully and looks intentional. The method you choose depends on whether you opted for an inside or outside mount, and your personal preference for hardware. Thankfully, for a faux shade, installation is much simpler than for a working one. You do not deal with complex mechanisms or pulley systems.

I often recommend keeping mounting options as simple as possible. This aligns with the "easiest DIY" philosophy of this project. Tension rods or self-adhesive hooks are fantastic choices for their simplicity and lack of drilling. However, if you want a more permanent or robust solution, small brackets or a slim wooden board with Velcro are also excellent options.

### Inside Mount with a Tension Rod

The tension rod method is by far the simplest for inside-mounted faux relaxed Roman shades. It requires no drilling and is easily adjustable.

1.  **Measure and Place:** Measure the inside width of your window frame. Purchase a tension rod that fits this measurement.
2.  **Insert Rod:** Gather the top hem of your faux Roman shade. Thread the tension rod through the channel you created in the top hem.
3.  **Install:** Position the tension rod inside the window frame, slightly above where you want the top of your shade to sit. Extend the rod until it is snug against the frame. The tension rod holds the shade in place.
4.  **Adjust:** Gently smooth out your shade. Ensure the relaxed curve hangs evenly and beautifully. This method is incredibly quick and perfect for renters or those who prefer not to make holes.

### Outside Mount with Velcro and a Board

For an outside mount, combining Velcro with a slim wooden board creates a polished, custom look. This method offers more stability and allows the shade to extend beyond the window frame.

1.  **Prepare the Board:** Get a slim piece of wood, often called a furring strip, that is the same width as your shade's top hem. You can paint or stain this board to match your window trim or wall.
2.  **Attach Velcro (Hook Side):** Cut a piece of the "hook" (scratchy) side of the self-adhesive Velcro tape to the length of your board. Peel off the backing and firmly press it along one long edge of the board. This is where your shade's loop-side Velcro will attach.
3.  **Mount the Board:** Hold the board above your window frame where you want the top of your shade to sit. Use a level to ensure it is straight. Mark the screw locations.
4.  **Drill and Secure:** Drill small pilot holes. Use small screws to attach the board securely to the wall, making sure it is level.
5.  **Attach Shade:** Align the loop side of the Velcro on your shade's top hem with the hook side on the mounted board. Press firmly across the entire width to secure the shade in place.
6.  **Final Adjustments:** Step back and admire your handiwork. Gently adjust the folds of your relaxed curve until they look perfect. This method offers a very clean finish, giving the illusion of a custom-made Roman shade.

### Outside Mount with Command Hooks

For a super easy, no-drill outside mount, especially for lighter fabrics, Command hooks are a fantastic option.

1.  **Mark Placement:** Decide where you want the top of your shade to sit. Use a pencil to mark two spots on the wall, spaced appropriately for the width of your shade. Ensure they are level.
2.  **Attach Hooks:** Follow the Command hook instructions to apply two hooks at your marked spots. Allow the adhesive to cure as directed (usually an hour).
3.  **Hang Shade:** You can attach small loops of fabric or even small metal rings to the top corners of your shade (using fabric glue) to hang it directly on the hooks. Alternatively, if your top hem has a slight opening, you can drape it over the hooks.
4.  **Adjust:** Make sure the shade hangs evenly. This method is incredibly simple and leaves no wall damage upon removal.

Regardless of the mounting method you choose, the goal is a secure and aesthetically pleasing display. Your finished faux relaxed Roman shade should look effortlessly elegant, completing the look of your room.

## Troubleshooting Common DIY Shade Issues

Even with the "easiest DIY" approach, sometimes small challenges arise. It is completely normal for a DIY project to have a few bumps along the road. The key is knowing how to address these common issues effectively, without getting discouraged. I have been there myself, staring at a slightly crooked hem or a fabric wrinkle that just will not disappear. Most problems with a faux relaxed Roman shade are minor and easily fixable.

The beauty of this no-sew project is that most fixes involve reapplying adhesive or re-pressing fabric. You do not need to pull out a seam ripper or re-sew anything. A little patience and a steady hand usually resolve things quickly. This section will guide you through common snags and offer simple solutions to keep your project on track.

### Dealing with Uneven Hems or Folds

One of the most frequent issues DIYers encounter is unevenness. This can happen with side hems, the bottom hem, or the relaxed curve itself.

*   **Wavy Side Hems:** If your side hems look wavy or are not straight, it is often due to rushing the gluing or ironing process.
    *   **Solution:** Gently peel back the hem (if the glue is still pliable or the iron-on tape hasn't fully set). Reapply glue or tape. Use a ruler to ensure the fold is perfectly straight as you press it down. Use pins or fabric clips to hold it securely while it dries or sets. Take your time, working in small sections.
*   **Crooked Bottom Hem:** A crooked bottom hem can make the entire shade look off-kilter.
    *   **Solution:** Check your initial measurement and cut. If the cut was straight, the fold might be uneven. Carefully un-adhere the hem. Re-measure and mark the fold line precisely with a fabric marker. Refold and re-adhere, using your ruler as a guide to keep it perfectly straight.
*   **Uneven Relaxed Curve:** If the relaxed curve sags too much on one side or looks lopsided, the initial marking or folding might have been inconsistent.
    *   **Solution:** This is usually the easiest to fix because it is a soft fold. Gently adjust the fabric. You might need to add a tiny dot of fabric glue at the points where it looks uneven to gently coax it into shape. If you used too much glue initially, you may need to carefully warm it with an iron (on low) to soften it slightly, then reshape.

### Adhesive Issues: Too Much, Too Little, or Not Sticking

Fabric adhesives are powerful, but they require proper application.

*   **Glue Bleeding Through:** Applying too much fabric glue can cause it to seep through to the right side of the fabric, leaving a visible mark.
