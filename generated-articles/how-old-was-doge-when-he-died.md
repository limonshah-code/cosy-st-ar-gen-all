### Signs of Seniority in Dogs, Like Kabosu

As dogs age, they show signs of seniority. These changes are natural parts of life. Observing these signs helps owners provide better care. For Shiba Inus, just like other breeds, these changes can be subtle at first. Owners need to pay close attention to their aging companions.

One common sign is a decrease in activity level. An older dog might sleep more often. They might become less eager for long walks or intense play. Mobility issues can also appear. Stiffness in joints, difficulty climbing stairs, or a slower gait are common. You might notice your old dog having trouble getting up. If your [old dog can't get up](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-when-old-dog-cant-get-up), it can be a sign of pain or weakness.

Changes in vision and hearing are also typical. An older dog might not respond to commands as quickly. They might bump into objects more often. Cognitive decline can also occur, similar to dementia in humans. Dogs might seem confused, forget routines, or stare blankly. Weight changes are also common; some dogs gain weight due to less activity, while others lose weight due to health issues. Regular vet check-ups become crucial to monitor these changes.

### Caring for an Aging Shiba Inu

Caring for an aging Shiba Inu means adjusting to their changing needs. Your goal is to keep them comfortable and healthy for their remaining years. This involves several important steps. Each step helps improve their quality of life.

First, adjust their diet. Senior dogs often need fewer calories to prevent weight gain. They also benefit from foods designed for older dogs. These foods often contain joint supplements and easy-to-digest ingredients. Your vet can recommend the best diet plan. Second, modify their exercise routine. While intense activity may be out, gentle walks are still important. Short, frequent walks can maintain muscle mass without overexertion. Consider swimming, which is easy on joints.

Third, ensure regular veterinary check-ups. Older dogs should see the vet at least twice a year. This helps detect age-related diseases early. Early detection means better treatment options. Blood tests and physical exams become vital. Fourth, make your home senior-friendly. Add ramps for steps or elevated beds. Provide soft, supportive bedding for tired joints. Ensure they have easy access to food and water. These changes make daily life much easier for them.
