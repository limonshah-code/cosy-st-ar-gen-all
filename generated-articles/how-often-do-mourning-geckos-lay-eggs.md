## Mourning Gecko Egg Laying: Frequency and Factors

Mourning geckos (Lepidodactylus lugubris) show a unique way to reproduce. These small lizards can create offspring without a male. This process is called parthenogenesis. If you keep these geckos, you likely ask, "How often do mourning geckos lay eggs?" Knowing their egg-laying schedule helps you care for them. It also helps you understand their breeding success. This article will explain their egg-laying cycle. It will also cover what affects their egg production. We will also look at how to support their reproductive health.

**Takeaway:**
*   Mourning geckos lay eggs frequently, typically every 4-6 weeks, because of parthenogenesis.
*   Each clutch of eggs always contains two eggs.
*   Good environmental conditions, a proper diet, and calcium are vital for consistent egg laying.
*   Proper care helps prevent problems like egg binding.

**Clear, Concise Answer:**
Mourning geckos lay eggs very often. They usually produce two eggs every 4 to 6 weeks. This consistent egg-laying happens because they reproduce through parthenogenesis. This means female geckos do not need a male to fertilize their eggs.

### The Unique Way Mourning Geckos Reproduce

Mourning geckos are special because they reproduce without a male. All geckos are female, and they create offspring that are genetic copies of themselves. This makes them a fascinating pet species. I find their biology truly amazing.

*   **Parthenogenesis Explained:** Parthenogenesis is a natural way of asexual reproduction. Embryos grow and develop without needing fertilization. In mourning geckos, every gecko is female. They produce offspring that are genetically the same as themselves. This method helps them spread quickly to new places. It ensures the species continues even when male geckos are not around.
*   **No Male Gecko Needed:** You do not need a male gecko to get baby mourning geckos. If you have only one female mourning gecko, she can still lay fertile eggs. This makes keeping them simple. You avoid problems that can arise from having males and females together for breeding. [Crested geckos can also lay eggs without mating](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/do-crested-geckos-lay-eggs-without-mating), which is a similar concept.
*   **Advantages of This Method:** This way of reproducing offers clear benefits in nature. A single gecko can start a new group. This ability helps them live in many different areas. It also means they will lay eggs consistently.

### How Often Do Mourning Geckos Lay Eggs?

Once they are grown, mourning geckos lay many eggs. I have seen my geckos lay eggs very often. Knowing their laying frequency helps you get ready. It also helps you give them the best care.

*   **Steady Egg Production:** A healthy adult mourning gecko usually lays two eggs every 4 to 6 weeks. Some may lay more often, like every 3 weeks. This happens especially when conditions are perfect. This regular cycle means you will get many eggs. This is exciting for people who breed them.
*   **How Many Eggs Per Laying:** Each time, a mourning gecko lays two eggs. These eggs are generally small, white, and have hard shells. They often stick to surfaces. This is an important detail for incubation. Their shells are not soft like some other gecko species.
*   **Things That Affect Laying:** Several things change how often mourning geckos lay eggs. These include their age, what they eat, their living conditions, and their general health. Providing good conditions helps them lay more eggs. It also keeps the mother healthy.

### Age and When Geckos Start Laying Eggs

A mourning gecko's age is important for when she starts laying eggs. It also affects how often she lays them. You need to know her stage of growth. This helps you give her the right food and care.

*   **When Laying Starts:** Mourning geckos usually start laying eggs when they are 6 to 12 months old. Some might start earlier, around 4-5 months, if everything is perfect. I remember the first time one of my young geckos laid eggs. It felt like a special achievement.
*   **Best Laying Years:** Geckos lay the most eggs during their main adult years. This is usually from 1 to 3 years old. During this time, they lay eggs most often. They can produce eggs regularly every few weeks.
*   **Less Production Later:** As geckos get older, they might lay eggs less often. Older geckos may also produce fewer eggs that can hatch. This is a normal part of their life. It is important to watch their health as they age.

### The Right Environment for Egg Production

Setting up the right environment is key for healthy and consistent egg laying. I always try to make my gecko enclosures feel like their wild home. This encourages regular laying.

*   **Heat and Moisture Levels:** The best temperature is 70-80°F (21-27°C) during the day. It should drop a little at night. Humidity should be around 60-80%. Misting the tank daily helps keep this level. Proper humidity stops eggs from drying out. Steady temperatures help their bodies work well.
*   **Tank Size and Set-Up:** A 5-gallon tank is okay for one gecko. However, a larger tank is better for more geckos. Give them many places to climb, hide, and live plants. Mourning geckos like to lay eggs in hidden, safe spots. These can be behind cork bark, under leaves, or in small cracks. You can also provide specific [gecko laying boxes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/do-leopard-geckos-lay-eggs-without-a-male) or containers. This gives them choices.
*   **Ground Material and Laying Spots:** Substrates like coco fiber or sphagnum moss hold moisture well. Offer several places where they can lay eggs. These include small, damp hide boxes or pieces of cork bark. Some geckos will stick their eggs directly to the glass or plastic of the tank.

### What to Feed Geckos for Frequent Egg Laying

Good food is a major part of healthy reproduction. A balanced diet directly affects how often mourning geckos lay eggs. I make sure my geckos eat a wide range of nutrient-rich foods. This supports their energy needs.

*   **Commercial Gecko Food:** A good quality powdered gecko diet, like those for crested geckos, should be their main food. These diets have all the necessary vitamins and minerals. Follow the directions on the package for mixing.
*   **Adding Insects to Their Diet:** Offer live insects such as small crickets, roaches, or fruit flies 1-2 times a week. Dust these insects with calcium powder (without D3) at every insect feeding. Use a multivitamin powder (with D3) once every two weeks. Enough [calcium intake](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-eggs-does-a-leopard-gecko-lay) is important for strong eggshells. It also helps prevent egg binding.
*   **Calcium and Vitamin D3 Importance:** Calcium is very important for making eggshells. It also stops bone problems. Mourning geckos are active at night. They get less natural sunlight. So, adding D3 helps their bodies use calcium. Make sure they always have calcium powder.

### Taking Care of and Incubating Mourning Gecko Eggs

After your mourning geckos lay eggs, you need a plan for them. This means deciding if you will leave them in the tank or move them to an incubator. I usually move mine to a separate incubator. This gives me better control.

*   **Finding Fertile Eggs:** Mourning gecko eggs are usually white with hard shells. Fertile eggs often look full and solid. Eggs that cannot hatch might be soft, discolored, or shriveled. They can sometimes lay eggs that will not hatch, especially young geckos.
*   **Setting Up for Incubation:** Many hobbyists use small plastic containers, often called "deli cup" incubators. Place the eggs in a small container with a little moist material like perlite or vermiculite. Keep temperatures between 70-80°F (21-27°C) and high humidity. Hatching usually takes 60-90 days, but this can change. For [crested gecko eggs](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-often-do-crested-geckos-lay-eggs), similar steps are often followed.
*   **Handling Eggs Carefully:** Mourning gecko eggs often stick to surfaces. If possible, leave them where they are. If you must move them, be very careful not to turn them. Turning the egg can cause the baby gecko to die. If eggs are on a piece of cork bark, you can move the whole piece. For eggs stuck to glass, you can gently slide a razor blade under them.

### Common Problems That Affect Egg Laying

Even with the best care, problems can come up. These problems can change how often mourning geckos lay eggs. Knowing about these issues helps you act fast. I have learned to watch for small signs of trouble.

*   **Egg Binding (Dystocia):** This happens when a female gecko cannot lay her eggs. It is a serious condition that can put her life at risk. It can be caused by bad food (especially not enough calcium), wrong temperatures, or eggs that are too big or not formed well. Signs include being tired, pushing, not eating, and seeing bumps in her stomach.
*   **Lack of Nutrients:** Not getting enough important nutrients, especially calcium, can cause eggs with soft shells. It can also make a gecko unable to lay eggs. This harms the mother's health. It also affects if the eggs can hatch. Make sure you regularly give [supplementation for your geckos](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-eggs-does-a-red-eared-slider-turtle-lay).
*   **Stress from Environment:** Wrong temperatures, low moisture, no good laying spots, or too many geckos can stress a gecko. Stress can make her lay fewer eggs. It can also cause her to hold onto her eggs. Keeping a calm, stable environment is very important.

### Why Your Mourning Gecko Might Not Be Laying Eggs

It can be worrying if your mourning gecko, who usually lays many eggs, stops. There are several reasons this might happen. I always check my care routine if egg production stops.

*   **Age and Growth:** Young geckos might not be old enough to start laying. Older geckos might naturally slow down their egg production. This is a normal part of their life.
*   **Poor Health or Food:** The most common reason for no egg laying is bad health or not enough food. A gecko needs a lot of energy and calcium to lay eggs regularly. Check her diet again and make sure she gets enough supplements.
*   **Environmental Issues:** Check your temperature and humidity levels. Make sure there are enough good places for her to lay eggs. If the tank is too cold, too dry, or has no safe spots, she might wait or stop laying.
*   **Seasonal Changes:** While mourning geckos lay eggs often, their activity might change a little with the seasons. Even if they are kept indoors, this is something to think about.
*   **Stress:** Any stress, like new tank mates, too much handling, or sudden changes in the environment, can affect egg laying. Keeping a stable and quiet place is very important.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: Do mourning geckos lay infertile eggs?**
Yes, mourning geckos can lay infertile eggs, though it is less common for them. This is because they reproduce without a male. Young geckos or those under stress might lay eggs that do not develop. These eggs often look shriveled, discolored, or soft. Always look closely at the eggs to see if they are viable.

**Q2: How long does it take for mourning gecko eggs to hatch?**
Mourning gecko eggs usually hatch within 60 to 90 days. The time it takes depends on the temperature. Warmer temperatures within the ideal range (70-80°F or 21-27°C) usually make them hatch faster. Cooler temperatures make development take longer. Steady conditions are important for successful hatching.

**Q3: Can mourning geckos lay eggs alone?**
Yes, they can. Mourning geckos are a special species that reproduce through parthenogenesis. This means a female gecko does not need a male partner to lay fertile eggs. Every mourning gecko is female. They can all produce healthy offspring on their own. This makes them easy to breed.

**Q4: What should I feed my mourning gecko to encourage egg laying?**
To help your mourning gecko lay eggs often, feed her a good quality commercial gecko diet. Also, give her appropriately sized live insects (crickets, roaches, fruit flies). Dust these insects with calcium (without D3) at every insect meal. Add a multivitamin with D3 every two weeks. Good nutrition is vital.
