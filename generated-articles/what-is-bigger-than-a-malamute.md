## What Is Bigger Than A Malamute? Giants Among Dog Breeds

The Alaskan Malamute is an impressive dog. Its strength and size make it a notable companion. Many people, myself included, often wonder: **what is bigger than a Malamute**? We admire these powerful sled dogs, but a whole world of even larger canine companions exists. Some breeds dwarf the Malamute in both height and weight, offering a truly grand presence.

This article will explore the magnificent dog breeds that generally surpass the Alaskan Malamute in size. We will look at their typical measurements, unique characteristics, and the special considerations required for owning these colossal canines. Join me as we discover the true giants of the dog kingdom. You might be surprised by how large some dogs can grow!

### Takeaway

*   Many dog breeds are significantly larger than the Alaskan Malamute.
*   Giant breeds include Mastiffs, Great Danes, Saint Bernards, and Irish Wolfhounds.
*   These larger dogs have specific needs regarding space, food, and veterinary care.
*   Research is important before bringing a giant breed into your home.

### The Concise Answer

Several dog breeds are larger than an Alaskan Malamute. These include Great Danes, Mastiffs (English, Neapolitan), Saint Bernards, Newfoundlands, and Irish Wolfhounds. These breeds typically exceed the Malamute's average weight of 75-85 pounds and height of 23-25 inches at the shoulder, with some reaching over 200 pounds and 30 inches tall.

### Understanding the Alaskan Malamute's Size

First, let us establish a baseline for the Alaskan Malamute's size. Malamutes are robust, powerfully built dogs. They are bred for pulling heavy sleds in harsh Arctic conditions. An adult male Alaskan Malamute typically stands 23 to 25 inches tall at the shoulder. Their weight usually ranges from 75 to 85 pounds. Females are slightly smaller, generally standing 23 inches tall and weighing about 75 pounds.

They possess a deep chest, strong bone structure, and a thick double coat. This gives them a very substantial appearance. This breed is often mistaken for a Siberian Husky, but Malamutes are noticeably larger and heavier. We can see this difference clearly when comparing the two breeds. Malamutes excel as working dogs, showcasing both strength and endurance. Their size makes them excellent companions for active families, but they are not the largest dog breed.

### The Mastiff Family: True Giants of the Dog World

When we discuss dogs bigger than a Malamute, the Mastiff family immediately comes to mind. These breeds are renowned for their massive size and imposing presence. The English Mastiff, for example, is one of the heaviest dog breeds. Males can easily weigh 150 to 250 pounds, with some individuals exceeding 300 pounds. They stand at least 30 inches tall at the shoulder, often more. Their sheer bulk and powerful build make the Malamute look quite small in comparison.

Other Mastiff types also showcase impressive dimensions. The Neapolitan Mastiff is another giant. These dogs have loose skin and a formidable appearance. They typically weigh between 110 and 150 pounds and stand 24 to 31 inches tall. While their height might be similar to a very large Malamute, their weight and overall mass are significantly greater. These dogs require significant space and a responsible owner. Their protective instincts are strong, reflecting their history as guardians.

### Towering Giants: Great Danes and Irish Wolfhounds

Some dogs exceed the Malamute in height, making them true towering giants. The Great Dane is often called the "Apollo of Dogs" for its majestic appearance. These dogs are one of the tallest breeds. Males typically stand 30 to 34 inches tall at the shoulder. They can weigh between 120 and 175 pounds. While some Great Danes might not weigh as much as the heaviest Mastiffs, their height is undeniable. A Malamute standing next to a Great Dane will look much shorter. Despite their intimidating size, Great Danes are known for their gentle and friendly nature, often called "gentle giants."

The Irish Wolfhound is another breed famous for its incredible height. These dogs are the tallest of all dog breeds. Males consistently stand 32 to 34 inches tall, sometimes even taller. Their weight ranges from 120 to 180 pounds. They possess a lean, muscular build with a rough, shaggy coat. Historically, they were used for hunting wolves, reflecting their powerful physique and keen eyesight. Both Great Danes and Irish Wolfhounds need ample space. Their large frames require plenty of room to stretch and move comfortably.

### The Gentle Powerhouses: Saint Bernards and Newfoundlands

Beyond height, some breeds offer immense bulk and strength, surpassing the Malamute significantly. The Saint Bernard is a prime example. These dogs are famous for their rescue work in the Alps. They are massive, powerful dogs with a sturdy build. Males typically weigh 140 to 180 pounds, and some can exceed 200 pounds. They stand 26 to 30 inches tall at the shoulder. Their strong bone structure and heavy muscle mass contribute to their impressive weight.

Newfoundlands are another magnificent giant breed. These dogs are known for their sweet temperament and their love for water. They possess a heavy bone structure and a thick, water-resistant double coat. Males generally weigh 130 to 150 pounds. They stand 28 inches tall at the shoulder. Their broad chest and powerful body make them incredibly strong swimmers. Both Saint Bernards and Newfoundlands are known for being gentle with children and devoted family pets. Their massive size requires a committed owner who can handle their physical needs and ample drool.

### Other Notable Breeds Exceeding Malamutes in Size

Several other large breeds consistently outsize the Alaskan Malamute. These dogs also offer unique characteristics and impressive statures.

*   **Leonberger:** This majestic breed originated in Germany. It combines the characteristics of the Saint Bernard, Newfoundland, and Great Pyrenees. Male Leonbergers typically weigh 120 to 170 pounds. They stand 28 to 31 inches tall. Their luxurious mane-like coat adds to their grand appearance. They are gentle, confident, and highly intelligent dogs.
*   **Anatolian Shepherd Dog:** This ancient Turkish breed serves as a livestock guardian. They are rugged and powerful. Males weigh 110 to 150 pounds. They stand 29 inches tall or more. Their strong, muscular build and watchful nature make them excellent protectors. They are independent thinkers and need experienced owners.
*   **Great Pyrenees:** Another impressive livestock guardian, the Great Pyrenees is known for its serene and majestic demeanor. Males typically weigh 100 to 160 pounds. They stand 27 to 32 inches tall. Their thick, white double coat makes them appear even larger. They are calm, patient, and protective of their families and flocks.
*   **Kangal Dog:** Closely related to the Anatolian Shepherd, the Kangal is a powerful Turkish livestock guardian. They are known for their speed and strength. Males weigh 110 to 145 pounds and stand 30 to 32 inches tall. They are serious working dogs, needing a job and extensive socialization.

These breeds, like the Mastiffs and Great Danes, require specific care. They are not simply larger versions of smaller dogs. They demand a significant commitment from their owners.

### Considerations for Owning a Dog Bigger Than a Malamute

Bringing a giant dog breed into your home is a huge decision. These dogs are not just bigger; they have different needs compared to smaller breeds or even a Malamute. I always advise considering several key factors before committing to one of these magnificent animals.

Firstly, space is paramount. A huge dog needs a spacious living environment. They need room to walk, turn around, and stretch out without knocking things over. A small apartment is usually not suitable for a Great Dane or a Saint Bernard. A home with a securely fenced yard is often ideal.

Secondly, the cost of ownership increases significantly with size. Giant breeds eat much more food than a Malamute. High-quality large-breed specific dog food can be expensive. Veterinary care for giant breeds also tends to be higher. They are prone to certain health issues like hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, and certain cancers. These conditions often require specialized and costly treatments. Regular vet check-ups are essential to monitor their health.

Training and socialization are critical for giant breeds. Their sheer size means they can accidentally injure someone if not properly trained. Early and consistent training is important. They must learn good manners and obedience commands. Socializing them from a young age with various people, places, and other animals is also vital. This helps them become well-adjusted adults. A poorly trained giant dog can be a danger to others and itself. These dogs need a firm but gentle hand and an owner who understands large breed behavior.

### Comparing Size Beyond Domestic Dogs: Wolves

Sometimes people wonder not just about domestic dogs but also about their wild relatives. They ask, "Is a wolf bigger than an Alaskan Malamute?" The answer is usually yes. Wolves are generally larger and heavier than even the biggest Alaskan Malamutes.

Gray wolves, the most common and largest wolf species, typically weigh between 80 to 180 pounds. They stand 26 to 32 inches tall at the shoulder. Some larger males can even exceed these weights. While a large male Malamute might approach the lower end of a wolf's weight range, wolves consistently have a more powerful and athletic build. They possess longer legs, a narrower chest, and a more streamlined body adapted for hunting. Their skulls are also larger and their jaws more powerful. ### FAQ Section

**Q1: What is the biggest dog breed in the world?**
The biggest dog breed in the world, in terms of sheer mass, is often considered the English Mastiff. These dogs can weigh over 200 pounds, with some individuals reaching over 300 pounds. For height, the Irish Wolfhound and Great Dane often compete for the title of tallest dog breed, regularly exceeding 30 inches at the shoulder.

**Q2: Are all Mastiffs bigger than Malamutes?**
Generally, yes, all recognized Mastiff breeds are bigger than Malamutes. English Mastiffs, Neapolitan Mastiffs, and Bullmastiffs consistently surpass the Alaskan Malamute in both weight and often height. Even a smaller Mastiff will typically outweigh a large Malamute due to their denser bone structure and muscle mass.

**Q3: What dog looks like a Malamute but is bigger?**
Several breeds have a similar "fluffy" or "Nordic" appearance but are significantly larger than a Malamute. The Leonberger is a prime example, possessing a similar double coat but much greater size. The Great Pyrenees also has a thick coat and substantial build, making it look like a bigger version of a Malamute in some ways.

**Q4: Do larger dogs need more exercise?**
Not necessarily. While they need regular exercise, "more" is not always accurate. Many giant breeds, like the Saint Bernard or English Mastiff, are prone to joint issues and prefer moderate exercise. Great Danes and Irish Wolfhounds need more vigorous play in their youth but are content with less as adults. The type of exercise often matters more than the duration.

**Q5: What are common health issues for giant breeds?**
Giant breeds face specific health concerns due to their rapid growth and large size. Common issues include hip and elbow dysplasia, a bone and joint condition. They are also prone to bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), a life-threatening stomach condition. Certain heart conditions and cancers also show higher prevalence in giant dog breeds.

**Q6: How much do giant dog breeds eat?**
Giant dog breeds eat significantly more than smaller dogs. A large adult Mastiff or Saint Bernard might consume 6-10 cups of high-quality large-breed kibble per day, sometimes more depending on their activity level. This translates to substantial food costs. Owners must factor in this expense, ensuring they provide adequate nutrition for their giant companion.
