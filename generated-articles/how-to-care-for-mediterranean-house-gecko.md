## Caring for Your Mediterranean House Gecko: A Complete Guide

Have you ever wondered about keeping a small, nocturnal companion? Mediterranean House Geckos are fascinating creatures. They make good pets for many people. These lizards are hardy and relatively easy to care for. Understanding their needs helps them live long, healthy lives. This guide will walk you through every step. We will cover habitat setup, feeding, health, and daily routines for your new gecko.

### Takeaway

Caring for a Mediterranean House Gecko means:
*   Setting up a proper enclosure with correct temperature and humidity.
*   Providing a consistent diet of appropriately sized insects.
*   Ensuring access to fresh water at all times.
*   Offering plenty of hides and climbing structures.
*   Performing regular cleaning and health checks.

### Quick Answer

To care for a Mediterranean House Gecko, provide a secure 10-gallon tank for adults, maintain temperatures between 75-85°F during the day and 70-75°F at night, ensure 60-70% humidity, and feed them a diet of live insects. Clean their enclosure regularly and offer hides for security.

### Understanding Your Mediterranean House Gecko

The Mediterranean House Gecko (Hemidactylus turcicus) is a small, common lizard. They are native to areas around the Mediterranean Sea. You can also find them in many other parts of the world now. These geckos are nocturnal. This means they are most active at night. They have distinctive large eyes with vertical pupils. Their skin is usually a mottled gray or tan color. This helps them blend into their surroundings. They also have sticky toe pads. These pads let them climb vertical surfaces, even glass.

Mediterranean House Geckos are generally calm. They do not mind being watched. They are not typically a gecko for frequent handling. This makes them a good choice for someone who enjoys observing pets. Their small size means they do not need a lot of space. However, they do need a very specific environment. A healthy gecko is an active gecko. You will see them exploring their tank or hunting insects. Knowing their natural behaviors helps you create a good home for them. These geckos can live for several years with good care. They are a wonderful addition to any reptile enthusiast's home.

### Creating the Perfect Gecko Enclosure

Setting up the right home is the first step in Mediterranean House Gecko care. A single adult gecko needs a minimum of a 10-gallon glass terrarium. A 15 or 20-gallon tank is even better. This gives them more space to explore. The tank must have a secure, screen lid. Geckos are excellent escape artists. They can squeeze through very small openings. The screen lid also provides proper ventilation. Good airflow prevents stale air and mold buildup. This is important for their health.

Choose a tank that opens from the front or has a secure top. Front-opening tanks make cleaning and feeding easier. They also stress the gecko less. You will need a place for their water bowl and food dish. These items should be easy to access. Make sure the tank is clean before adding anything. Wash it with a pet-safe disinfectant. Rinse it well to remove all traces of cleaner. A clean start helps prevent health problems. Position the tank in a quiet area. Avoid direct sunlight or drafts. Extreme temperatures can harm your gecko.

### Substrate Choices for Mediterranean Geckos

The substrate is the material at the bottom of your gecko's enclosure. Choosing the correct substrate is vital for Mediterranean House Gecko care. It helps maintain humidity and provides a safe living space. Paper towels or unprinted newspaper are simple and clean. They are easy to replace. This makes them good for new geckos or quarantine tanks. Reptile carpet is another option. It is reusable and relatively easy to clean. However, it can harbor bacteria if not cleaned often.

Loose substrates can also work. These include coco fiber, sphagnum moss, or a mix of reptile soil. These materials hold moisture well. This helps maintain humidity levels. When using loose substrates, be careful about impaction. Geckos might accidentally eat small pieces while hunting. This can cause digestive problems. If you use a loose substrate, feed your gecko in a separate container. Or, feed them on a flat piece of slate or a dish. Avoid sand or bark chips. These can be too rough or lead to impaction. Change loose substrates completely every few months. Spot clean them daily.

### Essential Heating and Lighting for Your Gecko

Proper heating and lighting are key elements of Mediterranean House Gecko care. Geckos are reptiles. They need external heat to regulate their body temperature. They need a temperature gradient in their tank. This means a warm side and a cooler side. The warm side should be about 80-85°F (27-29°C) during the day. The cool side can be 70-75°F (21-24°C). At night, the temperature can drop to 68-72°F (20-22°C). Use an under-tank heater (UTH) or a ceramic heat emitter (CHE). A UTH should cover one-third to one-half of the tank floor. Use a thermostat with your UTH to prevent overheating.

Mediterranean House Geckos are nocturnal. They do not need special UVB lighting. Regular room light is enough for a day/night cycle. If you want to view them at night, use a nocturnal bulb. This is a blue or red bulb that emits low light. It will not disturb your gecko's sleep cycle. Never use a white light at night. This confuses their natural rhythms. Always use thermometers to monitor temperatures. One on the warm side and one on the cool side helps you keep track. Accurate temperature control prevents stress and illness in your gecko.

### Decorating Your Gecko's Home: Hides and Climbing

A well-decorated enclosure is important for your Mediterranean House Gecko. It provides security and enrichment. Geckos need places to hide. Offer at least two hides. One hide should be on the warm side. The other should be on the cool side. This allows your gecko to thermoregulate while feeling safe. Hides can be made from half-logs, rock caves, or small plastic containers. Ensure the hides are sturdy and cannot tip over. A humid hide is also beneficial. You can make one by placing damp sphagnum moss inside a small plastic container with an entry hole. This helps with shedding.

Mediterranean House Geckos are climbers. They will use vertical space. Provide branches, cork bark, or artificial plants. Make sure climbing structures are secure. They should not fall and injure your gecko. Arrange decor to create interesting paths. This encourages natural behaviors like climbing and exploring. Artificial plants are easy to clean. Real plants can be used if they are non-toxic and pest-free. Pothos or snake plants are popular choices. Ensure any decor is free of sharp edges. This prevents cuts or scrapes on your gecko's delicate skin. A stimulating environment keeps your gecko happy and healthy.

### Feeding Your Mediterranean House Gecko

Feeding your Mediterranean House Gecko a proper diet is crucial. These geckos are insectivores. This means they eat insects. Crickets are a staple food item. You can also offer mealworms, small roaches, or dubia roaches. Always choose insects that are no larger than the space between your gecko's eyes. This prevents choking. Feed adult geckos every 2-3 days. Younger geckos need to eat daily. Watch your gecko's weight to adjust feeding frequency. A healthy gecko has a slightly plump tail. If you are ever wondering about how long can a gecko go without food, it's best to offer food regularly to avoid stress and ensure proper nutrition. They can go a few days, but consistent feeding is better. [how long can a gecko go without food](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-long-can-a-gecko-go-without-food)

It is important to gut-load your feeder insects. This means feeding the insects nutritious food before offering them to your gecko. Use a commercial gut-load diet or fresh vegetables. This transfers vital nutrients to your gecko. Dust insects with a calcium supplement before feeding. Do this at every feeding for young geckos. For adults, dust every other feeding. Use a calcium supplement with D3. Vitamin D3 helps geckos absorb calcium. Once or twice a month, also dust insects with a multivitamin supplement. This ensures they get all necessary vitamins and minerals. Place insects in a feeding dish or allow them to roam the tank for hunting practice. Remove any uneaten insects after a few hours. This prevents them from stressing your gecko or hiding in the tank.

### Water and Humidity Needs for Optimal Health

Water and humidity are very important for Mediterranean House Gecko health. Always provide a shallow, non-tip water dish. Change the water daily. Use purified or dechlorinated water. Tap water can contain harmful chemicals. The dish should be shallow enough for your gecko to drink easily. It should not be so deep that they could drown. Place the water dish on the cool side of the enclosure. This slows bacterial growth. Clean the dish regularly to prevent slime buildup.

Mediterranean House Geckos need moderate humidity. Aim for 60-70% humidity. You can achieve this by misting the tank once or twice a day. Use a spray bottle with purified water. Mist the sides of the enclosure. Do not mist the gecko directly. Misting also helps with shedding. Geckos need humidity to shed their skin properly. If humidity is too low, they can have stuck shed. This can lead to health problems. A hygrometer is a device that measures humidity. Place one inside the tank to monitor levels. A humid hide filled with damp sphagnum moss also helps maintain humidity. This also gives your gecko a place to go if they need more moisture. Proper humidity prevents dehydration and ensures healthy skin.

### Handling and Interaction with Your Pet Gecko

Mediterranean House Geckos are generally not keen on being handled. They are small and move quickly. Too much handling can stress them. It is best to appreciate them from outside their enclosure. If you must handle your gecko, do so gently. Scoop them up from underneath. Avoid grabbing them from above. This can make them feel like a predator is attacking them. Support their whole body with your hands. Allow them to walk from one hand to the other.

Keep handling sessions short. Five minutes or less is ideal. Always wash your hands before and after handling. Geckos have delicate skin. Oils and lotions on your hands can harm them. Children should always be supervised during any interaction. If your gecko shows signs of stress, put them back immediately. Signs of stress include erratic movements, loud vocalizations, or dropping their tail. A dropped tail will regrow. However, it will not look the same. It is also a stressful event for the gecko. The best interaction is often observation. Watch them hunt, climb, and explore. This is how you build trust and learn about their unique personality.

### Common Health Issues and Preventive Care

Understanding common health issues helps with Mediterranean House Gecko care. Early detection can save your gecko's life. One common issue is stuck shed. This happens when the humidity is too low. Patches of old skin remain on the gecko. Pay close attention to their toes and tail tip. Stuck shed can restrict blood flow and lead to tissue death. Increase humidity and provide a humid hide to prevent this. A gentle, warm bath can help remove stuck shed. Never pull off shed skin.

Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD) is another serious condition. It comes from a lack of calcium or Vitamin D3. Bones become weak and can deform. Symptoms include lethargy, tremors, and soft bones. Prevent MBD by dusting insects with calcium and D3. Provide a varied diet. Parasites can also affect geckos. Symptoms include weight loss, lethargy, and abnormal droppings. If you suspect parasites, take your gecko to a reptile vet. Regular cleaning of the enclosure prevents bacterial and fungal infections. Look for any unusual bumps, discoloration, or changes in behavior. A healthy gecko is active and alert. They have clear eyes and no visible injuries. Proper nutrition and a clean environment are the best preventive measures. ### General Maintenance and Cleaning Your Gecko's Habitat

Regular cleaning is a must for good Mediterranean House Gecko care. A clean habitat keeps your gecko healthy. Spot clean the enclosure daily. Remove any uneaten food items. Pick up any droppings. This prevents bacterial growth and odors. Use tongs or gloves for this task. Disinfect the water and food dishes daily. Wash them with hot water and dish soap. Rinse them thoroughly.

Perform a deep clean once a month. Remove your gecko to a temporary container. Take out all substrate and decor. Wash the tank with a pet-safe disinfectant or a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water). Rinse the tank completely until no smell remains. Let it air dry. Clean all decor items thoroughly. Scrub branches, hides, and plants. Replace loose substrate entirely. For reptile carpet or paper towels, replace or wash them. A clean environment reduces the risk of illness. It also makes for a more pleasant living space for your gecko. Keeping a consistent cleaning schedule helps prevent many common health problems.

### Lifespan and Long-Term Care for Your Gecko

They may eat less frequently. Keep a close eye on their weight and behavior. Adjust their diet or temperature as needed.

## FAQ Section

**Q1: What size tank does a Mediterranean House Gecko need?**
A: A single adult Mediterranean House Gecko needs a minimum of a 10-gallon glass terrarium. A 15 or 20-gallon tank provides more space. The tank must have a secure, screen lid to prevent escapes. It also needs proper ventilation for good airflow.

**Q2: What do Mediterranean House Geckos eat?**
A: Mediterranean House Geckos are insectivores. Their primary diet should consist of crickets. You can also offer mealworms, small roaches, or dubia roaches. Always gut-load insects before feeding them. Dust insects with calcium and vitamin supplements regularly.

**Q3: Do Mediterranean House Geckos need special lighting?**
A: No, Mediterranean House Geckos are nocturnal. They do not need special UVB lighting. Regular room light is enough to establish a day/night cycle. If you want to view them at night, use a low-wattage blue or red "moonlight" bulb.

**Q4: How often should I clean my gecko's enclosure?**
A: Spot clean your gecko's enclosure daily by removing droppings and uneaten food. Disinfect water and food dishes daily. Perform a deep clean of the entire enclosure once a month. Remove all substrate and decor, then wash the tank with a reptile-safe cleaner.

**Q5: Are Mediterranean House Geckos good for handling?**
A: Mediterranean House Geckos are generally not ideal for handling. They are small, quick, and can get stressed easily. It is best to appreciate them from outside their tank. If you must handle them, do so gently and for short periods. Always wash your hands.

**Q6: What temperature and humidity do Mediterranean House Geckos need?**
A: Mediterranean House Geckos need a temperature gradient. The warm side should be 80-85°F (27-29°C) and the cool side 70-75°F (21-24°C). Night temperatures can drop to 68-72°F (20-22°C). Humidity should be kept at 60-70%, achieved by misting.
