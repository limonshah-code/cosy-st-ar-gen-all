## Boost Performance: How to Clean Your AEM Air Filter

Is your vehicle feeling a little sluggish? Do you want to bring back that new car power? A dirty air filter might be the cause. Specifically, if you own a vehicle with an AEM air filter, proper maintenance is key. Cleaning an AEM air filter regularly ensures your engine breathes clean air. This simple task can significantly improve engine performance and even save you money on fuel.

AEM air filters are popular for their enhanced airflow and filtration capabilities. However, like all air filters, they collect dirt and debris over time. Learning how to clean an AEM air filter is a basic skill for any car enthusiast. This guide will walk you through the entire process. We cover everything from identifying your filter type to proper re-oiling. Get ready to revitalize your ride.

### Takeaway

Cleaning your AEM air filter is simple and boosts engine health.
*   Identify if your AEM filter is an oiled filter or a Dryflow filter.
*   Gather the correct AEM cleaning kit for your filter type.
*   Remove the filter carefully from your vehicle's intake system.
*   Apply the cleaner, rinse thoroughly, and allow it to dry completely.
*   Re-oil the filter if it is an oiled type, then reinstall it.

### What is the simplest way to clean an AEM air filter?

The simplest way to clean an AEM air filter involves using an AEM cleaning kit. First, apply the cleaner to remove dirt. Then, rinse the filter thoroughly with low-pressure water. Allow it to air dry completely. For oiled AEM filters, re-oil the filter evenly before reinstallation. This process restores airflow and filter efficiency.

## Why Cleaning Your AEM Air Filter Matters for Your Engine

Your engine needs clean air to perform its best. An AEM air filter protects your engine from harmful particles. It prevents dirt, dust, and debris from entering the combustion chamber. Over time, these filters collect a lot of contaminants. A dirty filter restricts airflow to the engine.

Restricted airflow leads to several problems. Your engine struggles to pull in enough oxygen. This affects the air-fuel mixture, reducing combustion efficiency. You might notice a decrease in horsepower and torque. Your vehicle may feel less responsive.

A clogged AEM air filter also impacts fuel economy. The engine works harder to overcome the airflow restriction. This means it consumes more fuel to produce the same power output. Regular cleaning restores optimal airflow. This helps your engine run efficiently. It maintains your vehicle's performance. It also helps in achieving better fuel mileage.

Moreover, a clean air filter prolongs engine life. By stopping abrasive particles, it protects internal engine components. These particles can cause wear and tear on pistons and cylinder walls. Keeping your AEM filter clean is a small task. It offers big benefits for your vehicle's longevity and health. This step is a vital part of routine car maintenance.

## Identifying Your AEM Filter Type: Dryflow or Oiled?

Before you clean your AEM air filter, you must know its type. AEM offers two main types of air filters. These are Dryflow filters and oiled filters. Each type requires a specific cleaning method. Using the wrong method can damage the filter. It can also reduce its filtration effectiveness.

**AEM Dryflow Filters:** These filters do not use oil. They use a synthetic, non-woven filter media. This material captures dirt without needing any oil. Dryflow filters are easy to identify because they have a distinct dry feel. They often come in a blue or gray color. When you inspect a Dryflow filter, it will not feel tacky or oily.

**AEM Oiled Filters:** These filters use cotton gauze filter media. The cotton gauze is treated with a special oil. This oil helps trap very fine dirt particles. Oiled filters usually have a reddish or pinkish color. If you touch an oiled filter, it will feel slightly sticky or damp from the oil. It is important to know if your AEM filter is a Dryflow filter. This will prevent you from applying oil when it is not needed.

You can often find the filter type marked on the filter itself. Check the edge of the filter or the instruction manual. Knowing the exact type of your AEM filter is critical. It guides you to use the correct cleaning solution and process. Improper cleaning can cause issues. It can lead to poor filtration or engine sensor problems.
