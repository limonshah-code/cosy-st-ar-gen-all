## Understanding Mouth Rot in Leopard Geckos

Mouth rot, or stomatitis, is a bacterial infection of a leopard gecko's mouth. It causes inflammation and tissue decay. This condition typically starts when a gecko has a weakened immune system. Poor husbandry is a major contributing factor. This includes incorrect temperatures, low humidity, and an unsanitary enclosure.

A gecko's mouth can also get injured. An injury can happen from aggressive feeding or from fighting with tank mates. These injuries create openings for bacteria to enter. Once bacteria enter, they can multiply rapidly. This leads to infection and swelling.

The bacteria involved are often common ones found in the environment. However, they become harmful when a gecko's defenses are down. Nutritional deficiencies, especially a lack of Vitamin A, also play a role. Vitamin A is vital for healthy mucous membranes. These membranes line the mouth. Without enough Vitamin A, the mouth tissue becomes vulnerable.

Stress also weakens a gecko's immune system. Stress can come from improper handling or an unsuitable habitat. A stressed gecko is more likely to get sick. It is important to provide a calm and stable environment. This helps your gecko stay healthy.

## Recognizing Symptoms of Leopard Gecko Mouth Rot

Spotting mouth rot early is very important for successful treatment. The first signs can be subtle. You might notice changes in your gecko's eating habits. They may refuse food or struggle to pick it up. This indicates pain in their mouth.

Look closely at your gecko's mouth area. Swelling around the jaw or lips is a key symptom. You might see a reddish discoloration inside the mouth. This indicates inflammation. In more advanced stages, a cheesy, pus-like discharge appears. This discharge can be yellowish or whitish.

Sometimes, you will see small lesions or ulcers. These are open sores on the gums, tongue, or roof of the mouth. Bleeding from the mouth can also occur. The gecko might rub its face against objects. This is a sign of discomfort.

Your gecko may also show general signs of illness. They might become lethargic. They may lose weight due to not eating. Their breathing might seem labored if the swelling is severe. If you notice any of these signs, act quickly. Early intervention can prevent the condition from worsening. You should contact a reptile veterinarian.

## When to Seek Veterinary Care for Leopard Gecko Mouth Rot

Mouth rot is a serious condition that almost always requires veterinary attention. You should schedule an appointment with a reptile veterinarian immediately if you suspect mouth rot. Waiting can lead to the infection spreading. It can affect the bones of the jaw or even the respiratory system. General symptoms of illness, like lethargy or weight loss, also warrant a vet visit.

A qualified reptile vet can accurately diagnose the problem. They will examine your gecko's mouth and overall health. They may take a swab for bacterial culture. This identifies the specific bacteria causing the infection. Knowing the bacteria helps the vet choose the most effective antibiotic.

Do not attempt to treat severe mouth rot with home remedies alone. Over-the-counter products are not strong enough. They can mask symptoms, delaying proper treatment. Untreated mouth rot leads to severe pain and suffering. It can result in permanent damage.

Even if you only see mild symptoms, a vet visit is still essential. Early diagnosis means faster recovery. It also prevents the condition from becoming chronic. Your vet will guide you through the correct treatment plan. They will also provide advice on preventing recurrence.

## Veterinary Treatments for Mouth Rot

When you visit the veterinarian, they will develop a comprehensive treatment plan. The primary treatment for mouth rot is often antibiotics. The vet may give injectable antibiotics. These work systemically throughout the gecko's body. They target the bacterial infection directly. The vet might also prescribe oral antibiotics. You will administer these at home.

The vet may also clean the affected area. This is called debridement. They remove dead tissue and pus from the mouth. This cleaning helps the healthy tissue heal faster. It also reduces the bacterial load. Sometimes, a topical antiseptic mouthwash is prescribed. This helps keep the mouth clean.

Supportive care is vital. Your gecko may be dehydrated. The vet might give subcutaneous fluids. This helps with hydration and overall health. If your gecko is not eating, the vet may show you how to assist-feed. This involves giving a liquid reptile diet using a syringe. Nutrition is important for healing.

In severe cases, surgery may be necessary. This happens if the infection has spread to the bone. The vet will explain all options. They will make sure you understand the treatment. They will also schedule follow-up appointments. These check your gecko's progress.

## Post-Treatment Care and Recovery

After your gecko receives veterinary treatment, proper home care is crucial for recovery. Continue to give all prescribed medications. Follow the exact dosage and schedule. Do not stop treatment early. Stopping early can lead to a relapse. The infection may return stronger.

Maintain excellent enclosure hygiene. Clean the quarantine tank daily. Replace the paper towel substrate often. Disinfect all decor and water dishes regularly. This prevents re-infection. Use a reptile-safe disinfectant.

Offer a variety of appropriate feeder insects. Make sure they are gut-loaded and dusted with calcium and vitamins. Proper nutrition supports healing. If your gecko is still refusing food, continue assist-feeding as instructed by your vet. It's important that your gecko gets enough nutrients.

Monitor your gecko's weight and activity levels. Watch for signs of improvement in their mouth. The swelling should decrease. The redness should fade. The discharge should clear up. Keep up with any follow-up appointments. Your vet will assess the healing process. They will tell you when it is safe to return your gecko to its main enclosure. Recovery can take several weeks. Be patient and consistent with care.

## FAQ Section

### What causes mouth rot in leopard geckos?

Mouth rot in leopard geckos usually stems from poor husbandry. This includes incorrect temperatures, high humidity, or unsanitary conditions. Nutritional deficiencies, especially a lack of Vitamin A, also weaken the immune system. Physical injuries to the mouth, such as from aggressive feeding or rough substrate, create entry points for opportunistic bacteria. Stress also plays a role in reducing a gecko's natural defenses.

### Can mouth rot heal on its own?

Mouth rot in leopard geckos rarely heals on its own without veterinary intervention. It is a bacterial infection that typically requires prescription antibiotics and professional cleaning. Leaving mouth rot untreated can lead to severe complications. These complications include bone infection, systemic illness, and even death. Prompt veterinary care is essential for a full recovery and to prevent the infection from worsening.

### How long does it take to treat mouth rot?

The duration of mouth rot treatment for leopard geckos varies. It depends on the severity of the infection and how early it was caught. Mild cases might resolve in a few weeks with antibiotics and supportive care. More severe or chronic cases can take several weeks to months to fully heal. Consistent follow-up with your reptile veterinarian is important to ensure complete recovery and prevent recurrence.

### Is mouth rot contagious to other geckos?

While mouth rot itself is a bacterial infection, the underlying causes, such as poor husbandry, can affect multiple geckos in the same environment. The bacteria involved are often opportunistic, meaning they thrive when a gecko's immune system is weak. While direct transmission of the bacteria is possible through shared food or water, focusing on improving the overall health and hygiene of all geckos and their environment is key to preventing spread.

### Can I use over-the-counter medications for mouth rot?

No, you should not use over-the-counter medications for mouth rot in leopard geckos. These products are generally not effective for serious bacterial infections like stomatitis. They can also delay proper diagnosis and treatment by a qualified reptile veterinarian. Delaying professional care allows the infection to worsen, making it harder to treat and potentially causing more severe health issues for your gecko. Always consult a vet.

### What happens if mouth rot is left untreated?

If mouth rot in a leopard gecko is left untreated, the bacterial infection will spread. It can lead to severe pain and suffering. The infection can erode jaw bones, cause tooth loss, and lead to systemic illness. Untreated mouth rot often results in your gecko refusing to eat, leading to severe weight loss, dehydration, and eventually death. Prompt veterinary intervention is vital to save your gecko's life.
