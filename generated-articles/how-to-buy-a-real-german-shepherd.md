## How To Buy A Real German Shepherd

Bringing a German Shepherd into your home is an exciting thought. This breed offers loyalty, intelligence, and a protective nature. You want to make sure you get a genuine one. Understanding **how to buy a real German Shepherd** means looking beyond a cute face. It involves research and careful choices. This article helps you find a truly authentic and healthy German Shepherd puppy. We will explain what to look for, where to search, and what questions to ask.

### Takeaway

Buying a real German Shepherd requires careful steps:
*   Choose a reputable breeder.
*   Verify health clearances for parents.
*   Understand the breed standard.
*   Meet the puppy and its parents.
*   Ask many questions about health and temperament.

To buy a real German Shepherd, you must find a reputable breeder who focuses on health, temperament, and adherence to breed standards. This ensures you get a purebred dog with predictable traits, rather than a mix or a dog with potential health issues.

### Understanding What Makes a German Shepherd "Real"

What does "real" mean when we talk about a German Shepherd? It means a dog that comes from documented purebred lines. This dog displays the standard physical and behavioral traits of the breed. It also means the dog does not have hidden health problems common in the breed. This usually comes from responsible breeding practices. A real German Shepherd is not just a dog that looks like one. It is a dog with a verifiable history and a predictable future in terms of breed characteristics.

Many dogs might look like German Shepherds. They might even be called German Shepherds. However, they might not be purebred. Or they might come from breeders who do not prioritize health. You want a dog that embodies the true spirit and health of the breed. This focus ensures your new family member lives a long, happy life. Knowing the characteristics helps you avoid common mistakes. This includes getting a puppy from a backyard breeder.

A **real German Shepherd** adheres to the standards set by breed clubs, such as the American Kennel Club (AKC) in the United States. These standards cover looks, size, coat color, and temperament. For example, a purebred German Shepherd usually has a strong, athletic build. They have distinct colors, often black and tan. Their ears stand erect. While some variations exist, like the white German Shepherd, their purebred status still matters. ### Finding a Reputable German Shepherd Breeder

Finding the right breeder is the most important step in buying a real German Shepherd. A good breeder cares deeply about their dogs and the breed. They breed for health, temperament, and conformation to breed standards. They do not breed just for profit. You can often find reputable breeders through national or local German Shepherd dog clubs. Online breeder directories from official kennel clubs are also good resources. These breeders follow strict codes of ethics.

Responsible breeders are transparent about their breeding practices. They welcome questions. They invite you to visit their facility. They will show you where the puppies live. They also want to meet you. They ask about your home and lifestyle. This shows they want their puppies to go to good homes. They will also educate you about the breed. They explain the specific needs of a German Shepherd. This partnership ensures a good start for your puppy.

Avoid breeders who have many different breeds for sale. Avoid those who always have puppies available. Also, avoid those who do not ask you any questions. These are signs of a "puppy mill" or backyard breeder. They often do not care for their dogs properly. Puppies from these sources often have health or behavioral problems. They may not even be true German Shepherds. A good breeder focuses on quality, not quantity. They typically have a waiting list. They might only breed a few litters per year. Always prioritize the breeder's reputation. This is key to finding a healthy puppy. ### Evaluating Health Clearances and Genetic Testing

A real German Shepherd should come with verifiable health clearances. These clearances show that the parents of the puppies were tested for common genetic diseases. German Shepherds are prone to certain health conditions. These include hip and elbow dysplasia. They also can have degenerative myelopathy. Reputable breeders screen their breeding dogs for these conditions. They get certifications from organizations like the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA). They also use DNA tests for other genetic issues.

Do not accept a breeder's word alone. Ask to see the official paperwork for all health clearances. These documents should list the names of the parent dogs. They should also show the results of their health tests. A good breeder will readily provide these documents. They will explain what the results mean. This transparency builds trust. It also protects you from potential vet bills down the line. A puppy from parents with clear health histories has a much better chance of being healthy itself.

Understanding the typical lifespan of a German Shepherd also helps you plan for their care. Healthy parents contribute to a longer life for the puppies. This information helps you understand the long-term commitment. Always ensure genetic testing results are available. This is a non-negotiable step for a real German Shepherd.

### Understanding German Shepherd Temperament and Socialization

A real German Shepherd has a distinct temperament. They are intelligent, confident, and brave. They are also loyal and protective of their family. However, they need proper socialization from a young age. A good breeder starts this process early. Puppies should grow up in a stimulating home environment. They should interact with people and other animals. This early exposure shapes their personality. It helps them become well-adjusted adults.

When you visit the breeder, observe the puppies' behavior. They should be curious and playful. They should not be overly shy or aggressive. A puppy that hides or growls excessively might have temperament issues. These issues could be genetic or due to poor early socialization. Ask the breeder about their socialization practices. What experiences do the puppies have before going to new homes? Do they interact with children? Are they used to household noises?

The parents' temperament is also important. Ask to meet the mother dog. Observe her behavior. Is she friendly and calm? Or is she anxious or aggressive? Temperament can be inherited. A good mother dog often produces good-tempered puppies. A real German Shepherd combines strong instincts with a stable mind. Proper breeding and early care are key to this. ### Recognizing True German Shepherd Physical Characteristics

Identifying a real German Shepherd also involves knowing its physical traits. The breed standard outlines these characteristics. A typical German Shepherd is a medium to large-sized dog. Males stand 24-26 inches tall. Females are 22-24 inches tall. Their weight ranges from 50 to 90 pounds. They have a strong, muscular body. Their gait is smooth and flowing. Their head is noble and well-proportioned.

Their coat is usually medium length. It is dense with a harsh outer coat and softer undercoat. Common colors include black and tan, sable, and solid black. White German Shepherds are also purebred, but not accepted in all show rings. Their ears stand erect. Their tail forms a slight curve. It hangs down when the dog is at rest. These are distinct features that define the breed. Dogs without these traits might not be purebred.

Be wary of breeders claiming "rare" or "giant" German Shepherds. These claims often indicate poor breeding practices. They may produce oversized dogs with structural problems. Stick to the recognized breed standard. This ensures you get a physically sound dog. A real German Shepherd embodies elegance and strength. Understanding these physical markers helps you recognize an authentic dog. ### Visiting the Breeder and Meeting Your Future Puppy

Visiting the breeder in person is a critical step. It allows you to see the living conditions of the puppies and their parents. A reputable breeder keeps their dogs clean and well-cared for. The puppies should look healthy and active. Their eyes should be clear. Their coats should be shiny. They should not have discharge from their nose or eyes. The environment should be stimulating and clean. This visit also lets you interact with the puppies. You can observe their personalities.

Observe the mother dog. She should be present with the puppies. Her condition tells you a lot about the breeder's care. She should look healthy and well-fed. She should be calm and attentive to her puppies. If the mother is absent, or looks unwell, this is a red flag. A responsible breeder will introduce you to both parents if possible. They want you to see the lineage. This connection helps confirm the puppies' heritage.

Do not rush this visit. Spend time with the puppies. Watch how they play. See how they interact with their littermates and with you. Look for a puppy that seems to fit your family's energy level. A responsible breeder will guide you. They help you choose a puppy that matches your lifestyle. This interaction is key. It confirms you are getting a genuine German Shepherd. It also ensures you find the right fit for your home.

### Key Questions to Ask Before You Buy a German Shepherd

Before you commit to buying, prepare a list of questions for the breeder. These questions cover health, lineage, and puppy care. Ask about the parents' health clearances. Request to see official documents for hips, elbows, and genetic tests. Ask about the pedigree of the puppies. A reputable breeder will have this information readily available. They should be able to trace lines back several generations. This proves the dog's purebred status. Consider asking [should I buy a dog without AKC](https://cosyhomecreation.com/should-i-buy-a-dog-without-akc) to gauge the breeder's response regarding registration.

Inquire about the puppy's early care. What vaccinations have they received? What deworming schedule did they follow? Ask what food they are eating. This helps you transition them to your home smoothly. A good breeder will provide a health guarantee. They also offer a contract. This contract should outline responsibilities for both parties. It should also include a spay/neuter clause for pet-quality puppies. Ask about their return policy. What if the puppy develops a health issue later?

Finally, ask about the breeder's support. Will they be available for questions after you take the puppy home? A responsible breeder offers lifetime support. They care about their puppies throughout their lives. This ongoing support is a sign of a truly dedicated breeder. These questions help you make an informed decision. They ensure you are getting a real German Shepherd from a trustworthy source.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: How can I tell if a German Shepherd puppy is purebred?**
A: A purebred German Shepherd puppy comes with AKC (or equivalent kennel club) registration papers. The breeder provides a pedigree showing the parents and their lineage. Also, the puppy should match the breed standard for physical traits and temperament. Reputable breeders perform health tests on parents. This ensures healthy offspring.

**Q2: Are there specific health tests a real German Shepherd should have?**
A: Yes. Real German Shepherds should have parents tested for hip and elbow dysplasia. These results come from organizations like OFA or PennHIP. They should also be tested for degenerative myelopathy (DM) and other genetic conditions. Good breeders provide documentation for all these tests.

**Q3: How much does a real German Shepherd puppy cost?**
A: The cost of a real German Shepherd puppy varies. It depends on the breeder's reputation, lineage, and location. Expect to pay anywhere from $1,500 to $4,000 or more for a well-bred puppy. This price includes health clearances, vaccinations, and initial vet checks. Lower prices often signal a backyard breeder or puppy mill.

**Q4: What is the best age to bring a German Shepherd puppy home?**
A: The best age to bring a German Shepherd puppy home is generally between 8 to 12 weeks old. At this age, puppies have had enough time with their mother and littermates. This time is important for learning social skills. They are also old enough to begin training and bond with their new family.

**Q5: What red flags should I look for when buying a German Shepherd?**
A: Red flags include breeders who do not allow visits, cannot provide health clearances, or push you to buy quickly. Avoid breeders with multiple litters or many breeds available. Also, be cautious if they offer no health guarantee or sell puppies before 8 weeks of age.
