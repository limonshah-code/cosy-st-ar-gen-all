Yes, the movie "Togo" is entirely based on a true story. It dramatizes the real events of the 1925 serum run to Nome, Alaska. ### What breed is Togo in the movie?
Togo in the movie is a Siberian Husky. This breed is known for its endurance, speed, and ability to thrive in cold climates. Siberian Huskies are popular sled dogs due to their strength, intelligence, and willingness to work. The dogs cast in the film closely resemble the appearance and characteristics of real Siberian Huskies.

### Are the dogs in "Togo" real, or are they CGI?
The dogs in "Togo" are predominantly real dogs. The filmmakers used a team of highly trained Siberian Huskies to portray Togo and the other sled dogs. While some visual effects might have been used for safety or enhancement, the core performances came from live animals. This added to the authenticity and emotional impact of the movie.

### What is the difference between "Togo" and "Balto"?
Both "Togo" and "Balto" are movies based on the 1925 Nome serum run. "Togo" focuses on the journey of Leonhard Seppala and his lead dog, Togo, who covered the longest and most dangerous leg of the relay. "Balto" (an animated film) highlights the final leg of the relay, led by the dog Balto and musher Gunnar Kaasen. Historians consider Togo the true hero of the entire run, though Balto received more public fame at the time.
