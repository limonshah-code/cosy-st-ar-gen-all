## Spotting Fakes: How Can You Tell If LPS Are Fake?

Collecting Littlest Pet Shop (LPS) toys brings joy to many people, including myself. These small, adorable figures hold a special place in the hearts of collectors worldwide. However, the popularity of authentic LPS has led to a rise in counterfeit versions. Knowing how to tell if LPS are fake is important for any collector.

Fake LPS can disappoint. They often lack the quality and charm of genuine figures. Understanding the differences helps protect your collection and your investment. In this comprehensive guide, we will explore key methods to identify counterfeit LPS toys. We cover everything from packaging clues to intricate details on the figures themselves. Get ready to become an expert at spotting fake Littlest Pet Shop pets.

### Takeaway

*   Examine packaging for misspellings, poor print quality, or missing details.
*   Check the toy's overall quality, including plastic texture and paint application.
*   Inspect eyes for distinct pupil and highlight shapes unique to real LPS.
*   Feel the head bobble and neck peg for proper movement and connection.
*   Look at the feet for magnet presence and authentic stampings.

You can tell if LPS are fake by closely examining several key areas. Look for poor quality plastic, sloppy paint, unusual eye designs, and incorrect neck peg or magnet details. Authentic LPS figures have specific markings and a consistent finish. Comparing a suspect pet with a known real one often reveals differences.

### Understanding the Threat: Why Fake LPS Are a Problem

The rise of fake LPS figures presents real challenges for collectors. These counterfeit toys are not just poor imitations; they can also harm the collecting community. When you buy a fake, you support unethical manufacturing practices. This takes away from the value of genuine items.

Fake LPS often have significant quality issues. The plastic might feel cheap or brittle. Paint can chip easily, and colors may not match the original. These problems mean the toy will not last as long. This reduces its play value and its collector appeal.

Counterfeit sellers exploit the demand for popular or rare LPS characters. They produce low-cost versions that look similar at first glance. This deceives buyers who seek specific pets or good deals. Many collectors, myself included, want to ensure their collection is genuine. Knowing how to tell if LPS are fake becomes a necessary skill.

Furthermore, these fakes can flood the market. This makes it harder for honest sellers to move authentic products. It also creates confusion for new collectors. They might not know what to look for when starting their journey. Protecting your collection starts with awareness. We help you learn to identify genuine pieces. Understanding signs of authenticity makes you a more confident buyer.

### The First Clues: Examining LPS Packaging

The packaging is often the first place to find hints about a fake LPS. Counterfeiters sometimes cut corners on the box or blister pack. Genuine LPS packaging has a distinct look and feel. Look for high-quality printing and clear images. Hasbro, the maker of LPS, uses specific branding elements.

Start by checking the logo. The "Littlest Pet Shop" logo should appear crisp and well-defined. Fake packaging might have blurry or pixelated logos. Misspellings are a huge red flag. Always read all text on the box carefully. Even small errors indicate a counterfeit product.

Next, examine the product information. Authentic LPS packages include legal information, barcodes, and sometimes a production stamp. These details should be clear and legible. Fake versions might have missing information or generic barcodes. Sometimes, the colors on the package are off. They might be too dull or too bright compared to official releases.

Feel the material of the packaging. Real LPS boxes use sturdy cardboard or clear plastic. Fake ones might use flimsy materials. The plastic might crack or crease easily. Pay attention to how the toy is secured inside. Real LPS are usually held firmly with plastic ties or a molded plastic insert. Fakes might be loose or secured with simple twist ties. These small differences in packaging can save you from buying a fake.

### Up Close and Personal: Inspecting LPS Body Features

Once you remove the LPS from its packaging, you can inspect the figure itself. The body features offer many clues to tell if LPS are fake. Authentic LPS figures have a consistent look and feel. They have smooth, high-quality plastic.

First, touch the plastic. Real LPS toys have a specific texture. It is smooth but not slippery. Fake LPS often feel different. The plastic might feel rough, greasy, or too light. Sometimes, it has an unpleasant smell. This smell can be a strong chemical scent.

Look at the seams where the plastic pieces join. Genuine LPS figures have clean, minimal seams. These seams should line up perfectly. Fake LPS often have noticeable mold lines. These lines can be thick, uneven, or have excess plastic, called "flashing." This shows poor manufacturing quality.

The paint application is another major indicator. Hasbro applies paint neatly on real LPS. Colors are vibrant and lines are sharp. Counterfeit LPS often have sloppy paint jobs. You might see paint outside the lines. Colors can be dull, inconsistent, or simply wrong for that character. Small details, like freckles or paw prints, might be missing or poorly rendered. Observing how colors blend or stop is important. You can often see the base plastic color under chipped paint on fakes. This attention to detail in the body features helps identify if an LPS is real or fake.

### Eyes, Ears, and Noses: Key Facial Indicators of Fake LPS

The face of an LPS is perhaps its most distinguishing feature. The eyes, in particular, are unique to authentic figures. Learning to identify their specific traits is crucial if you want to tell if LPS are fake. Hasbro has several distinct eye styles.

Real LPS eyes are usually glossy and have specific pupil shapes. They often feature a white "dot" or "starburst" reflection, giving the eyes depth. These highlights are placed precisely. Fake LPS eyes often look flat. The pupils might be misshapen or simply black dots. The white reflections may be missing, misplaced, or look like simple blobs. Sometimes, the eyes are not symmetrical. One eye might be larger than the other or positioned differently.

Examine the paint on the eyes. Authentic LPS have clean, crisp lines for the pupils and irises. Fake versions often have blurred lines or paint bleeding. The color of the eyes should also match known genuine figures. Some counterfeit LPS use off-colors for the eyes.

Beyond the eyes, look at the nose and mouth. These features should be well-defined and painted accurately. On fake LPS, the nose might be too small or too large. The mouth might be a simple line or poorly painted. The ears should also have the correct shape and texture for the specific animal. Some fake pets have simplified ear molds. Paying close attention to these facial details provides strong evidence of authenticity. ### The Tell-Tale Neck Peg and Head Bobble

One of the most loved features of original LPS toys is their bobble-head action. This feature is due to a specific neck peg design. This part is a key indicator when you want to tell if LPS are fake. Authentic LPS heads connect to the body with a sturdy neck peg. This peg allows for a smooth, springy bobble motion.

When you gently tap a real LPS head, it should bobble freely and return to its upright position. The motion feels natural. On fake LPS, the bobble might be stiff or wobbly. Sometimes, the head hardly moves at all. It might even feel loose or unstable on the body. This poor movement indicates a cheap or incorrectly designed neck peg.

Inspect the neck peg itself. If you can carefully remove the head (which is not recommended for new collectors unless you know how), you would see the peg. Real LPS pegs are usually clear plastic and fit snugly into the head and body. Fake pegs can be opaque, poorly molded, or too small/large for the hole. You might even see glue residue if the counterfeiter used it to secure a poorly fitting head.

The connection between the head and body should be seamless. There should not be a large gap. Fake LPS often have visible gaps at the neck joint. The head might not sit flush with the body. These details about the neck and head movement are important. They give clear signs of a counterfeit.

### Feet and Magnets: Subtle But Significant Details

The feet of an LPS figure hold several subtle clues about its authenticity. These details, though small, are hard for counterfeiters to replicate perfectly. Examining them closely can help you tell if LPS are fake.

Many older, authentic LPS figures have a magnet embedded in one of their feet. This magnet allows them to interact with playsets. The color and shape of this magnet are crucial. Early LPS (Generation 1, G1) often had red magnets. Later LPS (Generation 2, G2) usually had blue magnets. Some rare ones had no magnet, but a hole instead. Fake LPS might have a magnet of the wrong color. The magnet might also be poorly inserted or missing entirely from a character that should have one.

Beyond the magnet, look at the bottom of the feet. Authentic LPS usually have a small, debossed or engraved number. This number identifies the pet's mold or series. Sometimes, there is also a small "Hasbro" or "© L.P.S." stamp. These markings are typically clear and easy to read. Fake LPS might have no numbers, incorrect numbers, or numbers that are blurry or raised instead of debossed. The quality of the stamp itself can be poor. It might be uneven or incomplete.

Finally, consider the overall shape and structure of the feet. They should be well-formed and proportionate to the body. Some fake LPS have misshapen feet. They might not stand steadily on a flat surface. Pay attention to how the pet stands. A wobbly stance can be a minor sign of a fake. Understanding these specific markings and physical attributes helps you confidently distinguish real from fake LPS.

### Comparing Sound and Feel: The Sensory Test for Authentic LPS

Beyond visual inspection, your senses of touch and hearing can provide valuable insights into an LPS's authenticity. This "sensory test" can help you tell if LPS are fake, especially when combined with other methods.

First, consider the weight of the toy. Authentic LPS toys have a certain heft to them. They feel solid in your hand. This is because they use quality plastic. Fake LPS often feel significantly lighter. This lighter weight indicates a cheaper, thinner plastic was used. Hold a known real LPS in one hand and a suspect one in the other. The difference in weight can be quite noticeable.

Next, feel the texture of the plastic. As mentioned before, real LPS have a smooth, almost slightly velvety feel. Some collectors describe it as a premium plastic feel. Fake LPS plastic can feel rough, gritty, or overly slick. It might also feel sticky or have an oily residue. Run your finger over the surface of the toy. Any unusual texture could be a sign of a fake.

Listen to the sound the head makes when it bobbles. A genuine LPS head makes a soft, subtle clicking or "springy" sound as it moves. This sound is part of the bobble mechanism. Fake LPS often produce a different sound, or no sound at all. The head might creak or squeak. This indicates poor internal components. These sensory details add to your ability to identify genuine LPS figures. ### Where to Buy: Minimizing Your Risk of Fake LPS

The best way to avoid fake LPS is to be careful about where you buy them. Your purchase source plays a huge role in the authenticity of your new pets. Knowing reputable sellers helps you minimize your risk and ensures you get real LPS.

Official retailers are always your safest bet. Stores like Target, Walmart, or major toy chains sell genuine, new LPS products directly from Hasbro. Online, Hasbro's official website or authorized sellers on large platforms are reliable. Buying new releases from these places guarantees authenticity.

When buying from secondary markets, exercise caution. Online marketplaces like eBay, Etsy, or Facebook Marketplace are popular for finding older or rare LPS. However, these platforms also have many fake listings. Always check the seller's reputation. Look at their feedback score and read reviews from other buyers. A seller with many negative reviews about product authenticity should be avoided.

Ask sellers for clear, detailed photos of the actual item. Do not rely on stock photos. Request pictures of the packaging, the feet, the eyes, and any other specific details you want to examine. If a deal seems too good to be true, it probably is. Very low prices on supposedly rare LPS are a common sign of counterfeits. Be wary of sellers who offer multiple "rare" LPS at unusually low prices. Prioritizing reputable sources significantly reduces your chances of encountering fake LPS. ### Authenticity Checklist: Your Go-To Guide for LPS Validation

Creating a personal authenticity checklist helps you systematically identify fake LPS. This method ensures you cover all important aspects during your inspection. I find it makes the process much clearer and more effective.

Start with the packaging. Is the logo clear? Are there any misspellings? Does the barcode look real? These are quick checks that can immediately flag a counterfeit. Move on to the figure's body. Feel the plastic quality. Is it smooth and solid, or rough and light? Check for obvious mold lines and excess plastic. These physical attributes are often dead giveaways.

Next, focus on the details. The eyes are critical. Look for the distinct pupil shape and the unique white reflections. Are the lines clean or blurry? Is the paint precise? Then, test the head bobble. It should be smooth and springy, not stiff or wobbly. A good bobble is a strong sign of authenticity.

Finally, examine the feet. Look for the correct magnet color and placement. Check for the engraved number or "Hasbro" stamp on the bottom. These small markings are hard for fakes to replicate perfectly. Use your senses: feel the weight, and listen to the bobble. By going through this checklist, you can confidently determine if your LPS is genuine or a fake.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: Why are there so many fake LPS toys on the market?**
A1: The high demand for both new and discontinued Littlest Pet Shop figures makes them a target for counterfeiters. These individuals produce cheaper, lower-quality versions to profit from the brand's popularity. They exploit collectors looking for rare or affordable options. This creates a large supply of non-authentic toys.

**Q2: Are all LPS without magnets fake?**
A2: No, not all LPS without magnets are fake. While many older LPS figures have foot magnets, Hasbro produced some lines without them. Later generations, especially those from 2012 onwards, often lack magnets. Always check other authenticity indicators like plastic quality, eye details, and markings.

**Q3: Can I get my money back if I accidentally buy a fake LPS?**
A3: Your ability to get your money back depends on where you bought the LPS. Reputable online marketplaces or sellers often have buyer protection policies for counterfeit items. Always contact the seller first. If that fails, escalate the issue through the platform's dispute resolution system. Keep all purchase records.

**Q4: Do fake LPS toys pose any safety risks?**
A4: Yes, fake LPS toys can pose safety risks. They often use cheaper, untested materials that may contain harmful chemicals or lead paint. Small parts on poorly made fakes can break off easily, presenting a choking hazard for young children. Authentic toys meet safety standards.

**Q5: How can I confirm the authenticity of a specific rare LPS figure?**
A5: To confirm a rare LPS figure's authenticity, compare it against detailed photos of known genuine versions. Use multiple reliable online resources and collector databases. Look for precise details like specific eye patterns, body molds, and unique markings. You can also consult experienced collectors in online communities for their expert opinion.

**Q6: Is it possible for authentic LPS to have minor flaws?**
A6: Yes, authentic LPS can have minor factory flaws. These are usually small imperfections like tiny paint specks or slightly imperfect seams. These differ from the widespread, significant defects found in fakes, such as completely wrong eye shapes, poor plastic quality, or extensive paint bleeding.
