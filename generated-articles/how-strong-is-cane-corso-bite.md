## Debunking Myths About Dog Bites and Cane Corsos

Many myths exist about dog bites, especially concerning powerful breeds like the Cane Corso. One common myth is the "lock jaw." This refers to a belief that some dogs, once they bite, cannot release their grip. This is not true. No dog breed has a biological "lock jaw" mechanism. All dogs release their bite voluntarily. The Cane Corso's strong grip is due to powerful muscles, not a locking jaw.

Another myth suggests that all large dogs are aggressive biters. This is also false. A dog's size or breed does not determine its aggression. Factors like training, socialization, genetics, and environment play a much bigger role. A well-trained and socialized Cane Corso is often a calm and loyal companion. Their powerful bite is a tool, not a sign of inherent aggression.

Some people believe that a strong bite automatically means a dangerous dog. This is an oversimplification. Any dog, regardless of breed, can bite under certain circumstances. A small dog can cause harm. The difference is the *severity* of the injury. A Cane Corso's bite can indeed cause more serious damage due to its force. However, this fact highlights the need for responsible ownership, not inherent danger.

It is important to understand the truth about dog behavior. This helps us avoid unfair stereotypes. We ensure proper care for all dogs. Educating people helps break down these myths. *   **"Lock Jaw" Myth:** No dog has a physiological "lock jaw." Their powerful grip comes from strong muscles.
