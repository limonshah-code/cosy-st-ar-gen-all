## Understanding the History of Littlest Pet Shop Series

To truly appreciate the return of Littlest Pet Shop, understanding its past is helpful. The brand has a long and varied history. It began in 1992 with Kenner. These early pets were very different from the bobble-heads most people remember. They were smaller and often came with playsets that had interactive elements.

When Hasbro took over, they really expanded the line. The most popular era, often called "Generation 2" or "G2," started in the early 2000s. ### Key Milestones in LPS Production

The journey of Littlest Pet Shop is marked by several important phases:

*   **1992-1996: Kenner's Original LPS.** These toys focused on realistic pet interaction. They had specific actions, like a dog that wags its tail.
*   **2004-2012: Hasbro's G2 Bobble-Heads.** This is the most iconic period for many. It introduced the famous bobble-head design. Thousands of unique pets were released, along with playsets, vehicles, and accessories.
*   **2012-2017: Hasbro's G3 & G4.** These generations tried new styles, moving away from the classic G2 look. G3 had bigger eyes, and G4 (called "LPS Popular") introduced a more animated, simplified design. This era also saw animated TV series and video games.
*   **2017-2018: Hasbro's G5 & G6 (Littlest Pet Shop: A World of Our Own).** These were the last Hasbro iterations. They featured very stylized, small figures tied to a new animated series. They did not achieve the same popularity as G2.
*   **2024 Onwards: MGA Entertainment's G7.** This marks the current return. MGA aims to recapture the charm of the most popular eras while adding its own touch. They have put a lot of thought into design and collectibility.

Each phase shows how toy companies tried to keep the brand fresh. Now, under MGA, we are seeing a conscious effort to bring back the elements that made LPS truly special for so many. This new chapter has many people excited about the future.

### Identifying Authentic LPS Figures

When collecting any toy, especially older ones, authenticity is a major concern. The market for vintage Littlest Pet Shop figures has seen a rise in counterfeit products. These fakes can look very similar to the real thing, making it tricky for new and experienced collectors alike. Learning to spot these differences is crucial to protecting your investment and ensuring you get genuine items.

Here are a few tips for identifying authentic LPS figures:

*   **Check the neck peg:** Genuine G2 LPS pets often have a clear, distinct neck peg. Fakes might have cloudy or poorly formed pegs.
*   **Look at the eyes:** Authentic LPS eyes are usually glossy and have crisp paint lines. Fakes can have dull eyes, blurry pupils, or smudged paint.
*   **Feel the plastic:** Original LPS toys have a specific plastic feel, usually smooth and somewhat firm. Counterfeits might feel cheaper, lighter, or sticky.
*   **Examine the paintwork:** Real LPS pets have precise paint applications. Look for clean lines, especially around the eyes and small details. Fakes often have sloppy paint or colors that look "off."
*   **Check the magnet (for G2):** Many G2 pets have a magnet in their paw. The color of the magnet (blue, red, or clear) can indicate their release year. Fakes might omit the magnet or use an incorrect color.
*   **Buy from reputable sellers:** This is perhaps the most important tip. Use trusted online marketplaces and sellers with good reviews. Ask for clear photos from all angles before buying.

Collecting should be fun, not frustrating. Taking these steps helps ensure that every pet you add to your collection is a genuine Littlest Pet Shop. This careful approach applies whether you are looking for rare vintage pieces or the latest MGA releases.
