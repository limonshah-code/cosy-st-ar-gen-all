## Common Problems with Maltese Shih Tzu Dogs

If you are thinking about bringing a Maltese Shih Tzu into your home, you are choosing a delightful companion. These charming, fluffy dogs, often called "Malshi" or "Malt-Tzu," combine the playful nature of the Shih Tzu with the gentle spirit of the Maltese. They are known for their loving personalities and compact size, making them popular choices for many households. However, like all dog breeds, Maltese Shih Tzus come with their own set of unique challenges. Understanding these common problems with Maltese Shih Tzu is crucial for any potential or current owner. This article will help you prepare for and manage typical health concerns, grooming needs, and behavioral quirks of this wonderful mix. We will explore everything from keeping their coat pristine to addressing their occasional stubbornness, ensuring your furry friend lives a long, happy, and healthy life.

### Takeaway

*   **Prioritize Regular Veterinary Care:** Schedule annual check-ups and act quickly on any health concerns.
*   **Maintain Daily Grooming:** Brush their coat daily to prevent mats and keep eyes and ears clean.
*   **Focus on Early Training:** Start house training and socialization early to prevent behavioral issues.
*   **Manage Diet and Exercise:** Provide a balanced diet and regular, moderate exercise to prevent obesity and ensure vitality.
*   **Understand Breed-Specific Tendencies:** Be aware of common health predispositions and behavioral patterns unique to Maltese Shih Tzus.

The common problems with Maltese Shih Tzu dogs often include a range of health issues such as dental disease, respiratory concerns, and patellar luxation, alongside significant grooming demands like frequent brushing to prevent matting, and behavioral challenges like house-training difficulties or separation anxiety. Owners must prepare for consistent care across all these areas.

## Health Concerns in Maltese Shih Tzu

Maltese Shih Tzus are generally healthy dogs, but they inherit predispositions to certain conditions from their parent breeds. Understanding these health concerns is the first step in providing good care. Many of these issues are common in small breeds. Early detection and prevention are key to managing these conditions effectively.

### Respiratory Issues

Both Maltese and Shih Tzus are brachycephalic breeds, meaning they have shorter snouts. This can lead to various respiratory problems. Their shortened airways can make breathing difficult, especially in warm weather or during strenuous exercise. You might notice heavy panting, snoring, or a snorting sound. I make sure my Malshi has a cool, quiet place to rest during hot days. Avoid over-exercising them and always use a harness instead of a collar to prevent pressure on their delicate windpipe. This small change makes a big difference in their comfort.

### Dental Disease

Small dogs are very prone to dental problems. Their teeth are often crowded, which makes them a magnet for plaque and tartar buildup. This can lead to gum disease, tooth decay, and even tooth loss. Bad breath is a common sign. I brush my Malshi's teeth every day, and we also give him dental chews. Regular professional cleanings at the vet are also very important to maintain good oral health. Neglecting dental care can lead to more severe health issues down the line.

### Eye Problems

Maltese Shih Tzus often have large, expressive eyes that are prone to several issues. These can include tear staining, cherry eye, and even progressive retinal atrophy. Their long facial hair can also irritate their eyes, leading to excessive tearing and infections. I gently wipe around my dog's eyes daily to remove tear stains and keep the fur trimmed away. If I notice redness or discharge, I always check with the vet immediately. Early intervention is best for [eye problems](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-kind-of-eye-problems-do-australian-shepherds-have).

### Patellar Luxation

Patellar luxation is a common orthopedic problem in small breeds. This condition occurs when the kneecap slips out of place. You might notice your dog skipping a step, hopping, or holding up a back leg. It can cause pain and eventually lead to arthritis. While some cases are mild and require no treatment, others might need surgery. I ensure my Malshi maintains a healthy weight to reduce stress on his joints. Regular, gentle exercise helps keep his muscles strong.

### Skin and Allergy Issues

Maltese Shih Tzus can develop skin allergies, often triggered by environmental factors or certain foods. Symptoms include itching, redness, hot spots, and ear infections. Their long coat can also trap moisture, leading to skin irritation if not properly dried. I pay close attention to my dog's skin when I groom him. If I notice excessive scratching, I discuss it with our veterinarian. Identifying the allergen is key to managing these conditions. For specific concerns about food, knowing about [most common food allergies in dogs](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-are-the-most-common-food-allergies-in-dogs) can be helpful.

## Grooming Challenges for Maltese Shih Tzu

The beautiful, flowing coat of the Maltese Shih Tzu is one of their most defining features. However, this non-shedding, double coat requires significant maintenance. Many owners opt for a shorter "puppy cut" to make grooming easier. Still, even with a shorter style, consistent grooming is essential to prevent painful mats and maintain skin health. I commit to a daily routine for my Malshi.

### Matting and Tangles

The Maltese Shih Tzu's long, silky hair is highly prone to matting and tangling. If left unchecked, mats can pull on the skin, causing pain, irritation, and even infections. Daily brushing is not just a suggestion; it is a necessity. I use a slicker brush and a metal comb to work through his coat carefully, section by section. Regular trips to a professional groomer every 4-6 weeks are also a must. This keeps his coat healthy and manageable. For ideas on coat care, considering the [best cut for a Shih Tzu](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-best-cut-for-a-shih-tzu) might offer insights.

### Tear Stains

Tear stains are a very common cosmetic issue for Maltese Shih Tzus. These reddish-brown marks appear under their eyes due to excessive tearing and a pigment in their tears. While mostly aesthetic, severe staining can indicate blocked tear ducts or irritation. I gently wipe around my dog's eyes daily with a damp cloth or a specialized tear stain remover. Keeping the hair around their eyes trimmed short also helps prevent moisture buildup. Consistency in this small task makes a big difference.

### Nail Trimming

Small dogs like the Maltese Shih Tzu often do not wear down their nails naturally as much as larger, more active breeds. Long nails can cause discomfort, make walking difficult, and even lead to paw problems or injuries. Regular nail trimming is important for their comfort and health. I trim my Malshi's nails every 3-4 weeks, using a small dog nail clipper. It took some practice, but now he tolerates it well. If you are unsure, your vet or groomer can demonstrate the correct technique.

### Ear Cleaning

Their floppy ears, especially when covered with long hair, can create a warm, moist environment perfect for yeast and bacterial infections. Regular ear cleaning is crucial to prevent these painful issues. I check my dog's ears weekly for redness, odor, or discharge. Then, I gently clean them with a vet-approved ear cleaner. I also pluck or trim excess hair from inside his ear canal. This helps with air circulation. If I notice any signs of infection, a vet visit is always my next step.

## Behavioral Quirks of Maltese Shih Tzu

Maltese Shih Tzus are known for their affectionate and playful temperaments. However, like all dogs, they can develop behavioral issues without proper socialization and training. Their small size does not mean they are exempt from needing clear boundaries and consistent guidance. Addressing these quirks early helps them become well-adjusted family members.

### Separation Anxiety

Many Maltese Shih Tzus bond very closely with their owners. This strong attachment can sometimes lead to separation anxiety when left alone. Symptoms can include excessive barking, destructive chewing, or accidents indoors. My Malshi gets very anxious when I leave. To help him, I started with short absences and gradually increased the time. I also make sure he has plenty of toys and puzzles to keep him busy. A consistent routine and a designated safe space can also provide comfort.

### House Training Difficulties

House training can be a challenge with small breeds like the Maltese Shih Tzu. Their tiny bladders mean they need more frequent potty breaks. They can also be subtle about needing to go, making accidents more common. Consistency and patience are key. I took my Malshi out very often, especially after waking up and after meals. Positive reinforcement, like praise and treats for going outside, works best. Crate training can also be an effective tool for teaching them where to relieve themselves.

### Excessive Barking

Maltese Shih Tzus can be prone to excessive barking. They might bark at strangers, other dogs, or simply to get attention. This can become a nuisance if not managed. While a certain amount of barking is normal for dogs, constant noise can be frustrating. I taught my Malshi a "quiet" command using positive reinforcement. Identifying the triggers for their barking is also important. Often, it is a cry for attention or a reaction to boredom. Providing enough mental and physical stimulation helps reduce this behavior.

### Stubbornness and Training

Despite their intelligence, Maltese Shih Tzus can sometimes show a stubborn streak. This might make training feel a bit slow at times. They are intelligent and eager to please, but their independent nature can come through. I found that short, fun training sessions work best. Positive reinforcement, like treats and praise, keeps them engaged. Consistency is also vital. Do not give up if they do not get it right away. Patience and a sense of humor will go a long way in their training journey.

## Dietary and Nutritional Considerations

Proper nutrition is fundamental to preventing many health problems in Maltese Shih Tzus. Due to their small size and potential for certain health conditions, their diet needs careful consideration. Feeding them the right food in the correct portions can significantly impact their overall well-being. I am always mindful of what my Malshi eats.

### Picky Eaters

Some Maltese Shih Tzus can be notoriously picky eaters. This can make meal times frustrating and lead to nutritional deficiencies if not addressed. They might turn their nose up at dry kibble or prefer human food. I found a high-quality small-breed kibble that my Malshi enjoys. Sticking to a consistent feeding schedule and avoiding giving too many table scraps can help. If they skip a meal, I do not offer alternatives immediately. They will usually eat when they are truly hungry.

### Weight Management

Obesity is a serious concern for small breeds. Maltese Shih Tzus can easily gain weight if overfed or under-exercised. Extra weight puts stress on their joints, aggravates respiratory issues, and can lead to other health problems like diabetes. I measure my Malshi's food carefully according to his activity level and vet's recommendations. Regular, moderate exercise, like daily walks, is also very important. Keeping them at a healthy weight is one of the best things you can do for their long-term health.

### Food Sensitivities

Like many small dogs, Maltese Shih Tzus can develop food sensitivities or allergies. These can manifest as skin issues (itching, hot spots), gastrointestinal problems (vomiting, diarrhea), or ear infections. Identifying the culprit ingredient is crucial. My vet suggested trying a limited ingredient diet to pinpoint any problematic foods. I always check ingredient labels carefully. Knowing the [common cause of diarrhea in dogs](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-most-common-cause-of-diarrhea-in-dogs) can help distinguish between a sensitivity and other issues.

### Dental Diet Considerations

Due to their predisposition to dental disease, choosing the right diet can support their oral health. Some kibbles are specially formulated to help clean teeth as the dog chews. I also incorporate dental chews and raw bones (appropriate for their size) into my Malshi's routine. These can help reduce plaque buildup between brushings. Always discuss specific dental diet options with your veterinarian to ensure they are suitable for your dog's individual needs. Good nutrition plays a vital role in preventing severe dental issues.

## Preventing Common Problems

Prevention is always better than cure when it comes to dog health and well-being. For Maltese Shih Tzus, a proactive approach to their care can significantly reduce the likelihood of common problems arising. It involves a combination of routine veterinary care, consistent home care, and attentive observation. I believe investing time in prevention pays off immensely in the long run.

### Regular Vet Check-ups

Annual veterinary check-ups are absolutely essential for Maltese Shih Tzus. These visits allow your vet to catch potential health problems early, often before symptoms become severe. During these check-ups, my vet performs a thorough physical examination, checks his teeth, and discusses any concerns I have. They can also administer necessary vaccinations and parasite prevention. Early detection of conditions like patellar luxation or heart murmurs can lead to more effective treatment outcomes and improve their quality of life. Understanding potential [causes of death in Maltese dogs](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-cause-of-death-of-maltese-dogs) can highlight the importance of these regular visits.

### Consistent Training and Socialization

Starting training and socialization early is key to preventing behavioral problems. A well-socialized Maltese Shih Tzu is less likely to develop separation anxiety, excessive barking, or fear-based aggression. I enrolled my Malshi in puppy classes. These classes taught him basic commands and exposed him to other dogs and people in a controlled environment. Consistent, positive reinforcement training at home reinforces good behavior. This also builds a strong bond between you and your dog.

### Daily Grooming Routine

A daily grooming routine is non-negotiable for a Maltese Shih Tzu. This prevents mats, keeps their eyes and ears clean, and maintains skin health. I dedicate about 15-20 minutes each day to brush my Malshi's coat, clean his eyes, and check his ears. This routine is not just about aesthetics; it is a vital part of their health care. It also provides an opportunity to check for any new lumps, bumps, or skin irritations. Regular professional grooming appointments supplement this daily care.

### Balanced Diet and Exercise

Providing a balanced, high-quality diet and ensuring regular, moderate exercise are crucial for preventing weight-related issues and supporting overall health. I choose dog food specifically formulated for small breeds. This food meets their unique nutritional needs. Daily walks, even short ones, help maintain muscle tone and manage weight. Playtime indoors also counts as exercise. Avoiding overfeeding and providing appropriate exercise prevents stress on their joints and respiratory system.

### Creating a Safe and Stimulating Environment

A safe and stimulating home environment helps prevent boredom and destructive behaviors. Maltese Shih Tzus are curious and intelligent dogs. Provide them with a variety of toys, including puzzle toys that challenge their minds. Ensure your home is dog-proofed to prevent accidents and access to dangerous items. A comfortable bed and a designated safe space, like a crate, can also reduce anxiety. A happy dog is a well-behaved dog.

## FAQ Section

### Are Maltese Shih Tzu dogs high-maintenance?
Yes, Maltese Shih Tzus are generally considered high-maintenance, especially concerning their grooming needs. Their long, non-shedding coats require daily brushing to prevent matting. They also need frequent professional grooming appointments. Additionally, they often require consistent attention for potential health issues related to their brachycephalic features and small size, such as dental and respiratory care.

### What are common health issues for Maltese Shih Tzu?
Common health issues for Maltese Shih Tzus include respiratory problems due to their short snouts, dental disease, eye conditions like tear staining and cherry eye, and patellar luxation. They can also be prone to skin allergies and sensitivities. Regular veterinary check-ups and attentive care are vital to manage these potential health concerns effectively throughout their lives.

### Do Maltese Shih Tzus bark a lot?
Maltese Shih Tzus can be prone to excessive barking. They may bark at strangers, other dogs, or when they seek attention or feel anxious. This tendency can be managed with consistent training and proper socialization from a young age. Identifying the triggers for their barking and providing sufficient mental and physical stimulation can help reduce unwanted vocalization.

### How often should I groom my Maltese Shih Tzu?
You should brush your Maltese Shih Tzu's coat daily to prevent mats and tangles. Professional grooming every 4-6 weeks is also recommended to maintain coat length, trim nails, and clean ears. Daily care also includes wiping their eyes to prevent tear stains and brushing their teeth. Consistent grooming is vital for their health and comfort.

### Are Maltese Shih Tzus good with kids?
Maltese Shih Tzus can be good with older, respectful children. Their small size makes them vulnerable to accidental injury by very young children who may not understand how to interact gently with a small dog. They thrive in homes where children are taught to be calm and gentle. Supervision is always recommended to ensure positive interactions for both the dog and the child.
