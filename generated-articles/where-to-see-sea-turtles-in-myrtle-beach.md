## FAQ Section

**Q1: What is the best time to see wild sea turtles in Myrtle Beach?**
A1: The best time to potentially see wild sea turtles in Myrtle Beach is during the nesting and hatching season. This runs from May through October. Females nest at night. Hatchlings emerge mostly at night or early morning. Your chances are low, but early morning beach walks in protected areas, like Myrtle Beach State Park, offer the best opportunity.

**Q2: Can I touch a sea turtle if I see one on the beach?**
A2: No, absolutely not. It is illegal to touch, harass, or disturb sea turtles in the United States. All sea turtles are protected by federal and state laws, including the Endangered Species Act. Touching them can cause stress, disorient them, and transmit diseases. Maintain a safe distance of at least 20 feet.

**Q3: Where do sea turtles primarily nest in Myrtle Beach?**
A3: Sea turtles primarily nest on less-developed and protected stretches of beach in the Myrtle Beach area. Myrtle Beach State Park is a significant nesting site. Conservation groups and volunteers regularly monitor and mark nests there. Look for marked areas and always respect the boundaries to avoid disturbance.

**Q4: How can I help protect sea turtles in Myrtle Beach?**
A4: You can help by keeping beaches clean, especially removing plastic and fishing lines. Turn off beachfront lights at night during nesting season. Report any injured or stranded turtles to local authorities. Never disturb nests or approach wild turtles. Support local sea turtle conservation organizations through donations or volunteering.

**Q5: Are sea turtle sightings common in Myrtle Beach?**
A5: Wild sea turtle sightings are relatively uncommon in Myrtle Beach. These creatures are elusive. They spend most of their lives at sea. Nesting females come ashore rarely. Hatchlings emerge briefly. For guaranteed sightings, Ripley's Aquarium of Myrtle Beach is your best bet.

**Q6: What should I do if I find a stranded or injured sea turtle?**
A6: If you find a stranded, injured, or dead sea turtle, do not approach or touch it.
