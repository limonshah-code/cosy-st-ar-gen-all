## FAQ Section

### What is the most widely accepted theory for the Chihuahua's origin?
The most widely accepted theory suggests the Chihuahua descended primarily from the Techichi. This small companion dog lived in ancient Mexico with the Toltec and Aztec civilizations. Archaeological evidence, including skeletal remains and artwork, supports this connection. European toy breeds likely influenced their later development.

### Did Native Americans keep the Chihuahua breed?
Yes, the ancestors of the Chihuahua, the Techichi, were indeed kept by ancient Mexican civilizations like the Toltec and Aztec. These dogs were integral to their daily lives and spiritual practices. They were valued as companions and believed to guide souls in the afterlife.

### Are Chihuahuas naturally small, or were they bred to be tiny?
Chihuahuas are naturally small. Their ancient ancestor, the Techichi, was also a small dog. Over centuries, selective breeding by humans likely refined their size further. This made them even more compact. Their inherent small stature is a hallmark of the breed's history.

### What is the difference between an apple head and a deer head Chihuahua?
An apple head Chihuahua has a distinctively rounded, apple-shaped skull and often bulging eyes. A deer head Chihuahua has a more sloped forehead and a head shape resembling a young deer. Both are recognized purebred Chihuahuas, representing variations within the breed.

### How do modern Chihuahuas compare to their ancient ancestors?
Modern Chihuahuas are likely smaller and more refined than their Techichi ancestors. Genetic and archaeological evidence suggests the Techichi was slightly larger and perhaps less delicate. However, the core characteristics of being a small, alert companion dog have persisted through generations.

### Can genetic testing really tell what breeds make up a dog?
Yes, genetic testing can provide insights into a dog's breed makeup. DNA tests compare a dog's genetic markers to known breed databases. This helps identify ancestral breeds and their proportions. While not always exact for very ancient mixes, it offers valuable information.
