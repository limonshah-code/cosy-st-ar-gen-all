### Differentiating Kennel Cough from Other Chihuahua Coughs

It is important to know that not all coughs in Chihuahuas are kennel cough. Their small size and unique anatomy mean they can make other strange sounds. For example, Chihuahuas are prone to reverse sneezing. This sounds very different from a kennel cough, but can be alarming.

A reverse sneeze sounds like a sudden, rapid inhalation, often with a snorting noise. Your Chihuahua will stand still, extend their neck, and make a loud, gasping sound. This is usually caused by an irritation in the nasal passages. It is not a cough, and your dog is not trying to expel anything. While it looks scary, a reverse sneeze is usually harmless and passes quickly. Knowing [why does my Chihuahua sound like he's coughing up a hairball](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-does-my-chihuahua-sound-like-hes-coughing-up-a-hairball) can help differentiate between a foreign object sensation and a respiratory infection.

Another condition to consider is a collapsing trachea. Chihuahuas are a breed prone to this issue. This happens when the rings of the windpipe weaken and flatten. A collapsing trachea cough often sounds like a dry, harsh goose honk, similar to kennel cough. However, it is usually chronic and triggered by excitement or pressure on the neck. It does not come with other sickness symptoms like fever or lethargy. A veterinarian can diagnose a collapsing trachea through X-rays.

Heart conditions can also cause a cough in older Chihuahuas. A heart cough is often soft and wet, especially at night or when resting. It sounds more like a gentle choke or gurgle. This cough comes from fluid buildup in the lungs due to heart failure. It is important to consult a vet if you are unsure of the cough's origin. Correct diagnosis ensures proper treatment for your tiny companion.
