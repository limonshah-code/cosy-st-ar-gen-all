## Optimal Feeding Schedule: How Often Should You Feed A Leopard Gecko?

Bringing a leopard gecko into your home is an exciting step. These fascinating reptiles make wonderful pets, known for their unique personalities and relatively simple care requirements. However, one of the most common questions new owners have is: "How often should you feed a leopard gecko?" Establishing the right feeding schedule is important for your gecko's health and happiness. Proper nutrition directly impacts their growth, energy levels, and long-term well-being.

In this article, I will explain the best feeding practices for leopard geckos at different life stages. We will look at what food items are best and how to offer them. We will also cover common feeding mistakes and how to spot signs of a healthy gecko. By the end, you will feel confident in providing the best diet for your scaly friend.

### Takeaway

*   **Hatchlings (0-3 months):** Feed daily.
*   **Juveniles (3-12 months):** Feed every other day.
*   **Adults (12+ months):** Feed 2-3 times per week.
*   Offer appropriately sized live insects.
*   Always dust food with calcium and vitamin supplements.
*   Monitor your gecko's weight and activity.

### Clear, Concise Answer

The frequency of feeding a leopard gecko depends on its age. Hatchlings need food daily, juveniles every other day, and adults 2-3 times per week. Always offer appropriate live insect prey items. Dust these insects with calcium and vitamin supplements to support your gecko's overall health and prevent deficiencies.

## Understanding Leopard Gecko Feeding Needs by Age

Leopard geckos have different nutritional requirements as they grow. A hatchling gecko eats much more often than an adult. This is because young geckos are growing rapidly. They need a lot of energy and nutrients to develop properly. As they get older, their growth rate slows down, and their metabolism changes. This means they need food less frequently. I always make sure to adjust my gecko's feeding schedule as it ages.

### Hatchling Leopard Geckos (0-3 Months Old)

Young leopard geckos, often called hatchlings, are like little eating machines. They grow very fast during their first few months of life. Their bodies are busy building bones and muscle. Because of this rapid development, hatchlings need a consistent supply of nutrients. I feed my hatchlings every single day. This daily feeding helps ensure they get enough energy to grow strong.

When feeding hatchlings, it is important to offer small insects. The general rule is that the food item should be no larger than the space between their eyes. Small crickets or appropriately sized [dubia roaches](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-dubia-roaches-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) are good choices. You can offer 5-7 appropriately sized insects at each feeding. Always remove any uneaten prey after about 15-20 minutes to prevent stress or injury to your gecko. I also make sure to dust their food with calcium powder with D3 at every feeding, and a multivitamin once a week. This ensures they absorb calcium properly for bone health.

### Juvenile Leopard Geckos (3-12 Months Old)

As a leopard gecko moves from a hatchling to a juvenile, its growth rate begins to slow slightly. They are still growing, but not as quickly as before. This means they do not need to eat every day. I typically transition my juvenile geckos to an every-other-day feeding schedule. This still provides enough food for their continued development without overfeeding. Overfeeding can lead to obesity, which is unhealthy for geckos.

For juvenile geckos, you can offer slightly larger insects. Crickets, small mealworms, and [small superworms](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-superworms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) are suitable. I usually give them 6-8 insects per feeding, again, ensuring they are not too large for the gecko to easily swallow. It is important to continue dusting their food. I use calcium with D3 at every other feeding, and a multivitamin supplement 1-2 times a week. Observing your gecko's body condition is important. If they seem too thin, you might increase the amount or frequency slightly. If they look chunky, reduce it.

### Adult Leopard Geckos (Over 12 Months Old)

Once a leopard gecko reaches about 12 months of age, it is generally considered an adult. At this stage, their growth has mostly stopped. Their metabolism is slower, so they need even less frequent feedings. I find that feeding adult leopard geckos 2-3 times per week is ideal. This schedule helps maintain a healthy weight without leading to obesity. Obesity can cause many health problems for geckos.

For adult geckos, you can offer larger insects. Crickets, medium mealworms, [wax worms](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-wax-worms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) (as occasional treats), and dubia roaches are good staple foods. I usually offer 6-10 appropriately sized insects per feeding. The exact number can vary based on the gecko's individual metabolism and activity level. Some geckos are more active than others. Continue to dust food with calcium with D3 2-3 times a week and a multivitamin once a week. I always make sure to offer a variety of insects to ensure a balanced diet.

## Choosing the Right Food for Your Leopard Gecko

Leopard geckos are insectivores. This means they eat insects. Providing a varied diet is very important for their health. Just like humans, geckos need different nutrients from different food sources. Feeding only one type of insect can lead to nutritional deficiencies over time. I always offer a mix of staple insects and occasional treats to ensure my geckos get all they need.

### Staple Insects

Staple insects form the main part of your leopard gecko's diet. These are insects that provide good nutrition and can be fed regularly.

*   **Crickets:** Crickets are a classic choice for leopard geckos. They are readily available and provide good protein. I always gut-load crickets for at least 24 hours before feeding them. Gut-loading means feeding the crickets nutritious food so your gecko gets the benefits.
*   **Dubia Roaches:** Dubia roaches are excellent staple insects. They are highly nutritious, easy to keep, and do not smell. They also cannot climb smooth surfaces or fly, making them easy to contain. I often use dubia roaches as a primary food source for my geckos because of their nutritional value. You can learn more about feeding quantities at [how many dubia roaches to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-dubia-roaches-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko).
*   **Mealworms:** Mealworms are another common staple. They are easy to digest for many geckos. However, their chitinous exoskeleton can be harder for some geckos to process. I offer mealworms as part of a varied diet, but not as the sole food source. Always ensure they are fresh and healthy.
*   **Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL):** BSFL are becoming increasingly popular. They are high in calcium and relatively low in fat. This makes them an excellent choice, especially for younger geckos. I find that my geckos enjoy these small, wiggly larvae. They are a great addition to any gecko diet.

### Occasional Treats

Treat insects should be offered sparingly. They are usually higher in fat or less nutritious than staple insects. Think of them like candy for your gecko.

*   **Wax Worms:** Wax worms are very fatty and delicious to geckos. They are like a special dessert. I only offer wax worms occasionally, maybe once or twice a month, and only 1-2 at a time. Overfeeding wax worms can lead to obesity and liver problems. Learn more about the right amount at [how many wax worms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-wax-worms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko).
*   **Superworms:** Superworms are larger and have a higher fat content than mealworms. They are also a good source of protein. I offer superworms as an occasional treat for adult geckos. Similar to wax worms, moderation is key to prevent your gecko from becoming overweight. Consider the appropriate amount by checking [how many superworms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-superworms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko).
*   **Silkworms:** Silkworms are a very nutritious treat, high in calcium and protein. They are also soft-bodied, making them easy to digest. I sometimes offer silkworms as a treat, especially if my gecko needs a little extra nutrition. They are a good choice for geckos that might be a bit underweight.

## The Importance of Supplementation for Leopard Geckos

Providing insects alone is not enough for a healthy leopard gecko. Wild geckos eat a variety of insects that have naturally occurring vitamins and minerals from their diet. Captive-bred insects often lack these essential nutrients. This is why supplementation is extremely important. Without proper supplements, geckos can develop serious health issues like metabolic bone disease (MBD). I always make sure to dust my gecko's food before offering it.

### Calcium with D3

Calcium is vital for strong bones and proper bodily functions. Leopard geckos need Vitamin D3 to absorb calcium from their diet. Since they do not get natural sunlight indoors, they cannot produce enough D3 on their own.

*   **Frequency:** I dust my geckos' staple insects with calcium powder containing D3 at most feedings. For hatchlings, I dust daily. For juveniles, every other feeding. For adults, 2-3 times per week.
*   **Method:** Place the insects in a small bag or container with a pinch of calcium powder. Gently shake until the insects are coated. Then offer them to your gecko.
*   **Dish:** I also keep a small, shallow dish of pure calcium powder (without D3) in the gecko's enclosure at all times. This allows the gecko to self-regulate and lick up extra calcium if it feels the need.

### Multivitamin Supplement

A multivitamin supplement provides other essential vitamins and minerals that calcium with D3 alone does not. These include Vitamin A, Vitamin E, and various B vitamins, which are crucial for eye health, skin health, and overall immune function.

*   **Frequency:** I use a multivitamin supplement containing Vitamin A (preformed Vitamin A is best) once or twice a week. It is important not to over-supplement, as some vitamins can be toxic in high amounts.
*   **Method:** Similar to calcium, dust the insects lightly with the multivitamin powder. Make sure not to overdo it.
*   **Rotation:** I often rotate my supplement schedule. For example, on a feeding day, I might use calcium with D3. On the next feeding day, I might use the multivitamin. This ensures a balanced intake of all necessary nutrients.

## Feeding Techniques and Best Practices

How you offer food to your leopard gecko is almost as important as what you offer. Proper feeding techniques ensure your gecko eats safely and efficiently. I have learned over time that consistency and a calm approach work best. It is also important to observe your gecko during feeding. This helps you understand its preferences and eating habits.

### Hand-Feeding vs. Bowl Feeding

You have a few options for how to offer insects to your gecko.

*   **Hand-Feeding/Tongs:** I often use long feeding tongs to offer insects directly to my gecko. This allows me to control how many insects my gecko eats and ensures each insect is dusted with supplements. It also helps me bond with my gecko. This method prevents insects from hiding in the enclosure, where they might stress the gecko or go uneaten.
*   **Bowl Feeding:** For certain insects like mealworms or dubia roaches, you can use a smooth-sided, escape-proof bowl. This is a good option if you want to leave food available for a short period. However, it is harder to ensure each insect is properly dusted. I typically use bowls for insects that are slower or cannot escape easily. Always remove uneaten insects from the bowl after a few hours.

### Gut-Loading Insects

Gut-loading is crucial. It means feeding the feeder insects a nutritious diet before you give them to your gecko. The nutrients from the insects' food then transfer to your gecko.

*   **What to Feed Feeder Insects:** I feed my crickets and roaches a mix of fresh vegetables (carrots, leafy greens), fruits (apple slices), and a commercial gut-load diet.
*   **Timeframe:** I gut-load insects for at least 24-48 hours before feeding them to my geckos. This ensures they are full of nutrients.

### Water Availability

Freshwater must always be available to your leopard gecko. They drink by licking water droplets or from a shallow water dish.

*   **Water Dish:** I keep a shallow, heavy water dish in the enclosure at all times. This prevents spills and ensures constant access to water.
*   **Cleaning:** I clean and refill the water dish daily to prevent bacterial growth. Clean water is just as important as clean food for your gecko's health.

### When to Feed Your Leopard Gecko

Leopard geckos are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk. This is when they would naturally hunt in the wild.

*   **Optimal Time:** I usually feed my leopard geckos in the late afternoon or early evening. This aligns with their natural activity cycle. Feeding them at this time also gives them time to digest their meal before they become fully active at night. ## Recognizing Signs of Healthy and Unhealthy Feeding

Observing your leopard gecko's physical condition and behavior is key to ensuring its diet is correct. A healthy gecko shows certain characteristics, while an unhealthy one might display warning signs. I pay close attention to my geckos to catch any issues early. This proactive approach helps keep them thriving.

### Signs of a Healthy, Well-Fed Gecko

A leopard gecko that receives proper nutrition and a good feeding schedule will exhibit several signs of good health.

*   **Healthy Tail:** The tail is a major indicator of a gecko's health. A healthy leopard gecko stores fat in its tail. A well-fed gecko will have a thick, plump tail, about as wide as its head. It should taper down gradually to a point.
*   **Clear Eyes:** Healthy geckos have clear, bright eyes. There should be no discharge, swelling, or cloudiness. Their eyes should be alert and responsive.
*   **Active and Alert:** A well-fed gecko is usually active during its waking hours. It will explore its enclosure, climb, and be generally curious. Lethargy can be a sign of illness or underfeeding.
*   **Shedding Regularly:** Healthy geckos shed their skin regularly, usually every few weeks. The skin comes off in one piece. This is a sign of proper growth and nutrient absorption.
*   **Firm Stool:** Your gecko's droppings should be firm, cylindrical, and have a white urate cap. Loose or runny stools can indicate digestive issues or improper diet. I check their waste regularly during tank cleaning.

### Signs of Underfeeding or Overfeeding

Both underfeeding and overfeeding can lead to serious health problems for your leopard gecko.

*   **Underfeeding Signs:**
    *   **Thin Tail:** A tail that is thinner than the gecko's neck is a clear sign of undernourishment. The gecko is not getting enough fat reserves.
    *   **Prominent Bones:** You might see their hip bones or spine becoming very visible. This indicates a lack of body fat.
    *   **Lethargy:** An underfed gecko will often be less active and may appear weak. They might not have the energy to hunt or explore.
    *   **Weight Loss:** Any significant, unexplained weight loss is a serious concern. I weigh my geckos occasionally to monitor their progress. If your leopard gecko is refusing food, consider checking [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating). Also, understand that a gecko can go without food for some time; information on [how long can a leopard gecko go without eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-long-can-a-leopard-gecko-go-without-eating) might be helpful in such situations.
*   **Overfeeding Signs:**
    *   **Obesity:** The gecko will appear very round and plump, with fat deposits visible in areas other than the tail, such as around the armpits or belly.
    *   **Very Thick Tail:** While a thick tail is good, an excessively thick, lumpy tail can indicate too much fat.
    *   **Lethargy:** Overfed geckos can also become lethargic due to their heavy body weight. They might show less interest in hunting.
    *   **Digestive Issues:** Constant loose stools or regurgitation can sometimes be a sign of overfeeding or an improper diet.

Adjusting the feeding schedule and food quantity based on these observations is crucial. Remember, every gecko is an individual, and their needs may vary slightly.

## Common Feeding Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced owners can make mistakes. Being aware of common feeding errors helps ensure your leopard gecko stays healthy. I have learned from my own experiences and from others what to avoid. It is all part of becoming a good gecko parent.

### Not Dusting Food

This is one of the most critical mistakes. As mentioned, feeder insects lack sufficient calcium and D3. Without these supplements, your gecko is at high risk of developing Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD). MBD is a painful and often irreversible condition that weakens bones and can deform the gecko. It is vital to dust every time, as suggested.

### Feeding Wild-Caught Insects

Never feed your leopard gecko insects caught from outside. Wild insects can carry parasites, pesticides, and diseases. These can be very harmful or even fatal to your gecko. Always buy feeder insects from reputable pet stores or breeders. These sources ensure the insects are clean and safe.

### Offering Incorrect Food Size

Feeding insects that are too large for your gecko can cause serious problems. It can lead to impaction, meaning the gecko cannot digest or pass the food. This can be life-threatening. The general rule is that the feeder insect should be no longer than the space between your gecko's eyes. Always size your feeder insects correctly based on your gecko's age and size.

### Not Gut-Loading Insects

Ignoring gut-loading means your gecko misses out on vital nutrients. Feeder insects, even healthy ones, are just carriers. Their nutritional value depends on what they eat. A "gut-loaded" insect is like a living vitamin pill for your gecko. Always feed your feeder insects a healthy diet for at least 24 hours before offering them to your gecko.

### Over-Reliance on One Food Type

While it is easy to stick to one type of insect, a varied diet is best. Feeding only crickets or only mealworms can lead to nutritional imbalances over time. Different insects offer different nutrient profiles. Offering a variety of staple insects ensures your gecko gets a wider range of vitamins and minerals. Think of it like humans eating a balanced diet with different food groups.

### Overfeeding or Underfeeding

Consistently giving too much or too little food leads to obesity or malnutrition. Both compromise your gecko's health and lifespan. Monitor your gecko's weight and body condition regularly. Adjust the amount and frequency of feeding as needed. If you notice a very thin tail or excessive fat deposits, it is time to change the feeding plan. ## Creating a Consistent Feeding Routine

Consistency in feeding is not just about the schedule; it is also about the environment. A stable environment helps your gecko feel secure. This promotes good eating habits and overall health. I always aim for a predictable routine to minimize stress for my geckos.

### Setting Up the Feeding Area

Your gecko's enclosure should support healthy eating. This means providing proper heat and hiding spots.

*   **Heat Gradient:** Leopard geckos need a temperature gradient in their enclosure. This means one side is warmer than the other. The warm side allows them to properly digest their food. I always ensure my heating mat or under tank heater provides a belly heat source. Without proper heat, geckos cannot digest food efficiently.
*   **Hiding Spots:** Geckos need secure hiding spots to feel safe. A stressed gecko may not eat well. I always provide at least three hides: a warm hide, a cool hide, and a moist hide. These hides give them security and regulate their body temperature.
*   **Substrate:** The type of substrate you use can also affect feeding. Loose substrates like sand can be dangerous if ingested during feeding, leading to impaction. I prefer solid substrates like paper towels or reptile carpet for feeding, especially for young geckos. If using loose substrate, I feed my geckos in a separate container or on a solid surface.

### Monitoring and Adjusting

Your gecko's needs can change over time. Being observant is crucial for long-term health.

*   **Weight Checks:** I occasionally weigh my leopard geckos to track their growth and weight stability. A kitchen scale works well for this. Consistent weight (for adults) or steady growth (for juveniles) indicates a good diet.
*   **Activity Levels:** A healthy gecko is active, especially in the evenings. If your gecko becomes unusually lethargic or spends all its time hiding, it might be a sign of a problem.
*   **Shedding Cycle:** Geckos eat their shed skin for nutrients. Regular, easy shedding indicates good health. If your gecko struggles to shed, it might point to humidity issues or nutritional deficiencies.
*   **Food Refusal:** Sometimes, geckos will refuse food. This can be normal for a short period, especially during shedding or if they are stressed. However, prolonged food refusal needs attention. Understanding [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating) is very helpful. If food refusal lasts more than a few days (for an adult), or a day (for a hatchling), it is wise to consult a reptile vet.

### The Role of Metabolism and Activity

A gecko's individual metabolism and activity level influence its feeding needs. Some geckos are naturally more active than others. An active gecko burns more calories. This gecko might need slightly more food or more frequent feedings than a less active one. Conversely, a very sedentary gecko could gain weight easily if fed too much. Observe your gecko's energy and adjust accordingly. This personalized approach ensures your gecko gets exactly what it needs to thrive.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Leopard Gecko Feeding

### How many crickets should I feed my leopard gecko?

The number of crickets depends on the gecko's age and size. Hatchlings can eat 5-7 small crickets daily. Juveniles might eat 6-8 medium crickets every other day. Adults typically consume 6-10 medium-large crickets 2-3 times per week. Always ensure the crickets are no bigger than the space between your gecko's eyes to prevent choking or impaction.

### Can I feed my leopard gecko once a week?

Feeding an adult leopard gecko once a week can be sufficient if you provide a larger meal. However, most experts recommend feeding adults 2-3 times per week to ensure consistent nutrient intake and maintain a healthy weight. Hatchlings and juveniles need food more often. For detailed information, see [can I feed my leopard gecko once a week](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-i-feed-my-leopard-gecko-once-a-week).

### What if my leopard gecko stops eating?

If your leopard gecko stops eating, it can be concerning. Common reasons include shedding, stress, improper temperatures, impaction, or illness. First, check your enclosure's temperature and humidity. Offer food with tongs to see if they are interested. If refusal persists for more than a few days (adults) or a day (hatchlings), or if other symptoms appear, consult a reptile veterinarian.

### Do leopard geckos need to drink water?

Yes, leopard geckos absolutely need to drink water. They get some moisture from their food, but they also need a constant source of fresh, clean water. Always provide a shallow, heavy water dish in their enclosure. Change the water daily to keep it fresh and clean. This is vital for their hydration and overall health.

### How do I know if my leopard gecko is overweight?

An overweight leopard gecko will appear very round and plump. They may have noticeable fat deposits in their armpits, along their belly, or in their neck area. While a thick tail is healthy, an excessively large, lumpy tail can also indicate obesity. Adjusting their feeding frequency and offering leaner staple insects helps manage their weight.

### Can leopard geckos eat fruits and vegetables?

No, leopard geckos are insectivores. Their digestive systems are designed to process insects only. They cannot properly digest fruits, vegetables, or other plant matter. Offering these foods can lead to digestive upset, impaction, and nutritional deficiencies. Stick to a diet of appropriate live feeder insects, gut-loaded and dusted with supplements.
