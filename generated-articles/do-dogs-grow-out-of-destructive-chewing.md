## Do Dogs Grow Out Of Destructive Chewing?

Coming home to chewed-up furniture or destroyed shoes is never a pleasant experience. Many dog owners face this frustrating problem. You might wonder if your furry friend will ever stop this behavior. It is common to hope that dogs simply grow out of destructive chewing. This article explores the root causes of destructive chewing and provides actionable steps to guide your dog toward appropriate chewing habits.

This comprehensive guide will explain why dogs chew. We will cover the role of age, from puppyhood to adulthood. We will also discuss effective training methods and environmental management. My goal is to help you create a harmonious home where both you and your dog can thrive. Let us dive into understanding and managing this common canine behavior.

### Takeaway:

*   **Destructive chewing** is a behavior that dogs do not always outgrow.
*   **Puppy teething** is a natural phase, but adult chewing often indicates underlying issues.
*   **Identify the cause** (boredom, anxiety, lack of exercise) to address chewing effectively.
*   **Provide appropriate chew toys** and rotate them to maintain interest.
*   **Implement consistent training** using positive reinforcement.
*   **Dog-proof your home** to prevent access to forbidden items.
*   **Seek professional help** from a vet or behaviorist for persistent or severe chewing.

No, dogs do not always grow out of destructive chewing on their own. While puppies often reduce chewing after teething, adult dogs can continue or develop destructive habits due to boredom, anxiety, lack of exercise, or insufficient appropriate chew outlets. Owners must address the root cause and provide proper training and management.

### Understanding Destructive Chewing: Is It a Phase?

Many dog owners hope that their beloved pet will simply stop destructive chewing as they age. This is often true for puppies going through their teething phase. Puppies explore the world with their mouths. They also need to relieve discomfort when new teeth emerge. This natural process usually subsides around six to eight months of age.

However, chewing can continue well into adulthood for many reasons. If a dog chews destructively as an adult, it is rarely just a phase. It signals an unmet need or a behavioral issue. Ignoring it will not make it disappear. Instead, the behavior might become more ingrained. Understanding the specific reasons behind your dog's chewing is the first step toward finding a solution. We must teach them appropriate behaviors.

### Why Dogs Chew: Unpacking the Instincts Behind Destructive Behavior

Dogs chew for many reasons. It is a natural and essential behavior for them. Chewing helps dogs explore their environment. It also helps them relieve stress and keep their jaws strong. When chewing becomes destructive, it is a sign that something is amiss. We need to identify the specific trigger.

One common reason is boredom. Dogs need mental and physical stimulation. A dog with too much energy or not enough to do will find its own entertainment. This often means chewing on household items. Anxiety is another major factor. Dogs experiencing separation anxiety or general stress may chew to self-soothe. Some dogs also chew due to hunger or nutritional deficiencies, although this is less common. Understanding these root causes helps us address the behavior effectively.

#### Boredom and Lack of Stimulation

A bored dog is a mischievous dog. Dogs need activity to stay happy and well-behaved. Without enough exercise or mental challenges, they often turn to chewing. This helps them release pent-up energy. They may also do it to entertain themselves.

Consider how much exercise your dog gets daily. Also, think about their mental stimulation. Interactive toys, puzzle feeders, and training sessions can prevent boredom. Ensure your dog receives adequate physical activity for its breed and age. A tired dog is less likely to chew on your furniture.

#### Anxiety and Stress

Anxiety is a significant cause of destructive chewing. Dogs experiencing stress often chew to cope. This can be separation anxiety when you leave them alone. It can also be generalized anxiety from changes in their environment. Look for other signs of anxiety. These include pacing, whining, or excessive drooling.

Helping your dog manage anxiety is key to stopping the chewing. This might involve counter-conditioning for separation anxiety. It could also mean creating a safe space for them. Consult a veterinarian or a certified dog behaviorist if anxiety is severe. They can offer specific strategies.

#### Puppy Teething and Exploration

Puppies chew intensely during their teething phase. This is a normal part of their development. They chew to relieve discomfort in their gums. They also use their mouths to explore the world around them. This stage typically lasts from three to six months of age. Provide many appropriate chew toys during this time.

It is vital to redirect their chewing onto these toys. If puppies are not taught what is okay to chew, they will choose on their own. This sets a bad precedent for future chewing habits. Always keep plenty of safe, durable puppy chew toys available.

### The Age Factor: When Does Chewing Change?

The "growing out of it" question mostly applies to puppies. Puppy chewing, driven by teething and exploration, often decreases naturally. Most puppies complete teething by six to eight months. At this point, the intense need to chew for discomfort relief lessens. However, some chewing will always be a part of their lives. It just shifts from a desperate need to a normal activity.

Adolescent dogs, typically between six months and two years, might show renewed chewing. This phase comes with increased energy and independent thinking. Adult dogs chew for mental stimulation, stress relief, or maintaining dental health. If destructive chewing continues into adulthood, it indicates an ongoing issue. This issue requires owner intervention, not just waiting.

#### Puppyhood: The Teething Phase

During puppyhood, chewing is a fundamental behavior. It helps relieve the pain of new teeth coming in. Puppies also learn about their surroundings by mouthing objects. This phase peaks when their adult teeth emerge. This is usually around 4 to 6 months old. Providing a variety of appropriate chew toys is crucial.

Offer items with different textures and hardness. This helps satisfy their natural urges. Redirect your puppy every time they chew on something inappropriate. A firm "No!" followed by offering a proper chew toy works well. Consistency is vital to teach them correct chewing habits.

#### Adolescence: A Challenging Period

Adolescence in dogs is like human teenage years. This period runs roughly from 6 months to 2 years. Dogs often test boundaries during this time. They have increased energy and a strong urge for independence. Destructive chewing can resurface or even worsen during adolescence. This is not necessarily due to teething. It is often due to increased energy, boredom, or a lack of clear rules.

It is important to maintain consistent training and supervision during this phase. Increase their exercise and mental stimulation. Continue to reinforce appropriate chewing. This is not a time to relax your efforts. Instead, double down on positive training.

#### Adulthood: Beyond Teething

Once a dog reaches adulthood, destructive chewing is less likely to be a "phase." Adult dogs chew for specific reasons. These can include anxiety, boredom, or insufficient exercise. Some dogs develop compulsive chewing. This requires professional intervention. Regular chewing is healthy. Destructive chewing is not.

If your adult dog chews inappropriately, you must identify the cause. Provide plenty of appropriate outlets for chewing. Ensure they get enough physical and mental activity. Without intervention, destructive chewing often persists. It can even worsen over time.

### Effective Strategies to Manage Destructive Chewing

Managing destructive chewing requires a multi-faceted approach. There is no single magic cure. You must address the underlying reasons for chewing. You also need to provide alternatives and set clear boundaries. Prevention is often better than trying to stop the behavior after it starts. This includes dog-proofing your home.

Environmental management plays a big role. So does consistent training. By combining these strategies, you can guide your dog toward appropriate chewing. This helps protect your belongings and keeps your dog safe. Every step you take reinforces good habits.

#### Prevention Through Dog-Proofing

The easiest way to stop destructive chewing is to prevent it from happening. Puppy-proof your home, even if you have an adult dog. This means removing tempting items from their reach. Put away shoes, remote controls, and anything valuable. Block access to areas where they chew inappropriately. This might mean using gates or closing doors.

Supervise your dog closely, especially when they are young or new to your home. Crate training can be an excellent tool for prevention. A crate provides a safe, confined space where they cannot chew forbidden items. When you cannot supervise, a crate or a dog-proofed room prevents accidents. You can find more tips on how to stop a dog from chewing everything he sees by ensuring your environment is set up for success.

#### Providing Appropriate Chew Outlets

Dogs need to chew. It is a natural instinct. The key is to redirect this instinct onto appropriate items. Offer a variety of chew toys. Different textures, shapes, and sizes keep dogs interested. Consider durable rubber toys, nylon bones, or treat-dispensing toys. Rotate these toys regularly to keep them novel. If your dog loves to chew on wood, ensure you offer safe, dog-specific wood chews, not outdoor sticks or furniture. Learn more about how to stop a dog from chewing on wood effectively by providing better alternatives.

Ensure the chew toys are safe for your dog's size and chewing style. Toys that are too small can be a choking hazard. Toys that are too hard can damage teeth. Always supervise your dog with new chew toys.

#### Exercise and Mental Stimulation

A tired dog is a good dog. Sufficient physical exercise can greatly reduce destructive chewing caused by boredom. Daily walks, runs, or playtime in the yard burn off excess energy. The amount of exercise needed varies by breed and age. High-energy breeds require more activity.

Mental stimulation is equally important. Dogs need to use their brains. Puzzle toys, training sessions, and interactive games provide mental enrichment. Learning new tricks or commands can also tire them out. A dog that is mentally stimulated is less likely to chew out of boredom.

#### Using Taste Deterrents

Taste deterrents can be a temporary solution for specific items. These sprays contain a bitter taste that most dogs dislike. You can spray them on furniture legs, shoes, or other forbidden items. This makes the item unappealing to chew. However, deterrence is not a long-term solution. It teaches your dog what *not* to chew. It does not teach them what *to* chew.

Always test the spray on an inconspicuous area first. This ensures it does not stain or damage the item. Combine deterrents with providing attractive chew toys. This way, your dog learns what is acceptable. For example, if your dog chews on beds, a homemade bitter spray might help, as discussed in how do you make homemade spray to stop dogs from chewing on beds.

### Training Your Dog to Chew Appropriately

Training is essential to modify chewing behavior. We must teach dogs what they *can* chew on. This is more effective than just punishing them for what they *shouldn't* chew. Positive reinforcement is the most effective method. Reward your dog when they choose appropriate chew toys. Consistency and patience are key here.

Never use harsh punishment. This can create fear or anxiety, which might worsen destructive chewing. Focus on redirecting and rewarding good choices. Your dog will learn what makes you happy. This helps build a stronger bond too.

#### Positive Reinforcement

Positive reinforcement involves rewarding desired behaviors. When your dog chews on their designated toy, praise them. Give them a treat or engage in playtime. This teaches them that chewing on their toys earns them good things. Catch them in the act of chewing the right item. Then, reinforce that behavior immediately.

Make their chew toys highly desirable. You can stuff Kongs with peanut butter or treats. This makes the toy more appealing than your furniture. The more rewarding appropriate chewing is, the more likely your dog will choose it.

#### Redirection and Substitution

Redirection is a powerful tool. If you catch your dog chewing something inappropriate, interrupt them calmly. Say "No" or "Leave it" in a firm but not angry tone. Then, immediately offer them an appropriate chew toy. As soon as they take the toy, praise them. You can even offer a small treat.

This teaches them that if they drop the forbidden item, something better is available. Consistency is vital. Every time you redirect, you reinforce the desired behavior. This method is far more effective than just punishing, which might teach your dog to chew when you are not looking. If your dog chews on things like the leash, immediate redirection is critical, similar to how do I get my dog to stop chewing the leash.

#### Teaching "Leave It" and "Drop It"

These commands are invaluable for managing chewing. "Leave It" teaches your dog to ignore an item. "Drop It" teaches them to release an item they already have. Start practicing these commands in a low-distraction environment. Use high-value treats to reward success.

First, hold a treat in your hand. Tell your dog "Leave It." When they look away, reward them. Gradually increase the difficulty. Once they master these commands, you can use them to interrupt inappropriate chewing. This gives you control in the moment.

### When to Seek Professional Help for Persistent Chewing

Sometimes, destructive chewing persists despite your best efforts. This can be frustrating and worrisome. If your dog's chewing is severe, constant, or causes significant damage, professional help may be necessary. A veterinarian can rule out medical causes. A certified dog behaviorist can help identify complex behavioral issues. They can also create a tailored training plan.

Do not feel discouraged if you need to ask for help. Many common behavioral issues benefit from expert guidance. Addressing these problems early can prevent them from becoming more severe. Your dog's well-being is the top priority.

#### Consulting Your Veterinarian

Your first step should be a visit to your veterinarian. They can rule out any underlying medical issues. Pain, dental problems, or nutritional deficiencies can sometimes lead to excessive chewing. Your vet can perform a physical exam. They might recommend blood tests.

If no medical cause is found, your vet can still offer advice. They might refer you to a reputable dog trainer or behaviorist. They can also discuss potential anxiety medications if your dog's chewing is stress-related. It is always best to rule out health problems first.

#### Working with a Certified Dog Behaviorist

If chewing is due to complex behavioral issues, a certified dog behaviorist can provide expert guidance. They specialize in understanding and modifying canine behavior. A behaviorist can observe your dog and identify the root cause of the destructive chewing. This might be severe separation anxiety, compulsive disorder, or deep-seated fear.

They will develop a comprehensive behavior modification plan. This plan might include specific training exercises, environmental changes, and management techniques. They can teach you how to address anxiety or other complex issues effectively. Their expertise can make a significant difference in resolving stubborn chewing problems.

### Creating a Chewing-Friendly Home Environment

A chewing-friendly home environment is key to success. This means setting your dog up for success. It involves making forbidden items unavailable. It also means making appropriate items highly appealing. A secure and stimulating environment reduces the urge for destructive chewing. You want your dog to instinctively choose their own toys.

This also means understanding your dog's preferences. Some dogs love soft toys, while others prefer hard chews. Provide variety. Supervise new toys. This approach helps prevent accidents and promotes good habits. A safe and happy chewing environment benefits everyone.

#### Safe Zones and Supervision

Establish safe zones where your dog can be unsupervised without getting into trouble. This could be a dog-proofed room or a crate. Ensure this area contains only appropriate chew toys and bedding. When you are home, supervise your dog closely. This allows you to interrupt and redirect inappropriate chewing immediately.

Supervision is especially important for puppies and new dogs. As they learn what they can and cannot chew, you can gradually increase their freedom. But always err on the side of caution. A dog that is left alone with too much access might relapse into destructive habits.

#### Toy Rotation and Variety

Dogs, like children, can get bored with the same toys. Rotate your dog's chew toys regularly. Keep a selection of 3-4 toys available at any given time. Store the others out of sight. Every few days or weeks, swap out the current toys for the ones you have stored. This keeps the toys novel and exciting.

Offer a variety of textures, shapes, and materials. Some dogs prefer soft rubber, others like hard nylon bones, and some enjoy rope toys. Different toys can satisfy different chewing needs. Providing variety ensures your dog always has an interesting and appropriate outlet for their chewing instinct. For some dogs, specific chew toys, like those designed for eating socks, might be helpful if they tend to chew on fabric items like those mentioned in will my dog grow out of eating socks.

#### Engaging Play and Training Sessions

Incorporate engaging play and short training sessions into your dog's daily routine. Playtime can involve fetch, tug-of-war, or hide-and-seek. These activities burn physical energy. They also strengthen your bond with your dog. Training sessions, even short ones, provide mental stimulation. Teaching new tricks or practicing obedience commands challenges their minds.

A dog that feels engaged and has a routine is often a happier dog. This reduces the likelihood of boredom-induced destructive chewing. Consistency in these activities helps maintain good behavior. Make playtime fun and rewarding for your dog.

### FAQ Section

#### Do puppies always grow out of destructive chewing?
Puppies often reduce destructive chewing after teething, usually by six to eight months of age. This happens as their adult teeth come in and the discomfort lessens. However, they do not entirely stop chewing. They will continue to chew for exploration and enjoyment. Owners must still provide appropriate chew toys and guidance to establish good habits early. Without proper redirection, some puppies may continue destructive habits into adulthood.

#### What are the main reasons adult dogs chew destructively?
Adult dogs chew destructively for several reasons. Common causes include boredom due to lack of physical or mental stimulation, anxiety (especially separation anxiety), fear, or stress. Some dogs chew out of habit, for attention, or if they lack appropriate chew outlets. In rare cases, medical issues like dental problems or nutritional deficiencies can contribute. Identifying the specific reason is key to stopping the behavior.

#### How can I stop my dog from chewing furniture or shoes?
To stop a dog from chewing furniture or shoes, first dog-proof your home by removing tempting items. Provide a variety of appealing, appropriate chew toys and redirect your dog to them every time they try to chew forbidden objects. Ensure your dog gets enough exercise and mental stimulation daily. You can also use bitter taste deterrent sprays on items you want them to leave alone. Consistent training and supervision are vital.

#### Is punishment effective for stopping destructive chewing?
Punishment is generally not effective for stopping destructive chewing and can even be harmful. Harsh punishment can make your dog fearful, anxious, or confused, which might worsen the chewing or lead to other behavioral issues. Instead, focus on positive reinforcement. Redirect your dog to appropriate chew toys and reward them for choosing those items. This teaches them what you want them to do, rather than just what not to do.

#### How many chew toys should my dog have?
It is beneficial to have a variety of chew toys for your dog, typically 3-5 different types available at any given time. Rotate these toys every few days to keep them fresh and exciting for your dog. Offer different textures, shapes, and materials to satisfy various chewing needs. This variety helps maintain your dog's interest in their designated chew toys, making them less likely to seek out inappropriate items.

#### When should I consult a professional for my dog's chewing?
You should consult a professional if your dog's destructive chewing is severe, constant, causing significant damage, or if it is accompanied by other signs of anxiety or aggression. First, see your veterinarian to rule out any medical causes. If no medical issue is found, a certified dog behaviorist can help identify complex behavioral problems and develop a tailored plan to address the chewing effectively. Do not hesitate to seek help for persistent issues.

### Conclusion

Understanding destructive chewing in dogs means looking beyond just the act itself. Dogs do not simply "grow out" of destructive chewing. While puppies often reduce chewing after teething, adult dogs often chew due to boredom, anxiety, or lack of proper outlets. Effective management requires identifying the root cause, providing appropriate chew toys, and implementing consistent training.

By dog-proofing your home, offering plenty of exercise and mental stimulation, and positively reinforcing good chewing habits, you can guide your dog. Do not hesitate to seek professional help if chewing persists or is severe. Your efforts create a harmonious home where both you and your furry companion can thrive. Take action today to transform your dog's chewing habits and protect your belongings.
