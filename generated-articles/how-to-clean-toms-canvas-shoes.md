## Revive Your Toms: How To Clean Canvas Shoes Easily

My Toms canvas shoes are my go-to for comfort and style. They are perfect for daily wear. However, constant use leaves them looking dull and dirty. I often wonder how to clean Toms canvas shoes effectively without ruining them. This guide shows you simple steps to bring your beloved pair back to life. You will learn easy methods for hand washing and machine washing. We will also cover stain removal and proper drying techniques. Let's make your Toms look new again.

### Takeaway:
*   Always remove loose dirt before wet cleaning.
*   Spot clean small stains promptly.
*   Hand washing is generally safer for Toms canvas shoes.
*   Use a gentle cycle and cold water if machine washing.
*   Air dry your Toms completely away from direct heat.

To clean Toms canvas shoes, first remove loose dirt. Then, spot clean small marks with a mild detergent and a brush. For deeper cleaning, hand wash them with cold water and gentle soap, or machine wash on a delicate cycle in a mesh bag. Air dry them thoroughly away from direct sunlight or heat.

### Gather Your Cleaning Supplies: What You Need

Before you start cleaning, gather all necessary items. Having everything ready saves time and makes the process smooth. You likely already own most of these common household products. I always lay out my supplies on a clean surface. This helps me stay organized throughout the cleaning process.

You will need a few basic tools and cleaning agents. A soft-bristled brush is essential for scrubbing away grime. An old toothbrush works perfectly for small areas and seams. Choose a mild detergent or dish soap. Alternatively, baking soda and white vinegar are natural cleaning options. You will also need a few clean cloths, a small bucket or basin, and some paper towels. For specific stains, you might want a stain remover. I prefer gentle products to protect the canvas material.

*   **Soft-bristled brush or old toothbrush:** For scrubbing.
*   **Mild laundry detergent or dish soap:** For general cleaning.
*   **Baking soda and white vinegar:** Natural cleaning alternatives.
*   **Clean cloths or sponges:** For wiping and rinsing.
*   **Small bucket or basin:** For mixing solutions and rinsing.
*   **Paper towels:** For absorbing excess moisture.
*   **Stain remover (optional):** For tough spots.

Ensure your detergent is color-safe, especially for colored Toms. Strong chemicals can strip color from the canvas. Always test any cleaning solution on an inconspicuous area first. This prevents unexpected damage to your shoes.

### Preparing Your Toms: The First Steps to a Fresh Look

Proper preparation is crucial before any wet cleaning begins. This step helps remove loose dirt and debris. It also prevents dirt from turning into mud during washing. I always start by removing the shoelaces if my Toms have them, though many classic Toms are slip-ons. This allows for thorough cleaning of all parts of the shoe.

Next, vigorously clap the shoes together outside. This action dislodges dry mud and significant chunks of dirt. After clapping, use your soft-bristled brush to scrub away remaining loose dirt. Pay attention to the soles and sides. A dry brush effectively removes surface grime without spreading it around. This is a critical step to ensure a deeper clean later.

Remove any insoles if they are removable. Some Toms have fixed insoles, but if yours come out, take them out. You can clean these separately. This prevents water from getting trapped inside the shoe. It also helps the shoe dry faster. Now your Toms are ready for a proper wash. This preparation makes the cleaning process much more effective.

### Hand Washing Your Toms Canvas Shoes: A Gentle Approach

Hand washing is often the safest and most recommended method for cleaning Toms canvas shoes. It allows for precise control over the cleaning process. This helps prevent damage to the canvas material and stitching. I find hand washing gives me the best results for maintaining the shape and color of my shoes. This method is especially good for delicate or brightly colored Toms.

First, mix a cleaning solution in your bucket or basin. Combine about one teaspoon of mild laundry detergent or dish soap with two cups of cool water. Stir until suds form. You can also use a paste of baking soda and a small amount of water for a natural approach. Dip your soft-bristled brush into the cleaning solution. Gently scrub the entire canvas surface of your Toms. Work in small sections, applying light pressure. Focus on areas with visible stains or dirt. For more detailed cleaning, like around the seams and eyelets, use an old toothbrush.

After scrubbing, use a clean, damp cloth to wipe away the soap residue. Rinse the cloth often in clean water. Continue wiping until all suds are gone from the canvas. Avoid fully submerging the shoes if possible, especially if they have leather accents. Over-wetting can extend drying time and potentially damage the shoe's structure. For cleaning the bottom of your shoes, a separate scrub brush works well. This gentle hand washing method helps keep your Toms looking their best.

### Dealing with Stubborn Stains on Canvas Toms

Stubborn stains require a bit more attention and a targeted approach. Common stains on canvas shoes include mud, grass, oil, and food marks. Addressing these stains promptly increases your chances of complete removal. I always tackle stains as soon as I notice them. Waiting can make them harder to remove from the canvas fabric.

For mud or dirt stains, let the mud dry completely first. Then, scrape off as much dry mud as possible with a dull knife or brush. For more set-in dirt, I sometimes refer to techniques for cleaning other canvas items, which often share similar principles, like those in [how to clean canvas ballet shoes by hand](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-canvas-ballet-shoes-by-hand). Create a paste with baking soda and a small amount of water. Apply this paste directly to the stain. Let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then scrub gently with a toothbrush. Rinse with a damp cloth.

For oil or grease stains, sprinkle cornstarch or talcum powder directly onto the fresh stain. This absorbs the oil. Let it sit for several hours, then brush it off. Follow up with a small amount of mild dish soap and a brush. For tougher spots, a diluted white vinegar solution can work wonders. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water. Apply it to the stain with a cloth, let it sit for a few minutes, and then blot it clean. Always remember to test any stain remover on a hidden part of the shoe first. This simple step protects your Toms from accidental damage or discoloration.

### Machine Washing Toms: When and How to Do It Safely

Machine washing offers a convenient option for cleaning Toms, but it requires caution. Not all Toms are suitable for machine washing. I only machine wash my most durable canvas pairs. Always check the care label inside your shoes if you can find one. Machine washing can be harsh on delicate materials or embellishments. If your Toms have any leather details or are particularly old, hand washing is a better choice.

If you decide to machine wash, prepare your shoes properly. Remove any insoles and place the shoes in a mesh laundry bag. This protects them from tumbling directly against the machine drum. It also keeps shoelaces (if present) from getting tangled. You can also add a few old towels to the wash with your shoes. This helps balance the load and provides extra cushioning. For similar principles on washing canvas items, the advice in [can you machine wash canvas bags](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-you-machine-wash-canvas-bags) can be helpful.

Select the right wash settings. Use cold water and a delicate or gentle cycle. Hot water can cause the canvas to shrink or colors to bleed. Choose a mild, color-safe detergent. Use only a small amount. Too much soap can leave residue on the canvas. After the wash cycle finishes, remove your Toms immediately. Avoid leaving them in the machine, as this can encourage mildew growth. This careful approach helps your Toms emerge clean and undamaged from the washing machine.

### Drying Your Toms Canvas Shoes Properly

Drying your Toms canvas shoes correctly is as important as the washing process itself. Improper drying can lead to shrinkage, damage, or an unpleasant smell. I never put my Toms in a clothes dryer. The high heat can warp the rubber soles and shrink the canvas material. Air drying is the only way to go.

After washing, reshape your Toms by hand. Gently press out any excess water. You can stuff the inside of each shoe with paper towels or small, clean cloths. This helps absorb internal moisture and maintain the shoe's shape as it dries. Change the paper towels every few hours if they become saturated. This speeds up the drying process.

Place your Toms in a well-ventilated area away from direct sunlight or heat sources. Direct sun can fade the canvas color. Heaters or hair dryers can cause materials to become brittle. A shaded outdoor spot on a warm, breezy day is ideal. Alternatively, an indoor area with good air circulation works well. Prop the shoes up to allow air to flow around all sides. This includes the soles and inside. Drying can take anywhere from 12 to 24 hours, depending on humidity and airflow. Ensure they are completely dry before wearing them again. This prevents mildew and odors from forming inside the shoes.

### Maintaining Your Clean Toms: Tips for Longevity

Keeping your Toms clean between deep washes extends their lifespan and appearance. Regular maintenance saves you from frequent intensive cleaning sessions. I incorporate a few simple habits into my routine to keep my Toms looking fresh. This ensures they are always ready for my next adventure.

First, always address small marks and scuffs as they happen. Use a damp cloth or a magic eraser for quick spot cleaning. This prevents stains from setting into the canvas. I also wipe down the soles regularly to remove dirt buildup. Using a protective spray designed for canvas shoes can also help. These sprays create a barrier against water and stains. They make future cleaning much easier.

Store your Toms properly when not in use. Keep them in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Consider using shoe trees or stuffing them with acid-free tissue paper. This helps them maintain their shape. If your Toms start to develop a slight odor, sprinkle a small amount of baking soda inside. Let it sit overnight, then shake it out. This absorbs moisture and neutralizes smells. By following these simple maintenance tips, you can enjoy your clean Toms for much longer.

### FAQ Section

**Q: Can I put my Toms in the washing machine?**
A: Yes, you can machine wash most canvas Toms, but do so with caution. Place them in a mesh laundry bag, use cold water, and select a delicate cycle. Always air dry them afterward. Avoid machine washing if your Toms have delicate embellishments or leather details.

**Q: How often should I clean my Toms canvas shoes?**
A: The frequency depends on how often you wear them and where you wear them. For daily wear, a quick spot clean once a week is good. A deep clean every 1-3 months helps maintain their appearance. Clean immediately after they get very dirty or stained.

**Q: Can I use bleach on white Toms canvas shoes?**
A: I do not recommend using bleach. Bleach can weaken canvas fibers and turn them yellow over time. Instead, try a paste of baking soda and hydrogen peroxide for brightening white canvas. Apply, let sit, then rinse thoroughly. This offers a safer alternative.

**Q: What if my Toms start to smell after washing?**
A: A smell often indicates they did not dry completely. Ensure your Toms are 100% dry before storing or wearing. You can sprinkle baking soda inside the dry shoes and let it sit overnight to absorb odors. Sunlight can also help kill odor-causing bacteria.

**Q: How do I remove scuffs from the rubber soles of my Toms?**
A: Use a magic eraser or a mixture of baking soda and dish soap applied with a toothbrush. Gently scrub the rubber soles. This effectively removes most scuffs and dirt. Wipe clean with a damp cloth afterward for a fresh look.
