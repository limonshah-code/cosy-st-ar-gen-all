## Revive Your Finds: Clean Thrift Store Clothes & Decor

Stepping into a thrift store feels like a treasure hunt. You never know what unique items you might find. However, many amazing second-hand finds come with a lingering scent. This "thrift store smell" can be a mix of old fabric, dust, or even unknown origins. Knowing how to **clean thrift store clothes decor** and **get the smell out** is vital.

It ensures your vintage clothes feel fresh and your decorative pieces fit seamlessly into your home. I often discover fantastic items, but their odors need attention before use. This guide shares proven methods to **remove odors** from various thrift store treasures. We will cover everything from clothes to non-fabric decor. Get ready to transform your finds into fresh, beloved possessions.

### Takeaway:
*   Always inspect thrift items for care labels and potential damage before cleaning.
*   Ventilate items first to allow fresh air to begin the odor removal process naturally.
*   Use targeted cleaning methods, like baking soda or vinegar, for specific types of odors.
*   Deep clean textiles based on their fabric type to ensure lasting freshness.
*   Apply gentle but effective cleaning to non-fabric decor to remove dirt and smells.

### Clear, Concise Answer:
To effectively clean thrift store clothes and decor and get the smell out, begin by airing items outdoors. Then, apply specific methods like washing with vinegar, soaking in baking soda, or using enzymatic cleaners for textiles. For non-fabric decor, wipe surfaces with appropriate cleaners and use odor absorbers in enclosed spaces.

### Unboxing Your Treasures: First Steps for Thrift Finds

Bringing home a bag full of thrift store treasures is exciting. The first step is not to immediately toss everything into the wash. Instead, you need a careful assessment. I always lay out my finds and inspect them one by one. This helps determine the best cleaning approach for each item. This initial inspection protects your new items and makes cleaning easier.

Start by checking for any visible damage or stains. Look for care tags on clothing items. These tags provide important washing instructions. Some items might be dry-clean only or require hand washing. Ignoring these instructions can ruin your new clothes. Next, shake out clothing and textiles outdoors to remove loose dust and debris. This simple action can significantly reduce the initial "thrift store smell."

For decorative items, examine their material. Is it wood, ceramic, metal, or something else? Different materials need different cleaning solutions. Gently wipe down surfaces with a dry cloth first. This helps remove surface dirt. Sometimes, airing out items in fresh air is enough to dissipate mild odors. I often hang clothes outside for a day or two. This natural ventilation works wonders before any deep cleaning begins. Proper assessment sets the stage for successful odor removal.

*   **Initial Inspection Checklist:**
    *   **Care Labels:** Check clothing tags for washing instructions (e.g., "dry clean only," "hand wash").
    *   **Material Type:** Identify the fabric or material of each item (cotton, silk, wool, wood, metal, ceramic).
    *   **Damage/Stains:** Look for rips, tears, or noticeable stains that may need special treatment.
    *   **Loose Debris:** Shake out clothes and textiles to remove dust, lint, or pet hair. [Learn how to get rid of dog hair in clothes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-get-rid-of-dog-hair-in-clothes) from other sources.

### Tackling Stubborn Odors in Thrift Clothes

Getting unpleasant smells out of thrift store clothes can be a challenge. The key is to understand the source of the odor. Sometimes it is just a general musty smell. Other times, it can be smoke, pet odors, or persistent body odor. My preferred strategy involves a few steps to ensure thorough cleaning. We want to eliminate smells, not just mask them.

First, always pre-treat smelly clothes. I find that a good soak can make a big difference. Fill a basin or tub with cool water. Add half a cup of white vinegar and a quarter cup of baking soda. Let the clothes soak for at least an hour, or even overnight for strong odors. Vinegar helps neutralize odors. Baking soda absorbs them. This combination is powerful.

After soaking, wash the clothes according to their care instructions. Use a high-quality laundry detergent. You can add another half cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle. This boosts odor removal and softens fabrics naturally. For items that cannot be wet-washed, such as delicate vintage silks, consider [how to clean clothes without water](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-clothes-without-water) by airing them or using a fabric freshener designed for dry cleaning. If you notice a lingering smell even after washing, like a "dog smell," you might need to repeat the process or try specialized enzymatic cleaners. [Find out why clothes smell like dogs after washing](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-do-my-clothes-smell-like-dogs-after-washing).

*   **Deep Cleaning Methods for Clothes:**
    *   **Vinegar Soak:** Mix 1/2 cup white vinegar and 1/4 cup baking soda in water. Soak clothes for 1-24 hours.
    *   **Enzymatic Cleaners:** These cleaners break down odor-causing molecules. Use them for tough smells like pet urine.
    *   **Sunlight and Air:** Hang clothes outside on a sunny day. UV rays can kill bacteria and neutralize odors naturally.
    *   **Activated Charcoal:** Place activated charcoal packets in garment bags with clothes. They absorb odors over time.

### Deep Cleaning Fabric Decor and Upholstery

Thrift store decor often includes fabric items like throw pillows, small rugs, or even upholstered chairs. These items can hold onto smells even more tenaciously than clothes. Cleaning these pieces requires a gentle yet effective approach. My goal is always to refresh them without causing damage.

For removable covers on pillows or cushions, check their care labels. If machine washable, treat them like clothing. Use the vinegar and baking soda soak method. Then wash them on a gentle cycle with a good detergent. Always air dry these items completely to prevent new mildew smells. Moisture left behind can create new problems.

For non-removable fabric upholstery or rugs, you need a different strategy. Start by thoroughly vacuuming the item. Use a brush attachment to get deep into the fibers. This removes dust, dirt, and any surface debris. Next, create a cleaning solution. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Lightly mist the fabric surface. Do not saturate it. You can also sprinkle baking soda generously over the item. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight. Baking soda is excellent at absorbing embedded odors. After it sits, vacuum it up completely. For a [leather purse from a thrift store](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-leather-purse-from-a-thrift-store), special leather cleaners are necessary to preserve the material. Remember to always spot test any cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area first. This prevents potential discoloration or damage.

*   **Cleaning Fabric Decor Without Removal:**
    *   **Vacuum Thoroughly:** Use a powerful vacuum with appropriate attachments to remove surface dust and embedded dirt.
    *   **Baking Soda Application:** Sprinkle a generous layer of baking soda over the entire fabric surface. Let it sit for 6-24 hours before vacuuming.
    *   **Vinegar Spray:** Lightly mist the fabric with a 50/50 white vinegar and water solution. Allow it to air dry fully.
    *   **Steam Cleaning:** For some durable fabrics, a steam cleaner can sanitize and refresh. Always test a small area first.

### Refreshing Non-Fabric Thrift Decor

Thrift store finds are not just about clothes. You can discover amazing non-fabric decor too. These can include wooden furniture pieces, ceramic vases, metal sculptures, or plastic containers. Each material demands a specific cleaning technique to **get the smell out** and restore its shine. I always start with the least invasive method.

For items like ceramic, glass, or hard plastic, a simple wash with warm soapy water often works wonders. Use a mild dish soap and a soft cloth or sponge. Rinse thoroughly and dry completely. For persistent odors, these items can often handle a soak in a diluted white vinegar solution. For example, if I get a smelly vase, I might fill it with vinegar and water overnight. This helps neutralize any lingering scents.

Wooden items need more care. Avoid saturating wood with water. This can cause warping or damage. Instead, wipe down wooden pieces with a slightly damp cloth using a wood cleaner. For odors, place a bowl of activated charcoal or baking soda near the item in an enclosed space for a few days. This absorbs smells without damaging the wood. For **thrift store shoes** or similar items, specific cleaning for various materials like leather or canvas is needed. [Learn how to clean shoes from the thrift store](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-shoes-from-the-thrift-store) to keep them looking good. Metal decor can usually be cleaned with appropriate metal polish or a mild soap and water solution, followed by thorough drying to prevent rust. Always test cleaners in an inconspicuous spot on any decor item. This ensures the cleaner does not harm the finish.

*   **Non-Fabric Cleaning Strategies:**
    *   **Ceramics/Glass/Plastic:** Wash with warm soapy water. For odors, soak in a 50/50 vinegar-water solution.
    *   **Wood:** Wipe with a damp cloth and wood cleaner. For smells, use activated charcoal or baking soda nearby in an enclosed space.
    *   **Metal:** Clean with appropriate metal polish or mild soap and water. Dry thoroughly to prevent rust.
    *   **Baskets:** If you get [baskets from the thrift store](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-baskets-from-the-thrift-store), dust them off. Then wipe with a damp cloth and mild soap. Air dry completely.

### Special Odor Challenges: Smoke, Mildew, Pet Smells

Some thrift store items carry particularly strong or stubborn odors. Smoke, mildew, and pet smells are common culprits. These smells embed themselves deep into fibers and surfaces. They require more intense and targeted cleaning methods. I have encountered all these challenges, and each needs a specific approach.

**Smoke Odor:** Smoke particles cling to everything. For clothing and washable fabrics, multiple washes with a strong detergent are often needed. Add a cup of white vinegar or borax to the wash cycle. For non-washable items, professional cleaning might be an option. Otherwise, extensive airing out combined with activated charcoal or odor-absorbing sprays designed for smoke can help. Placing items in an airtight container with coffee grounds or crumpled newspaper for a few days can also absorb smoke smells.

**Mildew Odor:** Mildew indicates mold growth. This requires disinfection. For fabrics, soak items in a solution of one cup borax or oxygen bleach per gallon of water. Wash thoroughly afterwards. Sunlight is a natural mildew killer. Hang items outside on a sunny day. For non-fabric items, wipe surfaces with a diluted bleach solution (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) or white vinegar. Ensure proper ventilation during this process.

**Pet Smells:** Pet odors, especially urine, contain proteins that need special enzymes to break down. For textiles, use an enzymatic cleaner directly on the affected areas. Let it sit as directed, then wash. For upholstery or carpets, apply the enzymatic cleaner, allow it to soak, and then blot dry. You may need to repeat this process. Baking soda also works well to absorb general pet odors from fabrics. Remember, identifying the specific odor helps you choose the most effective removal method.

*   **Targeting Tough Odors:**
    *   **Smoke:** Use borax or vinegar in laundry. Air items extensively. Try coffee grounds or charcoal in sealed containers for non-washables.
    *   **Mildew:** Soak fabrics in borax or oxygen bleach. Disinfect non-fabrics with diluted bleach or vinegar. Sun exposure helps.
    *   **Pet:** Apply enzymatic cleaners directly to affected spots. Use baking soda as an absorbent for general pet smells.

### Natural Odor Eliminators for Second-Hand Items

You do not always need harsh chemicals to remove odors from thrift store finds. Nature provides powerful, safe alternatives. I often reach for natural solutions first. They are gentle on fabrics and good for the environment. These methods are effective for many types of smells.

**Baking Soda:** This common household item is a champion at absorbing odors. Sprinkle baking soda generously over clothes, rugs, or upholstered items. Let it sit for several hours or overnight. Then, vacuum it up. For smaller items like [thrift store jewelry](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-thrift-store-jewelry) or trinkets, place them in a sealed bag with a bowl of baking soda for a few days. The baking soda draws out the smells.

**White Vinegar:** Vinegar is a natural deodorizer and disinfectant. Its acetic acid neutralizes alkaline odors. Add a half to one cup of white vinegar to your washing machine's rinse cycle. This works for clothes and removable fabric covers. For non-washable items, mist surfaces lightly with a 50/50 solution of white vinegar and water. Always ensure items dry completely.

**Sunlight and Fresh Air:** This is perhaps the simplest and most effective method. UV rays from the sun kill odor-causing bacteria. Hang clothes, blankets, or even small rugs outside on a sunny, breezy day. Let them air out for several hours or a full day. This natural ventilation significantly reduces many types of smells. It also provides a wonderful fresh scent. Make sure to rotate items for even exposure.

**Activated Charcoal:** This substance is highly porous. It traps and absorbs odors at a molecular level. Place activated charcoal pouches in drawers, closets, or near smelly furniture. Leave them there for a few days to a week. This is especially good for delicate items or those that cannot be washed. These natural methods offer effective ways to freshen your second-hand items.

*   **Eco-Friendly Odor Solutions:**
    *   **Baking Soda:** Sprinkle on fabrics and vacuum. Place items with baking soda in sealed containers for deep absorption.
    *   **White Vinegar:** Add to laundry rinse cycles. Mist fabrics or hard surfaces with a diluted solution.
    *   **Sunlight and Air:** Hang items outdoors on a sunny day. UV rays kill bacteria and neutralize smells.
    *   **Activated Charcoal:** Use charcoal packets near or inside smelly items to absorb odors over time.

### Preventing Future Smells and Proper Storage

Once you have successfully cleaned your thrift store clothes and decor, you want to keep them fresh. Proper storage and regular maintenance are key. This prevents new odors from developing. My goal is always to enjoy my finds for a long time.

First, always ensure items are completely dry before storing them. Even a little dampness can lead to mildew. This causes new musty smells. Store clothes in a well-ventilated closet. Avoid packing them too tightly. Air circulation prevents smells from getting trapped. You can use cedar blocks or lavender sachets in your closets and drawers. These naturally repel moths and impart a pleasant scent.

For decor items, especially those with fabric elements, regular dusting and light cleaning help. If you have upholstered pieces, vacuum them periodically. This removes dust and prevents odors from settling. Consider placing small bowls of baking soda or activated charcoal in enclosed cabinets or shelves where you store decor. They act as passive odor absorbers.

Avoid storing thrift items in damp basements or attics. These areas often have high humidity and poor ventilation. This creates perfect conditions for mildew and musty smells. If you must store items in such places, use airtight containers with silica gel packets to absorb moisture. By taking these preventive steps, you can keep your beloved thrift store finds fresh and enjoyable.

*   **Maintaining Freshness:**
    *   **Ensure Dryness:** Always dry items completely before storing to prevent mildew.
    *   **Ventilated Storage:** Store clothes and fabrics in well-ventilated areas. Avoid overcrowding.
    *   **Natural Fresheners:** Use cedar blocks, lavender sachets, or activated charcoal in storage areas.
    *   **Regular Cleaning:** Dust and vacuum decor items periodically to prevent odor buildup.
    *   **Avoid Damp Spaces:** Do not store items in humid basements or attics; use airtight containers with desiccants if necessary.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Can I use laundry detergent alone to remove strong smells from thrift clothes?
A1: Laundry detergent alone may not be enough for strong odors like smoke or mildew. It often only masks the smell temporarily. For better results, pre-soak items in a solution of white vinegar and baking soda. Then wash them with your regular detergent. This combination tackles odors more effectively.

### Q2: Is it safe to use white vinegar on all types of fabrics and decor?
A2: White vinegar is generally safe for most fabrics and hard surfaces. However, avoid using it on delicate materials like silk, acetate, or rayon without testing first. It can also harm certain natural stone surfaces. Always spot-test a small, inconspicuous area to prevent damage or discoloration.

### Q3: How do I remove a persistent musty smell from a thrift furniture piece?
A3: For musty furniture, first clean all surfaces. Then, place bowls of activated charcoal or baking soda inside drawers and compartments. Close them for several days. Air the furniture outdoors on a dry, sunny day. You can also lightly mist fabric parts with a diluted vinegar solution.

### Q4: What if an item still smells after multiple cleaning attempts?
A4: If an item still smells after repeated cleaning, the odor might be deeply embedded. Consider an enzymatic cleaner for organic smells (like pet odors). For smoke or mildew, a professional cleaner might be necessary. Sometimes, an item's condition simply makes complete odor removal impossible.

### Q5: Can essential oils help get rid of thrift store smells?
A5: Essential oils can provide a pleasant scent. However, they typically mask odors rather than eliminate them. You can add a few drops to your laundry rinse cycle or a diluted vinegar spray. Always combine essential oils with true odor-neutralizing agents like baking soda or vinegar for best results.

### Q6: How long should I air out thrift items before cleaning?
A6: Airing out time varies based on the item and odor strength. For mild smells, a few hours outdoors might suffice. For stronger odors like smoke or heavy mustiness, allow items to air for several days. Good air circulation and sunlight accelerate this natural deodorizing process.
