## The AKC and German Shepherd Breed Standards Explained

The American Kennel Club, or AKC, is a registry of purebred dog pedigrees in the United States. It also promotes events for purebred dogs. The AKC maintains breed standards for each recognized breed. These standards describe the ideal characteristics of a dog for that breed. They cover appearance, temperament, and movement.

For German Shepherds, the AKC breed standard lists specific colors as desirable. The standard states that strong, rich colors are preferred. It also lists several colors as "disqualifying faults" for the conformation show ring. This means dogs with these colors cannot compete for championships in those shows. White, for example, is a disqualifying fault. Blue and liver colors are also listed as serious faults, typically leading to disqualification from the conformation ring.

It is important to understand the difference between AKC registration and AKC conformation show eligibility. A dog can be fully AKC registered. This means its lineage is recorded and traceable through the AKC database. However, it may not meet every aspect of the breed standard. This could include a specific coat color. Therefore, while a blue German Shepherd is a purebred GSD and can be registered, its color prevents it from excelling in the show ring. The AKC focuses on preserving breed integrity through its standards. This includes specific physical traits considered ideal for the breed. ## Understanding the Blue German Shepherd's Genetics

The captivating blue color in German Shepherds comes from a specific genetic trait. It is caused by a "dilute" gene. This gene is recessive. This means a puppy must inherit two copies of this gene, one from each parent, to express the blue coat. If a puppy receives only one copy, it will not be blue. It will carry the gene and pass it on.

The dilute gene affects the black pigment in the dog's coat. It changes black to a grayish-blue shade. This gives the dog its distinctive "blue" appearance. This color can range from a light steel blue to a deep, dark slate blue. Blue German Shepherds are not a separate breed. They are simply German Shepherds with a dilute gene. Their structure, temperament, and other traits are still those of a standard German Shepherd.

Some people worry that the dilute gene causes health problems. This is not true for German Shepherds. The blue color is a cosmetic trait. It does not lead to inherent health issues. Dogs of other breeds with the dilute gene might have coat or skin problems. However, this is not typical for blue German Shepherds. The color is merely a variation. ## AKC Registration vs. Conformation Show Eligibility

It is very important to understand the difference between AKC registration and eligibility for AKC conformation shows. Many people confuse these two concepts. AKC registration means your dog's lineage is recorded with the American Kennel Club. This confirms your dog is a purebred animal whose parents are also AKC-registered. The AKC records the parentage of a puppy based on the breeder's submission. The breeder provides the litter registration form.

Conformation shows are different. These events judge a dog against its breed standard. Judges look for how well a dog conforms to the ideal physical and mental traits of its breed. For German Shepherds, the AKC breed standard has specific color requirements. The standard states that strong, rich colors are preferred. It lists blue and liver as "serious faults" that disqualify a dog from the conformation ring. This means a blue German Shepherd cannot win a championship in these shows.

However, this disqualification only applies to conformation shows. It does not affect the dog's ability to be registered. A blue German Shepherd can compete in other AKC events. These include obedience, agility, rally, tracking, and herding. These events focus on the dog's performance and training, not its coat color. So, if you own a blue German Shepherd, you can still participate in many AKC activities. Being registered gives you access to these opportunities. For many owners, a healthy, well-tempered dog is the most important thing, regardless of its show potential. Blue German Shepherds typically have lighter colored eyes. Many puppies are born with clear blue eyes. These eyes often darken as the dog matures. ## FAQ Section

### Q1: Is a blue German Shepherd recognized by the AKC?

Yes, a blue German Shepherd is recognized by the AKC for registration purposes. This means if both parents are AKC-registered German Shepherds, their blue offspring can also be registered. However, the blue color is considered a disqualifying fault under the AKC breed standard for conformation shows. This means blue German Shepherds cannot compete to win championships in these specific events.

### Q2: Are blue German Shepherds purebred?

Absolutely, blue German Shepherds are purebred German Shepherds. Their blue coat color results from a recessive dilute gene. This gene causes the black pigment in their coat to appear gray-blue. They are not a crossbreed. They possess all the genetic characteristics of the German Shepherd breed, aside from this single color variation.

### Q3: Do blue German Shepherds have health problems?

No, the blue coat color itself does not cause inherent health problems in German Shepherds. The dilute gene is merely a cosmetic trait. Blue German Shepherds are susceptible to the same health issues as any other German Shepherd. These include hip and elbow dysplasia or degenerative myelopathy. Health problems are linked to genetics of the overall breed, not specifically the blue color.

### Q4: Why are blue German Shepherds controversial?

Blue German Shepherds are not controversial for their health or temperament. The "controversy" stems from their coat color not meeting the strict AKC conformation show standard. Some traditional breeders may view them as undesirable for the show ring. However, they are perfectly healthy and viable as family pets or working dogs outside of the conformation ring.

### Q5: How much does a blue German Shepherd puppy cost?

The cost of a blue German Shepherd puppy can vary widely. Prices often range from $1,500 to $3,000 or more. This price depends on factors like the breeder's reputation, the puppy's lineage, and geographic location. The unique color can sometimes lead to a higher price. It is important to remember that price does not always reflect quality.

### Q6: Can a blue German Shepherd compete in AKC events?

Yes, a blue German Shepherd can compete in many AKC events. While they are disqualified from conformation (breed) shows, they are eligible for performance events. These include obedience, agility, rally, tracking, and herding. These events focus on the dog's training, ability, and temperament. Their coat color does not impact their eligibility in these competitions.
