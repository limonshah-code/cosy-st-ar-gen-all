## What Is A Blue Long Haired Chihuahua

Imagine a tiny dog with silky, flowing fur and a coat color that shimmers like twilight. This unique appearance describes the Blue Long Haired Chihuahua, a highly sought-after and distinct variation within the Chihuahua breed. These charming companions stand out because of a special combination of genetic traits. They possess the gene for long hair and another rare gene responsible for their blue, or dilute, coat color.

Understanding what makes a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua so special involves looking at its genetics, its appearance, and the specific care it needs. We will explore how these genes create their unique look, discuss their personality, and offer advice on their grooming and health. If you are curious about these beautiful small dogs, you have come to the right place. I will guide you through everything you need to know about this wonderful breed.

### Takeaway

*   Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas feature a rare dilute grey coat and flowing long hair.

### What is a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua?

A Blue Long Haired Chihuahua is a purebred Chihuahua that displays a rare dilute grey or silver coat color. This color happens due to a recessive dilute gene (dd) that changes black pigment. It also has the long-coat gene, which gives it flowing fur. These dogs have a distinct, often striking, appearance. They share standard Chihuahua characteristics but require specific grooming for their unique coat.

### Understanding the Blue Long-Haired Chihuahua's Appearance

The appearance of a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua is truly striking. When we say "blue," we are not talking about a vibrant blue like the sky. Instead, "blue" refers to a dilute grey or silver shade. This coat color can range from a light silver-blue to a deeper charcoal grey. The color affects all areas that would normally be black, including the nose, paw pads, and often the eye rims.

Their long, flowing fur is another defining characteristic. This coat is soft and can be straight or slightly wavy. It forms feathery furnishings on the ears, legs, and tail. The tail often has a beautiful plume of hair, making it look very elegant. The combination of this unique dilute color and the luxurious long hair makes them stand out among other Chihuahuas. Many people find their appearance quite captivating.

The eyes of a blue Chihuahua often complement their coat. They can be amber, light brown, or even blue. This eye color is also part of the dilute gene's effect. Their small size and expressive faces complete their endearing look. ### The Genetics Behind the Blue Coat and Long Hair

The unique look of a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua is entirely due to its genetic makeup. Two specific recessive genes must be present for this appearance to happen. First, let us look at the "blue" coat color. This color comes from the dilute gene, often called the 'd' locus. A dog needs two copies of the recessive 'd' gene (dd) to express the dilute color.

If a dog has at least one dominant 'D' gene, its black pigment will appear black. However, with two recessive 'd' genes, the black pigment dilutes to a grey or blue shade. This gene affects only the black pigment, turning it into blue. Brown pigment would turn into Isabella if diluted, which is a different color. This means the dog must carry the gene for black pigment, which then dilutes.

Second, the long hair comes from another distinct recessive gene. Chihuahuas naturally come in both smooth coat (short hair) and long coat varieties. A long-haired Chihuahua must inherit two copies of the recessive long-hair gene from its parents. If it inherits even one dominant short-hair gene, it will be a smooth-coat Chihuahua.

The Blue Long Haired Chihuahua is a result of inheriting both these recessive traits. The dog receives the 'dd' genotype for dilute color and the 'll' genotype for long hair. This combination makes them relatively rare. It also means that a blue long-haired Chihuahua cannot be bred from two short-haired Chihuahuas, unless both parents carry the long-hair gene. The parents must also carry the dilute gene to produce blue offspring.

### Are Blue Long-Haired Chihuahuas Rare?

Yes, Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas are considered quite rare. Their rarity stems directly from the specific genetic combination needed for their unique appearance. For a Chihuahua to be blue, it must inherit two copies of the recessive dilute gene (dd), one from each parent. For it to have long hair, it must also inherit two copies of the recessive long-hair gene (ll), one from each parent. The chances of both these recessive genes combining in a single puppy are lower than for more common coat colors and lengths.

Many Chihuahuas carry the dominant genes for black pigment (D) and short hair (L). Breeding two Chihuahuas that are both blue and long-haired is the most direct way to get blue long-haired puppies. However, such breeding pairs are not common. Breeders sometimes must breed two dogs that carry both recessive genes but do not show the traits themselves. For example, a black long-haired Chihuahua might carry the dilute gene, and a blue smooth-coat Chihuahua might carry the long-hair gene.

Because of their rarity and unique beauty, Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas often come with a higher price tag. Reputable breeders who specialize in these colors and coat types invest a lot in genetic testing and responsible breeding practices. They work to ensure the health and proper genetic lineage of their puppies. This extra effort adds to the cost. The demand for these distinct Chihuahuas also contributes to their higher value. Their uncommon nature makes them very desirable pets for those seeking something truly special.

### Essential Grooming for a Blue Long-Haired Chihuahua

The long, flowing coat of a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua requires regular and dedicated grooming to keep it healthy and free of mats. Their beautiful fur is not just for looks; it needs proper care to prevent skin issues and discomfort. I find that regular grooming sessions are also a wonderful way to bond with my pet. Start a grooming routine early in their life so they become used to it.

Brushing is the most important part of their grooming. You should brush your Blue Long Haired Chihuahua at least two to three times a week. Daily brushing is even better if you can manage it. Use a soft slicker brush or a pin brush to gently remove loose hair and prevent tangles. Pay special attention to areas where mats often form, such as behind the ears, under the legs, and around the tail. These areas have finer hair that can tangle easily. You should avoid cutting their long hair too much; you can read more on [should you cut a long-haired Chihuahua](https://cosyhomecreation.com/should-you-cut-a-long-haired-chihuahua).

Bathing is also necessary, but not too often. Over-bathing can strip natural oils from their skin and coat, leading to dryness and irritation. Bathe your Blue Long Haired Chihuahua every three to four weeks, or as needed if they get dirty. Use a dog-specific shampoo and conditioner formulated for long-haired breeds. Make sure to rinse thoroughly to prevent residue build-up, which can cause skin problems. After bathing, gently towel dry your dog and then use a cool setting on a blow dryer to fully dry their coat, brushing as you go to prevent tangles. You can find more specific advice on [how often should I bathe my long-haired Chihuahua](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-often-should-i-bathe-my-long-haired-chihuahua). Regular nail trims, ear cleaning, and dental care are also important parts of their overall grooming routine.

### Health Considerations for Blue Long-Haired Chihuahuas

Like all purebred dogs, Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas can be prone to certain health issues. It is important for potential owners to be aware of these. Their small size makes them delicate. They are susceptible to conditions common in the Chihuahua breed in general. These include patellar luxation, where the kneecap slips out of place, and hypoglycemia, which is low blood sugar. Dental problems are also common in small breeds, so regular dental care is crucial. Hydrocephalus, a build-up of fluid in the brain, is another concern, though it is less common.

Additionally, some blue-coated dogs, including Chihuahuas, can be at a higher risk for a condition called Color Dilution Alopecia (CDA). This genetic skin condition is linked to the dilute gene that causes their blue coat. CDA can cause thinning hair, patchiness, or complete hair loss in affected areas. The skin in these areas might also become flaky, dry, or prone to infections. While not every blue Chihuahua will develop CDA, it is a risk that responsible breeders test for and inform potential owners about. There is no cure for CDA, but management often involves special shampoos and moisturizers to ease skin discomfort.

Choosing a reputable breeder who performs health screenings on their breeding dogs can help reduce the risk of inheriting many genetic conditions. They should provide health clearances for the parents. Providing a balanced diet, regular exercise, and routine veterinary check-ups are also vital for keeping your Blue Long Haired Chihuahua healthy throughout its life. Knowing the potential health issues allows you to be prepared and provide the best care. The typical [lifespan of a long-haired Chihuahua](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-lifespan-of-a-long-haired-chihuahua) can be quite long with proper care, so proactive health management is beneficial.

### Finding a Reputable Blue Long-Haired Chihuahua Breeder

Finding a reputable breeder is crucial when you are looking for a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua. This step ensures you get a healthy puppy from a responsible source. A good breeder prioritizes the health and welfare of their dogs above all else. They do not just focus on producing a specific color or coat type. They breed for good temperament and robust health.

Here are some points to consider when searching for a reputable breeder:

*   **Health Clearances:** A reputable breeder will perform health screenings on their breeding dogs. They will test for genetic conditions common in Chihuahuas, such as patellar luxation and heart issues. They should be transparent about these test results and willing to share them with you.
*   **Knowledge and Experience:** Good breeders have deep knowledge of the Chihuahua breed. They can answer your questions about genetics, health, and temperament. They understand the specific traits of blue and long-haired Chihuahuas.
*   **Clean Environment:** The breeder's facility should be clean, safe, and provide a stimulating environment for the puppies and adult dogs. The dogs should appear well-cared for, socialized, and happy. I always recommend visiting in person if possible.
*   **Parent Information:** A reputable breeder will allow you to meet the puppy's parents. This helps you assess their temperament and health. They should also provide pedigree information.
*   **No Pressure Sales:** Avoid breeders who pressure you into buying a puppy immediately. A good breeder cares about finding the right home for their puppies. They will ask you questions to ensure you are a suitable owner.
*   **Contract and Guarantees:** Expect a contract that outlines health guarantees, return policies, and spay/neuter agreements. This contract protects both you and the puppy.
*   **Support After Purchase:** A responsible breeder offers ongoing support and advice after you take your puppy home. They care about their puppies throughout their lives.

Be wary of breeders who offer multiple breeds, sell puppies at very young ages, or do not ask you any questions. These might be signs of a puppy mill or an irresponsible operation. Taking your time to find the right breeder ensures a positive experience and a healthy, happy Blue Long Haired Chihuahua.

### Frequently Asked Questions About Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas

#### Q1: Is the "blue" color in Chihuahuas truly blue?
No, the term "blue" for Chihuahuas refers to a dilute shade of grey or silver. This color ranges from light silver to dark charcoal. It results from a recessive gene that changes black pigment into this muted tone. The eyes and nose often match this dilute color.

#### Q2: Do Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas have any unique health issues because of their color?
While they share common Chihuahua health concerns like patellar luxation, blue-coated dogs can be prone to Color Dilution Alopecia (CDA). CDA is a genetic skin condition that causes hair thinning, hair loss, or skin problems in dilute-colored dogs. Not all blue Chihuahuas get it, but it is a risk.

#### Q3: How much grooming does a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua need?
Their long, flowing coat requires regular grooming to prevent mats and tangles. You should brush them at least 2-3 times a week, ideally daily. They need bathing every 3-4 weeks, using dog-specific shampoo and conditioner. Regular nail trims and ear cleaning are also essential.

#### Q4: Are Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas good family pets?
Yes, they can be excellent family pets, especially for families with older, respectful children. They are loyal and loving, forming strong bonds with their owners. Early socialization is key to ensuring they are well-adjusted around strangers and other pets. They thrive on companionship.

#### Q5: What is the typical size of a Blue Long Haired Chihuahua?
Blue Long Haired Chihuahuas are small dogs, just like other Chihuahuas. They typically weigh between 2 to 6 pounds (0.9 to 2.7 kg) and stand about 5 to 8 inches (13 to 20 cm) tall at the shoulder. Their long coat can make them appear slightly larger than smooth-coat Chihuahuas.
