## Protecting Loggerheads: Hilton Head's Main Species

The Loggerhead sea turtle (Caretta caretta) is the most common species that nests on Hilton Head Island. These impressive marine reptiles are easily recognizable by their large heads and powerful jaws. They are named for their relatively large heads. This feature gives them a powerful bite, useful for crushing the shells of their prey. Their diet primarily consists of shellfish and crustaceans. Loggerheads are found in temperate and tropical regions of the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans.

Loggerheads are listed as a threatened species under the Endangered Species Act. This means their populations are declining, and they face significant threats. These threats include habitat loss, accidental capture in fishing gear (bycatch), and pollution. The protection efforts in Hilton Head are vital for their survival. Female Loggerheads return to the same general area where they were born to lay their eggs. This incredible migratory instinct reinforces the importance of protecting specific nesting beaches like those on Hilton Head. By understanding and protecting these magnificent creatures, we contribute to the health of the global marine ecosystem.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**1. What types of sea turtles hatch in Hilton Head?**
The vast majority of sea turtles that hatch in Hilton Head are Loggerhead sea turtles. They are easily identified by their large heads. While other species like Green turtles or Leatherbacks might occasionally appear, Loggerheads are the primary nesters here. This makes Hilton Head a critical habitat for their continued survival and protection.

**2. Is it legal to touch a sea turtle hatchling in Hilton Head?**
No, it is illegal to touch, disturb, or interfere with sea turtle hatchlings or nests in Hilton Head and throughout South Carolina. Sea turtles are protected under federal and state laws, including the Endangered Species Act. Observing them from a distance is the only responsible way to witness this natural event.

**3. What should I do if I see a disoriented hatchling?**
If you see a hatchling heading away from the ocean or struggling, do not touch it. Immediately contact the Hilton Head Island Sea Turtle Protection Project or the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources (SCDNR). They have trained personnel who can properly assess the situation and provide assistance without harming the turtle.

**4. Can I use a flashlight on the beach at night during hatching season?**
You should avoid using white flashlights on the beach at night during nesting and hatching season (May-October). Artificial white light disorients hatchlings, drawing them away from the ocean. If you need light, use a special red-filtered flashlight or avoid lighting altogether.

**5. How long does it take for sea turtle eggs to hatch?**
Sea turtle eggs typically take about 55 to 60 days to hatch. This incubation period can vary slightly depending on factors like sand temperature. Warmer sand tends to shorten the incubation period, while cooler sand can extend it. This period is crucial for the development of the tiny turtles inside.

**6. Where can I find more information about sea turtle activities in Hilton Head?**
For the most current information, including specific nesting data and public educational programs, you should visit the official website of the Hilton Head Island Sea Turtle Protection Project. Local nature centers, like the Coastal Discovery Museum, also offer valuable insights and educational opportunities.
