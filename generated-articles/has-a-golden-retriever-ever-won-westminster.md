## Westminster's Elite Stage: Understanding Best in Show

The Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show is an annual event. It has a rich history dating back to 1877. This makes it the second-longest continuously running sporting event in the U.S., after the Kentucky Derby. Held traditionally in New York City, it draws top-tier dogs. These dogs come from across the country and the world. The show is often called "America's Dog Show." It truly is a big event for dog enthusiasts. For more details on the venue, you can check out [where is the Westminster Dog Show held](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/where-is-the-westminster-dog-show-held).

The judging process is rigorous and multi-tiered. First, dogs compete within their own breed. The judges select Best of Breed winners. These winners then move on to compete in their respective groups. There are seven groups recognized by the American Kennel Club (AKC). Each group winner then proceeds to the Best in Show ring. This is the final stage of the competition.

The seven groups are:
*   **Sporting Group**: Dogs developed to assist hunters, like Golden Retrievers.
*   **Hound Group**: Dogs bred for hunting by scent or sight.
*   **Working Group**: Dogs bred to perform tasks, such as guarding or pulling.
*   **Terrier Group**: Energetic dogs, originally bred to hunt vermin.
*   **Toy Group**: Small companion dogs.
*   **Non-Sporting Group**: A diverse group of dogs not fitting other categories.
*   **Herding Group**: Dogs bred to control the movement of livestock.

The Best in Show judge then evaluates the seven group winners. This judge must choose the single dog that best represents its breed standard. They also select the dog that has the most overall balance, health, and presence. It is a challenging task, as all group winners are exceptional dogs.
