## Understanding Your Roush Air Filter Type: Oiled vs. Dry

Before you begin cleaning, you must identify your Roush air filter type. Roush offers both oiled and dry filters. Each type requires a different cleaning approach. Using the wrong method can damage the filter. It can also reduce its effectiveness. This step is critical for successful maintenance.

Oiled air filters are very common in performance applications. These filters typically use layers of cotton gauze. The gauze material gets treated with a special filter oil. This oil helps trap very fine dust particles. The oil creates a sticky barrier. Air passes through, but dirt sticks to the oil. Many Roush cold air intake systems use this type of filter. These filters need specific cleaning solutions and re-oiling kits. [How to clean Roush cold air intake filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-roush-cold-air-intake-filter) provides more details on this common type.

Dry air filters are a different option. These filters often use synthetic fibers or paper as filtration media. They do not require oil to trap particles. The dense material itself catches dirt and dust. Dry filters are popular because they offer easy maintenance. They do not involve the re-oiling process. Some owners prefer them for their simplicity. These filters still require regular cleaning. The cleaning process differs significantly from oiled filters.

You can often tell the difference by looking at the filter media. Oiled filters usually have a red or blue tint from the oil. They might also feel slightly sticky to the touch. Dry filters appear plain white or gray. They will not have any oily residue. If you are unsure, check your Roush system’s instruction manual. The manual will clearly state the filter type. It is important to know this detail.

Choosing the correct cleaning method preserves your filter's life. It ensures it continues to filter air properly. Do not try to oil a dry filter. Do not clean an oiled filter without re-oiling it afterward. This knowledge forms the foundation of proper Roush air filter care. ## Step-by-Step Guide: Cleaning an Oiled Roush Air Filter

Cleaning an oiled Roush air filter is a precise process. Following these steps ensures thorough cleaning. It also protects the filter media from damage. I will guide you through each stage. This ensures your filter performs like new again.

### **1. Remove the Air Filter**

First, you need to access the filter. Open your vehicle's hood. Locate the air intake system. The Roush air filter sits inside the airbox or cold air intake housing. You may need a screwdriver or a wrench. Loosen the clamps that hold the housing together. Carefully remove the filter. Be gentle during removal. Do not bend or tear the filter pleats. Place the filter on a clean surface.

### **2. Apply Filter Cleaner**

Now, apply the specialized air filter cleaner. Spray the cleaner generously onto the entire filter surface. Focus on the dirty areas. Ensure it saturates the pleats from both sides. Let the cleaner soak in for about 10-15 minutes. This allows the solution to break down old oil and trapped dirt. Do not let the cleaner dry on the filter. If it starts to dry, apply more. This step is crucial for effective dirt removal. Many performance filters, like the [Roush cold air intake filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-roush-cold-air-intake-filter), follow this same cleaning principle.

### **3. Rinse the Filter**

After soaking, rinse the filter. Use low-pressure running water. Start rinsing from the clean side of the filter. This means rinsing from the inside out. The water pushes dirt out of the pleats. Continue rinsing until the water runs clear. Do not use high-pressure water. High pressure can damage the filter material. It can tear the delicate cotton gauze. This would ruin the filter's ability to trap particles. A gentle stream is all you need.

### **4. Gently Tap to Remove Excess Water**

Once rinsed, gently tap the filter against a hard surface. Do this several times. This removes excess water from the pleats. You can also lightly shake the filter. The goal is to speed up the drying process. Do not wring or twist the filter. This can damage its structure.

### **5. Air Dry Completely**

This step is perhaps the most important. Allow the filter to air dry completely. Place it in a clean, dry area. Direct sunlight is acceptable, but avoid extreme heat. Never use compressed air, heat guns, or blow dryers. These methods can damage the cotton fibers. Drying can take several hours, or even overnight. The filter must be bone dry before you apply oil. Any moisture remaining can prevent the oil from adhering properly. It can also lead to mold growth. Patience here ensures the best results. The process for cleaning an [S&B air filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-s-and-b-air-filter) is very similar, emphasizing careful rinsing and drying.

By following these steps, you prepare your oiled Roush air filter for its crucial re-oiling stage. Proper cleaning ensures maximum filtration efficiency. It sets the stage for optimal engine performance. I always make sure each step is completed thoroughly.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Cleaning a Dry Roush Air Filter

Cleaning a dry Roush air filter is generally simpler than cleaning an oiled one. However, specific care is still necessary. You must avoid practices that can damage the synthetic media. I will walk you through the correct method for dry filters. This keeps them effective and long-lasting.

### **1. Remove the Air Filter**

Just like with oiled filters, start by removing the dry Roush air filter. Locate your airbox or cold air intake housing. Loosen any clamps or bolts holding it shut. Carefully lift the filter out. Avoid bumping it or causing any tears. Place it on a clean work surface. This prevents adding new dirt to the filter.

### **2. Remove Loose Debris**

Begin by removing any large, loose debris. Gently tap the filter against a hard, clean surface. This dislodges leaves, bugs, and bigger dirt particles. You can also use a soft-bristle brush. Lightly brush the exterior of the filter. Brush along the pleats to sweep away surface dirt. Be careful not to press too hard. Hard brushing can embed dirt deeper into the filter material. It can also damage the pleats. This initial step is key for efficient cleaning.

### **3. Use Low-Pressure Air or Vacuum**

For deeper cleaning, use low-pressure compressed air. Blow the air from the inside of the filter outwards. This pushes dirt out of the pleats. Maintain a safe distance. Do not hold the nozzle too close to the filter. High-pressure air can tear the synthetic media. If you do not have an air compressor, use a shop vacuum. Attach a brush attachment. Gently vacuum the exterior of the filter. Move the vacuum along the pleats. This lifts trapped dust. The goal is to remove as much dry dirt as possible without damage. This technique is also common for other dry filters, like when you [clean a Spectre air filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-spectre-air-filter).

### **4. Rinse (If Recommended by Manufacturer)**

Some dry synthetic filters can be rinsed with water. Check your Roush air filter's instructions first. If rinsing is allowed, use low-pressure water. Rinse from the clean side outwards. This washes away fine dust. Continue until the water runs clear. If your specific Roush dry filter instructions say not to rinse, skip this step. Only follow manufacturer guidelines here. Never use harsh detergents or cleaning chemicals. Plain water is best if rinsing is an option.

### **5. Air Dry Completely**

If you rinsed the filter, it must dry completely. Place the filter in a clean, dry area. Allow plenty of time for it to air dry. This might take several hours.
