### Mastering the Piping: Crafting Your Own Cord Trim

Piping adds a professional, tailored look to your **piping zipper pillow cover**. You can buy pre-made piping, but making your own allows for perfect fabric matching. It is also a very satisfying part of the sewing process. This technique gives you full control over the final aesthetic.

To make your own piping, you need to cut fabric strips on the bias. Cutting on the bias means cutting fabric at a 45-degree angle to the selvage. This allows the fabric to stretch slightly, which helps the piping curve smoothly around corners. Your strips should be wide enough to wrap around your cording with seam allowance. For example, if your cord is 1/4 inch thick and you use a 1/2 inch seam allowance, your strips might be 1.5 to 2 inches wide.

After cutting bias strips, sew them together to create one long continuous strip. Place right sides together and sew at a 90-degree angle. This creates a diagonal seam that will lay flat. Press these seams open to reduce bulk. You want a smooth, consistent strip for your piping.

Now, it is time to encase the cord. Lay your piping cord down the center of the wrong side of your bias strip. Fold the fabric over the cord, aligning the raw edges. Using a zipper foot or piping foot on your sewing machine, stitch as close to the cord as possible. This creates your custom piping.

When attaching the piping to your pillow cover fabric, align the raw edge of the piping with the raw edge of your pillow fabric. Pin it in place around the perimeter of the pillow front. Clip into the seam allowance of the piping around curves or corners. This helps it lay flat. Stitch the piping to the fabric, again using your piping foot. Go slowly to ensure a neat, even application.

### Installing the Zipper: A Seamless Finish for Your Pillow

An invisible zipper is the secret to a professional-looking **piping zipper pillow cover**. It disappears almost entirely into the seam, providing a clean edge. While it may seem daunting at first, it becomes easy with practice. Our video tutorial offers excellent visual guidance for this step.

First, prepare your zipper and fabric panels. You will typically install the zipper along the bottom edge of your pillow cover. Place the two back fabric panels right sides together. Mark where your zipper will start and end. This ensures the zipper is centered. You will sew the ends of this seam closed and leave the middle open for the zipper.

For the zipper installation itself, you need an invisible zipper foot for your sewing machine. This foot has grooves that guide the zipper teeth, helping you stitch very close to them. With right sides of your fabric together and the zipper open, pin one side of the zipper tape to one raw edge of your fabric. Make sure the zipper teeth align with your seam line. Stitch down one side, getting as close to the teeth as possible without stitching on them.

Repeat the process for the other side of the zipper. Pin the second zipper tape to the other raw edge of your fabric, again aligning teeth to the seam line. Stitch this side. When you close the zipper, it should look like a continuous seam. You can find more tips on zipper care by looking at our article on [how to clean zipper on bag](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-zipper-on-bag).

Finally, close up the remaining seam. With the zipper closed, fold your fabric right sides together. Stitch the small sections of the seam on either side of the zipper opening. Backstitch at the start and end of these stitches for reinforcement. Press the seam flat. The invisible zipper should now be perfectly integrated, creating a neat finish for your **DIY piping pillow cover**.

### Assembling Your Pillow Cover: The Final Sewing Steps

With the piping attached to the front panel and the zipper installed in the back, you are ready to assemble your **piping zipper pillow cover**. This stage brings all your hard work together. Accurate measurements and careful sewing are crucial for a perfectly shaped pillow. Ensure your pillow insert fits well inside. Sometimes, making a pillow slightly smaller than the insert makes it look fuller, similar to how one might consider [how do I make a pillow for my dog](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-do-i-make-a-pillow-for-my-dog) for pet comfort.

Lay your front pillow panel (with piping attached) right side up. Place your back pillow panel (with the zipper installed) on top, right side down. Ensure all raw edges line up perfectly. The piping will be sandwiched between the two main fabric pieces. Pin all around the perimeter of the pillow cover.

When sewing, use your regular zipper foot or a piping foot again. You need to stitch just inside the previous line of stitching you made when attaching the piping. This ensures the piping stands out nicely once the pillow is turned right side out. Sew slowly and carefully, especially around the corners. Reinforce your stitches at the beginning and end.

Once sewn, clip the corners diagonally. Do not cut through your stitching. This reduces bulk in the corners, allowing them to turn out sharply. If your fabric frays easily, you might want to finish the raw edges with a serger or a zigzag stitch. This adds durability to your **custom cushion cover**.

Turn your pillow cover right side out through the zipper opening. Use a blunt object, like a chopstick or a point turner, to gently push out the corners. This creates crisp, defined corners. Give the entire cover a good press with an iron to smooth out any wrinkles. Your **zippered pillow cover with piping** is now ready for its insert.

### Tips for a Professional-Looking Piping Zipper Pillow Cover

Achieving a professional finish on your **piping zipper pillow cover** requires attention to detail. Small steps can make a big difference. These tips will help elevate your handmade project. They ensure your pillow looks store-bought, but with that special handmade charm.

Always pre-wash and dry your fabric before cutting. This prevents any future shrinkage of the pillow cover. It also helps remove any sizing or chemicals from the fabric. Iron your fabric thoroughly before cutting as well. A smooth, flat fabric allows for precise cuts and accurate seams.

Use a generous seam allowance, typically 1/2 inch or 5/8 inch. This gives you room to make adjustments if needed. It also makes your seams stronger and less likely to fray. Trim your seams after stitching to reduce bulk, especially at the corners. For example, if you sew a 1/2 inch seam, trim it down to 1/4 inch.

When sewing around corners, reduce your stitch length slightly. This makes the corner stitches stronger and smoother. Also, backstitch at the beginning and end of every seam. This locks the stitches in place and prevents them from unraveling. A strong seam creates a durable **DIY piping pillow**.

Finally, press, press, press! Pressing after every major sewing step is crucial. It flattens seams, helps fabric lay smoothly, and gives your project a crisp, finished appearance. For example, press the zipper seam open before sewing the ends. Press the piping seam allowance down before attaching it to the back panel. This careful pressing is key to a polished **zippered pillow cover with piping**.

### Caring for Your Handmade Piping Zipper Pillow Covers

You have poured your time and effort into creating a beautiful **piping zipper pillow cover**. Now, you want to ensure it stays lovely for years to come. Proper care and maintenance are essential. The way you clean your pillow cover depends largely on the fabric you chose.

Always check the care instructions for your fabric before washing. Most home decor fabrics can be spot cleaned or hand washed. For machine washable fabrics, use a gentle cycle with cold water. Turning the pillow cover inside out before washing can protect the piping and zipper. This also reduces wear on the fabric's right side.

Avoid harsh detergents or bleach, as these can damage fabric colors and fibers. If you need to treat a stain, do so gently with a mild stain remover. Test the remover on an inconspicuous area first. This ensures it does not discolor or damage your fabric. Gently scrub the stain, then rinse the area thoroughly.

When drying, air drying is often the best option for **DIY piping pillow** covers. Lay the cover flat or hang it to dry. Avoid direct sunlight, which can fade colors. If you must use a dryer, tumble dry on low heat. Remove the cover while it is still slightly damp. This helps prevent excessive wrinkling.

Iron your pillow cover on a medium setting if needed. Pay attention to the fabric content when selecting your iron temperature. Be careful around the zipper and piping, using a pressing cloth if necessary. Store your covers in a clean, dry place when not in use. Regular rotation of your pillows also helps reduce wear and tear.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: What fabric types are best for a piping zipper pillow cover?**
**A1:** Medium-weight home decor fabrics like cotton duck, linen, twill, or upholstery fabric work very well. These fabrics are durable and hold their shape, giving your pillow cover a professional finish. Avoid very stretchy or very thin fabrics for your first project. They can be challenging to work with, especially when adding piping and zippers.

**Q2: Can I use a regular zipper instead of an invisible zipper?**
**A2:** Yes, you can use a regular zipper, but it will be visible in the seam. An invisible zipper creates a cleaner, more professional look where the zipper teeth are hidden. If you use a regular zipper, choose a decorative one or install it carefully so it lays flat and neat.

**Q3: How do I ensure my piping is even around the corners?**
**A3:** To ensure even piping around corners, clip into the seam allowance of the piping tape before sewing it onto your pillow front. Make small cuts, about 1/4 inch deep, every half inch or so around the curve. This allows the piping to bend smoothly without puckering. Go slowly and adjust the fabric as you sew.

**Q4: What is the best way to cut fabric for square pillow covers?**
**A4:** The best way is to use a rotary cutter, a large cutting mat, and a clear ruler. This method allows for precise, straight cuts. If you do not have these tools, use sharp fabric scissors, tailor's chalk, and a measuring tape. Carefully mark your cutting lines on the fabric before you cut.

**Q5: My zipper often puckers. How can I prevent this?**
**A5:** Puckering often happens when the fabric is stretched or pulled during zipper installation. To prevent this, use plenty of pins to secure the zipper tape evenly to the fabric. You can also baste the zipper in place by hand or with long machine stitches before final sewing. Using an invisible zipper foot helps guide the fabric smoothly.

**Q6: Can a beginner sew a piping zipper pillow cover?**
**A6:** Yes, a beginner can absolutely sew a **piping zipper pillow cover**. This project introduces key sewing techniques like working with piping and installing zippers. Our detailed video tutorial breaks down each step, making it accessible. Start with a simple fabric and take your time. Practice on scrap fabric first to build confidence.
