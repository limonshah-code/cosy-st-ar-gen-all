## Initial Costs Beyond the Purchase Price

Buying a blue Frenchie involves more than just the puppy's purchase price. You must also account for essential upfront expenses. These initial costs set your puppy up for a healthy start. They ensure your new companion has everything needed to thrive in your home. I want to make sure you know about these important expenditures.

### First Vet Visit

Your new blue Frenchie puppy needs a vet check-up soon after coming home. This initial visit confirms the puppy's health. The vet will check for parasites and overall well-being. They will also discuss a vaccination schedule. This first visit typically costs between $50 and $150. It is a vital step to ensure your puppy is healthy.

### Vaccinations

Puppies need a series of vaccinations. These shots protect them from serious diseases. The core vaccines include distemper, parvovirus, and adenovirus. Rabies vaccination is also required by law. The full series of puppy shots can cost between $100 and $300. Booster shots are given over several weeks. Your vet will advise the best schedule.

### Spay or Neuter

Spaying (for females) or neutering (for males) is an important procedure. It prevents unwanted litters. It also offers significant health benefits. For females, it reduces the risk of certain cancers. For males, it can prevent testicular cancer and prostate issues. The cost for this surgery ranges from $200 to $500. Prices vary based on your vet and location. This is a one-time cost that contributes to your dog's long-term health.

### Microchipping

Microchipping is a small, permanent ID chip. It is inserted under your dog's skin. This chip helps identify your dog if it gets lost. Shelters and vets can scan the chip to find your contact information. The cost is usually around $30 to $50. Some breeders include this in the puppy's price. I highly recommend microchipping for all pets.

### Initial Supplies

You need basic supplies before your blue Frenchie arrives home. This includes:

*   **Crate:** A safe space for training and rest. Expect to pay $50 to $150.
*   **Bed:** A comfortable sleeping spot. Costs range from $30 to $100.
*   **Food Bowls:** Stainless steel or ceramic are good options. Around $15 to $40 for a set.
*   **Collar and Leash:** Essential for walks and identification. Budget $20 to $50.
*   **Puppy Food:** Start with the food the breeder uses. A quality bag costs $20 to $60.
*   **Toys:** Important for play and mental stimulation. Plan for $20 to $50 for a starter set.
*   **Grooming Supplies:** Brush, nail clippers, ear cleaner. About $30 to $70.

These initial supplies can total $200 to $600. These are one-time purchases for setting up your home.

Monthly premiums for a Frenchie can range from $30 to $70. It provides financial security in case of emergencies.

Blue Frenchies need regular grooming. Their short coats require minimal brushing. However, their skin folds need daily cleaning. This prevents infections. Their nails also need regular trimming. If you do this at home, costs are minimal. If you use a professional groomer, expect $20 to $50 per session. I clean my Frenchie's folds with a damp cloth every day. Training helps your blue Frenchie become a well-behaved companion. Puppy classes offer socialization and basic obedience. They can cost $100 to $200 for a series of classes. ### Toys and Enrichment

French Bulldogs enjoy playing. Providing a variety of toys keeps them entertained. Chew toys, puzzle toys, and squeaky toys are popular. Budget $10 to $30 per month for new toys. Mental enrichment is also important. This includes short training sessions or puzzle feeders. It helps prevent boredom.

It is wise to have an emergency fund for unexpected vet visits. French Bulldogs, like all dogs, can have accidents or sudden illnesses. An emergency fund of $500 to $2,000 can cover these unexpected costs. This money provides peace of mind. It allows you to get your dog the care it needs quickly.

## Finding a Reputable Blue Frenchie Breeder

Finding a good breeder for your blue Frenchie is crucial. It impacts your puppy's health and temperament. A responsible breeder cares deeply about their dogs. They do not just focus on making money. I always emphasize this point to new puppy owners.

### Prioritize Health Testing

A reputable breeder performs health tests on their breeding dogs. These tests screen for common French Bulldog health issues. This includes hip and elbow dysplasia, patella luxation, and heart conditions. They also test for genetic diseases relevant to the breed. These tests ensure the parents are healthy. This reduces the chance of passing on problems to puppies. Ask for proof of these health clearances. A good breeder will provide them willingly.

### Visit the Breeder and Parents

Always try to visit the breeder's home or kennel. See the living conditions of the puppies and parent dogs. Look for a clean and safe environment. Observe the puppies interacting with their mother and littermates. The mother dog should appear healthy and well-socialized. This visit gives you a chance to see where your puppy grew up. It also lets you meet the adult dogs.

### Ask Questions About Health Guarantees and Contracts

A good breeder offers a health guarantee. This guarantee outlines protection for you. It covers certain genetic health issues for a specific period. They also provide a clear contract. This contract details the puppy's health, return policy, and spay/neuter agreements. Read this contract carefully. Ask any questions you have.

### Avoid Puppy Mills or Backyard Breeders

Be cautious of breeders who:
*   Sell multiple breeds.
*   Always have puppies available.
*   Do not allow you to visit their facility.
*   Cannot provide health clearances for parents.
*   Sell puppies at very low prices.
*   Do not ask you questions about your experience or living situation.

These are signs of a puppy mill or a backyard breeder. They often prioritize profit over dog welfare. Puppies from these sources often have health or temperament issues. My experience shows that avoiding these sources saves future heartache and high vet bills.

### Inquire About Socialization and Care

Ask how the puppies are socialized. Good breeders expose puppies to various sights, sounds, and people. This helps puppies grow into well-adjusted adults. Ask about their diet and vaccination schedule. They should be able to provide records of all vet visits. They also should explain [what not to do with a Frenchie puppy](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-not-to-do-with-a-frenchie-puppy) to ensure proper development. A responsible breeder will also ask you many questions. They want to ensure their puppies go to good homes. This is a sign they care about their dogs' future.

This rarity contributes to their higher price compared to other Frenchie colors.
