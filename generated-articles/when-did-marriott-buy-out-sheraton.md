### When Did Marriott Buy Out Sheraton?

Marriott International officially completed its acquisition of Starwood Hotels & Resorts Worldwide, Inc., which owned the Sheraton brand, on **September 23, 2016**. This historic deal resulted in Marriott becoming the largest hotel company globally. The merger brought Sheraton and other Starwood brands into the Marriott portfolio.

## The Origins of Starwood and Sheraton's Journey

Before discussing the acquisition, it helps to understand where Sheraton came from. Sheraton Hotels has a long and interesting history. It started way back in 1937 in Springfield, Massachusetts. Two friends, Ernest Henderson and Robert Moore, bought their first hotel.

They quickly expanded, buying more hotels in the Northeast. Henderson and Moore focused on offering consistent service. Their goal was to make guests feel comfortable no matter which Sheraton they chose. This commitment to quality helped the brand grow fast.

By the mid-20th century, Sheraton was a household name. It became known for its upscale properties and reliable service. The brand often represented innovation in the hotel sector. They were among the first to introduce features like reservations systems.

Over the years, Sheraton changed hands several times. In 1998, Starwood Hotels & Resorts Worldwide acquired Sheraton. Starwood was a dynamic company. It built a diverse collection of brands. These brands included Westin, W Hotels, St. Regis, and Four Points by Sheraton. Starwood positioned Sheraton as its flagship full-service brand. It became a core part of their global strategy.

Under Starwood, Sheraton continued its global expansion. It maintained its position as a major player in the upscale segment. Many travelers recognized the distinct S-shaped logo. Starwood fostered a unique culture within its brand portfolio. This culture set the stage for the massive shift when Marriott entered the picture.

## Marriott's Strategic Vision and the Starwood Bid

Marriott International has always been a company with big ambitions. It aimed to grow its global footprint and expand its brand offerings. By the early 2010s, Marriott was a leader in the hotel industry. However, it still looked for ways to increase its market share. The company wanted to reach new customer segments.

Starwood Hotels & Resorts presented a compelling target. Starwood had a strong presence in luxury and lifestyle segments. Brands like W Hotels, St. Regis, and Westin appealed to a different clientele. Marriott saw an opportunity to gain these valuable brands. This would round out Marriott's existing portfolio.

The sheer size of Starwood also made it attractive. Acquiring Starwood meant adding thousands of hotel rooms. It also meant gaining a loyal customer base. Starwood Preferred Guest (SPG) was one of the industry's most respected loyalty programs. Marriott recognized the value of combining SPG with its own Marriott Rewards program. This promised an even more powerful loyalty offering.

Marriott's leaders believed a merger would create significant efficiencies. They could combine administrative functions. They could leverage shared technology and marketing resources. This would lead to cost savings and higher profits. The goal was to build an undisputed global leader. This leader would offer an unparalleled choice of brands.

The decision to pursue Starwood was a strategic move. It aimed to solidify Marriott's position. It also allowed them to compete more effectively with other global chains. This ambition set the stage for one of the largest mergers in hospitality history. The integration of brands like Sheraton into a larger system offered a comprehensive set of choices for every type of traveler.

## The Bidding War: Marriott vs. Anbang

The path to acquiring Starwood was not smooth. Initially, Marriott and Starwood announced their merger plans in November 2015. The deal was valued at approximately $12.2 billion at that time. Both companies saw a clear benefit in joining forces. They began the process of getting shareholder and regulatory approvals.

However, a new player entered the scene unexpectedly. Anbang Insurance Group, a Chinese insurance company, made a competing offer for Starwood. Anbang's initial bid came in March 2016. It was an all-cash offer. This offer surpassed Marriott's initial stock-and-cash proposal. The Anbang consortium, which included J.C. Flowers & Co. and Primavera Capital Group, increased their bid to about $14 billion. This presented a serious challenge to Marriott.

Starwood's board had to consider this higher offer. They weighed the financial benefits of Anbang's cash offer against the long-term strategic advantages of merging with Marriott. The situation created a tense bidding war. Marriott quickly responded. They increased their offer to approximately $13.6 billion, a combination of cash and stock. This showed Marriott's strong desire to win Starwood.

The back-and-forth bidding lasted a few weeks. It captured the attention of the entire financial world. Anbang eventually withdrew its offer in late March 2016. They cited "various market considerations" for their decision. This withdrawal cleared the path for Marriott. It allowed Marriott to proceed with its acquisition.

The bidding war highlighted Starwood's immense value. It proved that others also recognized the potential of its brands, including Sheraton. Marriott's persistence and revised offer ultimately secured the deal. This moment was crucial in shaping the future of global hospitality. It allowed Marriott to achieve its strategic goal.

## The Official Acquisition Date and Completion

The journey to the acquisition's completion involved many steps. After the bidding war, Marriott and Starwood worked towards final approvals. Both companies needed to satisfy various regulatory requirements worldwide. This included antitrust reviews in multiple countries. Shareholders of both Marriott and Starwood also had to vote to approve the merger.

Once these hurdles were cleared, the official closing date arrived. Marriott International officially completed its acquisition of Starwood Hotels & Resorts Worldwide, Inc. on **September 23, 2016**. This date marked a historic moment for the hospitality industry. It brought an end to an independent Starwood and started a new chapter under Marriott.

The deal's final value was approximately $13 billion. This figure reflected the increased offer Marriott made during the bidding war. It combined cash and Marriott stock. On this day, Starwood ceased to be an independent publicly traded company. Its brands, including the well-known Sheraton hotels, became part of the Marriott portfolio.

This completion created the world's largest hotel company. Marriott's brand count grew significantly. Its room count expanded dramatically. The acquisition instantly boosted Marriott's global presence. It also gave travelers more options. They could now choose from a vast array of hotels under one loyalty program. This made a significant difference for many frequent travelers.

The period immediately following September 23, 2016, was busy. Marriott began the complex process of integrating Starwood's operations. This included combining technology systems, marketing efforts, and, most importantly, loyalty programs. This date symbolizes the moment Marriott truly bought out Sheraton. It marked a new era for both companies and for travelers everywhere.

## Integrating Sheraton into the Marriott Ecosystem

The completion of the acquisition on September 23, 2016, was just the beginning. The real work began with integrating Starwood's extensive portfolio, including Sheraton, into Marriott's existing ecosystem. This was a massive and complex undertaking. It involved many different aspects of the business.

One of the most critical integration points was the loyalty programs. Starwood Preferred Guest (SPG) was highly regarded by its members. Marriott Rewards also had a large and loyal following. The challenge was to combine these into a single, cohesive program. In August 2018, Marriott launched "Marriott Bonvoy." This new program merged Marriott Rewards, SPG, and The Ritz-Carlton Rewards. It offered a unified platform for earning and redeeming points across all brands. This was a huge change for many. Now, when I stay at a Sheraton, I earn Marriott Bonvoy points. Marriott also focused on integrating operational systems. This included reservations, property management, and financial reporting. They worked to standardize processes where appropriate. However, they also aimed to maintain each brand's unique identity. Sheraton, for example, kept its distinct brand character. Yet, it benefited from Marriott's scale and resources.

The integration also involved a review of the brand portfolio. Marriott analyzed how each Starwood brand fit within its larger family. They sought to minimize overlap and differentiate offerings. ## FAQ Section

### Is Sheraton still called Sheraton after the acquisition?
Yes, Sheraton Hotels & Resorts continues to operate under its original name. Marriott International acquired the parent company, Starwood Hotels & Resorts, but retained all of Starwood's popular brands, including Sheraton. The brand maintains its distinct identity while benefiting from Marriott's extensive resources and global reach. It is a key part of the Marriott Bonvoy portfolio.

### What was the cost of the Marriott-Starwood acquisition?
Marriott International completed the acquisition of Starwood Hotels & Resorts for approximately **$13 billion**. This final price was a combination of cash and Marriott stock. The initial agreement was lower, but it increased after a bidding war with Anbang Insurance Group. This made it one of the largest mergers in hospitality history.

### How many hotels did Marriott acquire from Starwood?
Through the Starwood acquisition, Marriott gained approximately **1,300 properties** across more than 100 countries. This instantly added a significant number of hotels to Marriott's portfolio. It increased their total property count to over 6,000 worldwide. This expansion made Marriott the largest hotel company globally by a substantial margin.

### What happened to Starwood Preferred Guest (SPG) loyalty program?
The Starwood Preferred Guest (SPG) loyalty program was merged with Marriott Rewards and The Ritz-Carlton Rewards. In August 2018, Marriott launched a new unified program called **Marriott Bonvoy**. SPG members saw their points and status transition to Marriott Bonvoy. This created a single, comprehensive loyalty program across all Marriott brands, including Sheraton.

### Does Sheraton have its own loyalty program now?
No, Sheraton does not have a separate loyalty program anymore. As part of the Marriott acquisition, all Sheraton hotels are integrated into the **Marriott Bonvoy** loyalty program. Guests staying at Sheraton properties earn and redeem Marriott Bonvoy points. They also receive elite benefits based on their Bonvoy status. This simplifies loyalty for frequent travelers.

### Did the acquisition improve Sheraton hotels?
Marriott launched a significant "Sheraton Transformation" initiative after the acquisition. This program involved substantial investment in property renovations and upgrades. Many Sheraton hotels have seen modernized lobbies, improved dining, and updated technology. The goal was to revitalize the brand and enhance the guest experience, making Sheraton more competitive.
