## What is a Mixed French Bulldog?

A mixed French Bulldog combines a purebred French Bulldog with another dog breed. This creates a hybrid, or designer dog. People seek these mixes for different reasons. They might want unique looks or traits. Some hope for better health traits from the other parent breed.

These mixes often inherit characteristics from both parent breeds. This means their appearance and temperament can vary greatly. For example, a Frenchton mixes a French Bulldog with a Boston Terrier. A Frug is a French Bulldog Poodle mix. Learning about the parent breeds helps you understand the potential characteristics of the mix. Different mixes bring different traits. For instance, a French Bulldog mixed with a Pomeranian creates a "Frenchie Pom." This mix might have more energy and fluffier fur than a purebred French Bulldog. Each mix offers a unique set of qualities. Understanding these combinations is key. We have more information on [what is a French Bulldog mixed with a Pomeranian called](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-a-french-bulldog-mixed-with-a-pomeranian-called).

Mixed French Bulldogs are not recognized by major kennel clubs like the AKC. This is because they are not purebreds. Their varied genetics often make them less predictable than purebreds. Breeders often market them as designer dogs. This can affect their price.

## Factors Influencing the Cost of a Mixed French Bulldog

Many elements play a role in the price you pay for a mixed French Bulldog. These factors can cause significant price differences. It is important to consider each one. This helps you understand where the cost comes from.

### Parent Breeds

The specific breeds involved in the mix greatly impact the price. Some breeds are more expensive than others. For example, a mix with a costly purebred parent might cost more. A French Bulldog mixed with a Poodle, creating a "French Boodle," might be pricier due to the Poodle's popularity and demand. High-demand parent breeds often lead to higher puppy prices.

The rarity of the parent breed also matters. If one parent breed is uncommon, the mix could be more expensive. This is basic supply and demand. Unique combinations might command higher prices too. ### Breeder Reputation and Practices

A reputable breeder charges more than a backyard breeder or puppy mill. Good breeders invest in health testing for parent dogs. They provide proper nutrition and early socialization for puppies. These practices ensure healthier, well-adjusted puppies. This added investment reflects in the price.

Puppy mills often sell dogs at lower prices. However, these puppies frequently have health or behavioral problems. Buying from such sources can lead to high vet bills later. It is always better to choose a responsible breeder. They offer health guarantees and support.

### Location

The geographical location affects dog prices. Cities with higher living costs often have more expensive puppies. Demand for specific breeds also varies by region. A mixed French Bulldog might cost more in a populous area.

Good breeders perform health screenings on parent dogs. This helps prevent genetic diseases. Common French Bulldog health issues include breathing problems and spinal conditions. Testing for these adds to the breeder's costs. These costs are passed on to the buyer.

A health guarantee from a breeder offers peace of mind. It means the breeder stands behind their puppies' health. This added assurance often justifies a higher initial price. It can save you money on vet bills in the long run.

## Average Price Ranges for Popular Mixed French Bulldog Breeds

The "mixed French Bulldog cost" is not a single number. It depends heavily on the specific mix. Some combinations are more popular and therefore command higher prices. Let's look at some common mixes and their general price ranges.

### Frenchton (French Bulldog x Boston Terrier)

The Frenchton is a very popular mix. It blends the French Bulldog with the Boston Terrier. These dogs are typically lively and affectionate. Their price often ranges from $800 to $2,500. This depends on the breeder's reputation and the puppy's traits.

Frenchtons often inherit fewer breathing issues than purebred French Bulldogs. This is because Boston Terriers have slightly longer snouts. Their popularity keeps their price competitive. Many people find them a good alternative to purebred French Bulldogs.

### Frug (French Bulldog x Pug)

The Frug is a mix of a French Bulldog and a Pug. They are known for being playful and charming. Their price range is typically $1,000 to $2,800. This mix often carries the charming wrinkles of both breeds.

Both French Bulldogs and Pugs can have breathing issues. A Frug might inherit these tendencies. Prospective owners should be aware of potential health considerations. Their sweet nature makes them highly desirable.

### French Boodle (French Bulldog x Poodle)

The French Boodle combines a French Bulldog with a Poodle. This mix often creates a dog with a wavy or curly coat, which can be low-shedding. Their price can range from $1,500 to $4,000, sometimes higher. Poodle mixes are popular for their hypoallergenic potential.

The Poodle parent can introduce new health considerations but also potentially reduce breathing problems. These dogs often have a good temperament. They are intelligent and eager to please. The coat type can influence grooming needs and costs.

### Frotbull (French Bulldog x English Bulldog)

The Frotbull is a mix of a French Bulldog and an English Bulldog. This combination creates a very sturdy and often larger dog. Their price range typically falls between $1,200 and $3,500. Both parent breeds are known for their strong build.

This mix can have more significant health challenges due to both parent breeds. They might inherit pronounced breathing issues and joint problems. Owners must commit to managing these potential health needs. ### Other Less Common Mixes

Many other French Bulldog mixes exist. These include mixes with Beagles, Corgis, or Chihuahuas. Their prices can vary widely. They might be cheaper if the other parent breed is common. Rare or unique mixes could be more expensive.

For example, a French Bulldog mixed with a Dachshund might be called a "French Doxie." Their price could be anywhere from $800 to $2,500. The cost largely depends on demand and the availability of such specific mixes. Each combination offers a unique personality and set of needs.

## Beyond the Purchase Price: Initial Costs of Owning a Mixed French Bulldog

The purchase price of your mixed French Bulldog is just the beginning. There are many other initial expenses. You need to prepare for these costs. They are crucial for your new puppy's well-being. Planning helps avoid financial surprises.

### Veterinary Check-ups and Vaccinations

Your new puppy needs immediate veterinary care. This includes a thorough check-up shortly after you bring them home. Puppies require a series of vaccinations to protect them from common diseases. These shots usually start at around 6-8 weeks of age.

The cost for these initial vet visits and vaccinations can range from $100 to $300. This includes deworming treatments as well. Some puppies may also need microchipping. This is a small procedure for identification purposes.

### Spay/Neuter Surgery

Spaying or neutering your mixed French Bulldog is highly recommended. This procedure prevents unwanted litters. It also offers significant health benefits. For females, it reduces the risk of uterine infections and certain cancers. For males, it lowers the risk of prostate problems and testicular cancer.

The cost of spay or neuter surgery varies. It can range from $150 to $500, depending on your location and vet clinic. Some rescue organizations include this cost in their adoption fees. This makes adoption a more budget-friendly option.

### Initial Supplies

When you bring your mixed French Bulldog home, you need essential supplies. These items are necessary for comfort and safety. They include a dog crate, food and water bowls, a leash, collar, and initial toys. A comfortable dog bed is also important.

You also need puppy food specifically formulated for their age and size. Training treats are helpful for positive reinforcement. The total for these initial supplies can be between $200 and $500. Some items, like a crate, are a one-time purchase.

### Training and Socialization

Early training and socialization are vital for puppies. They help your mixed French Bulldog become a well-behaved adult dog. Puppy classes teach basic commands and appropriate interactions. These classes also offer a chance for your puppy to socialize safely with other dogs and people.

Group puppy classes often cost $100 to $250 for a series. Private training sessions are more expensive. They can range from $50 to $150 per hour. Consider investing in training early on. This can prevent behavioral problems in the future.

## FAQ Section

### Is a mixed French Bulldog cheaper than a purebred?
Generally, yes, a mixed French Bulldog is often cheaper than a purebred. Purebred French Bulldogs can cost anywhere from $2,500 to $10,000, especially for rare colors. Mixed French Bulldogs typically range from $800 to $4,500. The specific mix, breeder, and demand still influence the final price. However, their hybrid status usually makes them more accessible.

### What is the most expensive French Bulldog mix?
The most expensive French Bulldog mix usually involves a purebred parent that is also costly or a very desirable Poodle mix. For example, a "French Boodle" (French Bulldog x Poodle) can be quite expensive, sometimes reaching $4,000 or more. This is due to the Poodle's popularity for its low-shedding coat and intelligence. Rare colors or specific traits can also drive up the price.

### Do mixed French Bulldogs have fewer health issues?
Mixed French Bulldogs often have a wider genetic pool, which *can* potentially lead to fewer health issues compared to purebreds. This is known as hybrid vigor. However, they can still inherit health problems common to either parent breed. For instance, brachycephalic (flat-faced) mixes might still have breathing issues. It is essential to research both parent breeds' common health concerns.

### How much do French Bulldog mixes cost from a rescue?
Adopting a French Bulldog mix from a rescue or shelter is generally the most affordable option. Adoption fees typically range from $150 to $500. These fees often include important initial services. This covers spay/neuter surgery, initial vaccinations, and microchipping. Rescues focus on finding good homes for dogs in need.

### What are common French Bulldog mixes?
Common French Bulldog mixes include the Frenchton (Boston Terrier mix), Frug (Pug mix), French Boodle (Poodle mix), and Frotbull (English Bulldog mix). Other mixes combine French Bulldogs with Chihuahuas, Beagles, or Corgis. Each mix brings unique characteristics. Their appearance and temperament depend on which parent's traits they inherit more strongly.
