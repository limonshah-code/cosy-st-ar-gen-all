## Understanding Why Your Dog Is Crying And Shaking

Imagine this: you come home, eager to greet your best friend, only to find your dog hiding, whimpering, and trembling uncontrollably. This sight can fill any pet parent with worry. When your dog is crying and shaking, it is natural to feel concern. These behaviors are not typical. They are strong signals your dog tries to send you. Your dog feels discomfort, fear, or pain.

My own dog once started shaking after a loud thunderstorm. He then cried at my feet. I knew I had to act fast. This article explores the common reasons behind these distress signals. We will look at both health issues and emotional problems. We will cover how to respond, when to seek veterinary help, and how to support your dog. Understanding these signs helps you give your dog the best care.

### Takeaway

When your dog cries and shakes, it shows distress. Immediate action helps them.

*   Observe other symptoms. Look for changes in eating or activity.
*   Check the surroundings. Loud noises or new people can cause fear.
*   Provide comfort if your dog accepts it. Speak softly and gently.
*   Contact your veterinarian. This behavior often needs a vet's opinion.
*   Rule out medical issues first. Health problems can cause crying and shaking.

When your dog is crying and shaking, it means they need help. This is a clear signal of distress or discomfort. Your dog may experience pain, fear, anxiety, or illness. It is important to find the root cause quickly to help your pet feel safe and well again.

## Medical Causes: When Illness Is the Reason Your Dog Cries and Shakes

When a dog cries and shakes, a medical issue is often the reason. These behaviors are common signs of physical pain or illness. It is important to consider health problems first. Your dog cannot speak words. Its actions become its voice.

Dogs might cry because of pain. They might shake because of weakness or discomfort. Many conditions can cause this. This includes injuries or internal problems. Always check for other physical symptoms. These symptoms help you understand the problem.

### Pain and Injuries

Pain is a major reason a dog cries and shakes. Dogs hide pain well, but sometimes it becomes too much. An injury might not be visible. Your dog could have stepped on something sharp. It might have a sprain or a broken bone. Pain from an injury causes vocalization. It also causes trembling.

Arthritis is another common pain source, especially in older dogs. Joint stiffness and pain make movement hard. This pain can cause a dog to cry when it tries to stand or walk. Trembling may result from weakness in painful joints. Internal pain is also possible. Stomach upset, bladder infections, or organ pain can make a dog cry and shake. Your dog may guard a painful area of its body.

### Nausea and Gastrointestinal Issues

Digestive problems can make dogs feel very unwell. Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea often cause distress. A dog with an upset stomach might cry. It might also shake its body. This shaking comes from general discomfort. It may also come from weakness.

Conditions like bloat are severe. Bloat causes extreme pain and can be deadly. A dog with bloat will cry, pace, and shake. They may try to vomit but cannot. Pancreatitis is another painful condition. It causes abdominal pain. Dogs with pancreatitis often cry, shake, and refuse food. If your dog is [shaking and not eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-shaking-and-not-eating), especially with other symptoms like vomiting, it needs urgent vet care.

### Neurological Conditions

Some neurological problems affect a dog's movements and behavior. Seizures are a clear example. During or after a seizure, a dog might shake. It might cry out. These behaviors result from uncontrolled brain activity. The dog is often disoriented afterwards.

Other neurological conditions can also cause these signs. Distemper is a viral disease. It affects the brain and nervous system. Dogs with distemper can show tremors and pain. They may also cry. Vestibular disease affects balance. It can cause shaking and disorientation. The dog might cry due to confusion or dizziness.

### Metabolic Disorders and Organ Dysfunction

Metabolic imbalances can lead to trembling and distress. Hypoglycemia, or low blood sugar, is one such condition. Dogs with low blood sugar become weak. They might shake uncontrollably. They can also cry from general weakness or confusion. This is common in small puppies if they do not eat enough. It can also happen in diabetic dogs.

Kidney or liver disease can also make a dog feel sick. These conditions cause toxins to build up in the body. This makes the dog feel nauseous or weak. A sick dog might cry or shake. This indicates their body is not functioning properly. These conditions usually show other signs like increased thirst or changes in appetite.

### Poisoning or Toxin Ingestion

Accidental poisoning is an emergency. Many common household items are toxic to dogs. Ingesting toxins can cause a wide range of symptoms. These include crying and shaking. The type of toxin changes the specific symptoms. Common toxins include certain plants, human medications, and household cleaners.

Symptoms of poisoning appear suddenly. They often worsen fast. Other signs might include vomiting, diarrhea, drooling, or weakness. If you suspect your dog ate something harmful, seek immediate vet attention. Do not wait for symptoms to worsen. Early intervention is critical for good outcomes.

### Infections and Fevers

Infections can make a dog feel unwell. A high fever often causes shivering. This shivering might look like shaking. A dog with a fever might also cry. This indicates discomfort. The infection could be anywhere in the body. It could be a urinary tract infection or a severe bacterial infection.

Dogs become lethargic when they have an infection. They might lose their appetite. They may show other signs of sickness. The combination of fever, crying, and shaking suggests a significant infection. This needs veterinary diagnosis and treatment.

## Behavioral Causes: Understanding Your Dog's Emotional Distress

Sometimes, a dog cries and shakes because of its feelings. Dogs experience complex emotions. These include fear, anxiety, and stress. These feelings can cause physical reactions. Your dog's body language tells you much. Shaking and crying are clear distress signals.

It is important to understand your dog's daily life. Recent changes can cause emotional upset. For example, a new home or a new family member. Recognizing behavioral causes helps you address them without medication. It helps you support your dog's emotional health.

### Fear and Phobias

Fear is a powerful emotion in dogs. It often causes crying and shaking. Loud noises are a common trigger. Thunderstorms, fireworks, and even vacuum cleaners can cause intense fear. Many dogs develop noise phobias. They tremble uncontrollably. They may whine or howl loudly. My own dog cries and shakes during summer thunderstorms. This is his way of showing extreme fear.

New experiences can also cause fear. A first visit to the vet can be scary. Meeting a new person or animal can feel threatening. If your dog is scared, it might try to hide. It might also cry and shake. This behavior aims to show its distress. It also asks for comfort or safety. Understanding what your dog fears helps you protect it.

### Anxiety and Stress

Anxiety is a common behavioral problem in dogs. It differs from fear. Fear is a reaction to a specific threat. Anxiety is a response to the thought of a threat. Separation anxiety is a well-known type. Dogs with separation anxiety become very distressed when left alone. They might cry loudly. They might shake. They may also chew on things or have accidents inside. This happens because they miss their human companions. For tips on managing this, read about [how to get a dog to stop crying when left alone](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-get-a-dog-to-stop-crying-when-left-alone).

Generalized anxiety is also possible. Dogs with this type of anxiety worry about many things. Small changes in routine can trigger it. Stress can also cause crying and shaking. A new pet in the house creates stress. Moving to a new home creates stress. Conflict in the household also causes stress. Dogs are sensitive to our emotions. They react to stressful environments. When your dog is [shaking but seems fine](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-shaking-but-seems-fine), anxiety might be a hidden cause.

### Excitement or Submissive Behavior

Sometimes, crying and shaking are not distress signals. Extreme excitement can cause trembling. This is often seen in puppies. They might get so excited they shake with energy. They might also make small cries or whimpers. This is usually temporary and resolves quickly. It happens during playtime or when greeting a favorite person.

Submissive urination is another related behavior. A submissive dog might shake when greeted. It might also whimper or cry. This behavior shows the dog recognizes your dominance. It is trying to avoid conflict. This behavior is more common in shy or insecure dogs. It is important to avoid punishing this behavior. It is a sign of respect, not defiance.

### Attention Seeking

Dogs are smart. They learn what behaviors get your attention. If crying and shaking worked once, they might try it again. This is a learned behavior. It is more common if you rewarded the behavior in the past. Your dog might be bored or lonely. It might want to play or go for a walk.

It is important not to reinforce this behavior. Do not give attention when your dog cries and shakes for no clear reason. Wait until your dog calms down. Then, reward calm behavior. This teaches your dog that quiet behavior gets attention. However, first ensure no medical or genuine emotional distress causes the behavior. If your dog is [crying for no reason](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-crying-for-no-reason) or [shaking for no reason](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-shaking-for-no-reason), consider if it is learned behavior or a subtle underlying issue.

## Environmental Factors: External Triggers for Crying and Shaking

Your dog's environment plays a big role in its behavior. External factors can cause crying and shaking. Dogs are sensitive creatures. They react strongly to changes around them. Understanding these environmental triggers helps you protect your pet.

The home environment should feel safe. Unexpected changes or harsh conditions can cause distress. It is important to control what your dog experiences. This helps keep them calm and happy.

### Temperature Extremes

Dogs react to extreme temperatures. Both cold and heat can cause shaking. When a dog is cold, it shivers. This shiver looks like shaking. It is a natural response to warm the body. Small dogs with thin coats are more prone to cold. Older dogs might also feel cold more easily. Crying could happen if they are too cold and uncomfortable.

Heatstroke is a serious emergency. Dogs overheat easily, especially in hot weather. They cannot sweat like humans. Excessive panting and shaking are signs of heatstroke. A dog might cry from discomfort or distress. Other signs include lethargy, drooling, and red gums. If your dog is [panting and shaking](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-panting-and-shaking) in hot weather, get it to a cool place immediately and seek vet care.

### Loud Noises and Sudden Events

Sudden loud noises often trigger fear in dogs. Fireworks, thunderstorms, and construction sounds are common culprits. Dogs have sensitive hearing. These noises can be overwhelming. They often react by crying and shaking. They might try to hide under furniture. This is a clear sign of distress.

Unexpected events also cause distress. A sudden car backfiring can startle a dog. A strange person entering the home can be alarming. These events cause a sudden surge of adrenaline. This leads to physical reactions like trembling and vocalization. Your dog needs comfort and a safe space during these times.

### Unfamiliar People or Animals

Dogs can feel anxious around new people or animals. Some dogs are naturally shy. They might cry or shake when meeting strangers. This is a sign of discomfort or fear. It is their way of saying, "I am unsure about this." For some dogs, unfamiliar people represent a potential threat.

Introducing new pets can also be stressful. Dogs need time to adjust. A new cat or dog in the home changes the established routine. This can cause anxiety. The resident dog might cry or shake. This shows its stress about the new situation. Always introduce new pets slowly and carefully.

## Age-Related Issues: Senior Dogs and Puppies

The age of your dog matters. Puppies and senior dogs have different needs. They show distress in unique ways. What causes crying and shaking in a puppy is different from an old dog. Understanding these age-specific issues helps you provide proper care.

Puppies are still learning about the world. Older dogs experience the effects of aging. Both groups need special attention. Their behaviors reflect their developmental stage or physical decline.

### Cognitive Dysfunction Syndrome (CDS) in Senior Dogs

Senior dogs can develop Cognitive Dysfunction Syndrome. This is similar to Alzheimer's in humans. Dogs with CDS become disoriented. They might forget familiar routines or people. This confusion can cause anxiety. They might cry aimlessly. They might shake from confusion or stress.

CDS also affects their sleep-wake cycles. An old dog might cry at night. They may also pace and shake. This happens because their internal clock is off. They are confused about the time of day. This condition needs vet diagnosis. There are treatments available to help manage symptoms. If your old dog is [crying at night](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-stop-an-old-dog-crying-at-night), especially if combined with shaking, CDS could be a factor.

### Arthritis and Joint Pain in Older Dogs

Aging often brings arthritis. This condition causes joint pain and stiffness. Older dogs with arthritis find movement painful. Standing, walking, or climbing stairs causes discomfort. They might cry when they move. They might also shake their legs due to weakness or pain.

The pain from arthritis can be constant. This leads to chronic distress. Your dog might seem restless. It might find it hard to get comfortable. This pain can cause a general trembling throughout the body. There are pain management options. These options improve their quality of life.

### Puppy Crying and Shaking

Puppies cry for many reasons. They are babies. They get lonely easily. A new puppy might cry in its crate at night. It misses its littermates and mother. This crying is often accompanied by shivering. This shivering comes from cold or fear. For help, read about [how to stop a dog from crying in crate](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-stop-a-dog-from-crying-in-crate).

Puppies also cry when hungry or needing to go potty. They shake if they are cold. They shake if they are scared. Introducing a puppy to its new home is a big change. They need comfort and reassurance. They need a warm, safe place to sleep. Socialization is key for puppies. It helps them feel secure in new situations. This reduces fear-based crying and shaking later on.

## What To Do When Your Dog Cries And Shakes: Immediate Steps

Seeing your dog cry and shake is alarming. Knowing what to do first helps immensely. Your immediate actions matter. They can provide comfort. They can also help you gather vital information for your vet. Do not panic. Remain calm. Your calm energy helps your dog.

Always prioritize safety. Both for your dog and yourself. Approach carefully. Observe first. Then, decide on the best next steps.

### Observe Other Symptoms and Body Language

Look closely at your dog. What else is happening? Is your dog refusing food? Is it limping? Does it seem disoriented? Are its gums pale? Note any other unusual behaviors. These details give clues. They help you narrow down the cause.

Pay attention to body language. Is your dog tucked away? Is its tail down? Is it drooling? Is it panting excessively? These signs, along with crying and shaking, paint a clearer picture. For example, [why is my dog shaking and throwing up](https://cosyhomecreation.com/why-is-my-dog-shaking-and-throwing-up) points to a different issue than just shaking from fear.

### Assess the Environment

Check your surroundings. Is there a loud noise? Did a stranger just arrive? Is the room too hot or too cold? Did your dog just encounter something new? Think about recent events. A change in routine can trigger anxiety. Look for anything that could cause fear or discomfort.

Remove the trigger if possible. Turn off loud music. Move your dog to a quiet room. Provide a safe space for your dog to retreat. This helps your dog feel secure. It also helps you see if the shaking stops once the trigger is gone.

### Provide Comfort (If Appropriate)

Some dogs want comfort when distressed. Speak in a calm, soothing voice. Offer gentle petting. Create a quiet, safe space. A cozy bed or a blanket can provide security. Your presence can be reassuring. For general guidance, consider [what should I do if my dog is shaking](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-should-i-do-if-my-dog-is-shaking).

However, not all dogs want to be touched. A dog in pain might snap if you try to touch it. Respect your dog's signals. If it backs away or growls, give it space. Do not force interaction. Offer comfort from a distance instead.

### Avoid Punishment

Never punish your dog for crying or shaking. These are involuntary responses. Your dog cannot control them. Punishment only increases fear and anxiety. It damages your bond. Your dog will learn to hide its symptoms. This makes it harder for you to help.

Focus on identifying the cause. Then, address the root problem. Use positive reinforcement for calm behavior. Reward your dog when it is relaxed and quiet. This builds trust and security.

### Contact Your Veterinarian

If crying and shaking continue, contact your vet. This is always the best next step. Even if you suspect a behavioral cause, a vet visit is important. They can rule out medical problems. Medical issues need prompt treatment.

Describe all symptoms. Tell your vet about recent changes. Explain what you observed. Your detailed information helps the vet diagnose the issue. Do not delay, especially if symptoms appear suddenly or worsen quickly. ## Veterinary Diagnosis and Treatment: Getting Professional Help

When your dog cries and shakes, a trip to the veterinarian is often necessary. A professional diagnosis is crucial. It helps find the exact cause. Self-diagnosis is risky. It can delay proper treatment. Vets have the training and tools to assess your dog's health thoroughly.

They perform various tests. They ask many questions. This helps them understand your dog's condition. Getting the right diagnosis leads to effective treatment. It ensures your dog gets the best care.

### The Veterinary Examination

Your vet will start with a full physical examination. They will check your dog's temperature, heart rate, and breathing. They will feel your dog's body for signs of pain or swelling. They will check the mouth, eyes, and ears. This initial exam provides a lot of information. It can pinpoint obvious injuries or abnormalities.

The vet will also ask you many questions. They will ask about your dog's medical history. They will ask about recent changes in behavior. They will ask about diet, medications, and any recent stressors. Be honest and detailed. Your observations are vital clues.

### Diagnostic Tests

Based on the physical exam and history, your vet might recommend tests. Blood tests are common. They check for infections, organ dysfunction, or metabolic imbalances. A complete blood count can show inflammation or infection. A blood chemistry panel checks kidney and liver function.

Urine tests can detect infections. X-rays help identify bone injuries or internal blockages. Ultrasounds can give a view of soft tissues. In some cases, specialized neurological tests are needed. These tests help rule out or confirm specific conditions. They provide clear data for an accurate diagnosis.

### Treatment Options for Medical Causes

Treatment depends on the diagnosis. If pain is the cause, your vet will prescribe pain relief medication. This might include anti-inflammatory drugs. For infections, antibiotics are necessary. If your dog has a metabolic disorder, the vet will manage it. For example, they might adjust diet or medication for diabetes.

In cases of poisoning, the vet acts fast. They might induce vomiting. They might administer activated charcoal. Sometimes, intravenous fluids are needed. For injuries, treatment ranges from rest to surgery. Follow all vet instructions carefully. This ensures a full recovery.

### Managing Behavioral Issues with Professional Guidance

If the vet rules out medical causes, the problem is likely behavioral. The vet might suggest working with a certified dog behaviorist. Behaviorists understand animal psychology. They can help identify triggers. They develop behavior modification plans. This might include counter-conditioning or desensitization.

Medication can sometimes help severe anxiety. Anti-anxiety medications calm your dog. They work best with behavior therapy. Your vet might prescribe these for a short time. This helps your dog learn new coping mechanisms. It is important to address the underlying anxiety. Only medication does not fix the issue.
