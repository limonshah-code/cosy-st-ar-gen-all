## Historical and Scientific Names: *Mustela Putorius Furo*

Beyond their common name, ferrets have a formal scientific classification. Their scientific name is *Mustela putorius furo*. The species name, *putorius*, links them to polecats. *Furo* is a Latin word meaning "thief," which playfully hints at their habit of stashing toys and treasures.

This scientific name helps biologists categorize ferrets accurately. It distinguishes them from other similar-looking animals. For example, some people might wonder if a ferret is a cat or a dog. ## The Ferret's Ancestry: Polecat Connections

To understand what a ferret is also known as, we must look at their ancestry. Domestic ferrets are descendants of the European polecat (*Mustela putorius*). This connection is evident in their scientific name, *Mustela putorius furo*. The domestication process likely began thousands of years ago. People probably started keeping polecats to control rodent populations. They were excellent hunters and could easily enter burrows. This made them valuable partners for humans.

Over generations, polecats bred in captivity became tamer and more accustomed to human presence. This gradual process led to the development of the domestic ferret we know today. While they share common ancestors, there are key differences between a wild polecat and a domestic ferret. Polecats are generally darker in color. They are also much more aggressive and difficult to handle than ferrets. My ferret is very friendly and enjoys being picked up. A wild polecat would not.

The domestication of ferrets allowed humans to use them for various purposes. Ferreting, the practice of using ferrets to hunt rabbits, became popular in Europe. This practice helped shape their reputation as working animals. Their natural hunting instincts are still present in domestic ferrets. They love to chase toys and explore tunnels. This strong link to their polecat ancestors explains many of their natural behaviors. It also explains why some people mistakenly refer to them as polecats. However, they are distinct due to centuries of selective breeding. No, a ferret is not a rodent. Rodents belong to the order Rodentia, which includes animals like rats, mice, and hamsters. Ferrets are part of the Mustelidae family, which is in the order Carnivora. This means they are carnivores, primarily eating meat, unlike herbivorous rodents.

### Are ferrets related to weasels?

Yes, ferrets are closely related to weasels. Ferrets belong to the genus *Mustela*, which also includes weasels, stoats, and minks. They are all members of the Mustelidae family. This family also contains badgers and otters.
