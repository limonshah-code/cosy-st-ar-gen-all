## The Real Cons of Owning a Shar Pei: What to Know

Owning a dog brings much joy and love into your life. However, every breed has its unique set of challenges. When considering a Shar Pei, it's crucial to understand the potential downsides before you bring one home. Many people are drawn to their distinctive wrinkled appearance, but the cons of owning a Shar Pei extend far beyond their looks. I want to share my insights on what you should prepare for. This article will explore the specific difficulties and commitments involved in owning this unique and sometimes demanding breed. We will cover common health issues, temperament quirks, and the significant care requirements that make Shar Peis a breed best suited for experienced and dedicated owners.

### Takeaway:

*   Shar Peis often face extensive and expensive health problems, especially skin and eye conditions.
*   Their stubborn nature requires consistent, early training and socialization.
*   Regular and specialized grooming is essential to prevent skin infections in their wrinkles.
*   They can be aloof or aggressive towards strangers and other animals without proper guidance.
*   High sensitivity means they struggle with separation anxiety and need consistent companionship.

### What are the cons of owning a Shar Pei?

Owning a Shar Pei involves significant commitment due to their specific needs. They are prone to extensive health issues like skin infections and eye problems, leading to high veterinary costs. Their strong-willed temperament demands consistent training and early socialization, making them less suitable for first-time dog owners. They also require diligent grooming to maintain their unique wrinkled skin.

## Extensive Health Problems and High Veterinary Costs

Shar Peis are beautiful dogs, but they come with a predisposition to many breed-specific health issues. This can translate into significant emotional and financial burdens for owners. Their famous wrinkles, while charming, are often the source of many problems. You might find yourself visiting the vet more often than with other breeds.

One common issue is **Shar Pei Fever**, also known as Familial Shar Pei Fever (FSF). This is a hereditary condition unique to the breed. Dogs experience recurrent fevers, lethargy, swollen joints, and facial swelling. FSF episodes can last 24-48 hours and can lead to serious kidney or liver amyloidosis, a potentially fatal condition. Managing FSF requires careful observation and prompt veterinary attention, which adds to healthcare expenses. I have seen owners spend a lot of time and money just tracking this condition.

Skin problems are another major concern. The deep wrinkles can trap moisture, dirt, and bacteria, leading to frequent skin fold infections (pyoderma). These infections cause itching, redness, and discomfort for your dog. They often require medication, special shampoos, and regular cleaning routines. Owners must diligently clean and dry their Shar Pei's folds, especially after baths or wet weather. Neglecting this care can lead to painful chronic skin issues.

Eye problems are also prevalent in Shar Peis due to their facial structure. **Entropion**, where the eyelids roll inward, is common. The eyelashes then rub against the cornea, causing irritation, pain, and potentially ulcers or blindness. This condition often requires surgery, sometimes multiple surgeries, to correct. Another eye condition is **Cherry Eye**, where the tear gland protrudes from behind the third eyelid. This also needs surgical correction. Preparing for these potential health issues is a must before owning a Shar Pei.

## Stubborn Temperament and Training Challenges

Shar Peis possess a strong-willed and independent nature, which can make training a significant challenge. They are not like some eager-to-please breeds that quickly pick up commands. You might find that your Shar Pei thinks things over before deciding to obey. This intelligence means they can learn quickly, but their stubbornness often means they choose when to apply that knowledge.

Early and consistent training is absolutely crucial for this breed. Without firm, patient, and positive reinforcement, a Shar Pei can become difficult to manage. They need an owner who can establish clear boundaries and leadership from a young age. I've learned that yelling or harsh methods simply do not work with a Shar Pei; they tend to shut down or become more resistant. Instead, short, fun training sessions with high-value treats are far more effective.

Their independent streak also extends to house training. While intelligent, some Shar Peis can be slower to grasp house-training concepts. You must be prepared for a consistent routine, frequent potty breaks, and praise when they go outside. Accidents inside are a normal part of puppyhood for any dog, but with a Shar Pei, patience is key. Their strong personalities require an owner who enjoys the training process and understands dog behavior. This breed thrives on structure and clear expectations. ## Intensive Grooming Needs for Skin Folds

The Shar Pei's signature wrinkles are charming, but they demand rigorous grooming. Their deep skin folds can easily become breeding grounds for bacteria and yeast if not maintained properly. This is not a low-maintenance coat. Neglecting their skin can lead to painful infections and bad odors. You must commit to a regular cleaning routine.

Daily cleaning of their wrinkles is often necessary, especially around the face and neck. I use a soft, damp cloth or a pet-safe wipe to gently clean inside each fold. After cleaning, it is vital to thoroughly dry the skin folds. Moisture left behind is the primary cause of irritation and infection. Some owners even use cornstarch or special powders to keep the folds dry. This meticulous attention is not something you can skip.

Bathing also requires special consideration. You must use a veterinarian-recommended shampoo formulated for sensitive skin. After bathing, drying the Shar Pei completely is even more critical. I spend extra time towel-drying and sometimes even use a low-heat hairdryer to ensure every fold is dry. If any moisture remains, it quickly creates an environment where skin issues can flare up. This level of grooming commitment is significant and cannot be overlooked when considering this breed. Proper grooming helps prevent many common skin conditions and keeps your Shar Pei comfortable.

## Socialization Challenges and Aloofness

Shar Peis are known for their reserved and often aloof nature, especially towards strangers. This is not a breed that typically greets everyone with a wagging tail and boundless enthusiasm. Their natural inclination is to be wary, which can be seen as a positive trait for a guard dog, but it also presents significant socialization challenges. Without proper early exposure, this wariness can escalate into fear or aggression.

From a young age, a Shar Pei puppy needs extensive and varied socialization. This means introducing them to many different people, sounds, sights, and other friendly dogs in controlled environments. Puppy classes are an excellent way to provide structured socialization. I made sure my Shar Pei met polite strangers and other calm dogs regularly. This helps them learn to react appropriately and build confidence.

However, even with diligent socialization, many Shar Peis retain their inherent aloofness. They may tolerate strangers but rarely seek affection from them. This can be surprising for owners used to more outwardly friendly breeds. They are fiercely loyal to their immediate family but can be indifferent to others. If you expect a dog that loves everyone, a Shar Pei might not be the right choice for you. Their guarding instincts are strong, and they can be protective of their territory and family members. Understanding this unique personality trait is key to a harmonious relationship. ## High Sensitivity and Separation Anxiety

Despite their tough appearance, Shar Peis can be surprisingly sensitive dogs. They bond very deeply with their family members and do not cope well with extended periods of solitude. This strong attachment often leads to separation anxiety. If left alone for too long or too frequently, a Shar Pei may exhibit destructive behaviors, excessive barking, or even self-mutilation. They crave companionship and consistent presence from their owners.

I learned that my Shar Pei preferred to be near me almost all the time. When I left, even for short periods, I noticed signs of distress. This means that owners who work long hours or travel frequently might find it challenging to meet a Shar Pei's emotional needs. Crating can help some dogs, but it is not a cure for true separation anxiety. It simply provides a safe space while you are gone.

Managing their sensitivity requires a structured routine and gradual desensitization to your departures. Short absences, positive reinforcement when they are calm, and puzzle toys can help. However, if severe separation anxiety develops, professional help from a dog trainer or behaviorist may be necessary. This commitment to being present and providing emotional support is a significant part of owning a Shar Pei. Their well-being depends on feeling secure and loved within their family unit.

## Strong Prey Drive and Dog Aggression

Shar Peis often possess a significant prey drive and can show aggression towards other dogs, especially those of the same sex. This is a common trait in many guardian breeds, stemming from their original purpose. Their independent nature and protective instincts mean they may not be naturally inclined to socialize peacefully with every dog they meet. This can make walks and visits to dog parks challenging.

I noticed early on that my Shar Pei had a low tolerance for unfamiliar dogs, particularly those that were overly boisterous. Without careful management, situations with other dogs can escalate quickly. Leash reactivity is common, where the dog barks, lunges, or pulls when seeing another dog on leash. This behavior requires consistent training and management. Owners must be prepared to be vigilant in public spaces.

Early socialization is critical, but it does not guarantee a universally friendly dog. It helps them learn appropriate canine etiquette and reduces the likelihood of aggression stemming from fear. However, some Shar Peis will always prefer to be the only dog in the household or at least require very careful introductions to other pets. If you have multiple dogs or plan to frequently visit dog parks, a Shar Pei might not be the best fit. Their potential for dog aggression means owners must prioritize safety and responsible pet ownership at all times. This includes using appropriate leashes and harnesses and avoiding situations that could trigger aggressive responses.

## Moderate Shedding and Coat Maintenance

While Shar Peis do not have long, flowing coats, they do shed. Their short, bristly hair can be quite stiff and may embed itself in carpets and upholstery. Many people assume short-haired dogs shed less, but this is not always true. Shar Peis have a double coat that sheds moderately year-round, with heavier shedding periods seasonally.

Regular brushing is essential to manage this shedding. I brush my Shar Pei several times a week, using a rubber curry brush or a shedding blade. This helps remove loose hair and distributes natural skin oils, promoting a healthy coat. Without consistent brushing, you will find more hair around your home. Their unique coat texture also means the shed hairs can be particularly noticeable.

Beyond shedding, their coat needs minimal bathing unless they get dirty or have skin issues. Over-bathing can strip their skin of natural oils and exacerbate dryness or skin problems. The key is to balance cleanliness with maintaining healthy skin. During seasonal shedding, you might need to increase brushing frequency to keep up with the loose hair. This level of coat maintenance, combined with their intricate skin fold care, means Shar Peis are not a wash-and-go type of breed. Owners must be prepared for the ongoing commitment to keep their coat and skin healthy.

## FAQ Section

### Are Shar Peis difficult to own?
Yes, Shar Peis can be challenging to own, especially for first-time dog owners. Their health issues require significant veterinary care and expense. Their independent and sometimes stubborn temperament demands consistent training and early socialization. They also need special grooming to maintain their unique skin folds and prevent infections.

### What is the biggest issue with Shar Peis?
The biggest issue with Shar Peis is their extensive genetic health problems. They are prone to conditions like Shar Pei Fever, various skin fold infections, and eye issues such as entropion. These conditions often lead to chronic discomfort for the dog and high, ongoing veterinary bills for the owner.

### Do Shar Peis bite a lot?
Shar Peis are not inherently prone to biting more than other breeds, but their protective and sometimes aloof nature means they can bite if they feel threatened or are not properly socialized. Early and consistent training is essential to manage their guarding instincts and ensure they are well-adjusted.

### Are Shar Peis aggressive towards other dogs?
Shar Peis often exhibit aggression towards other dogs, particularly those of the same sex, due to their strong prey drive and protective nature. Proper early socialization is crucial but does not guarantee complete dog-friendliness. Owners must be prepared to manage their Shar Pei around other canines.

### How much exercise does a Shar Pei need?
Shar Peis have moderate exercise needs. A daily walk of 30-60 minutes, combined with some playtime, is usually sufficient. They are not high-energy dogs, but regular activity helps maintain their physical health and prevents boredom. Over-exercising them can be detrimental due to their health predispositions.

### Do Shar Peis smell bad?
Shar Peis can develop a strong odor if their skin folds are not kept clean and dry. Moisture trapped in their wrinkles can lead to bacterial or yeast infections, which produce an unpleasant smell. With diligent cleaning and proper hygiene, a healthy Shar Pei should not have an offensive odor.

## Conclusion

Bringing a Shar Pei into your home is a decision that requires careful consideration of their unique needs and challenges. While their distinctive appearance and loyalty are appealing, the cons of owning a Shar Pei are substantial. We have explored their extensive health issues, which often lead to high veterinary costs and require constant vigilance. Their strong-willed temperament demands an experienced hand in training and early, consistent socialization to prevent behavioral problems. The intensive grooming required for their skin folds is also a major commitment, preventing infections and maintaining comfort.

Furthermore, their potential for aloofness towards strangers, sensitivity to separation, and challenges with dog aggression mean they are not suited for every household. I hope this comprehensive look at the downsides helps you make an informed choice. Owning a Shar Pei can be incredibly rewarding for those prepared to meet these specific demands. They give immense loyalty and companionship. If you are ready for the commitment, a Shar Pei can be a wonderful, if challenging, addition to your life. Make sure you are truly prepared for everything this unique breed entails.
