## General Cleaning Steps for All Reef Sandals

Every pair of Reef sandals benefits from a basic cleaning routine. This initial stage removes most loose dirt and prepares the sandals for a deeper clean. I always start with these steps, regardless of the sandal's material. It sets a good foundation for restoring their freshness.

Begin by shaking off any loose dirt or sand. You can also use a dry brush to gently remove surface debris. Next, create a mild cleaning solution. Mix warm water with a small amount of mild soap in a bucket or basin. Dip your soft-bristled brush into the solution. Gently scrub the entire surface of your sandals, focusing on the footbed and straps. The [footbed of sandals](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-footbed-of-sandals) often collects the most grime and requires extra attention. Scrub in small circular motions to lift dirt without being too harsh. Rinse the sandals thoroughly with clean water. Make sure all soap residue is gone. Remaining soap can dry out materials or attract more dirt. After rinsing, gently pat the sandals dry with a clean cloth. Set them aside to air dry completely before proceeding to specific care or storage.

## Specific Cleaning Methods for Different Reef Sandal Materials

Reef sandals come in a variety of materials. Each material requires a slightly different approach to cleaning. Using the correct method prevents damage and ensures a thorough clean. I have different pairs, so I know how important it is to adapt my cleaning technique.

### Cleaning Rubber and EVA Reef Sandals

Rubber and EVA (Ethylene-vinyl acetate) are common materials for Reef sandals. These materials are very durable and water-resistant. This makes them relatively easy to clean. Many of my everyday Reef flip-flops are made from these materials.

To clean rubber or EVA Reef sandals, start with the general cleaning steps. Use your mild soap and water solution with a soft brush. For stubborn stains or scuff marks, you can use a bit more pressure. A magic eraser can also work wonders on these surfaces. Simply dampen the magic eraser and gently rub the stained areas. Rinse the sandals well with clean water. Ensure no soap or cleaner remains. Air dry them completely. These materials dry quickly, which is a big plus. You can also find specific guides on [how to clean Reef flip-flops](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-reef-flip-flops) for more detailed tips.

### Caring for Leather Reef Sandals

Leather Reef sandals offer a more sophisticated look. They require gentle care to prevent drying, cracking, or staining. Harsh chemicals or excessive water can damage the leather. I always treat my leather Reefs with extra caution.

First, wipe down the leather with a damp cloth to remove surface dirt. Do not soak the leather. Mix a tiny amount of mild leather cleaner or gentle soap with water. Apply the solution to a soft cloth, not directly to the sandal. Gently wipe the leather straps and footbed. Avoid excessive scrubbing. For a leather footbed, be especially careful not to oversaturate it. You can find more specific advice on [how to clean leather sandals](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-leather-sandals) for various types of leather. After cleaning, wipe away any residue with a clean, damp cloth. Let the sandals air dry in a cool, shaded area. Once dry, apply a leather conditioner to moisturize and protect the material. This keeps the leather soft and prevents cracks.

### Tackling Suede and Nubuck Reef Sandals

Suede and nubuck materials give Reef sandals a luxurious, soft feel. However, they are also prone to water spots and stains. Cleaning these materials requires a dry approach whenever possible. I once ruined a pair by using too much water.

To clean suede or nubuck Reef sandals, start by brushing off loose dirt with a suede brush. This helps lift the nap and remove dried mud. For minor stains, use a suede eraser. Gently rub the eraser over the stain. For tougher stains or water spots, you might need a specialized suede cleaner. Always test any cleaner on an inconspicuous area first. Apply the cleaner sparingly according to product instructions. Use a clean cloth or suede brush. After cleaning, brush the suede again to restore its texture. Allow the sandals to air dry completely. Never expose suede or nubuck to direct heat or sunlight. This can cause permanent damage. For sandals with [suede footbeds](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-sandals-with-suede-footbed), gentle brushing is key.

### Refreshing Fabric Strap Reef Sandals

Fabric straps on Reef sandals are common and comfortable. They can absorb sweat and dirt, leading to discoloration and odors. Cleaning fabric straps helps restore their appearance and freshness. I often find fabric straps needing more frequent cleaning.

Start by gently scrubbing the fabric straps with a mild soap and water solution. Use a soft brush or an old toothbrush for this. Pay attention to any stained areas. For tough stains, you can create a paste with baking soda and a little water. Apply the paste to the stain, let it sit for 15-20 minutes, then gently scrub and rinse. Rinse the straps thoroughly with clean water. Make sure all soap residue is removed. Squeeze out excess water from the straps. Allow the sandals to air dry completely. Ensure the fabric is fully dry before wearing or storing. This prevents mildew growth and lingering odors.

### Removing Stubborn Stains from Reef Sandals

Stubborn stains can be frustrating. The key is to identify the type of stain and the sandal material. This guides your approach. Different stains respond to different treatments.

For oil-based stains on rubber or EVA, a degreasing dish soap works well. Apply a small amount directly to the stain. Let it sit for a few minutes before scrubbing and rinsing. For dark marks or scuffs, a pencil eraser can gently lift the mark from many surfaces. Alcohol can remove ink stains, but test it first on an hidden area. For fabric stains, a baking soda paste is often effective. Mix baking soda with a little water to form a paste. Apply it to the stain and let it sit before scrubbing. Always rinse thoroughly after treating stains. Repeat the process if necessary. [Cleaning foot marks on sandals](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-foot-marks-on-sandals) also often involves a similar spot-cleaning approach.

### Eliminating Foot Odor from Reef Sandals

Foot odor is a common problem, especially with frequently worn sandals. Bacteria thrive in damp, warm environments, causing unpleasant smells. You can tackle these odors effectively. I have found baking soda to be a true lifesaver for this issue.

After cleaning your sandals, sprinkle a generous amount of baking soda onto the footbed. Let it sit overnight, or even for 24 hours. Baking soda absorbs odors naturally. After this time, shake out the baking soda. You will notice a significant improvement in freshness. For persistent odors, a white vinegar solution can also help. Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in a spray bottle. Lightly mist the footbed and let it air dry completely. Vinegar kills bacteria that cause odors. The vinegar smell dissipates as it dries. You can also sun your sandals briefly after cleaning. UV rays help kill bacteria. For severe cases, consider a deodorizing spray specifically for shoes.

### Cleaning the Bottom of Reef Sandals

The bottom of your Reef sandals comes into direct contact with the ground. It collects a lot of dirt, mud, and grime. Cleaning the soles is important for hygiene and maintaining grip. I often overlook this part, but it is crucial.

Start by knocking off any large chunks of mud or debris. You can use an old stick or tap the sandals against a hard surface. Next, use a stiff brush, like a scrub brush or an old kitchen brush, with your mild soap and water solution. Vigorously scrub the soles, paying attention to the tread patterns. These patterns often trap dirt. Rinse the bottoms thoroughly with a hose or under running water. Ensure all dirt and soap are gone. Let the sandals air dry completely. This prevents dirt from caking on. Regular cleaning of the [bottom of sandals](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bottom-of-sandals) keeps them looking cleaner overall.

## Drying and Storing Your Reef Sandals Properly

Proper drying and storage are just as important as the cleaning process. Incorrect drying can lead to mildew, unpleasant odors, or material damage. Poor storage can deform your sandals or expose them to dirt. I learned this the hard way with a few pairs.

After cleaning and rinsing, shake off excess water from your Reef sandals. Pat them dry with a clean towel. Then, find a well-ventilated area away from direct sunlight or heat sources. Direct sun can fade colors and dry out materials, especially leather. High heat can warp rubber or EVA. Allow your sandals to air dry completely. This can take several hours, or even a full day, depending on the material and humidity. Never store damp sandals. This promotes bacterial growth and mildew, leading to odors. Once dry, store your Reefs in a cool, dry place. You can use shoe bags or boxes to protect them from dust. Keeping them flat prevents straps from stretching or crushing.

## Maintaining Your Reef Sandals: Tips for Long-Term Freshness

After each wear, quickly wipe down your sandals with a damp cloth. This removes surface dirt and prevents build-up. If your feet sweat a lot, lightly spray the footbed with a deodorizing spray. You can also sprinkle a little baking soda after wearing. Alternate between pairs of sandals if you have them. This allows each pair to fully air out between uses. Consider using a protective spray for leather or suede sandals. These sprays create a barrier against water and stains. Inspect your sandals regularly for any signs of wear or damage. Address small issues before they become bigger problems. Consistent care means your Reef sandals will be ready for your next adventure.

## FAQ Section

### How often should I clean my Reef sandals?
You should clean your Reef sandals regularly, depending on how often you wear them and where. For frequent wear, a quick wipe-down after each use is good. A deeper clean every few weeks or once a month is ideal. If they get muddy or sandy, clean them immediately.

### Can I put my Reef sandals in the washing machine?
Generally, no. Most Reef sandals are not designed for machine washing. The agitation can damage materials, glue, and stitching. It can also cause warping or discoloration. Stick to hand-washing methods to ensure their longevity and shape. Always check the manufacturer's specific care instructions.

### What is the best way to remove footbed discoloration?
Footbed discoloration, often from sweat and dirt, is common. For rubber or EVA, a paste of baking soda and water works well. Scrub it gently with an old toothbrush. For leather footbeds, use a mild soap specifically for leather and a soft cloth. Follow with a leather conditioner.

### How do I prevent my Reef sandals from smelling?
To prevent odors, clean your sandals regularly. Sprinkle baking soda on the footbed overnight to absorb smells. Allow your sandals to air out completely after each wear. Do not store them in enclosed, damp spaces. Consider using anti-bacterial shoe sprays for extra freshness.

### Can I use household cleaners on my Reef sandals?
Use caution with household cleaners. Harsh chemicals can damage materials, especially leather, suede, or fabric. Stick to mild dish soap, hand soap, or specialized sandal cleaners. Always test any new cleaner on a small, hidden area first. This prevents accidental damage or discoloration.

### What if my Reef sandals get wet in saltwater?
Saltwater can be harsh on sandal materials. Rinse your Reef sandals thoroughly with fresh water as soon as possible after exposure. This removes salt residue, which can dry out leather or cause discoloration. Then, clean them as usual and allow them to air dry completely.
