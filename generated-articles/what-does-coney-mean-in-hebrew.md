### What does Coney Mean In Hebrew? A Direct Answer

In Hebrew, the word "Coney" primarily translates to **`shafan` (שפן)**. This term refers to the rock badger or hyrax, a small, rabbit-like mammal native to the Middle East and Africa. It is not the modern domestic rabbit, which has a different Hebrew name. The distinction is crucial for understanding biblical texts where `shafan` appears.

## The Hebrew Translation of "Coney": Unveiling `Shafan` (שפן)

When we look for what Coney means in Hebrew, we arrive at the word `shafan` (שפן). This is the key term you need to know. `Shafan` specifically identifies an animal known in English as the rock badger or hyrax. These animals are small, herbivorous mammals.

Hyraxes are common in rocky areas across Africa and the Middle East. They are about the size of a domestic cat, with short ears and a small tail. While they might look like large rodents or small rabbits, they belong to a completely different order of mammals, the Hyracoidea. Their unique characteristics set them apart from other animals.

The `shafan` is mentioned several times in the Hebrew Bible. These mentions provide valuable insight into the ancient understanding of nature. The biblical descriptions often highlight the hyrax's dwelling in rocks and its perceived wisdom. This Hebrew term carries a specific zoological meaning. It tells us about the animal's natural habitat and behaviors.

For example, Psalm 104:18 states, "The high mountains are for the wild goats; the rocks are a refuge for the hyraxes (`shafanim`)." This verse clearly links `shafan` with rocky terrain. Understanding `shafan` helps us correctly interpret these ancient texts. It clears up any confusion that a simple English translation of "Coney" might create.

## Linguistic Nuances: Why "Coney" Isn't a Direct Rabbit Translation

It is important to understand that "Coney" in Hebrew (`shafan`) does not directly translate to what we commonly call a "rabbit" today. This distinction is a crucial linguistic nuance. While the English "Coney" *used* to mean rabbit, its Hebrew counterpart refers to a different animal. This difference can lead to misunderstanding if not clarified.

Modern Hebrew has a distinct word for "rabbit." The word for rabbit in contemporary Hebrew is **`arnav` (ארנב)**. This term specifically refers to the lagomorphs we recognize as rabbits or hares. `Arnav` is a separate and well-defined word. It is not interchangeable with `shafan`.

The confusion arises because of the historical usage of "Coney" in English. In older English, "Coney" was indeed the common word for a rabbit. When translators worked on the King James Bible, they used "Coney" for `shafan`. At that time, "Coney" was a familiar term for a rabbit-like animal. However, zoological understanding has advanced significantly since then.

We now know the hyrax (`shafan`) is not a rabbit (`arnav`). They belong to different biological orders. Rabbits are lagomorphs, while hyraxes are hyracoids. They look somewhat similar but are not closely related. This makes `shafan` a unique term with a precise meaning in Hebrew. It helps us avoid errors when reading ancient texts about animal life.
