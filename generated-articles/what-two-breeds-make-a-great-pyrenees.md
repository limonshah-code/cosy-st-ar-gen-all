### Q2: What other ancient breeds are similar to the Great Pyrenees?

Many other ancient livestock guardian dogs share similar traits. Examples include the Anatolian Shepherd, Akbash Dog, Kuvasz, and Maremma Sheepdog. ### Q3: How long have Great Pyrenees dogs existed?

The ancestors of the Great Pyrenees have existed for thousands of years. Evidence of similar large guardian dogs dates back to the Bronze Age. ### Q4: Do all Great Pyrenees have double dew claws?

Most purebred Great Pyrenees have double dew claws on their hind legs. This is a common and distinctive feature of the breed. It is even a requirement in the breed standard for many kennel clubs. While most have them, some individual dogs might not display this trait.

### Q5: What traits show a dog is a purebred Great Pyrenees?

Key traits of a purebred Great Pyrenees include a large, powerful build and a dense double coat. The coat is typically white, sometimes with badger, gray, or tan markings. They often have double dew claws on their hind legs. Their temperament is calm, patient, and protective. They have a strong guarding instinct.

### Q6: Where did the Great Pyrenees originally come from?

The Great Pyrenees originated in the Pyrenees mountain range. This region borders France and Spain. Their earliest ancestors were large guardian dogs from Asia. These dogs migrated west with nomadic people. They settled in the Pyrenees mountains, where they evolved into the breed we know today.
