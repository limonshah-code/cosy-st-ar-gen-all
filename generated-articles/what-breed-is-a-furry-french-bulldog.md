## What Breed Is A Furry French Bulldog? Understanding Their Unique Charm

Have you ever seen a French Bulldog with a long, flowing coat and wondered, "What breed is a furry French Bulldog?" It is a common question. These dogs truly stand out from their short-haired counterparts. They capture hearts with their unique appearance.

In this article, we will explore the fascinating world of the furry French Bulldog. We will uncover the genetic secret behind their distinctive coat. We will discuss their rarity, how to care for them, and important considerations for owners. Prepare to learn everything about these charming fluffy friends.

### Takeaway

*   Furry French Bulldogs are purebred French Bulldogs.
*   They possess a recessive gene (FGF5) that causes their long coat.
*   They are rare and often command higher prices due to their unique genetics.
*   Their care needs are similar to standard French Bulldogs, with added grooming for their coat.

A furry French Bulldog is not a separate breed. It is a purebred French Bulldog that carries a recessive gene, known as the FGF5 gene. This specific gene gives them their distinctive long, fluffy coat. This means they are 100% French Bulldog, just with a unique hair type.

## The Truth About the Furry French Bulldog Breed

Many people see a furry French Bulldog and believe it must be a mixed breed. This is a common misconception. I understand why people might think this. Their long hair is so different from the typical short coat of a standard French Bulldog.

However, the truth is simple. A furry French Bulldog is a purebred French Bulldog. They are not a crossbreed. They are simply a genetic variation within the French Bulldog breed. Their unique appearance comes from a specific gene. This gene makes their coat longer and softer than average. It is like having different hair colors in humans; they are still human.

This specific variant has gained popularity recently. People are drawn to their unique look. It is important for potential owners to understand their genetic background. They are recognized by some registries, but not all kennel clubs for show purposes. They still maintain all other French Bulldog characteristics.

## Genetics Behind the Long Coat French Bulldog

The long, luxurious coat of a furry French Bulldog is due to a specific gene mutation. This mutation occurs in the Fibroblast Growth Factor 5 (FGF5) gene. This gene is responsible for controlling hair length in dogs. When a dog inherits two copies of this recessive gene, it expresses a long coat.

For a French Bulldog to be born with a furry coat, both parent dogs must carry at least one copy of the FGF5 recessive gene. Even if the parents have short coats, they can still carry the gene. If both parents are carriers, there is a 25% chance that each puppy in a litter will inherit two copies of the gene and thus have a long coat. This makes these furry puppies quite rare. It is fascinating how genetics work to create such beautiful variations. Understanding these genetics helps us appreciate the furry French Bulldog even more. This genetic trait is what sets them apart visually.

## Distinguishing a True Fluffy French Bulldog

Identifying a true fluffy French Bulldog involves looking closely at its coat. Their hair is noticeably longer than the short, sleek coat of a standard French Bulldog. You will see longer hair around their ears, on their legs, and along their back and tail. Their fur often feels softer to the touch.

Some people might confuse them with a French Bulldog mixed with another long-haired breed. However, a purebred furry French Bulldog will still retain all other classic French Bulldog features. This includes their bat ears, compact body, flat face, and typical temperament. They also have the same body shape and size. For example, a [French Bulldog is considered a small to medium-sized breed](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/is-a-french-bulldog-a-small-or-large-breed). They do not have traits from other breeds like a longer snout or different body proportions.

Breeders can test parent dogs for the FGF5 gene. This test confirms if the parents carry the gene. It also confirms if a puppy is truly a fluffy French Bulldog. This genetic testing ensures you are getting a purebred French Bulldog with the specific long coat trait. It helps distinguish them from accidental mixes.

## The History and Origin of the French Bulldog

To truly understand the furry French Bulldog, we must first look at the history of the standard French Bulldog. The French Bulldog's origins trace back to the mid-19th century in England. Lace workers in Nottingham bred smaller, toy-sized Bulldogs. These smaller Bulldogs were popular companions. They helped control rats in their workplaces. When the lace industry moved to France, many of these small Bulldogs went with their owners.

In France, these dogs became immensely popular. They were particularly favored by Parisian women and artists. French breeders then crossed these small English Bulldogs with other terrier-type dogs. This crossbreeding helped develop the unique "bat ears" that are now a hallmark of the French Bulldog. This is [what makes a French Bulldog a French Bulldog](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/what-makes-a-french-bulldog-a-french-bulldog). They evolved from a working dog to a beloved companion.

The breed continued to develop distinct features in France. American enthusiasts later brought French Bulldogs to the United States. They helped establish the breed there. The furry French Bulldog variant emerged naturally within these established lines. It was a result of a spontaneous genetic mutation. This mutation was then selectively bred for, once discovered. The FGF5 gene was present in the breed's gene pool. The furry variant is simply another beautiful expression of the diverse [origin of the French Bulldog](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-original-breed-of-the-french-bulldog).

## Rarity and Cost of Furry French Bulldogs

Furry French Bulldogs are considerably rarer than their short-coated counterparts. This rarity directly impacts their price. The reason for their scarcity lies in the recessive nature of the FGF5 gene. As mentioned, both parents must carry the gene to produce long-haired offspring. Even then, only a quarter of the litter, on average, will inherit the trait. This makes responsible breeding of furry French Bulldogs a specialized process. It requires genetic testing and careful pairing of dogs.

Because of their rarity and the specific breeding practices needed, furry French Bulldogs often cost significantly more. A standard French Bulldog puppy can range in price. However, a furry French Bulldog puppy typically fetches a much higher price. I have seen prices that are considerably elevated. This reflects the investment breeders make. ## Caring for a Long-Haired French Bulldog

Caring for a long-haired French Bulldog is mostly similar to caring for a standard French Bulldog. However, their unique coat does require some extra attention. While their long fur might seem daunting, it is generally not high-maintenance compared to some other long-haired breeds. Still, regular grooming is essential.

Here are some tips for caring for your furry friend:

*   **Brushing:** Their longer coat can be prone to matting and tangles, especially behind the ears and under the legs. Daily or every-other-day brushing with a soft slicker brush or a deshedding tool helps remove loose hair and prevents mats. This keeps their coat healthy and shiny.
*   **Bathing:** Bathe them as needed, typically every 3-4 weeks, or when they get dirty. Use a dog-specific shampoo and conditioner to keep their fur soft and to prevent skin irritation. Ensure you rinse thoroughly to avoid residue buildup.
*   **Shedding:** Despite their longer coat, furry French Bulldogs typically do not shed excessively. They shed seasonally, like most dogs. Regular brushing helps manage any loose hair.
*   **Ear Cleaning:** Their bat ears can trap dirt and moisture, so regular cleaning is vital to prevent infections. Check them weekly.
*   **Skin Folds:** Like all French Bulldogs, they have facial folds that need regular cleaning. Wipe them daily with a damp cloth or pet wipe to prevent moisture and bacteria buildup. This helps avoid skin irritation.
*   **Health Considerations:** French Bulldogs, regardless of coat length, are prone to certain health issues. These include brachycephalic airway syndrome, spinal problems, and allergies. Regular vet check-ups are important. Avoid [things not to do with a French Bulldog](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/what-not-to-do-with-a-french-bulldog) such as over-exercising in heat or neglecting their breathing difficulties. Their long coat does not add significant health complications. However, owners should always be aware of the breed's general health needs. Always provide a balanced diet and appropriate exercise.

## Are Furry French Bulldogs Recognized by Kennel Clubs?

The recognition of furry French Bulldogs by major kennel clubs is a point of discussion. Most major kennel clubs, such as the American Kennel Club (AKC) and The Kennel Club (UK), do not recognize the long coat as part of their breed standard for show purposes. This means a furry French Bulldog cannot compete in conformation shows. Their long coat is considered a disqualifying fault according to current breed standards. This does not mean they are not purebred. It simply means they do not fit the specific appearance criteria for show rings.

However, this lack of show recognition does not diminish their status as purebred French Bulldogs. They still possess all the genetic markers and lineage of their short-haired relatives. Many dedicated breeders and enthusiasts appreciate them for their unique beauty. Some newer, more inclusive registries might acknowledge them. The trend is moving towards more acceptance of genetic variations. For pet owners, this distinction matters little. They are beloved companions regardless of show ring eligibility. Their charm lies in their unique look and classic French Bulldog personality.

## FAQ Section

### Are furry French Bulldogs purebred?
Yes, furry French Bulldogs are 100% purebred French Bulldogs. Their long coat is a result of inheriting a specific recessive gene (FGF5) from both parents. This gene only affects coat length. It does not indicate any crossbreeding.

### Do fluffy French Bulldogs shed?
Yes, fluffy French Bulldogs do shed, similar to their short-haired counterparts. Their shedding might be less noticeable than some other long-haired breeds. Regular brushing is important. Brushing helps remove loose hair. It also prevents mats and keeps their coat healthy.

### What causes a French Bulldog to be furry?
A French Bulldog becomes furry due to a recessive gene called FGF5 (Fibroblast Growth Factor 5). This gene controls hair length. For a French Bulldog to have a long coat, it must inherit two copies of this gene, one from each parent.

### Are furry French Bulldogs healthy?
Furry French Bulldogs generally have similar health profiles to standard French Bulldogs. They are prone to the same breed-specific health issues like breathing difficulties, spinal problems, and allergies. Their long coat does not typically introduce new health concerns. Responsible breeding is key for health.

### How rare are furry French Bulldogs?
Furry French Bulldogs are considered rare. Their rarity stems from the recessive nature of the FGF5 gene. Both parents must carry the gene to produce a fluffy puppy. Even then, only a small percentage of a litter will typically inherit the long coat trait. This makes them less common and often more expensive.

### Is a furry French Bulldog a mix?
No, a furry French Bulldog is not a mixed breed. This is a common misunderstanding. They are purebred French Bulldogs. Their long fur is a genetic variation within the breed. It is caused by a recessive gene, not by crossbreeding with another dog type.
