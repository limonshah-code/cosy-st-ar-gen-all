## Clean Shellac From Your Airless Sprayer: A Complete Guide

Cleaning an airless sprayer correctly is a vital step after any painting project. When you use shellac, this cleaning process becomes even more critical. Shellac dries very fast and can cause significant issues if not removed promptly and thoroughly. Neglecting to clean shellac out of an airless sprayer can lead to clogs, reduced performance, and costly repairs. This guide helps you understand the right methods and products for a deep clean. We will cover everything from immediate flush procedures to detailed component cleaning, ensuring your equipment stays in top condition. Follow these steps to protect your investment and maintain excellent spray quality for years to come.

#### Takeaway:

*   Immediately flush the sprayer with denatured alcohol after using shellac.
*   Disassemble and thoroughly clean all components, including the gun, tip, and filters.
*   Ensure multiple flushes of the pump and hose to remove all shellac residue.
*   Apply pump protector before storing the airless sprayer to prevent damage.

To clean shellac out of an airless sprayer, immediately flush the system with denatured alcohol until it runs clear. Then, disassemble the gun, filter, and tip, soaking them in alcohol. Manually clean all parts with brushes, ensuring no shellac remains before reassembly and storage with pump protector.

### Understanding Shellac and Your Airless Sprayer

Shellac is a unique finish often chosen for its fast-drying properties and excellent sealing capabilities. This natural resin dissolves in alcohol, which is why alcohol is the solvent for both thinning and cleaning. Unlike water-based or oil-based paints, shellac dries by evaporation of the alcohol solvent. This quick-drying nature makes immediate and proper cleaning essential for your airless sprayer. Shellac can harden quickly inside the sprayer, leading to serious clogs if left unattended.

Your airless sprayer works by pumping material at high pressure through a small tip. This process atomizes the shellac into a fine spray. Any dried shellac inside the pump, hose, or gun can restrict flow or completely block these pathways. Regular and correct cleaning prevents these problems. It also extends the lifespan of your sprayer's internal components, such as seals and packings. Taking care of your equipment ensures it performs reliably for future projects. I have learned that a little effort upfront saves a lot of headaches later.

### Essential Supplies for Shellac Sprayer Cleanup

Having the right tools ready before you begin cleaning makes the process much smoother. You will need a specific solvent for shellac, along with various cleaning aids. Gathering these items beforehand ensures you can clean your airless sprayer quickly after use. This immediate action is key to preventing shellac from drying inside.

Here is a list of essential supplies:

*   **Denatured Alcohol:** This is the primary solvent for shellac. Ensure you have plenty, as you will use it for flushing and soaking parts. Do not substitute with water or paint thinner.
*   **Protective Gear:** Always wear chemical-resistant gloves and safety glasses. Denatured alcohol can irritate skin and eyes. A respirator is also a good idea in poorly ventilated areas.
*   **Cleaning Brushes:** A set of brushes, including small wire brushes and nylon brushes, helps remove stubborn shellac from tight spots.
*   **Rags or Paper Towels:** These are useful for wiping down parts and absorbing excess solvent.
*   **Buckets:** You will need at least two buckets: one for flushing dirty solvent and another for clean solvent.
*   **Pump Protector/Storage Fluid:** This fluid helps lubricate the pump and prevent corrosion during storage.
*   **Empty Containers:** Use these to hold disassembled parts during cleaning.

Having these supplies on hand will simplify the cleaning process. This preparation ensures you can effectively remove all shellac residue from your airless sprayer. Proper tools make a big difference in maintaining your equipment.

### Immediate Post-Spraying Cleanup Steps

The moment you finish spraying shellac, start the cleaning process. Speed is very important here. Shellac begins drying quickly, even inside the sprayer's components. Following these steps immediately helps prevent difficult clogs.

First, you must relieve the pressure in your airless sprayer. Always refer to your sprayer's manual for specific instructions. Generally, you turn off the sprayer, engage the gun safety lock, and then trigger the gun into a waste bucket. This releases any residual pressure. Next, remove the suction tube from the shellac container.

Place the suction tube into a bucket of clean denatured alcohol. Turn the sprayer back on and run the alcohol through the system. Spray it into a separate waste bucket. Keep flushing until the alcohol coming out of the spray gun runs completely clear. This initial flush removes most of the liquid shellac from the pump, hose, and gun. I usually run at least a gallon of denatured alcohol through during this stage, sometimes more for longer jobs. This step is crucial for breaking down the shellac before it can harden. This process is similar to how you would [clean oil-based paint from a sprayer](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-oil-based-paint-from-a-sprayer), but with a different solvent.

After flushing the system, turn off the sprayer and relieve the pressure again. Disengage the spray gun from the hose. Remove the spray tip and guard. Soak these small parts in a separate container filled with fresh denatured alcohol. Use a small brush to clean any shellac from the tip and guard. This ensures no small particles remain to clog the tip later.

### Thorough Disassembly and Deep Cleaning of Components

After the initial flush, a deeper cleaning is necessary. Shellac can hide in small crevices and filters. Disassembling key components ensures you remove all hidden residue. This thoroughness prevents future clogs and maintains sprayer performance. I always find taking the time here pays off in the long run.

Start by fully disassembling your spray gun. This usually involves removing the tip guard, tip, filter, and sometimes the trigger assembly. Consult your sprayer's manual for precise disassembly instructions. Place all these small parts into a bucket or container filled with fresh denatured alcohol. Let them soak for at least 30 minutes. This soaking helps dissolve any stubborn, partially dried shellac. It makes manual cleaning much easier.

Next, remove the manifold filter, if your sprayer has one. This filter is usually located near the pump outlet. It catches larger particles before they reach the hose and gun. Unscrew the housing and pull out the filter. Add this filter to your denatured alcohol soak. Check if your main pump also has a filter or strainer on the intake. Clean that as well.

While the parts are soaking, use a small brush, like a toothbrush or a specialized sprayer cleaning brush, to scrub them. Pay close attention to the inside of the gun body, the tip, and the filters. Look for any visible shellac residue. Rinse each part thoroughly with fresh denatured alcohol after scrubbing. Make sure the small holes in the spray tip are completely clear. You can use a tip cleaning needle if needed. This step is vital for ensuring no tiny specks of shellac remain. Neglecting this part can lead to inconsistent spray patterns or blockages during your next project. For similar challenges with other materials, you might find tips on [how to clean a paint sprayer after using a stain](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-paint-sprayer-after-using-a-stain) helpful, as filters and tips are common areas for residue build-up.

### Flushing the Pump and Hoses Effectively

The pump and hoses are the heart and arteries of your airless sprayer system. Ensuring they are completely free of shellac is critical for proper function. This requires running the correct solvent through them multiple times. Do not rush this part of the cleaning process.

After you have disassembled and cleaned the gun components, reattach the spray gun (without the tip or guard initially) to the hose. Submerge the suction tube into a fresh bucket of denatured alcohol. Turn the sprayer on to a low-pressure setting. Let the alcohol circulate through the pump and hose. Direct the output from the gun into a waste bucket. This initial circulation helps to wash away any remaining shellac from the internal passages.

Increase the pressure gradually and continue to flush the system. You should see clear alcohol coming out. A good practice is to recirculate the alcohol for a few minutes. You can do this by placing the return tube (if your sprayer has one) into the clean alcohol bucket, or by simply running it through the system and back into the clean bucket. This maximizes the cleaning power of the solvent. Repeat this flushing process with fresh denatured alcohol at least two or three times. Each time, ensure the liquid coming out is perfectly clear. I find that a thorough flush here prevents any surprises when I next use the sprayer.

For extremely stubborn shellac, you might need to let the alcohol sit in the pump and hose for a short period. Turn off the sprayer after flushing, leaving the alcohol in the system. Let it sit for about 15-30 minutes, then flush again. This allows the solvent more time to dissolve any hardened shellac. Make sure to release the pressure before letting it sit. Always dispose of used denatured alcohol properly. Follow local regulations for hazardous waste. This diligent flushing ensures that your pump and hose are clean and ready for the next job, much like when you need to [clean paint sprayer after oil based paint](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-paint-sprayer-after-oil-based-paint) to prevent clogs.

### Cleaning Your Airless Sprayer After Dried Shellac

Sometimes, you might face a tougher challenge: shellac that has dried inside the sprayer. This can happen if cleaning was delayed or incomplete. Dried shellac forms a hard, resinous layer that is difficult to remove. Do not despair; it is often reversible with patience and the right approach.

First, assess the extent of the problem. If the sprayer is completely seized, you may need to consult your owner's manual for specific troubleshooting. For localized clogs, your main weapon remains denatured alcohol. Start by soaking any affected removable parts, like the gun, tip, and filters, in a strong solution of fresh denatured alcohol. A prolonged soak, perhaps overnight, can work wonders. Ensure the parts are fully submerged.

For the pump and hose, the process is similar but requires more iterations. Fill the system with denatured alcohol. Let it sit for several hours, or even overnight if the clog is severe. The alcohol needs time to break down the hardened shellac. After soaking, attempt to flush the system again. You might need to cycle the sprayer on and off repeatedly to encourage the alcohol to move through the restricted areas. If the pump is still struggling, manually try to dislodge any large pieces if possible.

You may need to gently use a small, non-abrasive tool, like a wooden skewer or a soft wire brush, to clear visible clogs in the gun or hose ends. Be very careful not to scratch or damage internal components. Running hot (not boiling) denatured alcohol through the system can also sometimes help. The heat aids in dissolving the shellac more quickly. Remember, dealing with dried shellac requires persistence. You might need to repeat the soaking and flushing steps several times. It's a testament to why immediate cleaning is so vital, but even dried shellac can often be overcome with enough effort. This situation is more challenging than cleaning [dried latex paint from a paint sprayer](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-dried-latex-paint-from-a-paint-sprayer), as shellac forms a much harder, more resistant residue.

### Final Steps: Protection and Storage for Longevity

After all the thorough cleaning, the final steps are crucial for protecting your airless sprayer. Proper storage ensures your equipment remains in excellent working order and lasts for many years. This phase helps prevent internal corrosion and keeps seals supple. I always tell people that good storage is just as important as good cleaning.

Once your sprayer is completely free of shellac and denatured alcohol, it is time for pump protector. Pump protector, also known as storage fluid, is an antifreeze and lubricant solution. It prevents the pump from rusting and keeps the packings and seals from drying out and cracking. To apply, place the suction tube into a container of pump protector. Turn on the sprayer at the lowest pressure setting. Run the fluid through the system until it flows out of the spray gun. Then, turn off the sprayer and relieve the pressure. This ensures the protector coats all internal components.

When storing your airless sprayer, choose a clean, dry, and climate-controlled environment. Avoid extreme temperatures, as they can damage seals and hoses. Store the sprayer with the pump protector inside, ready for its next use. Do not store it empty. The spray gun should be clean, reassembled, and stored separately or attached to the sprayer. Keep the spray tips and filters in a small container of mineral spirits or light oil to prevent corrosion, if recommended by your manufacturer. This extends their life significantly.

Maintaining a regular cleaning and storage routine after every shellac project is the best way to ensure your airless sprayer remains reliable. It prevents unexpected breakdowns and expensive repairs. A well-maintained sprayer provides consistent, high-quality finishes, making your future projects much smoother.

### FAQ Section

**Q1: Can I use paint thinner instead of denatured alcohol to clean shellac?**
No, you should not use paint thinner. Shellac is an alcohol-based product, and denatured alcohol is its specific solvent. Paint thinner is for oil-based paints and will not effectively dissolve shellac. Using the wrong solvent can leave residue and clog your sprayer. Always stick to denatured alcohol for shellac cleanup.

**Q2: How often should I clean my airless sprayer after using shellac?**
You should clean your airless sprayer immediately after every use of shellac. Shellac dries extremely fast. Even a short delay can cause the material to harden inside the pump, hose, and gun, leading to clogs. Prompt cleaning prevents these issues and ensures your sprayer stays functional.

**Q3: What if I don't have pump protector for storage?**
Pump protector is highly recommended for long-term storage. If you do not have it, you can temporarily fill the pump with a small amount of mineral spirits or a light oil, like motor oil. However, this is a temporary solution. Acquire pump protector as soon as possible for optimal protection.

**Q4: Can I reuse the denatured alcohol for cleaning?**
You can reuse the denatured alcohol for the initial flush, especially if it's still relatively clear. However, for subsequent flushes and soaking disassembled parts, always use fresh, clean denatured alcohol. Dirty alcohol will not clean effectively and can leave residue in your sprayer.

**Q5: How do I dispose of used denatured alcohol?**
Used denatured alcohol is considered hazardous waste. Do not pour it down the drain or dispose of it in regular trash. Collect it in a sealed container and take it to a local hazardous waste collection facility. Check with your local government for specific disposal guidelines in your area.

**Q6: My sprayer's filter is completely caked with dried shellac. What should I do?**
If a filter is completely caked with dried shellac, first try soaking it in denatured alcohol overnight. Then, use a stiff brush and compressed air to dislodge the residue. If it remains clogged, it is best to replace the filter. Filters are relatively inexpensive and crucial for preventing larger clogs.
