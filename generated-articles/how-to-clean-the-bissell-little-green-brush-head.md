## Step-by-Step Disassembly and Initial Rinse

Now that you have your tools, it is time to start. The first step involves taking the brush head off the machine. This allows you to clean it thoroughly. Always unplug your Bissell Little Green first. Safety is very important.

Most Bissell Little Green models have a detachable brush head or cleaning tool. Look for a release button or a latch. This is usually located near where the tool connects to the hose. Press the button or slide the latch. The brush head should then come off easily. If it feels stuck, do not force it. Check your user manual for specific instructions for your model. Understanding [Bissell Little Green parts](https://cosyhomecreation.com/bissell-little-green-parts-all-you-need-to-know) can help you identify these release points quickly.

Once the brush head is off, you can begin the initial rinse. Take the brush head to your sink or basin. Hold it under warm running water. Let the water flow over all surfaces. This helps rinse away loose dirt and fresh residue. You will see a lot of grime wash away quickly. This is a good start.

Next, focus on removing larger debris. Use your fingers to pull away any visible hair, lint, or string. These often get wrapped around the bristles. Scissors or a small knife can help cut through tough tangles. Be very careful not to cut the bristles or scratch the plastic. I have found that a good initial rinse and manual debris removal save a lot of scrubbing later. This step gets rid of the easy stuff. It prepares the brush head for a deeper clean.

Remember to also check the small opening where the water sprays out. Make sure it is clear of blockages. This early cleaning prevents tiny bits of grime from setting in. A clean start means a cleaner finish for your Bissell Little Green brush head.

## Deep Cleaning the Brush Head: Removing Dirt and Debris

After the initial rinse, it is time for a deep clean. This step targets the stubborn dirt. It also removes dried-on cleaning solution. These are the things that block your brush's full potential. I find this part of the cleaning very satisfying.

First, apply a small amount of mild dish soap. Put it directly onto the bristles of the brush head. You do not need much. A few drops are enough. Add a little warm water to create a light lather. The soap helps break down grease and caked-on residue. This makes scrubbing more effective.

Now, take your small brush or old toothbrush. Scrub the bristles vigorously. Work in different directions. Make sure you reach all sides of each bristle tuft. Pay close attention to the base of the bristles. Dirt and hair often gather there. You might see more grime coming off. This is good. It means you are getting a deep clean.

Also, examine the spaces between the bristles. Small bits of debris can hide there. Use the tip of your small brush to poke and dislodge these. For very tight spots, a toothpick can work. Ensure every part of the brush area is clean. It makes a big difference to the overall cleaning power. Remember that a clean brush head contributes to cleaner output, just as a clean tank improves overall performance. You can read more about [how to clean the dirty tank on Bissell Little Green](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-dirty-tank-on-bissell-little-green) to ensure your entire system is spotless.

Once you have scrubbed thoroughly, rinse the brush head again. Use plenty of warm, clean water. Keep rinsing until all soap suds and loosened dirt are gone. The water should run clear. Look closely at the bristles. They should stand up straight and look clean. This deep cleaning restores your brush head. It makes it ready to tackle new stains.

## Caring for the Suction Nozzle and Spray Components

The brush head is not the only part that needs attention. The suction nozzle and spray components are also crucial. They work together with the brush. A clog in these areas can stop your cleaner from working. I often find hidden grime here.

The suction nozzle is the clear plastic part of the attachment. It touches the surface you are cleaning. It pulls up the dirty water. Over time, lint, pet hair, and dried solution can get stuck inside. This creates blockages. Take a close look at this nozzle. You may see visible debris.

Rinse the suction nozzle under warm water. Use your fingers to clear any large pieces of debris. For smaller clogs, use a thin, stiff brush or a pipe cleaner. Push it through the nozzle opening. This helps clear any buildup inside. Sometimes, a paper clip can help dislodge stubborn bits. Be careful not to scratch the plastic. You want to make sure the air and water can flow freely through this path. Proper care of all [Bissell Little Green parts](https://cosyhomecreation.com/bissell-little-green-parts-all-you-need-to-know) is essential for your machine's longevity.

Next, look at the spray nozzle. This is usually a small hole or series of holes near the brush. This is where the cleaning solution comes out. It can get clogged with mineral deposits or dried solution. If your cleaner is not spraying evenly, this is likely the problem. Use a small, pointed tool like a toothpick. Carefully poke into the spray holes. This will clear any blockages. Do not force anything. A gentle poke is often enough.

After clearing any clogs, rinse the entire tool again. Ensure all components are free of debris and soap residue. Hold it up to the light to confirm the nozzle is clear. A clean suction path and clear spray ensure optimal cleaning. This step makes sure your Bissell Little Green sprays and sucks correctly. This means better, more efficient cleaning for you.

## FAQ Section

### How often should I clean my Bissell Little Green brush head?
You should rinse your Bissell Little Green brush head after every use. A deeper clean is needed every 3-5 uses, or whenever you notice visible buildup. If your machine's performance drops, or if you see dirt on the brush, it is time for a thorough cleaning. Regular rinsing helps maintain daily performance.

### Can I use harsh chemicals to clean the brush head?
No, avoid harsh chemicals. They can damage the plastic parts and bristles of your brush head. Stick to warm water and a small amount of mild dish soap. Strong cleaners can also leave residues that hurt your carpets or upholstery in the next cleaning session. Simple is often best.

### What if my brush head is still clogged after cleaning?
If clogs remain, re-examine the suction nozzle and spray holes closely. Use a small, stiff wire or a paper clip to gently probe these areas. Sometimes, mineral deposits or very compacted fibers need extra persuasion. Ensure the entire water path is clear. Do not force tools.

### Can I put the brush head in the dishwasher?
No, do not put your Bissell Little Green brush head in the dishwasher. The high heat can warp the plastic components. It can also damage the bristles. Always hand wash the brush head using warm water and mild soap. This protects the integrity of the plastic parts.

### How do I know if my brush head needs to be replaced?
Your brush head might need replacement if the bristles are permanently flattened, frayed, or missing. If the plastic housing is cracked or broken, and cleaning does not fix performance issues, a replacement part may be necessary. You can often find replacement parts on the Bissell website or authorized retailers.
