## Keep Your Grip: How To Clean Rock Climbing Shoes

Rock climbing shoes are essential gear. They provide the grip you need on the rock face. Over time, these shoes collect dirt, sweat, and grime. This buildup reduces performance and causes unpleasant odors. Learning how to clean rock climbing shoes properly ensures they perform well. It also extends their useful life. In this article, I will show you simple, effective ways to clean your climbing shoes. We will cover everything from basic exterior scrubbing to deep interior odor removal.

### Takeaway

Keeping your rock climbing shoes clean is vital for performance and hygiene.
*   **Wipe Down Regularly:** Remove surface dirt after each climb.
*   **Clean Soles:** Use a brush and water to restore friction.
*   **Address Odor:** Deodorize the inside to fight bacteria growth.
*   **Dry Thoroughly:** Air dry shoes completely to prevent mold.
*   **Store Properly:** Keep shoes in a cool, dry place.

To clean rock climbing shoes, gently scrub the rubber soles and rands with a brush and mild soap. Wipe the fabric or synthetic upper with a damp cloth. For the interior, use odor eliminators like baking soda or a diluted soap solution, then air dry completely. This process removes dirt, restores grip, and eliminates odors.

## Why Cleaning Your Climbing Shoes Matters

Your rock climbing shoes are more than just footwear; they are a critical part of your safety system. The rubber soles are engineered for maximum friction. Dirt, dust, and chalk can coat this rubber. This coating reduces the friction your shoes provide. A clean sole means better grip on small holds.

Unclean shoes also become a breeding ground for bacteria. Sweat and moisture trapped inside lead to strong odors. This makes your shoes unpleasant to wear. Regular cleaning helps control these smells. It also makes your shoes a more hygienic choice for your feet.

Proper shoe cleaning extends the lifespan of your climbing shoes. Dirt and abrasive particles can degrade the materials. Sweat and bacteria can also break down the shoe's lining. By keeping your shoes clean, you protect the fabric, rubber, and stitching. This means you can enjoy your favorite pair of shoes for many more climbs. Investing a little time in maintenance saves money. It delays the need to buy new shoes.

## Gathering Your Gear: What You Need to Clean Rock Climbing Shoes

Before you start cleaning your rock climbing shoes, gather all your supplies. Having everything ready makes the process smooth. You likely have most items already. I keep a dedicated shoe cleaning kit. This ensures I always have what I need.

Here is a list of essential tools and materials:

*   **Soft Brush:** An old toothbrush or a soft-bristled scrub brush works well. You need something gentle enough not to damage the shoe material. It must be firm enough to remove dirt.
*   **Mild Soap:** Dish soap is usually too strong. A gentle hand soap or a specific shoe cleaner is better. You can also use baby shampoo. Avoid harsh detergents.
*   **Warm Water:** A basin or a bucket of warm water will be helpful. This helps loosen dirt without damaging the shoe.
*   **Clean Cloths/Sponges:** You will use these to wipe down the shoes. They help apply soap and rinse away dirt.
*   **Paper Towels or Old Towels:** These are useful for drying excess water. They also help absorb moisture.
*   **Odor Eliminators (Optional):** Baking soda, shoe deodorizer sprays, or activated charcoal inserts can fight smells. These are great for inside shoe freshness.
*   **Rubbing Alcohol (Optional):** This can help remove stubborn grime from rubber. Use it sparingly on specific spots.
*   **Shoe Dryer (Optional):** A dedicated shoe dryer can speed up the drying process. Ensure it uses low heat or air only.

Having these items ready helps you clean your rock climbing shoes efficiently. You can set up a small cleaning station. This makes regular maintenance simple. It protects your valuable climbing gear.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Cleaning the Exterior of Your Climbing Shoes

Cleaning the outside of your climbing shoes is important. It directly impacts your grip. Dirt and grime on the rubber reduce friction. This makes climbing harder and less safe. I always clean my soles after a dusty outdoor session. This section covers exterior cleaning.

### Removing Loose Dirt

Start by removing any loose dirt or debris. Take your shoes outside. Gently tap them together. This knocks off bigger chunks of mud or dried dirt. Use your soft brush to carefully brush away loose dust. Pay attention to the soles and edges. Get into the crevices where the rubber meets the upper. This initial step prevents you from grinding dirt into the shoe later. It also keeps your cleaning water cleaner.

### Scrubbing the Rubber

Now, focus on the most important part: the rubber soles and rands. These are the parts that touch the rock. Dampen your soft brush with warm water. Apply a small amount of mild soap to the brush. Gently scrub the rubber surfaces. Work in small circular motions. You will see dirt and chalk come off. Rinse the brush frequently in clean water.

For stubborn spots, you can add a drop of rubbing alcohol to a cloth. Wipe the specific area on the rubber. Alcohol removes some types of residue that soap might miss. Be careful not to get alcohol on the fabric parts. Rinse the rubber thoroughly with a damp cloth afterward. This removes all soap residue. Clean rubber gives you back that sticky grip. For more specific tips on keeping your soles pristine, you can refer to our guide on [how to clean the bottom of shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bottom-of-shoes).

### Caring for the Upper

The upper part of your climbing shoes can be synthetic, leather, or a mix. This part usually gets less dirty than the rubber. Dip a clean cloth in warm water with a tiny bit of mild soap. Gently wipe down the fabric or synthetic areas. Use light pressure. Do not saturate the material. If your shoes have leather parts, use a leather-specific cleaner or very mild soap. Avoid harsh scrubbing on leather. It can damage the finish. Wipe away any soap residue with a fresh, damp cloth. The goal is to remove surface dirt without soaking the shoe. This keeps the shoe looking good and prevents material breakdown.

## Battling the Funk: How To Clean the Inside of Climbing Shoes

Climbing shoes often get smelly. Your feet sweat a lot when you climb. This creates a warm, moist environment inside the shoe. Bacteria thrive in these conditions. They cause the unpleasant odors. Addressing the smell inside your shoes is key for hygiene. It also makes your shoes much more enjoyable to wear. I sometimes cringe at the smell of my own shoes.

### Deodorizing Techniques

The simplest way to fight odor is with baking soda. After each use, sprinkle a tablespoon of baking soda inside each shoe. Shake the shoes gently to spread it around. Let it sit overnight. Baking soda absorbs odors. Pour out the baking soda before your next climb. You can also buy shoe deodorizer sachets or sprays. These often contain activated charcoal. Activated charcoal is excellent at trapping smells. Place these inserts in your shoes when not in use. Some people even put cedar shoe trees inside. Cedar naturally absorbs moisture and offers a fresh scent.

### Deep Cleaning the Interior

For a deeper clean, you can wash the inside of your shoes. Mix a small amount of mild soap or baby shampoo with warm water. Create a diluted solution. Dampen a clean cloth with this solution. Gently wipe down the inside lining of the shoes. Do not pour water directly into the shoes. This can saturate the padding. It makes drying very difficult. Focus on wiping the areas where your foot makes contact.

After wiping with soap, use a separate cloth dampened with plain water. Wipe again to remove all soap residue. It is crucial to get all soap out. Soap left behind can irritate your skin. It can also attract more dirt. This process helps remove bacteria and dirt built up over time. It gives the inside of your shoes a fresh start. This internal cleaning is very similar to how you might approach keeping other footwear fresh. You can find more comprehensive advice on [how to clean the inside of used shoes](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-inside-of-used-shoes) for general guidance.

## The Drying Game: Essential Steps for Proper Shoe Drying

Proper drying is one of the most critical steps after cleaning your climbing shoes. If shoes do not dry completely, several problems can arise. Mold and mildew can grow. This creates more bad smells. It also damages the shoe materials. Wet shoes can lose their shape. They can also feel uncomfortable on your feet. I learned this the hard way with a pair that took too long to dry.

*   **Initial Water Removal:** After cleaning, use old towels or paper towels. Stuff them inside your shoes. This helps absorb excess moisture quickly. Replace the towels when they become damp. Continue this until the towels come out almost dry. This speeds up the overall drying time significantly.
*   **Air Circulation is Key:** Find a well-ventilated area for your shoes to dry. A spot near an open window is ideal. You can also place them in front of a fan. Good airflow is essential. It helps evaporate moisture from all parts of the shoe.
*   **Avoid Direct Heat:** Never place your climbing shoes in direct sunlight for long periods. Do not put them near a heater or in a clothes dryer. High heat can damage the rubber. It can also cause the adhesives to weaken. This makes your shoes delaminate. It can also shrink or deform the synthetic or leather uppers. Room temperature air drying is always the safest option.
*   **Position for Drying:** Position your shoes so air can reach both the inside and outside. You can prop them open. Some people use a clothespin to hang them upside down. This allows moisture to escape from the foot opening. Ensure they are not touching anything that can trap moisture.
*   **Patience is a Virtue:** Climbing shoes can take a long time to dry fully. This might be 24 to 48 hours. The drying time depends on how wet they got and the humidity. Do not rush this step. Make sure they are bone dry before wearing them again or storing them. Wearing damp shoes is uncomfortable. It also invites bacteria back.

Following these drying steps keeps your shoes in good condition. It prevents new odors from forming. It also preserves the shape and integrity of your expensive climbing gear.

## Maintaining Your Momentum: Post-Cleaning Care and Storage Tips

Cleaning your climbing shoes is a great start. Proper care after cleaning keeps them in top shape. It extends their life. It also keeps them ready for your next climb. Good maintenance habits save you money. They also improve your climbing experience. I always make sure my shoes are cared for after a session.

*   **Regular Wiping:** After every climbing session, wipe down your shoes. Use a damp cloth to clean the soles and rands. This removes fresh dirt and chalk. It prevents buildup. This simple habit keeps your rubber sticky. It also reduces the need for deep cleaning.
*   **Air Out After Every Use:** Even if you don't deep clean, always air out your shoes. Remove them from your climbing bag. Let them dry in a well-ventilated area. This prevents moisture buildup. It also reduces odor-causing bacteria. Never leave sweaty shoes in a closed bag.
*   **Use Shoe Deodorizers:** Consistently use baking soda or shoe deodorizers. Place them in your shoes after each use. This proactively fights odor. It absorbs moisture and neutralizes smells. This keeps the inside of your shoes fresh.
*   **Proper Storage:** Store your climbing shoes in a cool, dry place. Avoid direct sunlight. Do not store them in humid environments. Extreme temperatures can damage the rubber and adhesives. A gear closet or a dedicated shelf is ideal. Keep them separate from other sweaty gear.
*   **Avoid Crushing:** Do not stack heavy items on your climbing shoes. This can deform their shape. It can also damage the delicate rubber edges. Proper shape is critical for performance. Store them so they maintain their original form.
*   **Consider Resole:** As your soles wear down, consider getting your shoes resoled. A good cobbler can replace the rubber. This is often cheaper than buying new shoes. Regular cleaning helps you see sole wear. It informs you when a resole is needed. Maintaining the upper through cleaning helps ensure the shoes are worth resoling.
*   **Rotation:** If you have multiple pairs, rotate your shoes. This gives each pair time to fully dry and air out. It also reduces wear and tear on any single pair. This extends the life of all your shoes.

These post-cleaning and storage tips are easy to follow. They make a big difference. Your climbing shoes will last longer. They will perform better. They will also smell much better.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Cleaning Climbing Shoes

Cleaning your climbing shoes seems simple. However, some common mistakes can actually harm your shoes. They can shorten their lifespan or reduce performance. Knowing what not to do is as important as knowing what to do. I have made some of these mistakes myself in the past.

*   **Using Harsh Chemicals:** Avoid strong detergents, bleach, or aggressive cleaners. These chemicals can degrade the rubber. They can also damage the adhesives that hold the shoe together. Use only mild soap or cleaners specifically designed for shoes. Harsh chemicals might clean quickly but cause lasting damage.
*   **Machine Washing:** Do not put your climbing shoes in a washing machine. The agitation can damage the shoe's structure. It can also cause the rubber to delaminate. The high heat in a dryer is also very detrimental. Hand washing is always the safest method for climbing shoes.
*   **Soaking Shoes Completely:** Do not submerge your entire shoes in water. This can saturate the internal padding. It makes them extremely difficult to dry. Prolonged wetness can lead to mold and mildew. It can also weaken glues. Target specific dirty areas instead of drenching the whole shoe.
*   **Drying with High Heat:** Never use direct heat sources like hair dryers, radiators, or clothes dryers. High temperatures will melt the rubber. They can also shrink or deform the synthetic or leather materials. Always air dry your shoes in a cool, well-ventilated area. Patience is key for proper drying.
*   **Ignoring the Inside:** Focusing only on the outside is a mistake. The inside of the shoe traps sweat and bacteria. This leads to persistent odor. It also creates an unhygienic environment. Always address both the exterior and interior of your shoes.
*   **Storing Wet Shoes:** Putting wet or damp shoes back in your climbing bag is a big error. This traps moisture. It encourages mold, mildew, and bad smells. Always ensure your shoes are completely dry before storage.
*   **Aggressive Scrubbing:** While you want to clean thoroughly, do not scrub too aggressively. This can wear down the rubber prematurely. It can also damage the upper materials. Use a soft brush and gentle pressure. Let the soap and water do the work.

By avoiding these common cleaning mistakes, you will ensure your climbing shoes remain in excellent condition. This helps them perform their best. It also keeps them with you for many climbing adventures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I clean my climbing shoes?
You should wipe down the soles of your climbing shoes after every climb. This removes chalk and surface dirt. A deeper clean for the exterior and interior is necessary every few weeks. It depends on how often you climb. It also depends on how much your feet sweat. If shoes start to smell, clean them more often. Regular light cleaning prevents the need for intense deep cleaning.

### Can I machine wash climbing shoes?
No, you should not machine wash climbing shoes. The agitation and heat from a washing machine can damage the delicate rubber. It can also weaken the adhesives. This can cause the shoes to fall apart. Always hand wash your climbing shoes gently. Use mild soap and lukewarm water. Hand washing preserves the shoe's structure.

### What about specific types of climbing shoes (e.g., leather, synthetic)?
Most cleaning methods apply to all types. For synthetic shoes, mild soap and water are safe. For leather climbing shoes, be more careful. Use very mild soap. Avoid saturating the leather. You might also use a specific leather cleaner. Always air dry leather shoes. Do not expose them to direct heat. This can cause the leather to crack or shrink.

### How do I get rid of persistent odor?
Persistent odor needs consistent treatment. Sprinkle baking soda inside your shoes after every use. Let it sit overnight. Activated charcoal inserts or shoe deodorizer sprays also help. For deep-seated odor, a diluted solution of white vinegar (1 part vinegar to 3 parts water) can work. Wipe the inside with this solution. Then, wipe with plain water and dry thoroughly. Ensure shoes dry completely.

### Does cleaning affect shoe performance?
Yes, cleaning significantly affects shoe performance. Dirty soles have less friction. This means less grip on the rock. Cleaning the rubber restores its stickiness. It allows the shoe to perform as designed. Clean shoes also feel better. They make you more confident on the wall. Regular cleaning is a part of maintaining optimal shoe performance.
