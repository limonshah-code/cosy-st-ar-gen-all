## Feeding Your Leopard Gecko: How Many Mealworms to Offer?

Welcoming a leopard gecko into your home is exciting. These fascinating reptiles make wonderful pets. Providing proper nutrition is a key part of their care. A common question among new and experienced owners alike is about their diet. Specifically, many people ask, "How many mealworms should a leopard gecko eat?"

Mealworms are a popular and convenient feeder insect. They are often a staple in leopard gecko diets. However, feeding the correct amount is important. Too many can cause health problems like obesity. Too few can lead to malnutrition. This guide will provide clear answers. I will discuss age-specific feeding guidelines for mealworms. We will cover the role of supplements and other vital diet considerations. You will learn how to keep your leopard gecko healthy and happy through proper feeding.

### Takeaway

*   **Juvenile Geckos (0-6 months):** Offer 5-7 mealworms daily.
*   **Sub-Adult Geckos (6-12 months):** Offer 7-10 mealworms every other day.
*   **Adult Geckos (12+ months):** Offer 7-10 mealworms 2-3 times per week.
*   **Always Dust:** Supplement mealworms with calcium and vitamin D3 powder.
*   **Gut Load:** Feed mealworms nutritious food before offering them to your gecko.
*   **Monitor Weight:** Adjust feeding based on your gecko's body condition.

A leopard gecko's diet depends on its age and size. Juvenile geckos (under 6 months) need 5-7 mealworms daily. Sub-adult geckos (6-12 months) can eat 7-10 mealworms every other day. Adult geckos (over 12 months) typically require 7-10 mealworms 2-3 times a week. Always dust mealworms with calcium and D3.

### Understanding Leopard Gecko Nutritional Needs

Leopard geckos are insectivores. This means their natural diet consists solely of insects. In captivity, we must mimic this diet. We provide feeder insects to meet their nutritional needs. Protein is very important for their growth and muscle development. They also need fats for energy.

Calcium and phosphorus balance is crucial. Geckos need more calcium than phosphorus. An imbalance can lead to metabolic bone disease (MBD). This is a serious condition. Mealworms alone do not provide a complete diet. They must be part of a varied insect rotation. Proper nutrition helps maintain strong bones and healthy organs. This also helps with their immune system function.

Digestibility is another key factor. Mealworms have a chitin exoskeleton. This can be harder for geckos to digest in large amounts. Younger geckos need smaller, easier-to-digest insects. As they grow, their digestive systems become stronger. We must consider this when planning their meals. A healthy digestive system works better when the gecko receives proper heat. High heat can lead to stress. [How hot is too hot for a leopard gecko?](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-hot-is-too-hot-for-a-leopard-gecko) can affect their ability to process food.

My goal is to provide a balanced and safe diet for my gecko. I make sure to offer a variety of insects. This ensures they get all necessary nutrients. Supplements play a vital role in balancing their diet. They help prevent common deficiencies.

### Mealworm Feeding Guidelines for Juvenile Leopard Geckos

Baby and juvenile leopard geckos grow very fast. They need a lot of energy and protein. This supports their rapid development. Their metabolism is also much higher than an adult gecko. Because of this, they require more frequent feedings.

When your leopard gecko is a hatchling (0-3 months), they are small. Offer them small mealworms. Young geckos can eat about 5-7 small mealworms every day. They should have food available daily. This ensures they get enough energy. As they grow, they may eat a few more per feeding. The size of the mealworm should not be larger than the space between your gecko's eyes. This prevents choking and digestive problems.

For geckos between 3 and 6 months old, they are still juveniles. Their growth rate starts to slow slightly. You can continue to offer 5-7 mealworms daily. You might notice they are not eating every single one. This is normal as their appetite changes. Ensure you dust the mealworms with calcium with D3 at every feeding. This is vital for bone development. Always provide clean water. A young gecko needs consistent food and calcium.

It is important to watch their body condition. A healthy juvenile gecko should look plump but not obese. Their tail should be thick. If your gecko is not eating, you might need to adjust the environment. Sometimes, a gecko will stop eating for a short period. Learn [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating) to avoid problems.

### Feeding Adult Leopard Geckos: Mealworm Portions

Adult leopard geckos have different needs compared to juveniles. Their growth has stopped. Their metabolism has slowed down. This means they do not need to eat as often or as much. Overfeeding adult geckos can lead to obesity. Obesity causes many health issues.

For adult leopard geckos (12 months and older), feeding mealworms 2-3 times per week is generally enough. Each feeding can consist of 7-10 medium-sized mealworms. The exact number depends on your gecko's size and activity level. Some larger geckos may eat a few more. Smaller geckos might eat slightly less. Always aim for mealworms that are not wider than the space between their eyes. This size rule helps prevent impaction.

I adjust my adult gecko's mealworm portion based on their body condition. I check their tail for fat storage. A healthy adult leopard gecko has a thick tail base. This indicates good fat reserves. If the tail looks thin, I might offer a bit more food. If it looks very plump, I might reduce the amount slightly. Feeding too much too often can lead to weight gain. Some owners wonder [can I feed my leopard gecko once a week?](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-i-feed-my-leopard-gecko-once-a-week) For adults, this can be an option if combined with slightly larger meals.

Always dust mealworms with calcium and vitamin D3 at every feeding for adult geckos. I also use a multivitamin powder once a week. This ensures they get all necessary vitamins. Providing fresh water daily is also important. Adult geckos need a stable diet to maintain their health.

### The Importance of Gut Loading and Dusting Mealworms

Feeding mealworms straight from the container is not enough. Mealworms themselves are not nutritionally complete. They lack essential vitamins and minerals for geckos. This is why gut loading and dusting are very important. These practices enhance the nutritional value of the mealworms. They turn a basic food item into a nutritious meal for your gecko.

**Gut Loading:** This means feeding the mealworms themselves a nutritious diet before offering them to your gecko. Think of it as preparing the mealworms to be a healthy package. You should feed mealworms a mix of fresh fruits, vegetables, and a commercial gut-loading diet. Good options include carrots, sweet potatoes, apples, and leafy greens. Avoid citrus fruits. Offer these gut-loading foods for at least 24-48 hours before feeding the mealworms to your gecko. This ensures the mealworms' digestive tracts are full of nutrients.

**Dusting:** This involves coating the mealworms with a supplement powder. You need two main types of powder. One is calcium carbonate with vitamin D3. The other is a multi-vitamin powder.

*   **Calcium with D3:** Use this at every feeding. Place the mealworms in a small bag or container. Add a pinch of calcium powder. Gently shake the container to coat the mealworms evenly. Vitamin D3 helps geckos absorb calcium. Without enough D3, calcium cannot be used.
*   **Multivitamin:** Use this once a week. Some gecko owners use it once every two weeks. This powder provides other essential vitamins and trace minerals. These include Vitamin A, Vitamin E, and various B vitamins. Too much of some vitamins, like Vitamin A, can be harmful. So, follow the product instructions carefully.

Both gut loading and dusting prevent nutrient deficiencies. They help prevent metabolic bone disease. My gecko's bones stay strong because I consistently use these practices. Without these, even regular feeding of mealworms would not provide complete nutrition.

### Common Feeding Mistakes to Avoid with Mealworms

Feeding leopard geckos mealworms seems simple. Yet, common mistakes can lead to serious health issues. Avoiding these errors ensures your gecko's long-term well-being. I have learned to watch for these problems.

One major mistake is **overfeeding**. Offering too many mealworms or feeding too often leads to obesity. An obese gecko has thick fat deposits, especially in the tail and armpits. Obesity puts strain on organs. It can shorten their lifespan. It is best to stick to recommended mealworm quantities. Adjust based on your gecko's activity level and body condition.

Another issue is **underfeeding**. If your gecko does not get enough food, it becomes malnourished. This causes weight loss and weakness. Their tail will look thin. Malnutrition weakens their immune system. This makes them prone to illness. A gecko can go without eating for some time, but it's not ideal. Understanding [how long can a leopard gecko go without eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-long-can-a-leopard-gecko-go-without-eating) helps manage short fasts. However, consistent underfeeding is harmful.

**Impaction** is a serious risk with mealworms. Mealworms have a hard chitin exoskeleton. If a gecko eats too many, or if the mealworms are too large, this shell can be hard to digest. It can block the digestive tract. This is more common in young geckos or those kept on loose substrates like sand. Symptoms include lethargy, loss of appetite, and inability to pass stool. Using appropriately sized mealworms and providing proper heat helps digestion.

**Lack of diet variety** is also a common mistake. Feeding only mealworms, even with supplements, is not ideal. A gecko needs a varied diet for complete nutrition. Mealworms are good, but they are high in fat and low in calcium compared to some other insects. Rotating with crickets, Dubia roaches, and black soldier fly larvae provides a wider range of nutrients. This also makes mealtime more stimulating for your gecko. I make sure to offer a mix of insects.

Neglecting **gut loading and dusting** is a critical error. As discussed, these steps are vital. Without them, your gecko will develop nutrient deficiencies. This includes Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD). MBD causes bone deformities and can be fatal. Always make sure mealworms are gut-loaded and dusted. These simple steps prevent many serious health problems.

### Varying Your Leopard Gecko's Diet Beyond Mealworms

While mealworms are a common food, relying on them solely is not the best approach. A varied diet is essential for a leopard gecko's complete nutrition. It provides a broader spectrum of nutrients. It also keeps your gecko mentally stimulated. Offering different insects mimics their natural foraging behavior.

Think of mealworms as one part of a balanced meal plan. They are a good source of fat and protein. However, they are low in calcium. They also have a tougher chitin shell. Introducing other feeder insects complements their diet. This ensures all nutritional bases are covered.

Here are some excellent alternatives and additions to mealworms:

*   **Crickets:** Crickets are a staple for many leopard gecko owners. They are lean and provide good protein. They also encourage natural hunting behavior. Crickets should always be gut-loaded and dusted.
*   **Dubia Roaches:** These are considered one of the most nutritious feeder insects. They are easy to gut load and have a good calcium-to-phosphorus ratio. They are also quieter and cleaner than crickets. Many geckos enjoy hunting them.
*   **Black Soldier Fly Larvae (BSFL):** Often called "CalciWorms" or "NutriGrubs." BSFL are excellent because they are naturally high in calcium. They do not require dusting with calcium. This makes them a great option for geckos needing extra calcium. They are also soft-bodied and easy to digest.
*   **Superworms:** Superworms are larger and fatter than mealworms. They are a good occasional treat for adult geckos. Like mealworms, they have a tough shell. They should be fed sparingly to avoid impaction or obesity. They are also a good alternative to mealworms for variety. I find that [how many superworms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-superworms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) is similar in principle to mealworms, but they should be given less often due to their fat content.
*   **Silkworms:** These are soft-bodied and highly nutritious. They are a great source of calcium and protein. Silkworms are often used for geckos recovering from illness or for those with sensitive stomachs. They are generally more expensive and harder to find.

I aim to offer 2-3 types of insects regularly. For example, I might feed crickets and mealworms most often. Then I add Dubia roaches or BSFL every few feedings. This variety ensures my gecko receives a diverse range of nutrients. It also makes feeding time interesting for them. Remember to always gut load and dust all feeder insects.

### Choosing the Right Size Mealworms for Your Gecko

Selecting the correct size mealworm is just as important as the quantity. Feeding mealworms that are too large can cause serious problems. It puts your gecko at risk of choking or impaction. Impaction means the food blocks their digestive system. This can be life-threatening without veterinary care.

A simple rule guides mealworm size: The mealworm should be no wider than the space between your leopard gecko's eyes. This rule applies to all feeder insects. Following this guideline helps ensure the food can pass safely through their mouth and digestive tract.

*   **For Hatchlings and Small Juveniles:** Choose small mealworms. These are often labeled as "mini" or "small" mealworms. They are easy for tiny geckos to swallow and digest. Their small size reduces the risk of impaction.
*   **For Growing Juveniles:** As your gecko grows, you can offer slightly larger mealworms. Move from small to medium-sized mealworms. Always stick to the "space between the eyes" rule. They are still growing, so their digestive system adapts.
*   **For Adult Geckos:** Adult geckos can handle medium to large mealworms. They have fully developed digestive systems. Even then, avoid overly large mealworms. Larger ones might be tempting to buy, but they are not always safe. If a mealworm looks too big, choose a smaller one.

I always check the size of my mealworms before feeding. I do not want to risk my gecko's health. If I have mealworms that are too large, I save them for my larger reptiles. Or, I might wait for them to pupate and turn into beetles. Beetles are not typically fed to geckos. If you are unsure, always choose a smaller size. It is better to be safe than sorry.

### FAQ Section

#### Can leopard geckos eat only mealworms?
No, leopard geckos should not eat only mealworms. While mealworms are a good part of their diet, they lack some essential nutrients. They are high in fat and low in calcium. A varied diet with other insects like crickets, Dubia roaches, and black soldier fly larvae is important. This ensures your gecko receives a complete range of vitamins and minerals.

#### How often should I feed mealworms to a gecko?
Feeding frequency depends on your gecko's age. Juvenile geckos (under 6 months) need 5-7 mealworms daily. Sub-adults (6-12 months) can eat 7-10 mealworms every other day. Adult geckos (over 12 months) should eat 7-10 mealworms 2-3 times per week. Always adjust based on your gecko's body condition and appetite.

#### Why is my leopard gecko not eating mealworms?
There are several reasons a leopard gecko might stop eating mealworms. Environmental issues like incorrect temperatures or humidity can reduce appetite. Stress from a new environment, tank mates, or illness also plays a role. Sometimes, geckos just become picky. Ensure mealworms are properly gut-loaded and dusted. If refusal persists, consult a reptile veterinarian.

#### Can mealworms cause impaction?
Yes, mealworms can cause impaction, especially if they are too large or fed in excess. Their chitin exoskeleton is hard to digest. This risk is higher for young geckos or those kept on loose substrates. Always feed mealworms no wider than the space between your gecko's eyes. Ensure proper enclosure temperatures to aid digestion.

#### What size mealworms should I feed my gecko?
The size of the mealworm should not be wider than the space between your leopard gecko's eyes. For hatchlings and small juveniles, use small or "mini" mealworms. For growing juveniles and adults, medium-sized mealworms are usually suitable. Always choose a size that your gecko can easily swallow and digest to prevent choking or impaction.
