## Keeping Your Roomba 690 Clean: A Full Guide

Your Roomba 690 works hard to keep your home tidy. It picks up dirt, dust, and pet hair daily. However, all that hard work means it gathers debris inside its parts. Over time, this buildup can reduce its cleaning power.

I understand you want your Roomba to run at its best. Regular cleaning is key to ensuring it performs efficiently and lasts longer. This comprehensive guide will show you exactly how to clean Roomba 690 components, from the dust bin to the sensors. We will cover brushes, filters, wheels, and more. Following these steps will help your Roomba maintain its suction and navigation capabilities. Let's get your robot vacuum back to optimal shape.

### Takeaway

To keep your Roomba 690 performing its best, perform regular cleaning:
*   Empty the dust bin after each use.
*   Clean the filter weekly or bi-weekly.
*   Remove hair and debris from the main brushes (debris extractors) weekly.
*   Clean the side brush and front caster wheel monthly.
*   Wipe down sensors and charging contacts regularly.
*   Inspect all parts for wear and replace them as needed.

### How to Clean Roomba 690: A Quick Answer

To clean your Roomba 690, regularly empty the dust bin and clean the filter. Remove hair from the main brushes, side brush, and front caster wheel. Wipe down all sensors and charging contacts. This routine helps your Roomba 690 maintain its cleaning efficiency and extend its lifespan.

## Why Regular Roomba 690 Cleaning Matters for Peak Performance

I often hear people ask why cleaning their Roomba 690 is so important. The answer is simple: your Roomba cannot do its best work if it is clogged with dirt. Dust, hair, and debris accumulate in crucial areas. This buildup directly impacts the robot's ability to pick up new dirt.

Think about the spinning brushes or the filter. If these are full of old debris, they cannot effectively capture new particles from your floors. Your Roomba might leave behind streaks of dirt or miss spots entirely. Moreover, clogged parts force the motor to work harder. This extra strain reduces battery life and can shorten the lifespan of the entire unit. Regular cleaning prevents these problems. It ensures your Roomba keeps your home truly clean.

### Enhancing Cleaning Efficiency

A clean Roomba 690 picks up more dirt with less effort. The brushes rotate freely, sweeping debris into the suction path. The filter allows air to flow through properly, capturing fine dust particles. When these parts are dirty, the Roomba's ability to clean decreases. It cannot collect as much, and it might even spread some dust around.

I have seen many Roombas that struggle because of neglected maintenance. A clean machine means a cleaner home. It completes its cleaning cycles more effectively. You will notice a visible difference in your floor's cleanliness. This is the main goal of using a robot vacuum.

### Extending Your Roomba's Lifespan

Cleaning your Roomba 690 is an investment in its future. Dirt buildup can damage internal components over time. For example, hair wrapped around the brushes can strain the motor. Clogged filters can overheat the system. These issues can lead to costly repairs or even require a full replacement.

Regular maintenance helps prevent premature wear and tear. It keeps all parts moving smoothly. Your Roomba will run more quietly and efficiently. This simple habit saves you money in the long run. It ensures your Roomba 690 stays a reliable cleaning partner for years to come.

## Essential Tools for Cleaning Your Roomba 690

Before you start cleaning your Roomba 690, gather a few basic tools. Having everything ready makes the process smoother. You probably have most of these items around your house already. I always prepare my cleaning station first.

These tools are simple but very effective. They help you reach tight spaces and remove stubborn debris. Using the right tools also prevents damage to your Roomba's delicate parts. Let's look at what you will need.

### What You Need

Here is a list of the essential cleaning tools:

*   **Small screwdriver (Phillips head):** You will use this to remove the side brush. It is a common household tool. Make sure it fits the tiny screw.
*   **Cleaning tool (often included with Roomba):** This handy tool has a brush and a cutting blade. It is perfect for removing hair from the main brushes. If you lost yours, a pair of small scissors will work.
*   **Soft, dry cloth or microfiber cloth:** Use this for wiping down sensors and surfaces. It removes dust without scratching. Avoid using abrasive materials.
*   **Compressed air (optional):** This can help dislodge dust from hard-to-reach areas. Be careful not to spray directly into sensitive electronics.
*   **Small brush (like an old toothbrush):** Great for scrubbing stubborn dirt from wheels and other small components. It gets into grooves effectively.
*   **Trash can:** Have it nearby to dispose of all the dirt you remove. You will be surprised how much your Roomba collects.

Having these tools ready saves time and frustration. It ensures you can clean every part of your Roomba thoroughly. A prepared cleaner is a successful cleaner!

## Cleaning the Dust Bin and Filter of Your Roomba 690

The dust bin and filter are the first line of defense against dirt. They collect all the debris your Roomba picks up. Keeping these parts clean is crucial for effective vacuuming. I make sure to clean these after almost every use.

A full or clogged filter prevents proper airflow. This means your Roomba cannot create strong suction. A clean filter allows air to pass freely, trapping dust and allergens. This section guides you through emptying and cleaning these vital components. You will see a big difference in performance.

### Emptying the Dust Bin

Emptying the dust bin is the easiest and most frequent cleaning task. You should do this after every cleaning cycle, especially if you have pets. A full bin cannot hold more dirt.

Here are the steps to empty the dust bin:

1.  **Remove the bin:** Press the tab on the back of your Roomba 690. Pull the dust bin straight out. It slides out easily.
2.  **Open the bin:** Locate the small latch or button on the bin. Open it to access the collection compartment.
3.  **Discard debris:** Tip the bin over a trash can. Shake out all the collected dirt, dust, and hair. I often tap it gently to loosen trapped debris.
4.  **Wipe the bin:** Use a dry cloth to wipe the inside of the bin. This removes fine dust particles that cling to the plastic.

Some dust bins can be rinsed with water, but **check your specific Roomba 690 manual first.** The 600 series often has washable bins, but you must ensure it is completely dry before putting it back. Never wash the filter.

### Cleaning the Filter

The filter traps fine dust and allergens. It needs regular cleaning to maintain suction. I recommend cleaning the filter weekly, or more often if you have pets or allergies. This directly impacts the air quality in your home.

Follow these steps to clean the filter:

1.  **Remove the filter:** The filter is located inside the dust bin. Grasp the small tab on the filter. Pull it straight out of its slot.
2.  **Tap out dust:** Hold the filter over a trash can. Gently tap it against the side of the can. This dislodges trapped dust and debris. Do not hit it too hard.
3.  **Brush off remaining debris:** Use a small brush (like an old toothbrush) to brush away any clinging dust. You can also use a vacuum cleaner with a hose attachment to gently vacuum the filter.
4.  **Inspect and replace:** Check the filter for tears or excessive wear. If it looks damaged or very discolored, it is time for a new one. I replace my Roomba filters every two to three months. You can find more details on [how to clean a Roomba filter](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-roomba-filter) in our dedicated guide.

**Important:** Roomba 690 filters are **not washable**. Washing them will damage the filter material and render them unusable. Always use a dry method for cleaning the filter. ## Maintaining the Debris Extractors (Main Brushes)

The debris extractors, also known as the main brushes, are the heart of your Roomba 690's cleaning system. They spin rapidly, agitating dirt and sweeping it into the suction path. Pet hair, human hair, and carpet fibers often get tangled around these brushes. This entanglement significantly reduces their effectiveness. I know from experience that tangled brushes can make a Roomba very noisy.

Cleaning these brushes is critical. It ensures strong suction and prevents motor strain. I recommend cleaning the debris extractors weekly, especially if you have long-haired pets. This simple task makes a huge difference in your Roomba's performance.

### Removing and Cleaning the Brushes

Taking out the brushes is easy. Roomba designed this process to be straightforward for regular maintenance. Do not worry about breaking anything.

Here is how to remove and clean your Roomba 690's main brushes:

1.  **Turn Roomba over:** Flip your Roomba 690 upside down on a flat surface. Make sure it is powered off. You will see the underside, where the brushes are.
2.  **Open the brush guard:** Locate the two yellow tabs on the brush frame. Push these tabs to open the brush guard. It will swing up, revealing the brushes.
3.  **Remove the brushes:** Grasp the yellow square and yellow hex end of each brush. Lift them out of their compartments. They should come out easily.
4.  **Remove hair and debris:**
    *   Use the included cleaning tool (or small scissors) to cut away hair wrapped around the brushes. The blade on the cleaning tool is designed for this.
    *   Slide the yellow end caps off the brushes. Clean any hair or debris from under these caps. These are common spots for buildup.
    *   Wipe down the brushes with a dry cloth to remove any fine dust.
    *   Clean the brush bearings. These are small plastic parts that fit into the brush ends. Ensure they spin freely.

5.  **Clean the brush compartment:** Use your finger or a dry cloth to remove any hair or debris from the brush compartment itself. This space can also collect dust.

### Reinstalling the Brushes

Putting the brushes back in is just as simple as taking them out. Make sure they click into place correctly. Incorrect installation can cause noise or poor cleaning.

Follow these steps to reinstall the brushes:

1.  **Insert the square end first:** Place the brush with the square yellow end into the square opening on the cleaning head module.
2.  **Insert the hex end:** Then, place the brush with the hex (six-sided) yellow end into the hex opening.
3.  **Repeat for the second brush:** Do the same for the other main brush. Ensure both brushes are seated properly. They should spin freely when gently rotated.
4.  **Close the guard:** Close the brush guard until it clicks firmly into place. The yellow tabs should be secure.

Properly cleaned and reinstalled brushes mean your Roomba 690 can pick up dirt efficiently again. For more in-depth guidance on cleaning these specific components, check out our guide on [how to clean Roomba debris extractors](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-roomba-debris-extractors).

## Caring for the Side Brush and Wheels

Your Roomba 690's side brush and wheels are crucial for edge cleaning and movement. The side brush sweeps debris from corners and walls into the main cleaning path. The wheels allow your Roomba to navigate your home smoothly. Neglecting these parts can lead to poor edge cleaning and impaired movement. I always check these parts because they often get forgotten.

Hair and lint commonly tangle around the side brush. Dirt and debris can also pack into the wheel wells. Regular maintenance of these components ensures your Roomba reaches every corner and moves without getting stuck. This section shows you how to keep them spinning freely.

### Cleaning the Side Brush

The side brush works hard, constantly spinning to reach edges. It often collects a lot of hair. Cleaning it prevents tangles and ensures it can effectively sweep.

Here is how to clean the side brush:

1.  **Turn Roomba over:** Place your Roomba 690 upside down on a flat surface. Power it off.
2.  **Remove the screw:** Use a small Phillips head screwdriver to remove the screw holding the side brush in place. It is a single screw in the center of the brush.
3.  **Remove the brush:** Gently lift the side brush off its post.
4.  **Clear debris:** Remove any hair, string, or debris wrapped around the brush and its post. I use my fingers or the Roomba cleaning tool for this.
5.  **Clean the brush:** Wipe the side brush itself with a damp cloth if it is dusty. Ensure it is completely dry before reattaching.
6.  **Reattach the brush:** Place the brush back onto the post. Align the screw hole. Insert and tighten the screw until it is snug. Do not overtighten it.

Regular side brush cleaning helps your Roomba sweep more effectively. It prevents the brush from getting stuck or breaking bristles.

### Maintaining the Front Caster Wheel

The front caster wheel allows your Roomba 690 to pivot and change direction easily. It spins freely in all directions. Dirt, hair, and dust can get trapped in its housing. This can cause the wheel to stick, making your Roomba drag or move in circles.

Follow these steps to clean the front caster wheel:

1.  **Remove the wheel module:** Grip the front caster wheel firmly. Pull it straight out of its housing. It should pop out.
2.  **Clear debris from wheel and axle:** Remove any hair or debris wrapped around the wheel's axle. You can use the cleaning tool's blade or small scissors.
3.  **Clean the wheel housing:** Use a small brush or compressed air to clear dirt from inside the wheel's housing. This is where a lot of gunk collects.
4.  **Clean the wheel itself:** If the wheel is dirty, wipe it with a damp cloth. Ensure it is dry.
5.  **Reinsert the wheel:** Push the clean wheel module firmly back into its housing until it clicks into place. It should spin freely.

### Cleaning the Drive Wheels

The two main drive wheels propel your Roomba 690. While they do not tangle as easily as brushes, dirt can accumulate around their axles. This buildup can make them stiff or even cause them to stop rotating properly.

Here is how to clean the drive wheels:

1.  **Inspect for debris:** Push each drive wheel up and down several times. This action dislodges trapped debris.
2.  **Remove visible dirt:** Use a dry cloth to wipe down the surface of the wheels. Look for any hair or lint caught in the gaps around the wheel.
3.  **Use a small brush:** If necessary, use a small brush (like an old toothbrush) to scrub dirt from the grooves and edges of the wheels. This helps restore their grip.
4.  **Check for free movement:** Ensure the wheels move smoothly and do not feel stiff.

Keeping these wheels clean ensures your Roomba can navigate your home efficiently. It prevents it from getting stuck or reporting wheel errors. ## Wiping Down Sensors and Charging Contacts

Sensors and charging contacts are the "eyes and ears" of your Roomba 690. The sensors help it navigate, avoid cliffs, and detect dirt. The charging contacts ensure it gets power. If these parts are dirty, your Roomba might bump into walls, fall down stairs, or fail to charge. I find this part of the cleaning routine crucial for smart navigation.

Dust, grime, and pet hair can easily accumulate on these delicate surfaces. This interferes with their ability to function correctly. Regular wiping keeps them clear and operational. This simple step prevents many common Roomba error messages.

### Cleaning the Cliff Sensors

Your Roomba 690 has cliff sensors on its underside. These sensors detect ledges and stairs, preventing your robot from falling. If they get dirty, your Roomba might stop at edges unnecessarily or, worse, fall down stairs.

Here is how to clean the cliff sensors:

1.  **Locate sensors:** Turn your Roomba 690 upside down. You will see several small, clear windows around the edges of the underside. These are the cliff sensors.
2.  **Wipe gently:** Use a clean, dry microfiber cloth. Gently wipe each sensor window. Ensure no dust or smudges remain. Do not use water or cleaning solutions.
3.  **Check for obstructions:** Make sure no hair or debris is blocking the sensor windows. Sometimes, larger pieces of lint can stick there.

Clean cliff sensors ensure your Roomba navigates safely. This protects your robot and your home. For specific advice on keeping these critical parts clear, refer to our detailed article on [how to clean cliff sensors on Roomba](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-cliff-sensors-on-roomba).

### Cleaning the Dirt Detect Sensors

The Roomba 690 uses Dirt Detect sensors to find areas with more concentrated dirt. It then focuses its cleaning efforts there. These sensors are also on the underside. If they are dirty, your Roomba might miss heavily soiled spots.

Here is how to clean the Dirt Detect sensors:

1.  **Find the sensors:** These are usually small, clear or black circles located near the main brushes on the underside.
2.  **Wipe with a dry cloth:** Use your clean, dry microfiber cloth. Gently wipe the surface of these sensors. Remove any dust or grime.

Clean Dirt Detect sensors help your Roomba clean smarter. It ensures your robot truly focuses on the dirtiest areas of your home.

### Cleaning the Charging Contacts

The charging contacts on your Roomba 690 and its Home Base ensure a good connection. This connection is vital for recharging the battery. If these contacts become dirty, your Roomba might not charge properly. It could return to the base but not power up.

Here is how to clean the charging contacts:

1.  **Locate contacts:** On your Roomba, look for two metal strips on the underside, near the front. On the Home Base, find the corresponding metal strips.
2.  **Wipe with a dry cloth:** Use a clean, dry microfiber cloth or a melamine foam eraser (like a Magic Eraser, used dry). Gently rub the metal contacts on both the Roomba and the Home Base. This removes any film or corrosion.
3.  **Ensure dryness:** Make sure the contacts are completely dry before allowing the Roomba to charge.

Clean charging contacts guarantee your Roomba is always ready for its next cleaning mission. It prevents charging errors and keeps your robot powered. ## Battery Care and Long-Term Roomba 690 Maintenance

Beyond regular cleaning of individual parts, there are broader steps you can take. These steps ensure your Roomba 690 lasts as long as possible. Proper battery care and smart storage practices are crucial. They impact how well your Roomba performs over its lifetime. I always consider the long game when maintaining my appliances.

Thinking about long-term maintenance helps you avoid common pitfalls. It keeps your Roomba running efficiently year after year. This section covers important tips for battery health and general care. You will learn how to make your Roomba a lasting part of your home.

### Optimizing Battery Life

The battery is the power source for your Roomba 690. Its health directly affects how long your robot can clean. Poor battery care leads to shorter run times and eventual replacement. I want my Roomba to clean my whole house, and good battery life helps that.

Here are tips for extending your Roomba 690 battery life:

*   **Keep it charged:** Always return your Roomba to its Home Base when it is not cleaning. Roombas use a small amount of power even when idle. Keeping it on the charger ensures it is always ready and helps maintain battery health.
*   **Avoid deep discharges:** Do not let the battery completely drain and sit empty for long periods. This can damage the battery cells.
*   **Charge fully:** Allow your Roomba to complete a full charge cycle regularly. This helps calibrate the battery.
*   **Storage:** If you plan not to use your Roomba for an extended period (more than a few weeks), fully charge the battery. Then, remove the battery and store the Roomba in a cool, dry place. Charge the battery every six months if stored separately.
*   **Replacement:** Over time, all batteries degrade. If your Roomba's run time significantly decreases, it might be time for a new battery. The Roomba 690 battery is user-replaceable.

Good battery practices are vital for your Roomba's efficiency. They ensure your robot can clean larger areas without needing frequent recharges.

### Routine Checks and Part Replacements

Some Roomba 690 parts are consumables. They wear out over time and require replacement. Regular checks help you identify worn parts before they cause problems. I always keep an eye on these things.

Here are key routine checks and replacement guidelines:

*   **Filters:** Replace the filter every two months, or more often if you have pets. A new filter improves suction and air quality.
*   **Debris Extractors (Brushes):** Replace the main brushes every 6 to 12 months. Worn brushes become less effective at picking up debris.
*   **Side Brush:** Replace the side brush every 3 to 6 months. Worn or bent bristles do not sweep efficiently.
*   **Front Caster Wheel:** If the wheel starts to stick or drag even after cleaning, it might need replacement. The wheel can wear down or its axle can deform.
*   **Visual Inspection:** Regularly check the entire Roomba for any visible damage. Look for cracked plastic, loose screws, or frayed wires. Address minor issues early.
*   **Firmware Updates:** Ensure your Roomba's software is up to date through the iRobot HOME app. Updates can improve performance and fix bugs.

Establishing a regular maintenance schedule keeps your Roomba 690 in top condition. It ensures reliable cleaning and extends the life of your investment. This proactive approach saves you hassle and money.

## Troubleshooting Common Cleaning-Related Issues

Even with regular cleaning, your Roomba 690 might sometimes show signs of trouble. Many common issues are directly related to dirt or debris. Understanding these problems helps you diagnose and fix them quickly. I have seen many people frustrated, only to find a simple cleaning solution.

This section covers common Roomba 690 problems that cleaning can resolve. It provides practical solutions to get your robot back on track. You will learn to identify when a quick clean is all it needs.

### Roomba Not Picking Up Dirt

If your Roomba 690 is running but leaving dirt behind, cleaning is usually the first step. This is a common complaint I hear. It indicates a blockage or a worn-out part.

Consider these solutions:

*   **Empty Dust Bin:** The bin might be full. Empty it completely.
*   **Clean Filter:** A clogged filter severely reduces suction. Clean or replace it.
*   **Clean Brushes (Debris Extractors):** Hair wrapped around the main brushes prevents them from agitating and sweeping dirt. Remove all tangles. Ensure the yellow end caps and bearings are clean.
*   **Check Side Brush:** A tangled or worn side brush might not sweep dirt effectively into the main cleaning path. Clean or replace it.
*   **Inspect Suction Path:** Sometimes, debris can get stuck in the channel leading from the brushes to the dust bin. Use a finger or small tool to clear any blockages.

Addressing these areas usually restores your Roomba's cleaning power. It's often a simple fix.

### Roomba Moving Erratically or Getting Stuck

If your Roomba 690 is moving strangely, bumping into things, or getting stuck more often, dirty sensors or wheels are likely culprits. Its navigation system relies on clear data.

Try these solutions:

*   **Clean Cliff Sensors:** Dirty cliff sensors can cause the Roomba to stop unnecessarily or move cautiously. Wipe all clear windows on the underside.
*   **Clean Front Caster Wheel:** If the front wheel is jammed with hair or debris, the Roomba cannot pivot correctly. It might spin in circles or drag. Remove and clean it thoroughly.
*   **Clean Drive Wheels:** Ensure the two main drive wheels move freely. Clear any debris around their axles.
*   **Clean Bumper Sensors:** The bumper has small sensors that detect obstacles. Wipe the entire bumper area clean. Make sure it moves in and out freely.
*   **Clean Home Base Contacts:** If the Roomba struggles to dock, the charging contacts on either the robot or the Home Base might be dirty. Wipe them clean.

These cleaning steps often resolve navigation issues. They help your Roomba move smoothly and efficiently again.

### Roomba Making Unusual Noises

A Roomba 690 should operate with a consistent hum. If you hear grinding, squeaking, or unusually loud noises, it often signals debris interference. I always pay attention to new noises my Roomba makes.

Here are common noise-related issues and their solutions:

*   **Tangled Brushes:** Hair and debris caught in the main brushes or their bearings often create a grinding or squealing sound. Remove the brushes and clean them thoroughly.
*   **Dirty Front Caster Wheel:** A front wheel that is struggling to spin can produce a squeaking or dragging noise. Clean or replace the wheel.
*   **Side Brush Obstruction:** If the side brush is jammed, it might make a clicking or straining sound. Remove and clean the side brush and its post.
*   **Debris in Wheel Wells:** Debris around the main drive wheels can also cause grinding sounds as the wheels try to move. Push the wheels up and down and clear any visible debris.

Addressing these noise issues quickly prevents further damage to the Roomba's internal mechanisms. A quiet Roomba is a happy Roomba.
