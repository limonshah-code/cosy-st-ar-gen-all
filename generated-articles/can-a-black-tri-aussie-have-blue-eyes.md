## The Merle Gene: A Key to Blue Eyes (and Why Black Tris Are Different)

The merle gene plays a major role in eye color for many dog breeds, including Australian Shepherds. This gene, known as the M-locus, dilutes pigment irregularly. It creates patches of lighter color in the coat. This also impacts eye pigment. Dogs with the merle gene often have blue eyes. This is because the gene reduces pigment in the iris.

## Heterochromia: Blue Eyes in Black Tri Aussies

Heterochromia is a condition where an animal has different colored eyes. For dogs, this can mean one blue eye and one brown eye. It can also mean part of one eye is blue while the rest is another color. This is called sectoral heterochromia. Heterochromia is the most common reason a black tri Aussie might have blue eyes.

This trait occurs due to a lack of melanin in parts of the iris. Melanin is the pigment that gives eyes their color. If a specific area of the iris lacks melanin, it appears blue. This can happen independently of the merle gene. It often relates to genes involved in white spotting patterns. Many black tri Aussies have significant white markings on their faces. These white markings are caused by genes that prevent pigment formation in certain areas. Sometimes, this extends to the eyes. This is why a blue eye might appear where white fur surrounds it.

Heterochromia does not affect a dog's vision. A dog with two different colored eyes sees just as well as a dog with two eyes of the same color. It is purely a cosmetic trait. In fact, many people find heterochromia very striking and beautiful. It adds a unique charm to the dog's appearance. This condition is a natural genetic variation. It is not a sign of any health issue. This makes a black tri Aussie with a blue eye a truly special find.

## Are Blue Eyes a Health Concern for Australian Shepherds?

A common question concerns the health of blue-eyed dogs. Many people worry that blue eyes indicate vision problems. For Australian Shepherds, blue eyes themselves do not inherently cause health issues. A blue-eyed Australian Shepherd with normal genetics should have good vision. The color of the iris does not affect the function of the eye.

However, the *reason* for blue eyes can sometimes be linked to health concerns. This mainly applies to merle dogs. Specifically, double merles. A double merle dog inherits two copies of the merle gene. This results from breeding two merle dogs together. Double merles often have excessive white markings. They frequently have blue eyes or even no eye pigment. These dogs can suffer from severe health problems. These issues include deafness and blindness. This is why responsible breeders avoid breeding two merle dogs.

For black tri Aussies, blue eyes usually stem from heterochromia or other non-merle related genes. These genetic factors are typically benign. They do not pose a health risk to the dog's eyes or ears. Therefore, a black tri Aussie with blue eyes is usually healthy. Their vision is normal. Their hearing is normal. ### Do all Australian Shepherds with blue eyes have health problems?

No, not all Australian Shepherds with blue eyes have health problems. Blue eyes themselves are not a health concern. The primary risk is with "double merle" dogs. ### Can a black tri Aussie have two blue eyes?

### How does genetics determine eye color in dogs?

Eye color in dogs is determined by the amount and distribution of melanin in the iris. More melanin results in brown eyes. Less melanin results in blue or amber eyes. Several genes control melanin production and dispersal. The merle gene (M-locus) is a primary factor. Other genes, like those for white spotting, can also affect eye color by influencing pigment.
