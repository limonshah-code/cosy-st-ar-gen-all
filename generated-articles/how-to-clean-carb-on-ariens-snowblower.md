## Revive Your Ariens: How To Clean Carb On Your Snowblower

Winter arrives, and snow starts falling. Your Ariens snowblower needs to work perfectly when you need it most. Few things are as frustrating as pulling the starter rope repeatedly only to have your machine sputter or refuse to start. This problem often points to a dirty or clogged carburetor.

I understand this frustration well. Cleaning the carburetor on your Ariens snowblower can solve hard starting, rough running, and stalling issues. This crucial maintenance task ensures your snowblower operates reliably throughout the cold season. This comprehensive guide will walk you through every step of the process. ### Clear, Concise Answer

To clean the carburetor on an Ariens snowblower, drain the fuel tank, remove the air filter, detach fuel lines and linkages, then carefully remove the carburetor. Disassemble it, thoroughly clean all jets and passages using carburetor cleaner and compressed air, then reassemble and reinstall the unit.

## Why Your Ariens Snowblower Carburetor Needs Cleaning

The carburetor is a vital part of your Ariens snowblower engine. It mixes air and fuel in the correct ratio for combustion. A clean carburetor ensures smooth engine operation. However, over time, fuel can leave behind deposits.

Many modern fuels contain ethanol, which attracts water. This water can separate from the gasoline and sit in the carburetor bowl. This mixture causes corrosion and leaves sticky residue. These deposits clog tiny passages and jets inside the carburetor. When these parts are blocked, the engine does not get enough fuel, or the fuel-air mixture is incorrect.

You will notice symptoms like hard starting or failure to start. The engine might surge, run rough, or stall unexpectedly. It may not maintain a consistent RPM. Sometimes, the engine will only run with the choke on. If your snowblower exhibits any of these signs, a thorough carburetor cleaning is likely needed. Regular carburetor maintenance, especially before seasonal storage, prevents these common issues. It ensures your snowblower performs reliably year after year. ## Essential Tools and Safety Preparations for Carb Cleaning

Before you begin cleaning the carburetor on your Ariens snowblower, gather all necessary tools. Having everything ready saves time and ensures a smooth process. You will need a set of metric wrenches and screwdrivers, including Phillips and flat-head types. A can of specialized carburetor cleaner is essential; buy one designed for small engines.

Compressed air is very useful for clearing stubborn blockages from small passages. You will also need safety glasses to protect your eyes from spray and debris. Wear work gloves to protect your hands from chemicals. Prepare a drain pan to catch old fuel and a few shop rags for spills. Small wire brushes, like those found in welding tip cleaning kits, are perfect for clearing tiny jets. A utility knife might be needed for old fuel lines.

Safety is paramount when working with fuel and chemicals. Always work in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors. Disconnect the spark plug wire before you start any work to prevent accidental engine starts. Have a fire extinguisher nearby, just in case. Properly dispose of old fuel according to local regulations. Never use an open flame near fuel or carburetor cleaner. Your safety comes first in any repair job.

## Accessing and Removing the Ariens Snowblower Carburetor

Getting to the carburetor on your Ariens snowblower is the first major step. The specific process can vary slightly between models, but the general approach remains consistent. Start by removing any plastic shrouds or covers that obstruct access to the engine. These are usually held on by a few bolts or clips. Next, locate the air filter housing, which typically sits directly in front of or on top of the carburetor. Remove the air filter and its housing.

Now, you should clearly see the carburetor. Before disconnecting anything, place a drain pan under the fuel tank and carburetor. You need to drain all fuel from the tank. You can often do this by disconnecting the fuel line from the carburetor and letting it drain into the pan. This step prevents fuel spills during removal. After draining, carefully disconnect the fuel lines leading to the carburetor. Note their positions, as they must go back correctly.
