## How To Clean Pontoons In The Water

Pontoon boat owners often wonder how to maintain their vessel without pulling it from the water. Cleaning pontoons in the water seems like a big challenge. You want to keep your boat looking great all season long. This guide makes the process simple. We will show you effective methods. You can remove marine growth and grime from your pontoons. We cover everything from tools to techniques. Keep reading to learn how to keep your boat pristine. This happens even while it stays docked.

### Takeaway

*   Cleaning your pontoons while they are still in the water is easy to achieve.
*   Use specific tools and eco-friendly cleaners for the best cleaning results.
*   Always prioritize safety during any in-water cleaning tasks.
*   Regular light cleaning prevents heavy build-up of marine growth.

Cleaning pontoons in the water involves using long-handled brushes, pressure washers (if safe and accessible), or specialized underwater cleaning tools with marine-safe cleaners. Focus on removing algae, barnacles, and scum lines regularly to prevent severe buildup. This helps maintain performance without hauling your boat out.

### Why Clean Pontoons While They Are Still In The Water?

Keeping your pontoon boat clean is a key part of good ownership. Cleaning pontoons in the water offers many benefits. It saves you time and money. You do not need to pay for a haul-out. This also avoids transport costs.

Regular cleaning prevents marine growth from taking hold. Algae and barnacles can stick to your pontoons. This buildup slows your boat down. It also makes your engine work harder. A clean pontoon cuts through the water better. This improves your boat's speed. It also saves fuel.

You can perform quick clean-ups often. This prevents small problems from growing into big ones. Light scrubbing now saves hours of hard work later. In-water cleaning allows you to enjoy more time on the water. It extends the life of your pontoons too.

Many boat owners believe cleaning below the waterline requires dry dock. This is not always true. With the right approach, you can manage most cleaning tasks yourself. This keeps your boat looking its best. It helps it perform well every time you use it. Maintaining your pontoon in the water means less hassle overall.

### Essential Tools and Supplies for In-Water Pontoon Cleaning

To effectively clean pontoons in the water, you need the right gear. Choosing the correct tools makes the job easier. It also helps you achieve better results. Having everything ready before you start is important. This saves time and effort during the cleaning process.

First, you will need various brushes and scrubbing pads. Long-handled brushes are essential for reaching higher spots and below the waterline. Stiff bristles work well for stubborn algae. Softer brushes are better for lighter scum. Consider a scrub pad with a handle for hard-to-reach areas.

A pressure washer can be very useful if used correctly. Ensure your model is safe for marine use. Use a wide-angle nozzle. Keep the pressure low enough to avoid damaging pontoon surfaces. Always test a small area first. You can also look into specialized underwater cleaning tools. These tools often attach to a hose. They help remove growth without harsh chemicals.

Scrapers might be needed for tough barnacles. Choose plastic scrapers over metal ones. Metal scrapers can scratch aluminum pontoons. Wear a wetsuit or snorkeling gear if you plan to get in the water. This lets you access the entire pontoon surface. Safety gear like gloves and eye protection are also a must.

For cleaners, select marine-safe and eco-friendly products. These cleaners break down algae and grime. They do not harm marine life. Look for specific hull cleaners or aluminum pontoon cleaners. Always read product labels carefully. Make sure the cleaner is suitable for in-water use. These products help keep your boat clean and safe for the environment. Getting the right tools helps you [clean a boat in the water](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-boat-in-the-water) efficiently.

### Safety First: Preparing for Your In-Water Pontoon Cleaning Job

Before you begin cleaning pontoons in the water, safety must be your top concern. Working around water always carries some risks. Proper preparation helps prevent accidents. Taking a few moments to get ready ensures a smoother and safer cleaning experience. Your well-being is the most important part of this task.

Always wear a personal flotation device (PFD). Even if you are a strong swimmer, a PFD offers an extra layer of safety. It keeps you afloat if you slip or become tired. Wear appropriate clothing, such as a wetsuit, if you plan to enter the water. This helps maintain body temperature.

Protect your eyes and hands. Safety goggles shield your eyes from splashes of water and cleaning solutions. Waterproof gloves protect your hands from chemicals and rough surfaces. They also improve your grip on tools. Make sure your cleaning area is secure. Inform someone else that you are cleaning your boat. This way, someone knows your activity.

Check the weather conditions before you start. Avoid cleaning on windy days or when storms are expected. Choppy water makes working difficult and unsafe. Good visibility is also important. Ensure the water depth is safe for your activities. Avoid cleaning in strong currents.

Secure your boat firmly to the dock. Use extra lines if needed. This prevents your pontoon from moving unexpectedly. Clear the deck of any loose items that could fall into the water. Having a clear workspace makes your job easier and safer. These steps ensure a safe work environment.

### Step-by-Step Guide to Cleaning Pontoon Sides and Top Sections

Cleaning the visible parts of your pontoon boat is a straightforward process. These are the areas above the waterline. They often collect scum lines, dirt, and mildew. Regular cleaning here keeps your boat looking fresh. Follow these steps to make your pontoons shine. This process keeps your boat presentable all season.

Start by rinsing the pontoon surfaces with fresh water. Use a hose to spray away loose dirt and debris. This pre-rinse helps loosen grime. It also prepares the surface for cleaning. A good rinse reduces the amount of scrubbing needed. It also helps conserve cleaning solution.

Next, apply your chosen marine-safe cleaner. Spray or wipe the cleaner onto the pontoon sides. Focus on areas with visible scum lines or mildew. Let the cleaner sit for a few minutes. Check the product instructions for the recommended dwell time. This allows the cleaner to break down the grime effectively.

Use a long-handled brush or scrub pad to gently scrub the surfaces. Work in small sections. Apply steady pressure. Scrubbing helps remove tough stains and dirt. Pay attention to corners and crevices where grime can accumulate. Rinse your brush frequently to keep it clean.

After scrubbing, rinse the pontoons thoroughly with fresh water. Ensure all cleaner residue is gone. Any leftover cleaner can leave streaks. It can also attract new dirt. Stand back and check for any missed spots. Repeat the process if necessary. This will ensure your pontoon sides are spotless. This step-by-step approach keeps your boat clean with minimal effort.

### Tackling the Underside: Removing Algae and Barnacles from Pontoons

Cleaning the underside of your pontoons while they are in the water is critical for boat performance. This area attracts the most marine growth. Algae, barnacles, and slime reduce your boat's speed. They also increase fuel consumption. Addressing this buildup regularly is essential. This helps your boat operate at its best.

There are several effective methods for underwater pontoon cleaning. One common method involves getting into the water yourself. Wear a wetsuit or snorkeling gear. Use a stiff-bristled brush or plastic scraper. Gently scrape away barnacles and other hard growth. Be careful not to damage the aluminum surface. Work from front to back, covering all areas. This direct approach offers good control.

Long-reach tools are another option. These tools allow you to scrub the underside from the deck or dock. They often have angled heads or extendable handles. This lets you reach deep under the boat. Some specialized tools use water pressure to scrape growth away. These tools make the job easier if you cannot get into the water.

A pressure washer can sometimes be used from the dock or a nearby boat. Direct the stream carefully [to clean the bottom of the boat in water](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-the-bottom-of-the-boat-in-water). Use a low-pressure setting. Keep the nozzle a safe distance from the pontoon. High pressure can force water into seams. This could cause damage. Aim to wash away loose algae and slime. This is an effective way to remove lighter growth.

For persistent algae, use an environmentally friendly marine cleaner designed for underwater use. Apply it with a brush. Let it soak for a short time. Then scrub and rinse. Always confirm the cleaner is safe for aluminum and marine life. This careful approach ensures a clean and undamaged pontoon.

### Choosing Environmentally Friendly Pontoon Cleaners

Selecting the right cleaning products is vital for your pontoons and the marine ecosystem. Harsh chemicals can harm marine life. They can also damage your boat's finish. Choosing environmentally friendly pontoon cleaners helps protect our waterways. It also ensures your boat stays in great condition. Make smart choices for your cleaning tasks.

Look for products labeled "biodegradable" or "eco-friendly." These cleaners break down naturally in the water. They do not leave behind harmful residues. Many brands offer marine-specific cleaning solutions. These are formulated to be safe for boats and the environment. Always check the ingredients list. Avoid cleaners containing phosphates, chlorine, or ammonia. These substances are very damaging to aquatic life.

Consider the type of water your pontoon is in. Freshwater lakes and saltwater oceans have different ecosystems. Some cleaners work better in one environment than another. However, universal marine-safe cleaners are often available. They offer good performance across different water types. Read product reviews to see what other boaters recommend. Their experiences can guide your choice.

When applying cleaners, use them sparingly. More is not always better. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for dilution and application. This prevents waste and reduces environmental impact. Use brushes and scrubbers to help the cleaner work. This minimizes the amount of chemical needed. Always rinse thoroughly with fresh water after cleaning. This washes away any remaining product.

Using eco-friendly products is a small change. It makes a big difference. It helps preserve the beauty of our natural waters. It supports the health of marine animals and plants. It also ensures your pontoon cleaning routine is responsible.

### Maintaining Your Pontoons Between Deep Cleans

Regular maintenance keeps your pontoons in top shape. You do not need a full deep clean every time. Small, consistent efforts prevent heavy build-up. This saves you significant time and effort in the long run. Good upkeep extends the life of your pontoons. It keeps them looking new.

After each boat use, give your pontoons a quick rinse. Use a hose to wash away loose dirt, salt, or lake scum. This simple step stops grime from hardening onto the surface. It is especially important in saltwater environments. Salt can accelerate corrosion. A quick rinse helps here.

Perform a light scrub every few weeks. Use a soft-bristled brush and a bucket of fresh water. Gently brush along the waterline and sides. This removes early signs of algae or slime. This frequent action stops marine growth from becoming established. It makes future deep cleaning much easier.

Consider using a protective coating or wax. While polishing typically happens out of water, you can protect your aluminum pontoons. Some marine waxes are easy to apply from the dock or deck. These create a barrier. This barrier repels dirt and makes cleaning simpler. A good coating makes water bead off the surface. This prevents stains. You can learn more about how to [clean and polish aluminum pontoons on a boat](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-and-polish-aluminum-pontoons-on-a-boat) for a deeper shine.

The frequency of your maintenance depends on several factors. Water conditions play a big part. Pontoons in murky, nutrient-rich water will need more frequent cleaning. Boats used often will also accumulate growth faster. Stay consistent with these small steps. Your pontoons will thank you.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### Can I use a pressure washer to clean pontoons in the water?

Yes, you can use a pressure washer to clean pontoons in the water, but with great caution. Use a wide-angle nozzle. Keep the pressure low. Aim the stream away from seams and delicate boat parts. Always maintain a safe distance from the pontoon surface. Test a small, inconspicuous area first to prevent damage.

#### How often should I clean my pontoons in the water?

The frequency depends on your specific conditions. Factors include water quality, boat usage, and local marine growth rates. In most cases, a light in-water cleaning every month or two is sufficient. If you use your boat often or are in nutrient-rich waters, you might need to clean more frequently.

#### What is the best way to remove barnacles from pontoons in the water?

To remove barnacles in the water, first scrape them gently with a plastic scraper. Avoid metal tools that can scratch the aluminum. After scraping, use a stiff-bristled brush and a marine-safe cleaner. Scrub the remaining barnacle rings firmly. Rinse the area thoroughly with fresh water.

#### Are there special cleaners for aluminum pontoons that are safe for the water?

Yes, many cleaners are made specifically for aluminum pontoons and are safe for marine environments. Look for products labeled "biodegradable," "eco-friendly," or "marine-safe." These cleaners are formulated to be effective on aluminum grime without harming aquatic life. Always read product labels before use.

#### Will cleaning my pontoons in the water damage them?

No, cleaning your pontoons in the water will not damage them if you use the correct tools and methods. Avoid harsh chemicals or overly abrasive scrubbing. Always use soft to medium brushes and plastic scrapers. Follow product instructions for cleaners. This prevents surface scratches or chemical damage.

#### Can I prevent marine growth on my pontoons without hauling them out?

Yes, you can prevent marine growth without hauling out your boat. Regular in-water cleaning is the best prevention. Apply marine-grade protective coatings or waxes to create a slick surface. Some in-water antifouling coatings are available. These products make it harder for growth to attach.
