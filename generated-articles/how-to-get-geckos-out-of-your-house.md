## Easy Ways to Get Geckos Out of Your House

Have you ever walked into a room and spotted a small, quick lizard darting across the wall? It's likely a gecko. While these creatures can be fascinating outdoors, finding them inside your home might not feel so cozy. Many homeowners wonder how to get geckos out of their house safely and effectively. Geckos often enter homes seeking food, water, or shelter, especially in warmer climates.

I understand the desire for a gecko-free living space. This guide provides clear, simple steps to identify, remove, and prevent these tiny reptiles from becoming permanent houseguests. We will cover identifying common house geckos, understanding their entry points, using natural deterrents, sealing your home, and applying humane removal techniques. You will gain all the knowledge you need to reclaim your indoor spaces.

### Takeaway:

*   Identify common house geckos and their motivations for entering your home.
*   Seal all potential entry points like cracks, gaps, and poorly sealed windows.
*   Use natural repellents and control indoor insect populations.
*   Employ safe, humane methods for removing geckos already inside.
*   Maintain a clean and uncluttered environment to prevent future invasions.

### How do you get geckos out of your house?

To get geckos out of your house, first, eliminate their food sources by managing insects. Next, seal all entry points such as cracks, gaps, and unsealed windows or doors. Use natural deterrents like eggshells or coffee grounds. Finally, use humane traps or a simple catch-and-release method for any remaining geckos inside.

## Understanding Your Uninvited Gecko Guests

When you spot a gecko in your home, knowing what kind of gecko it is helps. Most house geckos are common house geckos, also known as Asian house geckos. They are small, usually light gray or brownish, and have distinct bumpy skin. These geckos have sticky pads on their feet, allowing them to cling to almost any surface.

House geckos are generally harmless to humans. They do not carry diseases that affect people directly. However, their droppings can be messy, and their presence can be unsettling. You want your home to feel clean and secure.

### Common House Gecko Species

The most frequent visitor is the common house gecko (Hemidactylus frenatus). These geckos are active at night. They have large eyes and a noticeable "chirp" sound. You might see them near lights, waiting for insects.

Other species, like Mediterranean house geckos, also adapt well to indoor living. They share similar traits, including their ability to climb walls and ceilings. Knowing their behavior helps you predict where to find them.

### Why Geckos Seek Shelter Indoors

Geckos come indoors primarily for three reasons: food, water, and shelter. Your home provides a stable environment away from predators. It also offers a consistent food supply.

Insects like moths, mosquitoes, and cockroaches are a gecko's main diet. Lights attract these insects, creating a perfect hunting ground for geckos around windows and lamps. Water sources like leaky pipes or condensation also draw them in. Your home offers warmth and protection during cooler weather or heavy rains. This makes it an ideal hideout.

## Identifying Gecko Entry Points

Geckos are small and very agile. They can squeeze through tiny openings. Finding these entry points is key to keeping them out. A thorough inspection of your home's exterior and interior is necessary.

I often start my inspection at ground level and work my way up. Look for any gaps, cracks, or openings that are large enough for a gecko to slip through. Remember, if a pencil can fit, a gecko probably can too. This step takes time but provides the best defense against future invasions.

### Common Entry Routes

Geckos often enter through unsealed gaps under doors. They can also get in through cracks in your foundation. Spaces around pipes or utility lines that enter your house are also common routes. Check attic vents and crawl space openings for breaches.

Windows with damaged screens or poor seals are another easy way in. Even a small tear in a window screen is an invitation. Look at the bottom edges of garage doors too. Small gaps there can let geckos inside easily.

### Inspecting Your Home's Perimeter

Begin your inspection around the entire exterior of your house. Walk slowly and look closely at the foundation walls. Pay special attention to areas where different materials meet, like brick and siding. Look for cracks in mortar or stucco.

Check all windows and door frames. Make sure they fit tightly and have no visible gaps. Examine window screens for any rips or holes. Don't forget to inspect your attic and crawl space vents. Sometimes, these areas lack proper screening. Keeping geckos out of your home means sealing every small opening. This is similar to [keeping geckos out of your lanai](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-keep-geckos-out-of-your-lanai), where meticulous sealing is essential.

## Natural and Humane Gecko Deterrents

Using natural deterrents helps make your home less appealing to geckos. These methods are safe for your family and pets. They also avoid harsh chemicals. My goal is always to create an environment where geckos simply do not want to stay.

These deterrents often work by affecting the gecko's senses. Geckos rely on smell and touch to navigate their environment. Disrupting these senses can encourage them to leave. Consistency is important for these methods to be effective.

### Aromatic Repellents

Certain smells can repel geckos. Garlic is one such repellent. Place garlic cloves near entry points or crush them to release a stronger odor. Geckos dislike strong smells.

Coffee and tobacco mixtures also work. Mix coffee powder with tobacco powder and spread it near cracks or windows. Mothballs are another option, though their smell is strong. Place them in small bags in secluded areas where geckos hide. Eggshells also deter them. Geckos see eggshells as a predator's eggs, making them feel unsafe. Place broken eggshells near entry points.

### Natural Insect Control

The best way to deter geckos is to remove their food source. Geckos eat insects. Fewer insects mean fewer geckos. Focus on reducing flies, mosquitoes, and cockroaches in your home.

Keep your kitchen clean. Wipe up crumbs and spills immediately. Store food in airtight containers. Use natural insect repellents like essential oils (peppermint or citronella) in areas where insects gather. Diatomaceous earth is a safe, natural powder that kills insects. Sprinkle it in cracks and crevices. If you manage your indoor insect population well, geckos will find your home unappealing. Geckos, like other reptiles, have a specific diet; for example, [what crested geckos eat in the wild](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-do-crested-geckos-eat-in-the-wild) is mostly insects and fruits. Reducing these food sources inside your home makes it less attractive for them.

## Sealing Your Home Against Geckos

Sealing your home is the most effective long-term solution for gecko problems. This step prevents new geckos from entering. It addresses the root cause of the invasion. I always tell people that prevention is better than removal.

This process involves carefully inspecting and fixing any vulnerabilities in your home's structure. It might seem like a big job, but it saves you hassle in the future. Small changes can make a huge difference in keeping unwanted guests out.

### Securing Doors and Windows

Check all your doors. Ensure they fit tightly in their frames. Install door sweeps under all exterior doors. These seals block gaps at the bottom, which are common gecko entry points. Replace any worn-out weatherstripping around door frames.

For windows, inspect the screens. Repair or replace any screens with holes or tears. Make sure window frames close snugly. Use caulk to seal any gaps between window frames and the wall. Properly secured windows and doors are your first line of defense.

### Patching Cracks and Gaps

Examine your home's foundation and exterior walls for cracks. Use a good quality sealant or caulk to fill these cracks. Pay attention to areas where pipes or wires enter the house. These often have small gaps around them. Fill any openings around utility lines.

Inspect your attic and crawl space vents. Ensure they have fine mesh screens to keep pests out. If your garage door has gaps when closed, consider installing a rubber seal along the bottom edge. Sealing all these small openings will make your home a fortress against geckos.

## Safe and Effective Gecko Removal Methods

Once geckos are inside, you need safe ways to get them out. Humane methods are preferred. You can remove geckos without harming them. My goal is always to guide the gecko back to its natural habitat outside.

Killing geckos is not necessary. They are part of the ecosystem and help control insects. Focus on guiding them out or trapping them gently. Patience is key with these methods.

### Gentle Catch and Release

For geckos on walls, you can use a simple catch-and-release technique. Place a cup or small container over the gecko. Then, slide a piece of cardboard or thick paper under the cup. This traps the gecko inside. Carry the container outdoors and release the gecko far from your house.

This method requires a quick hand but is very effective. It causes no harm to the gecko. You simply relocate it to where it belongs. This is the most humane way to handle an individual gecko.

### Humane Trapping Solutions

Sticky traps are available, but they are not humane. Geckos get stuck and often injure themselves trying to escape. If you must use a trap, consider making a simple homemade one. You can use a shoebox with a small hole. Place a piece of fruit or a dead insect inside as bait. The gecko enters and gets trapped when you close the box.

Another method is to use a water trap. Place a shallow container of water near a wall where geckos are seen. They sometimes fall in and cannot get out. Check these traps often to release the gecko quickly. You can also buy humane gecko traps online that catch the gecko without sticking or hurting it. ## Environmental Modifications to Discourage Geckos

Modifying your home's immediate environment can significantly reduce gecko presence. Geckos are attracted to specific conditions. Changing these conditions makes your property less appealing. I focus on making the outside less inviting, so they do not consider coming inside.

These modifications often overlap with general home maintenance. They improve the overall look and feel of your property too. A well-maintained yard and exterior help deter many pests, not just geckos.

### Landscaping and Vegetation Management

Overgrown vegetation near your house provides hiding spots for geckos. It also attracts insects, which are gecko food. Trim bushes, shrubs, and trees so they do not touch your house walls. Keep grass mowed short. Remove any leaf litter or debris near the foundation.

Consider creating a gravel or rock barrier around your foundation. This provides a less hospitable surface for geckos than dense plantings. Reduce clutter in your yard, such as old tires or woodpiles. These are perfect hiding places for geckos and their insect prey. A clear perimeter makes it harder for geckos to approach your home.

### Light Management

Outdoor lighting can be a major draw for geckos. Lights attract insects at night. These insects then become an easy meal for geckos. If you have bright lights near your doors or windows, consider changing them.

Use yellow or sodium vapor lights instead of white or mercury vapor lights. Yellow lights attract fewer insects. You can also install motion-sensor lights. These only turn on when needed, reducing the time insects are attracted. Direct outdoor lights away from your house. By managing your outdoor lighting, you reduce the gecko's food source near your entry points.

Keeping geckos out is an ongoing process, not a one-time fix. Regular maintenance and consistent effort are essential. You want to maintain a gecko-free environment year-round. I know that vigilance pays off in the long run.

Think of it as protecting your home from all kinds of unwanted guests. A clean, well-sealed house is less attractive to pests. These prevention steps integrate easily into your regular home care routine.

### Consistent Home Maintenance

Regularly check your home for new cracks or gaps. Small cracks can develop over time. Reapply caulk or sealants as needed. Inspect door sweeps and weatherstripping. Replace them if they show signs of wear. A simple monthly walk-around check can catch problems early.

Maintain your yard regularly. Keep bushes trimmed and clear debris. Ensure gutters are clean and drain water away from your foundation. Dampness attracts insects and geckos. A dry, tidy environment is less appealing to them. Just like maintaining a fresh smell in your home involves consistent cleaning and air circulation, such as [eliminating dog odors](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-get-dog-smell-out-of-house), preventing geckos requires continuous attention to home hygiene and sealing.

### Sustained Pest Management

The most important prevention strategy is continuous pest control. Geckos come inside for food. If there are no insects, geckos will not stay. Use natural pest control methods consistently. Regularly clean up food crumbs and spills. Store all food in sealed containers. Empty trash cans often.

If you have a persistent insect problem, consider professional pest control services. Eliminating their primary food source makes your home unattractive to geckos. Combine this with regular sealing. This dual approach offers the best long-term protection against geckos entering your house.

## FAQ Section

### Are house geckos dangerous to humans or pets?
House geckos are generally harmless. They are not poisonous and do not carry diseases that easily transfer to humans or pets. Their bite is rare and usually too weak to break human skin. They are more of a nuisance than a danger.

### What primarily attracts geckos to houses?
Geckos are primarily attracted to houses seeking food, water, and shelter. They feed on insects like mosquitoes, moths, and roaches, which are often drawn to indoor lights. Leaky pipes provide water, and the stable indoor temperature offers protection from predators and harsh weather.

### Do geckos bite humans if cornered?
Geckos rarely bite humans. If a gecko feels extremely threatened or cornered, it might attempt to nip as a defense mechanism. However, their bite is very weak and usually does not break the skin. It feels more like a small pinch.

### How long do geckos typically live indoors?
The lifespan of a gecko varies. Common house geckos in their natural habitat live for about 5-7 years. If they find a consistent food source and shelter indoors, they can live just as long. However, their survival depends on the availability of insects and water within the house.

### Can mothballs really deter geckos effectively?
Mothballs can deter geckos due to their strong naphthalene odor, which geckos dislike. However, mothballs are toxic to humans and pets if ingested and release fumes that can be irritating. Use them with caution and only in very secluded, well-ventilated areas, or consider safer, natural alternatives.

### When is it best to call a professional for gecko removal?
It is best to call a professional for gecko removal when you have a severe infestation that you cannot control with DIY methods. If geckos are causing significant damage or if you are uncomfortable handling them yourself, professionals offer effective, safe, and often long-term solutions.
