## The Legal Status of Ferrets in NSW

When you ask, "Can I own a ferret in Australia NSW?", the answer is clear: ferrets are illegal to own in New South Wales. The NSW Biosecurity Act 2015 lists ferrets as a prohibited species. This means you cannot keep, breed, sell, or release them in NSW.

The primary reason for this ban is ecological. Ferrets are not native to Australia. If they escape or are released, they can establish wild populations. This poses a severe threat to Australia's unique native animals, many of which are already endangered.

### Why Ferrets Are Prohibited in NSW

The decision to prohibit ferrets in NSW stems from a history of invasive species causing harm. Ferrets are efficient predators. They can hunt and kill small native mammals and birds. They also compete with native predators for food resources.

NSW regulations aim to prevent new environmental problems. The state works to protect its unique biodiversity. Owning ferrets would risk introducing a new feral animal. This risk is too high for NSW authorities.
