## Sparkling Exposed Aggregate: How to Clean Your Concrete

Exposed aggregate concrete surfaces add beauty and texture to any outdoor space. They feature small stones or pebbles visible on the surface, offering a unique, natural look. But over time, dirt, grime, moss, and stains can dull their appearance. Keeping these surfaces clean requires the right approach.

This guide helps you understand how to clean exposed aggregate concrete effectively. We will cover the tools you need, the best cleaning methods, and specific techniques for removing different types of stains. By following these steps, you can restore your concrete to its original brilliance and protect its longevity. Let's make your exposed aggregate shine again.

## Understanding Exposed Aggregate Concrete: Beauty and Cleaning Needs

Exposed aggregate concrete offers a distinct and attractive finish. This material features small, decorative stones visible on the surface. People often choose it for driveways, patios, and walkways because of its unique texture and slip-resistant qualities. The exposed stones provide a natural, varied look that concrete alone cannot offer.

However, this textured surface also creates challenges for cleaning. The rough, uneven texture can trap dirt, dust, and organic matter more easily than smooth concrete. Over time, moss, mildew, and algae can grow in the crevices, especially in shaded or damp areas. Spills like oil, grease, or rust can also seep into the gaps between the stones, making them harder to remove. Understanding these characteristics helps you approach the cleaning process more effectively. You must use methods that clean deep into the aggregate without dislodging the stones or damaging the concrete matrix.

## Basic Cleaning Steps for Exposed Aggregate Surfaces

Cleaning exposed aggregate concrete does not need to be complicated. Following a systematic approach helps you achieve the best results. These basic steps are suitable for routine maintenance and light to moderate dirt.

First, clear the area completely. Remove all furniture, potted plants, and loose debris. Use a broom to sweep away leaves, dirt, and any other loose particles. This step makes subsequent cleaning much more effective. If you are cleaning a patio area, consider how to protect nearby landscaping; learning [how to clean concrete patio without killing grass](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-concrete-patio-without-killing-grass) involves careful rinsing and choosing appropriate cleaners.

Next, pre-wet the entire surface with your garden hose. Saturate the concrete thoroughly. This helps loosen dirt and prevents cleaning solutions from drying too quickly. It also helps protect the concrete from harsh chemicals.

Now, apply your chosen concrete cleaner or degreaser. Follow the product instructions carefully regarding dilution and application. For example, if you are cleaning a typical concrete sidewalk, the methods here are very similar to [how to clean concrete sidewalk](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-concrete-sidewalk). Pour or spray the cleaner evenly across the wet surface. Allow the cleaner to sit for the recommended dwell time. This lets the solution break down dirt and stains.

After the cleaner has sat, begin scrubbing the surface vigorously. Use a stiff-bristled brush or push broom. Pay close attention to areas with visible grime or stains. Scrubbing helps agitate the cleaner and lift embedded dirt from between the aggregate stones. Ensure you work the cleaner into all the crevices.

Finally, rinse the area thoroughly with your garden hose or pressure washer. Start from the highest point and work your way down. Ensure all cleaning solution and loosened dirt are completely washed away. Leaving cleaner residue can cause streaks or damage. Repeat the scrubbing and rinsing process for any stubborn spots until the surface is clean.

## Tackling Stubborn Stains on Exposed Aggregate

Stubborn stains require specific treatments beyond basic cleaning. Different types of stains respond to different cleaning agents. Identifying the stain helps you choose the most effective removal method.

### Removing Oil and Grease Stains

Oil and grease stains are common on driveways and patios. These stains penetrate concrete easily. To remove them, you need a powerful degreaser. Apply the degreaser directly to the stain. Let it soak for the time recommended by the product manufacturer. You might need to use a stiff brush to scrub the area vigorously. Many homeowners face similar issues; for example, if you have a BBQ area, you might need to know [how to clean BBQ grease off concrete](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-bbq-grease-off-concrete) using similar degreasing techniques. Rinse thoroughly after scrubbing. For very old or deep stains, repeat applications or consider a poultice product designed to draw out embedded oil.

### Eliminating Rust Stains

Rust stains often come from metal furniture, tools, or fertilizer. These stains are reddish-brown and can be difficult to remove. Use a rust remover specifically formulated for concrete. Acid-based rust removers are effective but require careful handling. Apply the product only to the rust spot. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for dwell time. Always test a small, inconspicuous area first to ensure it does not discolor the aggregate. Rinse the area completely with water once the stain lifts. For specific spills like brake fluid, which can also stain, a different approach might be needed; learning [how to clean brake fluid off concrete](https://www.cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-brake-fluid-off-concrete) often involves absorbents first, then specialized cleaners.

### Combating Mold, Mildew, and Algae

Green or black growth indicates mold, mildew, or algae. These organic growths thrive in damp, shaded conditions. A solution of diluted bleach and water works well. Mix one part bleach with three parts water. You can also use commercial mold and mildew removers. Apply the solution to the affected areas. Let it sit for about 10-15 minutes. Scrub with a stiff brush to loosen the growth. Rinse the surface thoroughly with a garden hose or pressure washer. This treatment revitalizes the look of the concrete, especially relevant for surfaces like a pool deck; cleaning a concrete pool deck often requires addressing these same organic growths to keep it safe and beautiful.

### Addressing Other Tough Stains

Sometimes, other unique stains appear. For paint spills, use a paint stripper designed for concrete. Test it in a hidden spot first. For stubborn dirt or ground-in grime that resists regular cleaning, an alkaline concrete cleaner may be more effective. Always wear appropriate safety gear when working with strong chemicals. Thorough rinsing after any stain treatment is crucial to prevent damage or residue.

## Pressure Washing Exposed Aggregate Concrete Safely

Pressure washing is a highly effective way to clean exposed aggregate concrete. It uses strong streams of water to blast away dirt, grime, and organic growth. However, using a pressure washer improperly can damage the aggregate or the concrete matrix. Safety and technique are key for effective and safe cleaning.

First, select the right pressure for your machine. For most exposed aggregate, a pressure setting between 1500 and 2500 PSI is appropriate. Newer or softer concrete may require lower pressure. Using too high a PSI can dislodge the aggregate stones or etch the concrete surface. Always start with a lower pressure setting and increase it gradually if needed.

Choose the correct nozzle for your pressure washer. A 25-degree (green) or 40-degree (white) fan tip nozzle is generally recommended. These nozzles provide a wider spray pattern, which reduces the risk of damage. Avoid using a zero-degree (red) nozzle or a rotating turbo nozzle on exposed aggregate. These concentrated streams can easily damage the surface.

Before you start on the main area, test a small, inconspicuous spot. This test ensures your pressure and nozzle choice are safe for your specific concrete. Look for any signs of aggregate loosening or surface etching. Adjust your settings if necessary.

When pressure washing, keep the nozzle a consistent distance from the surface. Hold it about 12-18 inches away, depending on the pressure. Move the wand in smooth, overlapping strokes. Work in small sections, always moving the nozzle. Do not hold the spray in one spot for too long. This can cause damage. Work from the top of the slope downwards, if applicable, to allow dirty water to flow away.

After pressure washing, inspect the area carefully. Ensure all dirt and cleaning solution residues are gone. Sometimes, a second pass or a lighter rinse might be needed. Proper pressure washing can dramatically restore the appearance of your exposed aggregate, making it look new again.

## Essential Steps for Long-Term Concrete Protection

Cleaning your exposed aggregate concrete is a great start. However, maintaining its clean look and protecting it from future damage requires ongoing care. Regular maintenance and proper sealing are crucial for long-term protection. These steps help prevent dirt buildup and extend the life of your concrete.

First, establish a routine for basic cleaning. Sweep your exposed aggregate surfaces regularly to remove loose debris, leaves, and dirt. This simple act prevents dirt from settling into the crevices and becoming embedded. For patios or high-traffic areas, sweeping once a week or bi-weekly is ideal. You can also rinse the surface with a garden hose periodically to wash away surface dust.

Next, address spills immediately. If oil, grease, or other staining substances spill on your exposed aggregate, clean them up as quickly as possible. Fresh spills are much easier to remove than old, set-in stains. Blot liquids and use an absorbent material for solids before cleaning with an appropriate degreaser or cleaner.

Consider applying a high-quality concrete sealant. Sealing your exposed aggregate concrete provides a protective barrier against moisture, stains, and weather. The sealant penetrates the concrete, making it less porous. This reduces the ability of dirt and spills to seep into the surface. It also helps prevent the growth of moss, mildew, and algae by reducing moisture absorption. Look for sealants designed for exposed aggregate or decorative concrete.

Apply the sealant according to the manufacturer's instructions. Typically, this involves cleaning the concrete thoroughly and letting it dry completely before application. Use a roller or sprayer for even coverage. You might need two coats for optimal protection. Reapply the sealant every 2-3 years, or as recommended by the product, to maintain its effectiveness. This protective layer ensures your concrete looks good and lasts longer, minimizing how often you need to perform deep cleaning.

## FAQ Section

### How often should I clean my exposed aggregate concrete?
You should clean your exposed aggregate concrete at least once a year. For high-traffic areas or those prone to heavy staining, like patios near BBQ grills or areas with significant organic growth, cleaning twice a year is better. Regular sweeping and immediate spill cleanup also help reduce the frequency of deep cleaning.

### Can I use bleach on exposed aggregate concrete?
Yes, you can use diluted bleach on exposed aggregate concrete, especially for mold, mildew, and algae. Mix one part bleach with three parts water. Apply it to the affected areas, let it sit briefly, then scrub and rinse thoroughly. Always test a small spot first and protect surrounding plants from overspray.

### What PSI is safe for pressure washing exposed aggregate?
For most exposed aggregate concrete, a pressure washer setting between 1500 and 2500 PSI is safe. Always start with a lower PSI and increase it carefully. Use a 25-degree or 40-degree fan tip nozzle and maintain a consistent distance from the surface to prevent damage or dislodging of aggregate stones.

### How do I remove black spots from my aggregate concrete?
Black spots are often mildew, mold, or lichen. Treat them with a solution of diluted bleach (1 part bleach to 3 parts water) or a commercial mold and mildew remover. Apply the solution, let it dwell, scrub with a stiff brush, and rinse thoroughly. Repeated applications might be necessary for stubborn growth.

### Is sealing exposed aggregate concrete necessary?
Sealing exposed aggregate concrete is not strictly necessary but highly recommended. A good quality sealant protects the concrete from moisture, stains, and weathering. It also inhibits organic growth and makes future cleaning easier. Reapply sealant every 2-3 years for best results.

### What is the best way to prevent stains on exposed aggregate?
The best way to prevent stains is through regular maintenance and sealing. Sweep the surface often to remove debris. Clean spills immediately, especially oil or grease. Apply a high-quality concrete sealant every few years to create a protective barrier that resists penetration from dirt and stains.
