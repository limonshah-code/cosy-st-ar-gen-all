### Key Elements of the ASCA Breed Standard

The ASCA breed standard covers many aspects of the Australian Shepherd. Understanding these elements is crucial for anyone involved with the breed.

*   **General Appearance:** The standard describes a balanced, athletic dog. It should show intelligence and versatility.
*   **Size and Proportion:** Males typically stand 20-23 inches tall. Females range from 18-21 inches. Length should be slightly greater than height. This creates a rectangular shape.
*   **Head:** The head should be clean-cut and proportionate. The muzzle should be equal in length to the skull. Eyes can be various colors, including blue, amber, or brown. Ears are medium-sized and set high.
*   **Body:** The body should be strong and muscular. The topline is level. The chest is deep. Ribs are well-sprung.
*   **Coat:** The coat is medium texture and weather-resistant. It can be straight or wavy. Colors include black, blue merle, red, and red merle. White markings and tan points are permissible.
*   **Tail:** Tails can be naturally bobbed or docked. A natural bob tail is common.
*   **Gait:** Movement should be smooth and agile. It shows a powerful drive.
*   **Temperament:** The Australian Shepherd is intelligent, active, and loyal. It can be reserved with strangers. It is not shy or aggressive.

These elements guide judges and breeders. They ensure the preservation of the Australian Shepherd's unique traits.

### Ensuring Breed Health and Longevity

ASCA's commitment extends to the long-term health and longevity of the Australian Shepherd. The club understands that genetic health issues can affect any breed. It actively promotes health testing.

*   **Genetic Testing:** ASCA encourages testing for conditions such as:
    *   **MDR1 gene mutation:** This affects how dogs metabolize certain drugs.
    *   **Hereditary Cataracts (HSF4):** A common eye disease.
    *   **Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA):** Another eye condition leading to blindness.
    *   **Degenerative Myelopathy (DM):** A neurological disorder.
*   **Orthopedic Screenings:** Breeders are encouraged to screen for hip and elbow dysplasia. These are common joint issues in many medium to large breeds.
*   **Eye Clearances:** Regular eye examinations by a veterinary ophthalmologist (CERF exams) are important. These check for various ocular conditions.
*   **Health Research:** ASCA often funds or supports research into Australian Shepherd health. This helps identify new health concerns. It also develops strategies for prevention.

By promoting these health initiatives, ASCA helps breeders make informed decisions. It reduces the passing of genetic diseases to future generations.
