## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Is a delayed rabies vaccine still effective?
A delayed rabies vaccine may still be effective, but your pet remains unprotected during the overdue period. The veterinarian will determine if a single booster shot is sufficient or if your pet needs to restart the full vaccination series, depending on how long the vaccine has been overdue and local regulations. Always consult your vet immediately.

### Q2: What happens if an unvaccinated dog bites someone?
If an unvaccinated dog bites someone, the dog will likely be quarantined for a specific observation period, usually 10 days. The pet owner may face fines and legal penalties. In severe cases, or if the dog shows signs of rabies, euthanasia might be ordered to test for the virus and protect public health.

### Q3: Can my pet travel if their rabies vaccine is overdue?
No, most airlines, boarding facilities, and even some public places require proof of current rabies vaccination. Traveling with an overdue rabies vaccine is not recommended and may be illegal. Always ensure your pet's vaccinations are up-to-date before planning any travel or boarding arrangements.

### Q4: How often should my dog get a rabies shot?
The frequency of rabies shots for dogs varies by vaccine type and local laws. Many rabies vaccines are effective for three years after the initial series, while some are annual. Always consult your veterinarian for the specific schedule recommended for your pet and your geographic location.

### Q5: What are the signs of rabies in animals?
Signs of rabies in animals vary but often include changes in behavior (aggressiveness or unusual tameness), difficulty swallowing, excessive drooling, staggering, paralysis, and seizures. These symptoms typically worsen over time, and once they appear, the disease is almost always fatal for the animal.
