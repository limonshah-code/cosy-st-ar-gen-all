## Understanding Squishmallow Rarity Factors

Squishmallow rarity is not a simple concept. Many things contribute to how rare a plush toy becomes. Knowing these factors helps you find valuable pieces. It also helps you understand their market price. I always look for these signs when evaluating a Squishmallow.

First, production numbers are key. A Squishmallow made in limited quantities is naturally rarer. Some are released with only a few hundred pieces. These instantly become highly sought after. They are harder to find, which drives up demand.

Second, exclusive releases increase rarity. Some Squishmallows are sold only at certain events. Conventions like San Diego Comic-Con often have exclusive designs. Retailer exclusives also create rarity. A Squishmallow only sold at one store, like Target or Walgreens, can be hard to get elsewhere. This means fewer people have access to them.

Third, special features add value. These can include unique fabrics, embroidered details, or special tags. Holographic tags or tags with specific collector numbers make a Squishmallow stand out. Errors in production, like a misprinted tag or an unusual color, can also make a Squishmallow very rare and valuable to collectors. These mistakes are not common.

Fourth, the age of a Squishmallow matters. Older, retired Squishmallows are often harder to find. They are no longer in production, so the available supply shrinks over time. This makes them more valuable to collectors. They represent a piece of Squishmallow history.

## Mistakes and Production Variations

Not all rare Squishmallows are planned to be rare. Some become rare due to accidental variations or production mistakes. These "error" Squishmallows can be incredibly valuable to collectors. They are unique because they were not supposed to be made that way.

One common type of error is a misprinted tag. The hang tag might have the wrong name. It could show the wrong bio for the character. Sometimes, the tag material or color is incorrect. These small mistakes make the Squishmallow one-of-a-kind. Collectors value these oddities highly.

Another type of variation involves fabric or embroidery. A Squishmallow might be made with a different fabric than intended. Its embroidery might be in the wrong place. These differences make the plush unique. They are factory errors. They were not designed to be different.

Prototype Squishmallows are also extremely rare. These are early versions made before mass production. They might have slight design differences. They are often given to employees or used for testing. These are not typically sold to the public. If one makes it into circulation, it is very special.

These accidental rarities add excitement to collecting. You never know what unique piece you might find. They prove that true rarity can sometimes be unplanned. Always check for unusual details. This is especially true for items purchased from unexpected places. Finding an error Squishmallow is like finding a hidden treasure.

## Authenticity and Valuing Your Rare Squishmallow

Once you find a potentially rare Squishmallow, verifying its authenticity is crucial. The market for rare collectibles attracts counterfeiters. You want to be sure your prize is real. Proper valuation also helps you understand its worth.

First, examine the tags. Every authentic Squishmallow has two main tags: the hang tag and the butt tag. The hang tag is the paper tag attached to the ear or side. It should have the Squishmallow logo, character name, and bio. Many rare Squishmallows have special hang tags, like holographic ones or those indicating a limited edition number. The butt tag is sewn into the bottom or side of the plush. It should state "Original Squishmallows" and include care instructions. Check for clear printing and correct spelling on both tags.

Next, feel the fabric and stuffing. Authentic Squishmallows have a unique, soft, marshmallow-like feel. The fabric is smooth and plush. The stuffing is dense but squishy. Counterfeit Squishmallows often feel rougher. Their stuffing might be lumpy or too firm. These details are important for verifying authenticity.

Research market prices for similar items. Look at completed sales on eBay or Mercari, not just active listings. This gives you a realistic idea of what people are paying. Prices fluctuate based on demand and condition. A Squishmallow in perfect condition, with all its original tags, will command the highest price.

Be wary of deals that seem too good to be true. If a rare Squishmallow is listed for a very low price, it could be fake. Always ask sellers for clear photos of both tags and the entire plush. Ask for close-ups if needed. Protecting your investment means being diligent. ## FAQ Section

### What makes a Squishmallow rare?
A Squishmallow becomes rare due to limited production numbers. Exclusive releases, like convention-only Squishmallows, also add rarity. Special features such as holographic tags or production errors make them unique. Older, retired Squishmallows also increase in rarity over time.

### How can I tell if my Squishmallow is a limited edition?
Limited edition Squishmallows often have special hang tags. These tags might indicate a specific number in a series (e.g., "1 of 500"). Some tags are holographic or have unique designs not found on regular Squishmallows. Always check both the paper hang tag and the sewn-in butt tag for clues.

### Is Jack the Black Cat still the rarest Squishmallow?
Yes, Jack the Black Cat is still widely considered the rarest Squishmallow. Only 500 units were produced worldwide. This extreme scarcity maintains its top spot. While other Squishmallows are very hard to find, Jack's documented limited run sets him apart.

### How much do rare Squishmallows sell for?
