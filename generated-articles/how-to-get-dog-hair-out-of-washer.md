## Say Goodbye to Fur: How To Get Dog Hair Out Of Your Washer

If you are a dog owner, you know the struggle is real. Dog hair seems to get everywhere, even into your washing machine. You wash a load of laundry, only to find clothes still covered in fur, or worse, a washer drum lined with stubborn pet hair. This common issue can feel like a never-ending battle.

I know the frustration firsthand; it can make laundry day much more challenging. Lingering dog hair not only re-deposits onto freshly cleaned clothes but can also clog your washer's drainage system over time. This article will help you get dog hair out of your washer. I will share practical tips and tricks, from pre-washing strategies to post-wash cleaning and ongoing maintenance.

### Takeaway

To effectively remove dog hair from your washing machine and prevent future buildup:

*   **Pre-treat** clothes to remove loose hair before washing.
*   **Use in-wash aids** like pet hair catchers or white vinegar during cycles.
*   **Clean your washer regularly** by wiping the drum, checking filters, and running empty hot cycles.
*   **Adopt preventive habits** like grooming your dog and washing pet items separately.

**How to get dog hair out of washer?** To remove dog hair from your washer, first remove as much hair as possible from clothes before washing. During the cycle, use a pet hair catcher. After the wash, wipe the washer drum, clean the lint filter or gasket, and run an empty hot water cycle with vinegar or a washer cleaner to flush out residual fur.

## Pre-Washing Strategies for Reducing Dog Hair

The best offense is a good defense, especially when dealing with dog hair. Removing as much fur as possible *before* clothes ever enter the washing machine is a crucial first step. This prevents the hair from circulating in the wash water and sticking to other items or the washer itself. It saves you a lot of trouble later. I always start here to keep my laundry cleaner.

One simple yet effective method is to shake out clothing vigorously outdoors. This dislodges a surprising amount of loose fur that might otherwise cling stubbornly in the wash. You will be amazed how much hair falls off with just a few shakes. For items like pet beds or blankets, a thorough shake-out is essential. I have found this simple action prevents a lot of hair from entering the machine.

Another helpful tool is a lint roller or even packing tape. Go over the furriest items with a sticky lint roller or wrap packing tape around your hand, sticky side out. Press it firmly onto the fabric and pull it away to lift off hair. This works well for clothes that are heavily covered. It might take a few passes, but it makes a noticeable difference.

For larger items or tough jobs, consider vacuuming your clothes or pet bedding. Many vacuum cleaners come with upholstery attachments that are perfect for this. You can effectively suck up loose hair from blankets, towels, and even some clothing items. This is a very efficient way to remove a large volume of hair quickly. My powerful vacuum cleaner often helps me manage pet hair on various surfaces, including our rugs, which sometimes hold a surprising amount of fur. If you're struggling to get dog hair out of your rugs, a good vacuum can make a huge difference, much like it helps pre-clean laundry. Finally, for very hairy items like pet blankets, consider running them through a dryer on a "no heat" or "air fluff" cycle for about 10-15 minutes *before* washing. The tumbling action and cool air help loosen and collect hair in the dryer's lint trap. Remember to clean the lint trap immediately afterward. This pre-dryer step can drastically reduce the amount of hair that makes it into your washing machine.

## During-Wash Solutions to Trap and Remove Pet Fur

Once your items are in the washing machine, you can add specific aids to help collect and remove lingering dog hair during the cycle. These products and additives work by either attracting hair or making it easier for hair to rinse away. I have tried several of these methods, and they certainly make a difference in how much fur remains on clothes. They help ensure cleaner laundry outcomes.

One popular and effective solution is using pet hair catcher balls or sponges. These items, often made of a slightly sticky or textured material, are tossed into the washer with your laundry. As the machine tumbles, they attract and collect loose hair, preventing it from sticking to your clothes or the washer drum. I always throw in a few of these, especially when washing items that are known fur magnets. They are simple to use and surprisingly effective.

Adding a half-cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle can also work wonders. Vinegar helps relax fabric fibers, allowing trapped hair to release more easily. It also acts as a natural fabric softener and helps neutralize odors. Do not worry; the vinegar smell disappears as the clothes dry. This is a common and highly recommended home remedy for various laundry issues.

Another trick involves adding a dryer sheet into the wash cycle. While typically used in the dryer, some people report success with a dryer sheet in the washer. The anti-static properties of the dryer sheet can help repel hair from clinging to clothes. This method may not be as consistently effective as pet hair catchers, but it is worth trying if you have dryer sheets on hand. It is a simple addition to your wash routine.

You can also find specialized pet hair laundry detergents or additives designed to help release fur from fabric. These products often contain enzymes or polymers that break down the bond between hair and fabric. Read the product instructions carefully. They can be a good option for severe pet hair problems. These in-wash solutions directly address that common query by tackling the hair as it's being washed.

When washing, use cold water settings whenever possible for items with a lot of dog hair. Hot water can sometimes cause hair to "bake" onto fabrics and the machine, making it harder to remove. Cold water helps keep the hair loose so it can be more easily flushed away or caught by aids. A gentle cycle can also help prevent excessive agitation, which might break up hair into smaller, harder-to-remove pieces.

## Post-Wash Cleaning: Getting Hair Out of Your Washer

After you have washed your pet-friendly items, the battle is not over yet. Dog hair often remains inside the washing machine drum, gasket, or filters. Neglecting to clean this residual hair can lead to it transferring onto your next load of laundry. It can also cause buildup issues within the machine over time. I always take a few minutes after a "hairy" load to clean the washer.

The first and most immediate step is to wipe down the inside of the washer drum. Use a damp cloth or paper towel to wipe the drum's interior. You will be surprised how much hair you can collect this way. For front-loading washers, pay special attention to the rubber gasket around the door. This gasket is notorious for trapping water, mold, and, yes, a significant amount of dog hair. Carefully pull back the folds of the gasket and wipe out any accumulated fur and debris. This area needs regular attention. If you own a front-loader, you know how crucial it is to keep this seal clean, not just for hair but for preventing odors too. This is a key part of [how to clean a front-loading washer](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-a-front-loading-washer) effectively.

Many washing machines, particularly top-loaders, have a lint trap or filter. Consult your washer's manual to locate and clean this filter. It often looks like a mesh screen or a small removable compartment. This trap is designed to catch lint and debris, including pet hair. Regularly emptying and cleaning it is vital for your washer's efficiency and for preventing hair buildup. Some newer machines may have self-cleaning filters, but checking your manual is always best.

After cleaning the visible areas, run an empty wash cycle on the hottest water setting. Add two cups of white vinegar or a dedicated washing machine cleaner to the detergent dispenser. This hot cycle helps flush out any remaining hair, soap scum, or residue from the machine's internal components and hoses. It is like giving your washer a refreshing shower. This step is particularly helpful for machines that do not have easily accessible lint traps.

For a deeper clean, especially if you wash pet items frequently, consider running a specific "tub clean" or "self-clean" cycle if your washer has one. These cycles use high water temperatures and agitation to thoroughly clean the drum and internal parts. Pairing this with a washer cleaning tablet or solution can be highly effective. This comprehensive cleaning is essential for maintaining your machine's performance and cleanliness. Remember, performing a [how to deep clean washer machine](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-deep-clean-washer-machine) ritual periodically ensures your appliance stays in top condition.

## Essential Washer Maintenance for Pet Owners

Regular maintenance is key to keeping your washing machine fur-free, especially when living with shedding pets. Just like you maintain your pet's grooming, your washer needs consistent care to perform its best. Proactive steps prevent hair buildup and ensure your laundry always comes out clean. I have found that a little consistent effort saves a lot of headaches in the long run.

Establish a regular cleaning schedule for your washing machine. For pet owners, I recommend a quick wipe-down after every "hairy" load and a more thorough cleaning (like an empty hot water cycle with vinegar) once a month. If you wash pet bedding weekly, consider running an empty cycle every two weeks. Consistency is more important than sporadic deep cleans. This routine keeps small amounts of hair from becoming a large problem.

Always leave the washer door or lid ajar after use. This allows the drum to air dry completely. Moisture left in a closed washer can lead to mold and mildew growth, which traps hair more easily and causes unpleasant odors. A dry environment prevents hair from sticking to damp surfaces. It also keeps your machine smelling fresh.

Check and clean your washing machine's lint trap or filter regularly, as mentioned before. Some models have external filters, while others have them integrated into the agitator or drum. Refer to your owner's manual for its exact location and cleaning instructions. A clogged filter severely reduces your washer's ability to drain effectively and trap debris, leading to more hair clinging to your clothes. This is a critical step many people overlook.

Consider the type of detergent you use. While not directly related to hair removal, using too much detergent can lead to excessive suds and residue buildup. This residue can act like a magnet for pet hair, making it stick to the drum and clothes. Use the recommended amount of high-efficiency (HE) detergent if you have an HE washer. Using the right amount of detergent ensures proper rinsing and prevents sticky residue.

Finally, think about your water hardness. Hard water can make it more challenging for detergents to work effectively and for hair to rinse away. If you have hard water, consider using a water softener or increasing the amount of white vinegar in your rinse cycle. Soft water allows for better cleaning and hair removal. This helps your machine function better overall.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them When Washing Pet Hair

Even with the best intentions, it is easy to make mistakes when trying to manage pet hair in the laundry. These errors can make the problem worse, leading to more hair on your clothes and in your washing machine. I have learned some of these the hard way, so I want to share them with you. Avoiding these common pitfalls will make your laundry routine much smoother.

One major mistake is overloading the washing machine. When the washer is too full, clothes cannot tumble freely, preventing proper agitation and rinsing. This means dirt, detergent, and, most importantly, pet hair, do not get fully flushed away. The hair remains trapped within the packed load, often redepositing onto other items. Always ensure there is enough space for clothes to move around. A good rule of thumb is to fill the drum about three-quarters full.

Another common error is washing heavily furred pet bedding or towels with your regular clothes. This is a sure way to spread pet hair to every item in the load. Pet-specific items often carry the highest concentration of fur. Always wash these items separately. This prevents cross-contamination of hair to your everyday wardrobe. I use a dedicated laundry basket for pet items to keep them separate.

Ignoring your washer's internal filters or lint traps is also a significant oversight. As discussed, these components are designed to capture debris like hair. If they become clogged, they cannot do their job effectively, and hair will recirculate or remain in the machine. Make it a habit to check and clean these parts regularly, especially after washing pet-related items. Many machines have easily accessible filters that just need a quick rinse.

Using too much detergent is counterproductive. While it might seem like more detergent means cleaner clothes, it often leads to excessive suds and residue. This residue can trap pet hair, making it stick to fabrics and the washer drum. It also makes it harder for the machine to rinse everything cleanly. Always follow the detergent manufacturer's recommendations. Less is often more when it comes to laundry detergent.

Lastly, some people might overlook the pre-treatment steps entirely. Just tossing hairy clothes directly into the washer without shaking or de-linting them is a recipe for disaster. This introduces a large volume of loose hair directly into the washing machine's water, maximizing the chances of it clinging everywhere. Taking those few extra minutes beforehand can dramatically reduce the amount of hair you have to deal with in the washer. This simple step can save you so much hassle later.

## FAQ Section

### Can I wash pet beds in the washer?
Yes, many pet beds are machine washable. Always check the care label on the bed first. Before washing, shake the bed vigorously outdoors to remove as much loose hair as possible. Use a gentle cycle with cold water and add some white vinegar to the rinse cycle to help release hair and neutralize odors. Dry thoroughly to prevent mildew.

### Does vinegar help remove dog hair?
Absolutely, white vinegar is highly effective for removing dog hair. Adding half a cup to the fabric softener dispenser during the rinse cycle helps relax fabric fibers. This allows trapped hair to release more easily and rinse away. Vinegar also acts as a natural fabric softener and helps neutralize pet odors.

### Why is my washer full of dog hair?
Your washer is likely full of dog hair because the hair is not effectively removed from items before washing, or it is not properly flushed out during the cycle. Heavy shedding items, clogged lint filters, or an overloaded machine can all contribute. The hair then re-deposits onto the drum or other laundry.

### How often should I clean my washing machine to remove dog hair?
For pet owners, a quick wipe-down of the drum and gasket after every "hairy" load is ideal. Perform a more thorough cleaning, such as running an empty hot water cycle with vinegar or a dedicated washer cleaner, at least once a month. If you wash pet items frequently, consider doing this every two weeks.

### What is the best type of washing machine for pet owners?
Both top-loading and front-loading machines can work for pet owners, but those with easily accessible lint filters are advantageous. Some top-loaders have traditional lint traps that are easy to clean. Front-loaders benefit from thorough gasket cleaning. Features like a "pet hair" cycle or extra rinse options can also be beneficial.

### Are lint traps in washers effective for dog hair?
Yes, lint traps in washing machines are designed to catch lint, threads, and, importantly, pet hair. Their effectiveness depends on their design and how regularly they are cleaned. If your washer has one, routinely checking and emptying it after washing pet items is crucial for preventing hair buildup and ensuring proper drainage.
