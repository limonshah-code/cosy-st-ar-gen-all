## FAQ Section

### What makes a Chihuahua a good emotional support animal?
Chihuahuas excel as emotional support animals due to their deep loyalty and strong bond with their owners. Their small size makes them easy to travel with and discreet in public. They offer constant comfort, reduce stress, and can sense their owner's moods. Their affectionate nature provides significant emotional stability for individuals needing support.

### Are Chihuahuas good with children?
Chihuahuas can be good with older, respectful children. Their small size makes them fragile, so young children who might play roughly are not ideal. Supervision is always crucial when Chihuahuas interact with kids. Proper socialization for the dog and education for the children on gentle handling ensures a positive relationship.

### Do Chihuahuas bark a lot, and how can I manage it?
Yes, Chihuahuas can be prone to barking, often due to their protective nature or anxiety. Managing barking involves consistent training and addressing the root cause. Desensitization to triggers, positive reinforcement for quiet behavior, and ensuring they get enough mental and physical stimulation can help reduce excessive barking effectively.

### What are the main health concerns for Chihuahuas?
Chihuahuas commonly face a few health concerns, including dental disease due to their small mouths. Patellar luxation (slipping kneecaps) and tracheal collapse are also common in the breed. They can also be susceptible to hypoglycemia, especially as puppies. Regular veterinary check-ups and a healthy lifestyle help manage these risks.

### How much exercise does a Chihuahua need daily?
Chihuahuas have moderate exercise needs. About 20-30 minutes of daily activity is usually sufficient. This can include one or two short walks, ideally on a leash for safety. They also enjoy indoor playtime, such as chasing toys or engaging in puzzle games. Over-exercising a Chihuahua can be detrimental due to their small size.
