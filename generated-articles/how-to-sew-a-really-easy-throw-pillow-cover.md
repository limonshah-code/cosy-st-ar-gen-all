## Sew an Easy Throw Pillow Cover: Your Simple Guide

Have you ever looked at your living room and felt it needed a fresh look? Changing throw pillows is one of the quickest ways to update your home decor. ### Clear, Concise Answer:
To sew a really easy throw pillow cover, measure your pillow insert and cut fabric accordingly. Fold and hem the short edges of your fabric pieces. Overlap the hemmed edges to create an envelope opening. Stitch the sides, turn right side out, and insert your pillow for a quick, custom cover.

## Gathering Your Essential Sewing Supplies

Starting any sewing project requires the right tools. For **sewing an easy throw pillow cover**, you do not need much specialized equipment. Gather these basic items before you begin. Having everything ready saves time and prevents interruptions.

First, you need a sewing machine. Any basic machine capable of a straight stitch will work. Make sure your machine is threaded with good quality all-purpose thread. I usually use a neutral color that blends with my fabric, or a matching color if it's visible. You also need a pair of sharp fabric scissors. Dull scissors can fray edges and make cutting difficult.

Next, you will need a measuring tape or a clear ruler. Accuracy in measuring is key to a well-fitting pillow cover. A fabric marker or tailor's chalk is also helpful for marking your cutting lines. Do not use regular pens, as they might bleed or be permanent. Pins are essential for holding fabric layers together before stitching.

Finally, an iron and ironing board are crucial. Pressing seams as you go creates a professional finish. It makes your stitches lie flat and your overall project look much better. These simple tools will set you up for success in creating your beautiful new pillow cover.

## Choosing the Perfect Fabric and Pillow Insert

The success of your easy throw pillow cover begins with good choices for fabric and the pillow insert. The fabric you pick defines the look and feel of your finished pillow. Consider where the pillow will live in your home. For a decorative sofa pillow, a durable cotton, linen, or even velvet might be great. If you want something soft for a bedroom, flannel or a brushed cotton could be perfect.

Think about the fabric's weight and drape. Medium-weight cotton or canvas is easy for beginners to work with. Avoid very slippery or stretchy fabrics for your first project. Patterns can add visual interest. Stripes, florals, or geometric designs can really make a statement. Solid colors offer versatility, allowing the pillow's texture to stand out. Always wash and dry your fabric before cutting. This pre-shrinks it and prevents future distortion.

Next, select your pillow insert. The insert is the actual pillow that goes inside the cover. You can reuse an old pillow or buy a new one. Inserts come in various sizes and fillings. Polyester fiberfill inserts are common, affordable, and hold their shape well. Feather or down inserts offer a softer, more luxurious feel.

Measure your pillow insert accurately. This measurement dictates the size of your fabric pieces. A standard throw pillow is often 18x18 inches or 20x20 inches. Knowing your insert size helps you determine how much fabric to buy. Remember, the goal is a snug fit, so the cover looks plump and inviting.

## Taking Accurate Measurements and Cutting Fabric

Accurate measurements are critical for a great-fitting pillow cover. This step ensures your finished cover is snug and professional looking. First, measure your pillow insert from seam to seam. Do not include any puffiness, just the flat dimensions. Let's say your insert measures 18 inches by 18 inches.

For the front piece of your cover, you will cut one square. Add 1 inch to each side for seam allowance. So, for an 18x18 inch insert, your front fabric piece will be 19x19 inches. This extra inch gives you a half-inch seam on all four sides. Write down your measurements clearly before cutting.

For the back pieces, which form the envelope closure, you need two rectangles. The width of these pieces will be the same as your front piece: 19 inches. For the length, divide your insert's length in half, then add 4-5 inches for overlap and hems. So, for an 18-inch insert length, 18 / 2 = 9 inches. Add 5 inches, which gives you 14 inches. You will cut two back pieces, each 19 inches wide by 14 inches long. This overlap creates a secure opening.

Now, lay your fabric flat and smooth it out. Use your ruler and fabric marker to draw your cutting lines precisely. Double-check all measurements before you cut. Cut carefully along your marked lines with sharp fabric scissors. This precision makes the sewing process much easier and yields a professional result. Once cut, you will have one front square and two back rectangles.

## Sewing an Envelope Closure: Step-by-Step Guide

The envelope closure is a fantastic choice for an **easy throw pillow cover**. It avoids zippers or buttons, making it beginner-friendly. This method involves overlapping fabric pieces at the back, creating a pocket for your pillow insert. I find it remarkably simple to achieve a clean finish.

### Step 1: Hem the Back Pieces

Take one of your back fabric rectangles. Along one of the long edges (the 19-inch side in our example), fold the fabric edge in by ½ inch towards the wrong side. Press this fold flat with your iron. Now, fold it over again by another 1 inch. Press this second fold firmly. This creates a neat, double-folded hem. Stitch this hem down using your sewing machine. Sew a straight line close to the inner folded edge. Repeat this exact process for the second back fabric rectangle. These finished edges will form the opening of your envelope closure.

### Step 2: Assemble the Back

Lay your front fabric square right side up on your work surface. Take one of your hemmed back pieces. Place it on top of the front piece, right side down, aligning the unhemmed edges. Ensure the hemmed edge is towards the center of the pillow. Now, take the second hemmed back piece. Place it on top of the front piece, right side down, aligning its unhemmed edge with the opposite side. The two hemmed edges will overlap in the middle. Pin all layers securely around the entire perimeter. The overlap of the back pieces should be robust.

### Step 3: Stitch the Sides

Carefully take your pinned pillow cover to your sewing machine. Stitch all the way around the entire square using a ½ inch seam allowance. Start and end your stitches by backstitching. This secures the seams and prevents them from unraveling. Go slowly and maintain a consistent seam allowance for neatness. Trim any excess fabric from the corners close to the stitching line. This reduces bulk.

### Step 4: Finish and Turn

Clip the corners diagonally without cutting into your stitches. This helps the corners lie flat when turned. Now, reach inside the opening of the envelope closure. Gently pull the entire pillow cover right side out. Use a pointy tool, like a chopstick or a turning tool, to push out the corners fully. Press the entire pillow cover flat with your iron. Pay attention to the seams, making sure they are crisp and even. Your **easy throw pillow cover** is now ready for your pillow insert!

## Caring for Your New Throw Pillow Cover

After putting in the effort to **sew a really easy throw pillow cover**, you want it to last. Proper care ensures your beautiful creation stays fresh and vibrant for a long time. Different fabrics require different washing methods. Always consider your fabric choice when planning care.

## Troubleshooting Common Sewing Issues

Even when you **sew a really easy throw pillow cover**, you might encounter small challenges. Do not get discouraged. Most sewing issues have simple fixes. Learning to troubleshoot builds your confidence and improves your skills. I have faced many of these myself!

One common issue is uneven stitches. This often happens if you are pulling the fabric as you sew. Let your sewing machine feed the fabric through naturally. Guide it gently, but avoid forcing it. Check your tension settings if stitches look loose or too tight. A quick test on a scrap piece of fabric helps find the right balance.

Fabric shifting can also be a problem, especially with multiple layers. Using more pins is the easiest solution. Pin your fabric layers every few inches. This keeps them aligned as they go under the presser foot. You can also use a walking foot attachment on your machine. A walking foot helps feed multiple layers evenly.

Your needle might break or get dull. This is usually due to sewing over pins, or a dull needle trying to push through thick fabric. Always remove pins *before* stitching over them. Change your needle regularly, especially when starting a new project or working with different fabric types. A sharp needle makes all the difference.

Lastly, crooked seams can make your pillow cover look less polished. This often comes from not maintaining a consistent seam allowance. Use the guide marks on your sewing machine plate. Keep the edge of your fabric aligned with the half-inch mark (or whatever your seam allowance is). Practicing on scrap fabric helps you develop a steady hand. Remember, every stitch is a step towards improving your craft.

## FAQs About Sewing Throw Pillow Covers

### How much fabric do I need for one throw pillow cover?
For a standard 18x18 inch pillow insert, you generally need about 1 yard of fabric. This allows for cutting the front square and two back rectangles with seam allowances. Always check your specific insert size and fabric width. Buying a little extra is always a good idea for any mistakes or future projects.

### Can I sew a pillow cover without a sewing machine?
Yes, you can hand sew a pillow cover. It will take more time and effort. You will need a needle, thread, and good hand-sewing skills. A simple backstitch or running stitch can create strong seams. Hand sewing is perfect if you enjoy slow crafting or do not have access to a machine.

### What is the easiest type of pillow cover closure for beginners?
An envelope closure is the easiest type of pillow cover closure for beginners. It avoids zippers, buttons, or complicated fasteners. You simply hem and overlap two fabric pieces on the back. This creates an opening to insert your pillow without any tricky hardware.

### What fabric is best for an easy throw pillow cover?
Medium-weight cotton or canvas fabric is best for an easy throw pillow cover. These fabrics are stable and easy to cut and sew. They do not stretch or slip, making them perfect for beginners. They also offer good durability for home decor.

### How do I make sure my pillow cover fits snugly?
To ensure a snug fit, measure your pillow insert accurately from seam to seam. Cut your fabric pieces exactly to these measurements, adding only the necessary seam allowance (usually ½ inch per side). This slight reduction in cover size ensures a plump, fitted look once the insert is in place.

### How can I make my throw pillow cover more durable?
To make your throw pillow cover more durable, use high-quality, tightly woven fabric like canvas or upholstery fabric. Reinforce seams by backstitching at the beginning and end of each stitch line. You can also sew a second line of stitching or use a serger for added strength, especially on seams that will see a lot of stress.
