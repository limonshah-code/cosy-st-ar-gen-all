### Switching to Fresh Pet: What to Expect

Changing your dog's food should be a gradual process. This helps your dog's digestive system adjust. Sudden changes can cause stomach upset. I always introduce new foods slowly.

Monitoring your dog's health after switching is important. Look for signs of good digestion and overall well-being. Adjusting portions might be necessary as well. Fresh Pet is calorie-dense.

#### Gradual Transitioning Tips

Start by mixing a small amount of Fresh Pet with your dog's old food. Over 7 to 10 days, slowly increase the Fresh Pet portion. At the same time, decrease the old food portion. This allows your dog's stomach to get used to the new ingredients.

For example, on day one, mix 25% Fresh Pet with 75% old food. After a few days, move to 50/50. By the end of the week, you can feed 100% Fresh Pet. This prevents digestive issues like diarrhea or vomiting. It makes the switch smoother for your dog. #### Monitoring Your Dog's Response

After the transition, watch your dog closely. Look for improved energy levels, a shinier coat, and healthy stool. These are good signs that the food is agreeing with them. If you notice any negative changes, consult your veterinarian.

Fresh Pet is very nutrient-rich. Your dog might feel full on smaller portions than with kibble. Adjust the amount you feed based on your dog's activity level and body condition. This helps maintain a healthy weight.
