### Why Most Human Viruses Don't Affect Dogs

Most human viruses, including those causing the common cold (rhinovirus) and influenza (flu), do not affect dogs. Each virus needs specific cellular receptors to enter and replicate. Humans and dogs have different cellular structures. This means the viruses that infect humans cannot effectively bind to or replicate in dog cells. For example, the human flu virus targets human respiratory cells. It cannot recognize or infect canine cells.

While a human might sneeze on their dog, the virus particles simply cannot take hold in the dog's system. The dog's immune system will recognize them as foreign. It will clear them out without an infection. This biological specificity is nature's way of preventing widespread cross-species disease. My dog might sniff my used tissue. They will not catch my cold.

### Understanding Zoonotic Diseases (Rare Cases)

Zoonotic diseases are illnesses that can spread between animals and humans. While human stomach flu is rarely, if ever, zoonotic for dogs, some other diseases *can* cross the species barrier. Rabies, Lyme disease, and certain types of Salmonella or E. coli are examples. These are exceptions, not the rule. The transfer mechanisms for zoonotic diseases are specific. For example, rabies spreads through bites. Lyme disease spreads via tick bites.
