## Step-by-Step Guide to Cleaning Your Subaru Dashboard

Cleaning your Subaru dashboard is simple. Follow these steps for the best results. I use this method every time. It keeps my dash looking factory fresh.

### Pre-Cleaning and Dust Removal

First, remove all items from your dashboard. Take out papers, pens, and anything else. This makes cleaning easier. Next, use a soft detailing brush or a dry microfiber cloth. Gently dust all dashboard surfaces. Work from top to bottom. This pushes dust downwards.

Use your car's vacuum cleaner. Attach a brush or crevice tool. Vacuum all dust and debris from the dash. Pay attention to seams, vents, and the area near the windshield. Compressed air can also clear dust from tight spots. This initial step prepares the surface for deeper cleaning.

### Deep Cleaning Surfaces

Now, it is time to clean the surfaces. Spray your automotive interior cleaner onto a clean microfiber cloth. Do not spray directly onto the dashboard. This avoids overspray and streaks. Wipe down the dashboard in small sections. Apply gentle pressure. Turn your cloth often to a clean side.

For tough spots, spray a little more cleaner onto the cloth. Let it sit for a few seconds. Then wipe the area again. A clean dashboard makes your whole car feel new. Make sure to cover every part of the dash. For specific advice on other car dashboards, you can look at [how to clean Lexus RX 350 dashboard](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-lexus-rx-350-dashboard) for comparison.

### Tackling Vents and Crevices

Air vents and small crevices collect a lot of dust. Use a detailing brush to clean these areas. Work the brush into each vent slat. The brush loosens the trapped dust. Then, vacuum the loosened dust. Compressed air also helps here. Blast air into the vents to push out debris.

Small Q-tips or cotton swabs can clean very tiny spots. Dip the swab in a small amount of cleaner if needed. Gently wipe around buttons and switches. Be careful not to get too much moisture into electronic parts. This attention to detail makes a big difference.

### Screen and Gauge Care

Modern Subaru dashboards have touchscreens and digital gauges. These need special care. Use a screen-specific cleaner for your display. A normal cleaner can damage the anti-glare coating. Spray the screen cleaner onto a fresh microfiber cloth. Wipe the screen gently. Use a cloth meant for electronics.

Do not press hard on screens or gauges. This can cause damage. Use a separate, very soft microfiber cloth for these parts. Clean them last to avoid transferring dirt. A clear screen improves visibility and overall appearance.

## Choosing the Right Cleaning Products for Subaru Dash Materials

Subaru dashboards use different materials. Knowing your dash material helps you choose the right products. Most dashboards are vinyl or plastic. Some may have soft-touch materials or even leather accents. I always check my car's manual if I am unsure.

### Vinyl and Plastic Dashboard Care

Most Subaru dashboards use vinyl or plastic. These materials are durable but need proper care. Use an interior cleaner designed for vinyl and plastic. These cleaners remove dirt without drying out the material. They also do not leave a greasy shine.

After cleaning, apply a UV protectant. This product shields your dashboard from sun damage. It prevents fading and cracking over time. Choose a matte finish protectant for a factory look. High-gloss products can create glare. They also look unnatural.

### Special Considerations for Touchscreens

Touchscreens are common in newer Subarus. These screens are sensitive. Avoid cleaners with ammonia or alcohol. These chemicals damage anti-glare coatings. Use only screen cleaners specifically for automotive or electronics. These products are gentle. They clean without harming the screen.

Always spray the cleaner onto your microfiber cloth first. Then wipe the screen. Never spray directly onto the screen. This prevents liquid from getting into the screen's edges. A clean, clear touchscreen works better and looks nicer.

## Protecting Your Subaru Dashboard: Longevity Tips

Cleaning your Subaru dashboard is only half the battle. Protecting it ensures it stays clean longer. Prevention is a key part of car care. I take steps to protect my dash after cleaning it. This saves me time and effort in the future.

### UV Protection and Conditioners

Sunlight is a major enemy of your dashboard. UV rays cause fading and cracking. After cleaning, apply a UV protectant or dashboard conditioner. This creates a barrier against the sun's harmful rays. It also helps keep the material supple.

Apply the product with a clean microfiber cloth. Wipe it evenly across all surfaces. Let it soak in for a few minutes. Then, gently buff off any excess. Do this regularly, especially if your car sits outside. This simple step extends your dash's life.

### Preventing Dust Buildup

Dust is a constant problem in cars. You can reduce dust buildup. Regularly wipe your dash with a dry microfiber cloth. Do this once a week or every few days. This prevents dust from settling and becoming sticky. A quick wipe keeps the dash cleaner between deep cleans.

Parking in a garage helps too. It reduces dust and sun exposure. You can also use a car cover if you park outside. These small habits make a big difference. They help you keep your Subaru dashboard pristine.

## Common Dashboard Cleaning Mistakes to Avoid

Making mistakes while cleaning can damage your Subaru dashboard. It is important to know what not to do. I learned some of these the hard way. Avoiding these errors saves your dash from harm.

### Harsh Chemicals Dangers

Never use harsh household cleaners on your dashboard. Products like glass cleaner with ammonia, bleach, or strong degreasers are harmful. They strip protectants from materials. They can also cause discoloration or cracking. Always use cleaners made for car interiors. Just like you would be careful with carpet, where you need to know [what should you not clean carpet with](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-should-you-not-clean-carpet-with), the same applies to your dashboard. Using the wrong product can cause irreversible damage.

Petroleum-based products also cause issues. They can leave a greasy residue. This residue attracts more dust. It also creates a shiny, unnatural look. Stick to water-based, pH-neutral cleaners for safety.

### Over-Wetting Surfaces

Do not saturate your dashboard with liquid cleaner. Too much moisture can get into electronic components. It can also leave streaks or water spots. Always spray your cleaner onto a microfiber cloth first. Then wipe the surface.

Use a damp, not wet, cloth. If the cloth becomes too wet, wring it out. Dry the surface immediately after cleaning. This prevents water marks. It also ensures moisture does not seep into cracks. A little goes a long way when cleaning.

### FAQ

**Q1: How often should I clean my Subaru dashboard?**
A: You should dust your Subaru dashboard weekly. Do a full deep clean every one to three months. This depends on how much you drive and where you park. Regular light cleaning prevents heavy grime buildup.

**Q2: Can I use household cleaning wipes on my dashboard?**
A: No, avoid most household cleaning wipes. Many contain harsh chemicals that damage car interiors. They can dry out, fade, or crack your dashboard materials over time. Stick to automotive-specific interior wipes or cleaners.

**Q3: My dashboard is sticky. How do I fix it?**
A: A sticky dashboard usually means product residue or material breakdown. Clean it thoroughly with a mild automotive interior cleaner and a clean microfiber cloth. You may need multiple passes. Avoid abrasive scrubbing. Apply a good quality matte-finish UV protectant after cleaning.

**Q4: What should I use to clean the touchscreen on my Subaru?**
A: Use an electronics-specific screen cleaner and a soft microfiber cloth. These products are safe for delicate anti-glare coatings. Never spray cleaner directly onto the screen. Do not use ammonia-based glass cleaners.

**Q5: Are natural cleaners like vinegar safe for my dashboard?**
A: Vinegar is acidic and can damage some dashboard materials. It is best to avoid it. Stick to commercially available automotive interior cleaners. They are formulated to be safe for your car's surfaces.

**Q6: How can I protect my dashboard from sun damage?**
A: Apply a good quality UV protectant or dashboard conditioner after cleaning. Use a sunshade when parked, especially in direct sunlight. Parking in shaded areas or garages also helps greatly reduce sun exposure.
