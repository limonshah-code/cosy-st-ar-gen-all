### FAQ Section

#### Are dogs really more loyal than cats?
Dogs often display more overt loyalty and affection than cats. They bond deeply with their owners, greet them enthusiastically, and seek constant interaction. This strong desire to be part of their human's life is a hallmark of canine loyalty. Cats show affection too, but often in more subtle or independent ways.

#### Do dogs offer more health benefits than cats?
Dogs generally offer more direct health benefits due to their need for outdoor exercise. This encourages owners to be more active, leading to better cardiovascular health and reduced stress. Both pets offer companionship that helps mental well-being, but dogs often provide a stronger impetus for physical activity.

#### Can cats be trained as easily as dogs?
Cats can learn basic commands and house rules, but they are generally not as easily or extensively trained as dogs. Dogs are more eager to please and respond better to consistent reinforcement and structured training methods. Cats respond better to positive reinforcement and often on their own terms.

#### Are dogs more expensive to own than cats?
The cost of owning a dog can vary greatly by breed and size. Large dogs often cost more in food and veterinary care. However, cat ownership also comes with costs like specialized food, litter, and potential indoor enrichment needs. Overall, dog ownership often involves higher initial and ongoing expenses, but this depends on many factors.

#### Why do some people prefer cats over dogs?
Some people prefer cats due to their independent nature, quiet demeanor, and lower demand for outdoor activity. Cats can be a good fit for smaller living spaces or for people with busy schedules who still want a companion. Their self-sufficiency appeals to many who appreciate a less demanding pet.

#### Can dogs and cats live together peacefully?
Yes, dogs and cats can often live together peacefully. Successful integration depends on proper introductions, patient training, and understanding each animal's personality. Early socialization helps, but many adult dogs and cats can learn to coexist, especially when given their own space and resources.
