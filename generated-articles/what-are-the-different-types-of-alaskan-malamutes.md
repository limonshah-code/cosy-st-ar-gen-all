## Understanding the Alaskan Malamute Standard Breed

The Alaskan Malamute is a powerful and ancient breed. It comes from the Arctic region. These dogs are strong and built for pulling heavy sleds over long distances. The American Kennel Club (AKC) recognizes only one Alaskan Malamute breed. There are no official "types" like miniature or toy versions. Each Malamute should fit within the breed standard.

The breed standard describes the ideal Malamute. It includes details about size, coat, and temperament. A male Malamute typically stands 25 inches tall at the shoulder. He weighs about 85 pounds. A female is usually 23 inches tall and weighs about 75 pounds. These are ideal weights and heights for a working dog. Some Malamutes might be slightly larger or smaller. Yet, they are still considered within the single breed. Their structure is built for strength and endurance. They have a deep chest and a well-muscled body. The shape of their ears also contributes to their distinct appearance. Malamute ears are medium in size and triangular. They stand erect, giving them an alert expression. Malamutes have a thick, double coat. This coat protects them from harsh weather. Their tail usually curls over their back, like a plume. Their eyes are almond-shaped and dark brown. Blue eyes are not allowed in the breed standard. This helps differentiate them from other Nordic breeds. Understanding the standard ensures that breeders maintain the integrity of the breed. It ensures the dogs keep their unique traits and working ability.

## Dispelling the Myth of "Giant Alaskan Malamutes"

You might hear people talk about "Giant Alaskan Malamutes." This term often creates confusion. It implies there is a separate, larger type of Malamute. However, this is a common misunderstanding. There is no official "Giant Alaskan Malamute" breed or type. The AKC only recognizes one Alaskan Malamute breed.

Some Malamutes naturally grow larger than the breed standard. They might weigh over 100 pounds or stand taller than 25 inches. These dogs are simply oversized Alaskan Malamutes. They are not a distinct type. Their larger size can sometimes lead to health issues. It can put more strain on their joints. Reputable breeders aim to produce dogs within the standard size range. They focus on health and temperament, not just size.

Breeders might selectively breed larger dogs. This creates a line that consistently produces bigger puppies. Still, these puppies are just large Alaskan Malamutes. They are not a different breed. ## Historical Lineages: Kotzebue and M'Loot Alaskan Malamutes

While there are no different types of Alaskan Malamutes today, there were historical lineages. These lineages contributed to the breed's development. Two main lines are often discussed: the Kotzebue and the M'Loot. These were not separate breeds. They were different breeding programs. They focused on specific traits within the same overall Malamute population.

The Kotzebue line is named after Arthur Walden's Chinook Kennels. These Malamutes were generally smaller and more uniform in appearance. They had consistent gray and white coats. They were known for their strong pack drive and endurance. Many of the first Malamutes registered with the AKC came from this line. This line created a stable foundation for the breed. Their temperament was often described as more reserved.

The M'Loot line was developed by Paul Voelker. These Malamutes were often larger and had more varied coat colors. They could be black and white, red and white, or pure white. They were known for their impressive size and sometimes a more independent nature. The M'Loot line introduced more genetic diversity into the breed. It also contributed to the occasional "giant" Malamute seen today. These historical lines eventually merged. Modern Alaskan Malamutes descend from a blend of these foundational dogs. Breeders no longer strictly adhere to only one line. Instead, they breed for overall health, temperament, and adherence to the single breed standard. This blending helps maintain genetic health and diversity.

## Distinguishing Alaskan Malamutes from Siberian Huskies

It is very common to confuse Alaskan Malamutes with Siberian Huskies. They both come from cold climates and look similar. However, they are different breeds with distinct characteristics. Understanding these differences helps clarify why there are no "types" of Malamutes. Malamutes are built for power and strength. Huskies are built for speed and endurance.

Malamutes are generally larger and heavier than Huskies. They have a broader head and a stockier body. A Malamute typically weighs between 75 and 85 pounds. A Husky usually weighs between 35 and 60 pounds. Malamutes also have thick, dense coats designed for extreme cold. Their tails are plumed and curl over their backs. Huskies have slightly less dense coats. Their tails are usually carried lower or in a sickle shape.

A key difference is eye color. Malamutes always have brown eyes according to the breed standard. Blue eyes are a disqualifying fault. Huskies can have blue eyes, brown eyes, or even one of each color. Their temperament also differs. Malamutes are typically more laid-back and stoic. They can be stubborn. Huskies are often more energetic and mischievous. They have a higher prey drive. Both are intelligent and require consistent training. ## FAQ Section

### Are there different sizes of Alaskan Malamutes?

Yes, Alaskan Malamutes show some size variation. The breed standard gives ideal ranges for males (25 inches, 85 pounds) and females (23 inches, 75 pounds). Some individuals might naturally grow larger or smaller than this. These are still considered standard Alaskan Malamutes, not a separate "giant" type.

### What are the common Alaskan Malamute coat colors?

Alaskan Malamutes come in a range of colors. Common colors include various shades of gray and white, black and white, sable and white, and red and white. Pure white Malamutes also exist. All these colors are accepted within the single breed standard.

### Is a "Giant Malamute" a separate breed?

No, a "Giant Malamute" is not a separate breed. The term refers to an Alaskan Malamute that is larger than the breed standard. These larger dogs are still purebred Alaskan Malamutes. They are not recognized as a distinct type by kennel clubs.

### What is the difference between Kotzebue and M'Loot Malamutes?

Kotzebue and M'Loot refer to historical breeding lines, not current types. Kotzebue Malamutes were generally smaller, more uniform, and had a strong pack drive. M'Loot Malamutes were often larger, had more varied colors, and were sometimes more independent. Modern Malamutes descend from a blend of these lines.

### Do Alaskan Malamutes have blue eyes?

No, Alaskan Malamutes do not have blue eyes according to the breed standard. Their eyes are always dark brown and almond-shaped. Blue eyes are a disqualifying fault in the show ring. If a Malamute has blue eyes, it suggests mixed ancestry, usually with a Siberian Husky.

### How does Malamute temperament vary by "type"?

Alaskan Malamute temperament does not vary by "type" because there is only one breed. Individual personalities can differ. However, core traits like intelligence, independence, loyalty, and a strong prey drive are common to all Malamutes. Proper training and socialization are important for all of them.
