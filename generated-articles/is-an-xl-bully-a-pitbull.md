### Origins and Development of the American Bully

The American Bully breed began in the 1990s. Breeders in the United States wanted to create a new companion breed. They used several "Pitbull-type" dogs in their breeding programs. These dogs included the American Pit Bull Terrier and the American Staffordshire Terrier. They also incorporated other bulldog breeds.

The goal was to produce a dog with a bulkier build and a gentler demeanor. This focus on companionship shifted the breed's purpose. It moved away from the working roles of its ancestors. The result was a dog known for its impressive strength and calm nature. This new breed quickly gained popularity for its unique look and friendly disposition.

## Deconstructing the Term "Pitbull" Breeds

When people say "Pitbull," they often refer to a general type of dog. This term does not describe a single, distinct breed. Instead, it acts as an umbrella term. ### The Four "Pitbull-Type" Dogs

Traditionally, the term "Pitbull" most commonly refers to four breeds. These are the American Pit Bull Terrier, the American Staffordshire Terrier, the Staffordshire Bull Terrier, and sometimes the American Bully. However, the American Bully is distinct from the first three. It is a separate breed with its own standards.

The American Pit Bull Terrier is a true Pitbull breed. It has a lean, athletic build. The American Staffordshire Terrier is similar but slightly stockier. The Staffordshire Bull Terrier is smaller and compact. Each of these breeds possesses its own unique characteristics. They were bred for different purposes over time.

### Historical Context of Pitbulls

The history of Pitbull-type dogs traces back to the 19th century in England. They originated from crosses between bulldogs and terriers. Breeders aimed to combine the strength of bulldogs with the gameness of terriers. These dogs were initially used for blood sports like bull-baiting and bear-baiting. After these cruel sports were outlawed, their role changed.

They became farm dogs and family companions. Immigrants later brought these dogs to America. Here, they continued to serve as all-around working dogs. They helped with herding, hunting, and guarding. This history explains their strong drive and loyal nature.

### Physical Traits and Breed Standards

The most noticeable differences lie in their physical appearance. American Pit Bull Terriers (APBTs), a primary Pitbull breed, have a lean, athletic build. They are known for their speed and agility. Their heads are usually more refined, and their bodies are less bulky.

In contrast, the XL Bully boasts a much heavier build. They have a wider chest, a larger head, and significantly more muscle mass. Their bone structure is dense. The XL Bully prioritizes mass and bulk over the athletic lean frame of the APBT. Their stature is generally shorter and broader. This distinct look is a key identifier.

### Temperament and Purpose

Both types of dogs are known for loyalty and affection towards their families. However, their primary purposes differ. Pitbull-type dogs, especially APBTs, were historically bred for working roles. They often possess a higher prey drive and more intense energy. Their temperament often reflects their original purpose.

The XL Bully was specifically bred for companionship. Breeders focused on a more mellow and stable temperament. They aimed for a family-friendly dog. This means an XL Bully typically exhibits a calmer demeanor at home. They are often less driven by prey instincts than their Pitbull ancestors. This difference in breeding goals created distinct behavioral traits.

### Legal Distinctions

Legal classifications vary widely. Some regions or countries have breed-specific legislation (BSL). These laws often target "Pitbull-type" dogs. This typically includes American Pit Bull Terriers and American Staffordshire Terriers. In recent years, some jurisdictions have added the American Bully, specifically the XL type, to these lists.

For example, the UK has banned the XL Bully. This ban classifies the XL Bully as a "Pitbull type" dog under their Dangerous Dogs Act. This legal status difference highlights their separate identities.
