### Standard vs. Giant Alaskan Malamutes: What's the Difference?

When discussing Alaskan Malamute size, the term "Giant Alaskan Malamute" often comes up. It is important to clarify this term. The AKC breed standard defines a specific size range for Alaskan Malamutes. This range does not include "giant" variations. So, what does "Giant Alaskan Malamute" mean?

A "Giant Alaskan Malamute" is not a separate, recognized breed. It refers to a Malamute that exceeds the standard size. These dogs can be significantly larger. They might weigh over 100 pounds. Some even reach 140 pounds. Their height can also exceed the standard 25 inches. Breeders sometimes specifically breed for this larger size. They market these dogs as "Giants."

The existence of "Giant" Malamutes raises questions. The AKC views any Malamute exceeding the standard as simply a large Malamute, not a different type.

Some people prefer the larger "Giant" size. They find their immense presence appealing. However, breeding for extreme size can carry risks. Larger dogs may experience more joint problems. ### FAQ Section

**Q1: When do Alaskan Malamutes stop growing?**
Alaskan Malamutes generally reach their full height around 12 months of age. They continue to fill out and gain muscle mass until they are 18 to 24 months old. Some larger individuals may take slightly longer to reach their mature weight. Their bone structure is set by a year, but their body still develops.

**Q2: Are Giant Alaskan Malamutes a recognized breed?**
No, "Giant Alaskan Malamute" is not a separate, recognized breed by major kennel clubs like the AKC. It refers to Alaskan Malamutes that grow significantly larger than the breed standard. These dogs are still genetically Malamutes, just larger examples that fall outside the typical size range.

**Q3: Do female Alaskan Malamutes grow as big as males?**
No, female Alaskan Malamutes are generally smaller than males. Standard females typically stand 21-23 inches at the shoulder and weigh 65-75 pounds. Males are usually larger, standing 23-25 inches and weighing 75-85 pounds. There is a noticeable difference in their average size.

**Q4: What influences an Alaskan Malamute's adult size?**
A Malamute's adult size is primarily determined by genetics inherited from its parents. Other important factors include proper nutrition during growth, regular and appropriate exercise, and overall health. Good quality food and veterinary care ensure they grow strong and healthy.

**Q5: Is an Alaskan Malamute larger than a Siberian Husky?**
Yes, Alaskan Malamutes are significantly larger and heavier than Siberian Huskies. Malamutes are bred for power and pulling heavy loads, while Huskies are bred for speed and endurance with lighter loads. Malamutes typically weigh 75-85 pounds, while Huskies weigh 45-60 pounds.
