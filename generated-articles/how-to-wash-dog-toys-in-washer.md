## Wash Dog Toys in Washer: Simple Steps for Pet Parents

Dirty dog toys are a common sight in many homes. They collect grime, slobber, and germs, which can pose a risk to your pet's health. Knowing how to wash dog toys in a washer machine is essential for maintaining a clean and healthy environment for your furry friend. A regular cleaning routine keeps toys fresh and extends their life.

In this guide, I will walk you through the process of washing dog toys in your washing machine. We will cover which toys are machine-friendly, how to prepare them, the best wash settings, and proper drying techniques. By following these simple steps, you ensure your dog’s favorite items are always clean and safe. This helps your pet stay healthy and happy.

### Takeaway

Keeping your dog's toys clean is simple and important for their health.
*   Sort toys by material before washing.
*   Use a mesh laundry bag for small or delicate items.
*   Choose cold water and a gentle cycle for most toys.
*   Always use a pet-safe, mild detergent.
*   Air dry or machine dry on low heat for best results.

### A Quick Answer

You can wash many dog toys in a washing machine. First, sort toys by material, remove loose dirt, and place them in a mesh bag. Use a gentle cycle with cold water and a pet-safe detergent. Air dry or tumble dry on low heat to prevent damage and ensure toys are completely dry before returning them to your dog.

## Why Washing Dog Toys Matters for Pet Health

Dog toys are not just playthings; they are items your pet interacts with daily. Toys collect dirt, bacteria, and saliva quickly. Think about how often your dog chews, licks, and carries their toys around. This constant interaction means toys become breeding grounds for germs. These germs can lead to skin irritation or even digestive issues for your dog.

Regular cleaning helps remove these harmful pathogens. It reduces the risk of your dog getting sick from contaminated toys. A clean toy means a healthier dog. It also helps control odors in your home. Fresh toys make playtime more enjoyable for everyone. For detailed guidance on frequency, consider how often should you wash dog toys.

Clean toys also last longer. Dirt and grime can break down toy fibers over time. Washing removes these elements, preserving the toy's material. This saves you money in the long run. It also ensures your dog always has safe, durable toys to play with.

## Identifying Washer-Friendly Dog Toys

Not all dog toys are suitable for machine washing. It is important to check the toy's care label if it has one. Most plush toys and some rubber or fabric toys are safe for the washing machine. Toys with electronic components, batteries, or delicate embellishments should not go into the washer. Small parts can also break off and cause problems.

Sorting your dog's toys before washing prevents damage to the toys and your washing machine. I always sort my dog’s toys into categories: machine-washable soft toys, hand-washable items, and those that only need a wipe-down. This simple step protects your belongings and ensures effective cleaning.

### Plush Toys

Plush dog toys are generally safe for the washing machine. These are often made from fabric, cotton, or synthetic fibers. They get very dirty from slobber and general use. Placing them in a mesh laundry bag protects them from getting snagged or damaged during the wash cycle. This also prevents stuffing from escaping. When considering how to clean plush items, it helps to know what is the best way to clean plush toys, including those belonging to your pet.

Before washing plush toys, check for any tears or loose stuffing. Repair these areas if possible. This prevents the toy from falling apart in the wash. It also stops stuffing from clogging your drain pump. Wash plush toys with similar colors to avoid color bleeding.

### Rubber & Hard Plastic Toys

Many rubber and hard plastic dog toys can go in the washing machine. These toys include puzzle toys, chew toys, and fetch balls. They often have crevices where dirt and saliva can accumulate. Machine washing helps to clean these hard-to-reach areas effectively.

For best results, place smaller rubber or plastic toys in a mesh laundry bag. This keeps them from rattling around too much or getting stuck in the machine. Larger, heavier rubber toys can go directly into the washer without a bag. They are usually durable enough to handle the agitation.

## Pre-Washing Steps: Preparing Dog Toys for the Machine

Proper preparation makes a big difference in how well your dog's toys get clean. It also protects your washing machine from damage. Do not just toss dirty toys into the machine. Taking a few moments to prepare them ensures a more effective and safer wash. I always perform these steps before starting the laundry.

First, remove any loose debris from the toys. This includes hair, dirt, and dried food bits. You can use a brush, a vacuum cleaner attachment, or simply shake the toys vigorously outside. This step is crucial for preventing excessive dog hair from entering your washer. Learning how to get dog hair out of washer can save you trouble later. Excess hair can clog your machine's filter and pipes.

Second, inspect each toy for damage. Look for torn seams, exposed stuffing, or broken parts. Toys with significant damage might not withstand a machine wash. They could break apart further, creating a mess or harming your machine. It is better to hand wash or discard severely damaged toys.

Lastly, place smaller toys, plush toys, or those with delicate features into a mesh laundry bag. This bag acts as a protective barrier. It prevents small items from getting lost or stuck in the machine's agitator. It also keeps stuffing contained if a seam happens to break during the wash. Using a bag is a simple way to extend the life of your dog's favorite toys and protect your appliance.

## The Washing Cycle: Best Settings for Dog Toys

Choosing the right settings for your washing machine is important. This ensures dog toys get clean without damage. The goal is a thorough wash that removes germs and dirt, but gentle enough to preserve the toy's material. I always follow these guidelines for my pet’s laundry.

First, select a gentle cycle. Options like "delicates," "hand wash," or "gentle" are ideal. These cycles use less agitation, which reduces wear and tear on the toys. A harsh cycle can rip seams or damage delicate parts, especially on plush items. This also helps to prevent excessive noise and vibration during the wash.

Second, use cold water. Cold water is effective at cleaning most pet messes. It is also much gentler on fabrics and toy materials. Hot water can cause toys to shrink, colors to fade, or even melt certain plastic components. Cold water also saves energy, which is good for your utility bills.

### Detergent Choices

Selecting the right detergent is crucial for your dog's safety. Avoid harsh chemicals, dyes, and strong fragrances. These can irritate your dog's skin or respiratory system. Leftover residue on toys can cause an allergic reaction when your dog chews them. I use a fragrance-free, hypoallergenic detergent specifically designed for sensitive skin.

Look for detergents labeled "free and clear" or those marketed for baby clothes. These usually contain fewer irritating additives. You can also find pet-specific laundry detergents online or at pet supply stores. Always use the recommended amount of detergent. Too much can leave a residue, while too little may not clean effectively. Never use fabric softener, as it can be toxic to pets if ingested.

### Cycle Selection

The "gentle" or "delicates" cycle is best for nearly all machine-washable dog toys. This setting minimizes agitation, protecting toy seams and materials. For toys that are particularly dirty, or those made of robust rubber, a "normal" cycle on a cold setting might be acceptable. However, I always lean towards gentler options to be safe.

Consider adding an extra rinse cycle. This helps ensure all detergent residue washes away. Dogs often put their toys directly into their mouths. Any leftover soap can cause an upset stomach or irritation. An extra rinse provides peace of mind that toys are truly clean and safe. Remember, if you are also washing other items like shoes, the principles for washing shoes in the washer machine are similar regarding gentle cycles and cold water. ## Drying Dog Toys: Air Dry vs. Machine Dry

Properly drying dog toys is as important as washing them. Damp toys can grow mold or mildew, which can be harmful to your dog and cause unpleasant odors. The method you choose depends on the toy's material. I always make sure toys are completely dry before giving them back to my dog.

For most plush toys, air drying is the safest option. After washing, gently squeeze out excess water. Then, hang them in a well-ventilated area or lay them flat on a clean towel. A clothesline or a drying rack works well. Air drying prevents shrinkage and maintains the toy's shape. It also avoids potential heat damage that can occur in a dryer. Plush toys can take several hours or even a full day to dry completely, especially if they are large or densely stuffed.

Hard plastic and rubber toys can be towel-dried immediately after washing. You can also let them air dry. These materials dry much faster than fabric. For some rubber or plastic items, a very low heat setting in the dryer might be acceptable. However, always exercise caution. High heat can melt or deform plastic. It is safer to air dry or use a no-heat tumble setting if you choose to machine dry these.

If you choose to machine dry any toys, use the lowest heat setting available. This is often labeled "air fluff" or "delicate." Adding a few clean towels to the dryer can help absorb moisture and tumble the toys more gently. Regularly check the toys during the drying cycle. Remove them as soon as they are dry to prevent overheating or damage. Never put toys with electronic components in the dryer.

## Handling Tough Stains and Disinfection

Sometimes, dog toys have more than just general dirt. They might have stubborn stains from mud, food, or bodily fluids. Also, if your dog has been sick, disinfecting their toys becomes a critical step. A simple wash might not be enough in these situations. I approach these challenges with specific treatments.

For tough stains, pre-treatment is your best friend. Apply a small amount of pet-safe stain remover directly to the stained area before washing. You can also create a paste with baking soda and water. Rub it gently into the stain and let it sit for about 15-30 minutes before washing. This helps break down the stain, allowing the washer to clean it more effectively. Always test any stain remover on an inconspicuous part of the toy first. This ensures it does not cause discoloration.

Disinfection is crucial, especially if your dog has been unwell. Standard washing removes dirt and some germs, but true disinfection kills viruses and bacteria. You can add a pet-safe disinfectant to the wash cycle. Options include certain types of laundry sanitizers or a small amount of white vinegar. White vinegar is a natural disinfectant and deodorizer. Add half a cup to the rinse cycle. For specific steps on germ removal, learning how do you disinfect toys after being sick is very helpful.

For rubber toys that need a deeper clean, consider a specialized approach. These toys can harbor germs in their textures. You may want to review how do you deep clean rubber dog toys for techniques beyond just machine washing. This ensures every nook and cranny is free from contaminants. Always ensure any disinfecting agent is completely rinsed away. This prevents your dog from ingesting harmful residues.

## Maintaining Toy Cleanliness Between Washes

Washing dog toys regularly is important, but daily habits also contribute to cleanliness. You can keep toys cleaner for longer between washes. This reduces the buildup of dirt and germs. It also means you do not have to wash them as frequently. Small actions make a big difference in maintaining pet hygiene.

First, establish a quick daily wipe-down routine. For hard plastic or rubber toys, use a pet-safe wipe or a damp cloth to clean off surface grime. This removes fresh slobber and dirt before it dries and hardens. For plush toys, a quick shake outdoors can remove loose hair and dust. This simple action helps keep toys presentable and reduces overall dirt accumulation.

Second, store toys properly when not in use. Do not leave them scattered on the floor where they can pick up more dirt. Use a designated toy basket or bin. This keeps toys organized and somewhat protected from everyday dust and debris. If you have outdoor toys, keep them separate from indoor toys to prevent tracking outside dirt inside.

Finally, rotate your dog's toys. Instead of having all toys available all the time, offer a selection. This keeps your dog more interested in their toys. It also allows you to clean certain toys while others are in use. Rotating toys also prevents any single toy from becoming excessively dirty too quickly. This strategy helps extend the time between full machine washes. Remember, consistency in cleaning is key to a healthy pet environment.

## FAQ Section

### Can I wash all dog toys in the washer?

No, not all dog toys are machine-washable. Always check the care label first. Plush toys and many fabric or rubber toys are usually safe. Toys with electronic components, batteries, small bells, or delicate attachments should not go into the washer. These items can break or damage your machine.

### What detergent is safe for dog toys?

Use a mild, fragrance-free, hypoallergenic detergent. Look for "free and clear" labels or products designed for sensitive skin or babies. Avoid detergents with strong perfumes, dyes, or harsh chemicals. These can irritate your dog's skin or stomach. Never use fabric softener, as it can leave harmful residues.

### How often should I wash dog toys?

The frequency depends on how often your dog uses the toys and how dirty they get. A good general rule is to wash them at least once every two to three weeks. Highly used toys, or those used outdoors, might need washing weekly. If your dog has been sick, wash all toys immediately to disinfect them. For more details, refer to our guide on how often should you wash dog toys.

### Can I put dog toys in the dryer?

Many toys can go in the dryer, but use caution. Set the dryer to the lowest heat setting or "air fluff." High heat can shrink plush toys, melt plastic components, or damage delicate materials. It's often safer to air dry plush toys completely to preserve their shape and integrity. Hard plastic and rubber toys can be towel-dried or air-dried.

### How do I clean toys that cannot go in the washer?

For toys not suitable for machine washing, hand washing is a good option. Use warm, soapy water with a pet-safe soap and a brush to scrub them clean. For electronic toys, wipe the surface with a damp cloth and a mild disinfectant solution, being careful not to get water into the battery compartment. Air dry completely before use.

### What should I do if a toy is damaged in the wash?

If a toy becomes damaged during washing, like a torn seam or loose stuffing, it is best to repair it immediately or discard it. Loose stuffing or broken pieces can pose a choking hazard to your dog. If the toy is beyond repair, replace it with a new, safe toy.
