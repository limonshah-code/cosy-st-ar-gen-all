### The Federal Law Against Dog Meat Consumption

The United States has clear rules about dog meat. The Dog and Cat Meat Trade Prohibition Act became law in 2018. This act is part of the larger Farm Bill. It makes it unlawful to knowingly slaughter a dog or cat for human consumption. It also bans the sale, transport, or distribution of dog or cat meat for human consumption. This is a significant federal measure.

This law applies across all fifty states. It closes previous legal loopholes. Before this act, some states lacked specific bans. Now, commercial activities involving dog meat are universally prohibited. This law reflects a strong national stance. It aims to protect dogs and cats.

The act does include a very narrow exception. This exception applies only if the meat is part of a Native American religious ceremony. Even this exception is strictly limited. It does not permit general consumption or trade. The law ensures animals are protected. It sends a clear message about animal welfare.
