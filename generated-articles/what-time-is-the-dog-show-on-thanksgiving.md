### The Rich History Behind the Thanksgiving Tradition

The National Dog Show on Thanksgiving Day has a fascinating history. It adds depth to its holiday appeal. The show is officially known as the "National Dog Show Presented by Purina." It is hosted by the Kennel Club of Philadelphia. This event has a long and proud heritage. The first dog show organized by the Kennel Club of Philadelphia took place in 1879. This makes it one of the oldest benched dog shows in the country. A "benched" show means dogs are on display. Spectators can meet the dogs and their handlers. This tradition goes back a long way.

The specific Thanksgiving Day broadcast tradition began much later. NBC started airing the show in 2002. This was a new idea at the time. The network saw an opportunity to provide family-friendly programming after the popular Macy's Parade. It quickly became a holiday staple. The show offers a delightful contrast to the parade's grand floats and performances. It focuses on the elegance and athleticism of purebred dogs. This brings a different kind of joy to the holiday.

The show's historical roots tie into the larger world of dog breeding and showing. It highlights the dedication of breeders and handlers. They work hard to maintain breed standards and promote responsible dog ownership. The breeds shown are registered with the American Kennel Club (AKC). Each dog represents years of careful breeding. This ensures the unique traits of its breed continue. The show helps educate the public about the many different dog breeds. It also showcases their specific characteristics and purposes.

Many people find themselves drawn to the various dog breeds. They learn about their origins and unique qualities. The show brings the world of competitive dog shows into living rooms everywhere. ### What to Expect: Breeds, Judging, and Best in Show

When you tune into the National Dog Show on Thanksgiving, you will see a parade of stunning dogs. Each dog belongs to one of many recognized breeds. The show organizes dogs into seven distinct groups. Each group has dogs with similar characteristics or original purposes. These groups include:

*   **Hound Group:** Dogs originally bred for hunting by scent or sight. Examples are Beagles and Greyhounds.
*   **Sporting Group:** Dogs bred to assist hunters with game birds. This group includes Retrievers and Spaniels.
*   **Working Group:** Dogs bred for specific jobs like guarding, pulling, or rescue. Think of Great Danes and Boxers.
*   **Terrier Group:** Dogs originally bred to hunt vermin. This group includes energetic breeds like the Scottish Terrier.
*   **Toy Group:** Small companion dogs. Chihuahuas and Poodles fall into this category.
*   **Non-Sporting Group:** Diverse breeds not fitting into other categories. Bulldogs and Dalmatians are in this group.
*   **Herding Group:** Dogs bred to control the movement of other animals. German Shepherds and Collies are part of this group.

The judging process is precise. Each dog competes first within its breed. The best of each breed then moves on to compete within its group. One dog from each of the seven groups is selected as the "Group Winner." These seven group winners then compete for the ultimate title: "Best in Show."

Judges look for specific qualities. They assess each dog against its breed standard. This standard describes the ideal characteristics for that particular breed. These include appearance, temperament, and movement. A judge watches how the dog moves. They check its teeth, coat, and overall structure. The dog's handler plays a key role in presenting the dog well. The handler guides the dog. They show off its best qualities. They also make sure the dog is calm and confident. The competition is fierce but friendly. Every dog owner hopes their dog will be the one to earn the prestigious "Best in Show" ribbon. This is the moment everyone waits for. It is truly exciting to see which dog takes home the top honor. The suspense keeps viewers hooked until the very end.

### FAQs About the National Dog Show on Thanksgiving

#### 1. Is the National Dog Show broadcast live on Thanksgiving Day?
No, the National Dog Show is not broadcast live. It is pre-recorded a few weeks before Thanksgiving Day. The actual competition takes place in mid-November. This allows for post-production work. It also ensures a polished broadcast for viewers. While the results are known in advance, the excitement remains for viewers.

#### 2. How long does the National Dog Show broadcast last?
The National Dog Show broadcast typically lasts for two hours. It begins right after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade concludes. This two-hour window covers all the group judging. It also includes the highly anticipated Best in Show competition. It offers a good amount of entertainment for holiday viewers.

#### 3. Can I stream the National Dog Show online?
Yes, you can stream the National Dog Show online. NBC often provides a live stream of the event on their official website or through the NBC app. Many streaming services that carry NBC also offer the show. Check your specific streaming provider for availability. This makes watching flexible.

#### 4. Which dog breeds are typically featured in the show?
The National Dog Show features a wide range of purebred dogs. These are recognized by the American Kennel Club (AKC). They are categorized into seven groups: Hound, Sporting, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, and Herding. You will see popular breeds like Golden Retrievers and Poodles. You will also see rarer breeds.

#### 5. What are the main criteria judges use to pick the winner?
Judges evaluate dogs based on their adherence to the breed standard. This is a detailed description of the ideal dog for each breed. They look at structure, movement, temperament, and overall appearance. The dog that best exemplifies its breed's specific traits will move forward in the competition.

#### 6. Is there a way to watch past National Dog Shows?
Sometimes, past National Dog Show broadcasts are available for streaming. Check NBC's website or Peacock, NBC's streaming service. They may have previous years' shows available on demand. This allows you to relive memorable moments or catch up on past winners.
