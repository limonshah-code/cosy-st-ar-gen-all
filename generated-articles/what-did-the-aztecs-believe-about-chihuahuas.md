They believed a loyal dog would accompany the deceased on this arduous path. The dog would help the soul overcome obstacles. It would carry the soul across treacherous rivers. One of the most important deities associated with dogs was Xolotl. Xolotl was the god of lightning, death, and monstrous beings. He was also the twin brother of Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent god. Xolotl often took the form of a dog. Sometimes, he appeared as a human figure with a dog's head.

Xolotl’s role was complex. He guided the sun through the underworld at night. He helped the dead journey through Mictlan. Because of this, dogs, particularly the hairless Xoloitzcuintli and the Techichi, were sacred. They were seen as earthly manifestations of Xolotl's power. This connection meant dogs carried a divine spark. They were not mere animals but living links to the gods. This made their presence in Aztec society deeply revered.

The Aztecs also attributed other symbolic qualities to dogs. They viewed them as symbols of loyalty and protection. Dogs guarded homes and families. They also offered spiritual protection. ### Distinguishing Techichi from Xoloitzcuintli

Understanding the Aztec's dog beliefs benefits from knowing the specific types of dogs present. Two main ancient dog types existed: the Techichi and the Xoloitzcuintli. While related, they had distinct characteristics and likely played slightly different roles. The Techichi is considered the direct ancestor of the modern Chihuahua. The Xoloitzcuintli is a separate, ancient breed still existing today.

The Techichi was a small, long-haired dog. It resembled a modern Chihuahua in size and build. Its remains have been found in archaeological sites across Mexico. These finds date back thousands of years. The Techichi was likely the more common household dog. It served roles as a companion, guard, and sometimes food source. Its presence was widespread among various Mesoamerican cultures, including the Aztecs. This dog was central to the Aztec's belief in dogs guiding souls to the afterlife. Its compact size made it suitable for burial with human remains.

The Xoloitzcuintli, often called the "Xolo," is a hairless dog. It comes in three sizes: toy, miniature, and standard. The larger Xolos were more common in ancient times. Their striking appearance, with smooth skin and often a topknot of hair, made them distinct. The Xolo had a more specialized spiritual role. It was directly associated with the god Xolotl, the deity of lightning and the underworld. This made the Xolo particularly sacred. It was revered as a living embodiment of the divine. Ancient artwork frequently depicts the Xolo. This highlights its significant cultural and religious status.

While both dogs were important, their specific attributes influenced their perceived roles. The Techichi, being smaller and more abundant, might have been the everyday spiritual companion. The Xolo, with its unique hairless appearance, held a higher, more direct connection to the divine. Both breeds were important in Aztec cosmology.
