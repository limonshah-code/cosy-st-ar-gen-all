Bringing a new dog into your home is an exciting step. You might be dreaming of a tiny, loyal companion, and a full blooded Chihuahua perfectly fits that picture. Many people wonder about the exact cost when considering a purebred dog. Knowing the price of a full blooded Chihuahua helps you plan your budget. This guide will explore everything influencing the cost of these beloved small dogs. We will cover initial purchase prices, ongoing care expenses, and important factors to consider. Prepare to learn about the investment involved in welcoming a Chihuahua into your family.

*   **Purchase Price Varies:** A full blooded Chihuahua typically costs $500 to $2,500 from a reputable breeder.

*   **Beyond Purchase:** Account for significant ongoing costs like vet care, food, and supplies.

## Understanding "Full Blooded" in Chihuahuas: What It Means for Price

When we discuss a "full blooded" Chihuahua, we are simply referring to a purebred dog. This means both parents are registered Chihuahuas. They belong to the same breed and possess documented lineage. The term "full blooded" ensures the dog meets breed standards. This includes specific physical traits and temperament characteristics.

Proof of a full blooded Chihuahua often comes with pedigree papers. The American Kennel Club (AKC) is a primary registry in the United States. AKC papers certify a dog's purebred status. These papers trace the dog's family tree for several generations. Knowing a dog's purebred status provides predictability. It helps you understand what physical traits and temperament to expect. This certainty impacts the dog's price.

A dog without verified purebred status or papers is generally less expensive. This is true even if it looks like a Chihuahua. Breeders invest time and money in maintaining pure bloodlines. They also conduct health screenings on breeding dogs. This commitment to breed integrity justifies a higher price point. If you want to confirm a dog's purebred status, checking its lineage is vital. This is similar to how one might confirm the lineage of a Malamute to ensure it is full blooded. A true full blooded Chihuahua often comes from a background of responsible breeding. These breeders are concerned about the breed's future. They prioritize health and temperament in their puppies. This dedication often translates to a higher upfront cost for the puppy. However, it can save you money on potential health problems later.

The purebred designation also means the dog should fit the recognized Chihuahua standards. This includes size, head shape, and coat. Mixed breeds, while wonderful companions, do not carry this same predictability. Their price reflects this difference. ## Factors Influencing the Price of a Full Blooded Chihuahua Puppy

The price you pay for a full blooded Chihuahua puppy can vary greatly. Several key factors contribute to this difference. Understanding these elements helps you see why some puppies command higher prices than others. It also guides your decision-making process. I found that recognizing these factors helped me set realistic expectations.

### Breeder Reputation and Ethics

A reputable breeder invests heavily in their dogs. They perform health screenings on parent dogs. This helps prevent hereditary conditions. They provide excellent care for their puppies and mothers. This includes proper nutrition, early socialization, and clean environments. Such breeders offer health guarantees and contracts. They also provide ongoing support for new owners. These practices increase the overall cost of raising a litter. Therefore, puppies from reputable breeders are more expensive. They represent a safer and healthier investment.

### Puppy's Lineage and Pedigree

Puppies with a champion bloodline often carry a higher price tag. This means their parents or grandparents achieved titles in dog shows. A strong pedigree indicates excellent breed conformation and temperament. These puppies are often sought after for breeding programs themselves. Their genetic background adds significant value to their cost.

### Coat Color and Type

Chihuahuas come in many colors and coat types. Common colors like black, fawn, or chocolate are generally standard priced. Rare colors, such as blue, lavender, or merle, can significantly increase the cost. These colors are less common and more difficult to produce. Both long-coat and smooth-coat Chihuahuas exist. The coat type usually does not affect price as much as rare colors do.

### Size and "Type"

Chihuahuas are small dogs, but they come in different structural variations. The two main head types are "apple head" and "deer head." An apple head has a rounder skull and a shorter muzzle. A deer head has a flatter skull and a longer muzzle. Both are recognized as full blooded Chihuahuas. You can learn more about these distinctions by checking [what is the difference between a deer head Chihuahua and a regular Chihuahua](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-is-the-difference-between-a-deer-head-chihuahua-and-a-regular-chihuahua). Neither type is inherently more expensive. However, puppies marketed as "teacup" or "toy" often command higher prices. These terms are not official breed standards. They refer to unusually small Chihuahuas. Extreme smallness can lead to health challenges, making careful breeder selection even more important for these tiny dogs.

### Location and Demand

The price of a full blooded Chihuahua also varies by geographic region. Breeders in areas with high demand may charge more. Breeders in areas with fewer reputable options may also increase prices. Travel costs for picking up a puppy add to the total. This can include flight expenses if the breeder is far away. Economic factors in a specific region can also influence pricing.

## Where to Find a Full Blooded Chihuahua: Breeders vs. Rescues

Finding your perfect full blooded Chihuahua involves exploring different avenues. Each option has its own benefits and considerations. I remember researching these choices carefully for my own pet. Knowing where to look helps you make an informed decision. You want a healthy dog, regardless of where you find it.

### Reputable Breeders

Reputable breeders are the primary source for full blooded Chihuahuas with documented pedigrees. These breeders prioritize the health and temperament of their dogs. They perform genetic health testing on parent dogs. This helps ensure puppies are free from inherited conditions. A good breeder will welcome questions and provide references. They will also offer a health guarantee for their puppies. They follow ethical breeding practices, which means they do not overbreed their females. Expect to sign a contract with a reputable breeder. This contract often includes clauses about spaying or neutering the puppy. It also may state what happens if you cannot keep the dog. This option usually means a higher upfront cost. However, it often provides peace of mind regarding the puppy's health and background. I always recommend asking a lot of questions.

Red flags to watch for include breeders unwilling to show you the facility. Be wary of breeders who offer multiple breeds. Also, avoid those who do not ask you questions about your home environment. A good breeder cares where their puppies go. They want to ensure a good match. They often have waiting lists due to their dedication and quality.

## Initial Costs Beyond the Purchase Price: Setting Up Your Chihuahua Home

Bringing a new full blooded Chihuahua home involves more than just the purchase price. There are several essential initial costs to consider. These expenses ensure your new puppy has a healthy and comfortable start. I learned quickly that the initial setup adds up. Being prepared for these costs is important for responsible pet ownership.

### Veterinary Check-ups and Vaccinations

Your new Chihuahua puppy will need immediate veterinary attention. This includes a full health check-up shortly after arrival. The vet will assess their overall health. They will look for any potential issues. Puppies need a series of vaccinations to protect against common diseases. These include distemper, parvovirus, and rabies. They also need deworming treatments. The cost for these initial vet visits and treatments can range from $100 to $300 or more. This is a crucial investment in your puppy's long-term health.

### Spaying or Neutering

Most reputable breeders sell puppies with a spay/neuter contract. Rescue organizations always spay or neuter before adoption. If your puppy is not yet altered, this will be a significant initial cost. Spaying (for females) or neutering (for males) helps prevent unwanted litters. It also offers health benefits, such as reducing the risk of certain cancers. The cost for this procedure ranges from $150 to $500. This can vary based on your location and the vet's fees.

### Food and Bowls

High-quality food is vital for a growing Chihuahua puppy. They need small kibble designed for tiny breeds. This type of food provides proper nutrition for their size. Investing in good food supports their health and development. You will also need food and water bowls. Stainless steel or ceramic bowls are often recommended for hygiene. Expect to spend $30-$60 initially on quality food and bowls. The specific type of food you choose can impact this.

### Collar, Leash, and ID Tags

Your Chihuahua needs a comfortable collar or harness. A lightweight leash is also necessary for walks. An identification tag with your contact information is essential. This helps ensure your dog can return home if lost. These items are relatively inexpensive but crucial for safety. Expect to spend $20-$50 on these basic accessories.

### Crate and Bedding

A dog crate provides a safe space for your Chihuahua. It helps with house training and gives them a secure den. Choose a small crate appropriate for their size. Comfortable bedding inside the crate makes it inviting. A separate dog bed for other areas of the home offers another cozy spot. The cost for a small crate and bedding can be $50-$150. Many people find a crate invaluable for training.

### Toys and Grooming Supplies

Chihuahuas love to play. A variety of appropriate toys keeps them entertained and stimulated. Choose toys suitable for small breeds. These should be safe and durable. Grooming supplies include a small brush, nail clippers, and dog shampoo. Regular grooming helps maintain their coat and overall hygiene. These initial items might cost $30-$100. Investing in good grooming tools will benefit your dog's health and appearance. Considering all these initial expenses, the total cost to set up your home for a new Chihuahua can easily add up to several hundred dollars beyond the purchase price. ## Ongoing Expenses: The Long-Term Financial Commitment of a Chihuahua

Owning a full blooded Chihuahua is a long-term commitment. Their small size does not mean small expenses. Many ongoing costs ensure their health, happiness, and well-being throughout their life. I learned that planning for these recurring expenses is just as important as budgeting for the initial purchase. Chihuahuas can live for a long time, so these costs continue over many years.

### Annual Veterinary Care

Your Chihuahua needs annual check-ups. These visits include booster vaccinations and parasite prevention. The vet will monitor their weight and dental health. Regular check-ups help catch potential health issues early. This can save money in the long run. The cost for annual vet care typically ranges from $100 to $300. This amount can increase if your dog needs specific treatments or medications.

### Quality Dog Food

Chihuahuas require high-quality dog food formulated for small breeds. Their small mouths and sensitive stomachs benefit from appropriate kibble size and ingredients. Feeding them good food supports their energy levels and coat health. The monthly cost for quality food can be $20-$50. This depends on the brand and specific dietary needs. I found that a consistent, high-quality diet prevents many health issues.

### Grooming Supplies and Services

Chihuahuas, whether long-haired or smooth-coated, require regular grooming. This includes brushing, nail trims, and occasional baths. You can perform most grooming at home. This requires purchasing a small brush, nail clippers, and dog-specific shampoo. The cost of these supplies is minimal. If you prefer professional grooming services, this adds to your budget. Professional grooming for Chihuahuas might cost $30-$60 per session. This could be done every few months. Regular dental care is also crucial for Chihuahuas. Small breeds are prone to dental problems. Professional dental cleanings under anesthesia can cost $300-$800. This is often necessary every few years.

### Pet Insurance or Emergency Fund

Unexpected medical emergencies can be very expensive. Pet insurance helps cover these costs. Monthly premiums vary based on coverage and your dog's age. They typically range from $20-$60 per month for a Chihuahua. Alternatively, you can create an emergency savings fund specifically for your dog. This fund ensures you can afford sudden vet bills. Having this safety net provides immense peace of mind. Without it, a sudden illness could be a major financial strain.

### Toys, Treats, and Enrichment

Chihuahuas benefit from mental and physical stimulation. Toys keep them entertained and prevent boredom. Treats are useful for training and rewards. Other enrichment items like puzzle feeders contribute to their well-being. These items are relatively inexpensive. However, they add up over time. Budget $10-$30 per month for these necessities. These small expenses contribute greatly to your Chihuahua's quality of life.

### Miscellaneous Costs

Consider other miscellaneous costs. These include dog-sitting or boarding fees if you travel. Training classes can also be beneficial, especially for puppies. Replacement of worn-out leashes, beds, or bowls is also necessary over time. These variable expenses add to the overall financial commitment. Given that a Chihuahua can have a lifespan of 12 to 20 years, these ongoing costs represent a significant long-term investment. ## Understanding Chihuahua Types and Their Impact on Price (Deeper Dive)

When considering a full blooded Chihuahua, you often hear terms like "apple head" and "deer head." You might also encounter descriptions such as "teacup" or "toy." These terms describe variations within the Chihuahua breed. They sometimes influence the puppy's price. It is important to understand what these terms mean for purebred status and cost.

### Apple Head vs. Deer Head Chihuahuas

These terms refer to the shape of the Chihuahua's head. An **apple head Chihuahua** has a distinctive, rounded skull. It resembles an apple. This type features a short, pointed muzzle. They often have a prominent "molera" or soft spot on their skull. The AKC breed standard primarily recognizes the apple head. Many people associate this look with the classic Chihuahua appearance.

A **deer head Chihuahua** has a more sloped forehead. Their skull is less rounded. They possess a longer muzzle, similar to a young deer. This type has a less pronounced molera. Deer heads are also full blooded Chihuahuas. They are not a separate breed or mix. While they do not conform strictly to the AKC show standard, they are common. The price difference between apple head and deer head Chihuahuas is usually minimal. It often depends more on individual demand and breeder preference. Some breeders specialize in one type over the other. ### "Teacup" and "Toy" Chihuahuas

The terms "teacup" and "toy" are marketing labels. They describe Chihuahuas on the extreme small end of the breed's weight range. The AKC breed standard defines the Chihuahua weight between 2 and 6 pounds. There is no separate "teacup" or "toy" category recognized by breed clubs. These smaller dogs are just undersized Chihuahuas.

Puppies marketed as "teacup" or "toy" often carry a significantly higher price tag. This is because of their rarity and perceived cuteness. However, extreme smallness can come with health risks. These dogs are more fragile. They are prone to hypoglycemia, fragile bones, and other health issues. Responsible breeders typically do not aim to breed these extremely small Chihuahuas. They focus on healthy dogs within the standard weight range.

If a breeder emphasizes "teacup" size, proceed with caution. Ensure they are still conducting health screenings. Ask about the health of the parent dogs. Do not prioritize extreme smallness over health. While a "teacup" Chihuahua might be smaller, the true distinction between it and a "full-size" Chihuahua is simply weight, not a separate breed status.
