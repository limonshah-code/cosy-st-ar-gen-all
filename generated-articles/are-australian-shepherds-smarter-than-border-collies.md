## Understanding Dog Intelligence: What Does "Smart" Mean?

When we discuss if Australian Shepherds are smarter than Border Collies, we must first define "intelligence." Dog intelligence is not a single concept. It involves several components. Stanley Coren, a canine psychologist, identified three types of dog intelligence.

First, there is instinctive intelligence. This refers to what a dog is bred to do. A retriever naturally retrieves; a herding dog naturally herds. This intelligence is in their genes. It guides their natural behaviors.

Second, adaptive intelligence is important. This is a dog's ability to solve problems on its own. It involves learning from past experiences. A dog showing adaptive intelligence can figure out how to open a gate or find a hidden toy. This skill allows them to adjust to new situations. They use trial and error to succeed.

Third, working and obedience intelligence is widely recognized. This measures how well a dog learns from humans. It includes how quickly they understand commands. It also measures how consistently they obey them. This is the type of intelligence most people think of when they say a dog is "smart." Border Collies often excel in this area, ranking very high. Different intelligence types mean a "smarter" dog depends on the task.
