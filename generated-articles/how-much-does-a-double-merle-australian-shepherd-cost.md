## What is a Double Merle Australian Shepherd?

You might wonder what makes a "Double Merle" Aussie different. This unique genetic condition arises when two merle-patterned dogs breed together. Every dog carries two copies of each gene. A single merle dog has one copy of the merle gene (M) and one non-merle gene (m). A dog with the merle pattern has unique splotches of color on its coat. It also might have blue eyes.

When two merle dogs (Mm x Mm) mate, their offspring have a 25% chance of inheriting two copies of the merle gene (MM). This creates a Double Merle Australian Shepherd. These dogs are sometimes called "lethal whites" because of the severe health issues they face. They are not true albino dogs. They are dogs with too much white due to the extra merle gene. This extra gene affects pigment development. It also impacts the development of eyes and ears. I have learned that understanding this genetic basis is vital for responsible pet ownership. You can find more details about telling if your dog is a merle here: [How do you tell if your dog is a merle](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-do-you-tell-if-your-dog-is-a-merle).

The health consequences of being a double merle are usually serious. Vision problems are very common. Many double merles are blind or have microphthalmia. This means their eyes are abnormally small. They can also have colobomas, which are defects in the eye structure. Hearing issues are also prevalent. Many are deaf in one or both ears. These dogs need special care. Their challenges affect their overall well-being. Knowing this helps us understand the financial aspect of their care. You can learn more about how to identify a double merle by reading this article: [How can you tell if an Aussie is double merle](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-can-you-tell-if-an-aussie-is-double-merle).

## Understanding Health Issues and Their Financial Impact

The most significant financial impact of owning a Double Merle Australian Shepherd comes from potential health issues. These dogs are prone to a range of severe genetic conditions. These conditions demand consistent and often expensive veterinary care. I have found that preparing for these costs is essential. You need to provide a good quality of life for your dog.

The most common health problems relate to their vision and hearing. Many double merles are partially or completely blind. Others have microphthalmia, which means small, underdeveloped eyes. Some may have colobomas. This is a hole in part of the eye. These conditions often require regular visits to a veterinary ophthalmologist. These specialists charge more than general vets. Medications to manage eye comfort or prevent secondary issues are also common. Surgery is sometimes needed, but it can be very costly. A single eye surgery can cost thousands of dollars. You can read more about these specific issues here: [What are the side effects of a double merle](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-are-the-side-effects-of-a-double-merle).

Hearing impairment is another major concern. Many double merles are deaf in one or both ears. Diagnosing deafness requires a special test called a BAER (Brainstem Auditory Evoked Response) test. This test can cost hundreds of dollars. While deafness itself does not require ongoing medical treatment, it requires specialized training and safety measures. For example, you might need to install fences or use vibration collars. You might also need to attend special classes to learn sign commands. These things add to your overall financial outlay. For more detailed information, consider reading [What are the health issues with double merle dogs](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-are-the-health-issues-with-double-merle-dogs).

Beyond vision and hearing, double merles can also have other health problems. These include heart defects, skeletal abnormalities, and reproductive issues. Diagnosing these conditions involves more tests. These tests can be X-rays, ultrasounds, or blood work. Treatments can range from daily medication to surgeries. All of these medical interventions add up. Pet insurance can help offset some of these costs. However, many policies have exclusions for pre-existing conditions. Always check the policy carefully. The potential for these health expenses is a major factor in the true **cost of a Double Merle Australian Shepherd**.

## Ongoing Care Expenses for a Double Merle Australian Shepherd

Beyond the initial purchase and potential major medical bills, owning a Double Merle Australian Shepherd involves regular ongoing care expenses. These costs are important to budget for. They ensure your dog lives a comfortable and happy life. These costs apply to any dog. However, some might be higher for a double merle due to their special needs.

First, food is a significant ongoing expense. High-quality dog food is essential for good health. The type and brand of food affect the cost. Larger dogs, like Australian Shepherds, eat more than small dogs. Monthly food costs can range from $40 to $80 or more. This depends on the specific brand and your dog's size. I always recommend choosing food appropriate for their age and activity level. Some double merles might need special diets if they have certain health conditions. This can increase food costs further.

Grooming is another regular expense. Australian Shepherds have dense coats that shed. They need regular brushing to prevent mats. Professional grooming might be needed every few months. This can cost $50 to $100 per session. Nail trims and ear cleaning are also important. You can learn to do these at home. Or you can pay a groomer or vet technician to do them. This is a part of routine care for any Aussie.

Training and socialization are critical, especially for a double merle. If your dog has vision or hearing impairments, they will need specialized training methods. For a deaf dog, you might use hand signals. For a blind dog, you might use verbal cues and scent work. Basic obedience classes can cost $100 to $300. Specialized trainers for dogs with disabilities might charge more. Investing in training helps your dog feel secure. It also helps them navigate their world safely. Safety equipment like reflective collars or harnesses can also be necessary. This helps a blind or deaf dog.

Lastly, routine veterinary care is crucial. This includes annual check-ups, vaccinations, and parasite prevention. Heartworm medication and flea/tick prevention are monthly costs. These can range from $20 to $50 per month. Even healthy dogs need these basic protections. For a double merle, you might also factor in more frequent general vet visits. This is because they have higher health risks. These ongoing expenses contribute significantly to the true **cost of a Double Merle Australian Shepherd** over their lifetime.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are double merle Aussies expensive to buy?
Double Merle Australian Shepherds are not typically expensive to buy from ethical sources. Reputable breeders usually sell them at a very low price or even give them away. They do this because of the potential health issues. Backyard breeders might try to sell them for more, but this is unethical. Adoption from a rescue costs $150-$500, which includes initial vet care.

### What health problems do double merles have?
Double merles commonly have severe vision and hearing impairments. They can be blind or deaf in one or both ears. Other issues include microphthalmia (small eyes) and colobomas (eye defects). They can also have skeletal abnormalities, heart defects, or reproductive problems. These issues require ongoing medical attention.

### Can you breed two merles?
No, breeding two merle dogs (merle to merle) is highly irresponsible and unethical. This pairing carries a 25% risk of producing double merle puppies. These puppies are prone to severe health issues like blindness and deafness. Responsible breeders always avoid merle-to-merle breedings to protect the health of future generations.

### Is it cruel to breed double merles?
Yes, intentionally breeding two merle dogs, which results in double merle puppies, is considered cruel. It knowingly produces dogs with a high likelihood of suffering from serious and lifelong health problems. Ethical breeders prioritize animal welfare. They ensure their breeding practices do not create dogs with preventable genetic defects.

### Do double merle Aussies have a shorter lifespan?
The lifespan of a double merle Australian Shepherd is not necessarily shorter than a typical Aussie. Their primary challenges are sensory impairments like blindness and deafness. These conditions do not directly shorten their life expectancy. However, their quality of life depends heavily on dedicated care and management of their specific health issues.
