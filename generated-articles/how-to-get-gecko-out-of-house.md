## Safely Get a Gecko Out of Your House

Seeing a gecko scurry across your wall can be a surprise. While these small reptiles are often harmless and even eat insects, many people prefer them outside the house. You might worry about their presence or simply want your home to be gecko-free. Fortunately, you can safely get a gecko out of your house without harming it. This article will guide you through humane removal methods, prevention strategies, and how to maintain a comfortable, gecko-free living space. I will share practical steps to help you gently encourage these creatures to leave.

### Takeaway:
*   Use gentle capture tools like a cup and cardboard.
*   Lure hidden geckos out with light or food.
*   Seal cracks and openings to block entry points.
*   Reduce insect populations to eliminate food sources.
*   Keep your home clean and dry to deter geckos.

**How to get a gecko out of your house involves a calm, gentle approach. Guide the gecko into a container, such as a cup, and then slide a piece of paper or cardboard underneath it. Carefully carry the container outside and release the gecko in a safe, secluded spot away from your home.**

## Understanding Your House Guest: Why Geckos Enter

Geckos sometimes find their way into homes. They seek basic needs like food, water, and shelter. Your house offers warmth and protection from predators. It also provides a ready supply of insects, which are their primary diet. Common entry points include open doors, windows, and small cracks. Understanding their motivation helps you address the problem effectively.

Geckos are typically drawn to light sources at night. These lights attract insects, creating a buffet for the geckos. This is why you often see them near outdoor lights or windows. They are also attracted to damp areas for water. Leaky pipes or humid rooms can be appealing. Knowing these patterns helps you predict where you might find them. It also informs your prevention efforts. You can modify your home to be less inviting.

Many geckos found in homes are common house geckos. They are small, nocturnal lizards. They have special pads on their feet. These pads allow them to climb walls and ceilings easily. They are not harmful to humans. They do not carry diseases like rodents or cockroaches. They are more of a nuisance or a surprise. Still, most people prefer them outside. My goal is to help you move them safely.

## Gentle Capture Methods for Gecko Removal

Catching a gecko can seem tricky because they are fast. The best way to get a gecko out of your house is to use a gentle method. You want to avoid hurting the gecko or making a mess. A simple cup and a piece of cardboard or thick paper work very well. This method is effective and humane. It allows you to contain the gecko without direct contact.

First, approach the gecko slowly and calmly. Sudden movements might scare it, causing it to run and hide. Place a clear plastic cup or glass over the gecko. Make sure the rim of the cup is flat against the surface. This traps the gecko inside the cup. Next, slide a piece of sturdy paper or thin cardboard under the cup's opening. This creates a bottom for your temporary container. The gecko is now safely enclosed.

Once the gecko is inside the cup, keep the cardboard firmly pressed against the rim. Carry the cup outside to a safe spot. Release the gecko far from your house. Choose an area with some vegetation or cover. This gives the gecko a place to hide and feel secure. This method is straightforward and effective. ## Luring a Hiding Gecko Out Safely

Sometimes, geckos hide in hard-to-reach places. They might dart behind furniture or into cracks. If you cannot directly capture a gecko, you can try to lure it out. Patience is key when a gecko is hiding. You want to encourage it to move to an accessible spot. This makes its removal much easier.

One effective way to lure a gecko is with light. Geckos are attracted to light because it draws insects. Place a lamp near the hiding spot. Make sure the light is the only source of light in the room. Turn off other lights to make the lamp more appealing. The gecko might come out to hunt the insects drawn to the lamp. You can then use the cup-and-cardboard method. This works well for nocturnal geckos.

You can also use a small piece of fruit or a live insect as bait. Geckos eat various insects, but some might also snack on fruit. Place a tiny piece of overripe fruit or a cricket near the hiding spot. The gecko might come out to investigate the food source. You must be ready to act quickly once it appears. ## Humane Traps and Repellents to Deter Geckos

Sometimes, direct capture is not possible or desired. You might consider humane traps or repellents. These options can help you get geckos out of your house. They also work to discourage future visits. It is important to choose methods that do not harm the geckos. Sticky traps are not recommended. They cause injury and stress to the gecko.

One humane trap option is a sticky trap designed specifically for reptiles, with a release agent. However, these are still risky. A better choice is a DIY humane trap. You can use a cardboard box with a small hole. Place a light inside the box. Add a small dish of water or a tiny piece of fruit. The gecko might enter to investigate. Once inside, you can carefully close the box and release the gecko outside.

Natural repellents can also be effective. Geckos dislike strong scents. You can use mothballs, but these are toxic and not safe around pets or children. Better options include coffee and tobacco mixtures. Place these mixtures in small pouches near entry points. Garlic cloves or chili powder can also deter them. These strong smells make your home less appealing to geckos. Sprinkling eggshells around entry points is another old trick. Geckos might mistake them for predator eggs and stay away.

## Sealing Entry Points to Prevent Future Gecko Visits

Preventing geckos from entering is the most effective long-term solution. Once you get a gecko out of your house, you want to keep others from coming in. Geckos are small and can squeeze through tiny openings. Inspect your home thoroughly to find these entry points. Sealing them is a critical step in gecko control. This stops them at the source.

Start by checking all windows and doors. Ensure screens are in good condition. Repair any tears or holes in window screens. Install sweeps under doors to close gaps. Weatherstripping around door and window frames also helps. Even small gaps can be an invitation for a gecko. Make sure these seals are tight.

Next, examine your home's foundation and exterior walls. Look for cracks or holes. Use caulk or weather-resistant sealant to fill these gaps. Pay attention to utility pipe entry points. Wires or pipes often have small openings around them. Cover any open vents with fine mesh screens. These steps create a physical barrier. This barrier makes it very difficult for geckos to enter your living space. This proactive approach saves you time and effort in the long run.

## Maintaining a Gecko-Free Home Environment

Keeping your home less appealing to geckos is crucial for long-term prevention. This involves reducing their food sources and habitat within your house. Geckos come inside looking for food, water, and shelter. By eliminating these, you make your home an undesirable place for them. This is an important step after you get a gecko out of your house.

Focus on insect control first. Geckos primarily eat insects like spiders, cockroaches, and mosquitoes. Reduce the insect population inside your home. Clean up food spills promptly. Store food in sealed containers. Take out trash regularly. Fix leaky faucets and pipes to eliminate water sources. These actions reduce the insects that attract geckos. You can also use sticky traps for insects. This lessens the geckos' food supply.

Maintain a tidy and dry environment. Geckos like clutter and damp places for hiding. Reduce clutter in storage areas, basements, and garages. Keep these areas dry and well-ventilated. Use dehumidifiers if necessary. Trim vegetation close to your house. This removes hiding spots near entry points. Outdoor lights attract insects. Turn off unnecessary outdoor lights at night. Or, use yellow bug lights. These attract fewer insects. By managing your home environment, you deter geckos naturally. ## FAQ Section

### Are geckos in the house dangerous?
No, common house geckos are generally not dangerous to humans. They are non-venomous and do not bite unless severely provoked. They can even be beneficial, as they eat insects like mosquitoes and cockroaches. Their presence is usually more of a nuisance than a threat.

### How do I catch a fast gecko?
To catch a fast gecko, approach it slowly and calmly with a clear cup. Quickly place the cup over the gecko, trapping it against the surface. Then, gently slide a piece of cardboard or stiff paper underneath the cup to create a base. This technique minimizes the gecko's escape opportunities.

### What attracts geckos to a house?
Geckos are attracted to houses primarily for food (insects like moths, spiders, and roaches), water, and shelter from predators or harsh weather. Poorly sealed windows and doors, cracks in foundations, and outdoor lights attracting insects can draw them inside.

### Can geckos climb smooth walls?
Yes, geckos can climb smooth walls and even ceilings. They have specialized foot pads covered with millions of tiny hair-like structures called setae. These setae create strong Van der Waals forces with surfaces, allowing geckos to adhere to almost any material, even glass.

### What smells do geckos hate?
Geckos are known to dislike strong odors. Some natural deterrents include coffee grounds, tobacco, mothballs (use with caution due to toxicity), garlic cloves, and chili powder. Placing these items near entry points or in areas where geckos are seen can help repel them.

### How do I prevent geckos from coming back?
To prevent geckos from returning, seal all cracks and openings around windows, doors, and foundations. Reduce insect populations in your home, as they are a primary food source. Maintain a clean, clutter-free, and dry environment. Turn off unnecessary outdoor lights at night to avoid attracting insects.
