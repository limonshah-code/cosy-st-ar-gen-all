## How Much To Feed Your Leopard Gecko: A Complete Feeding Guide

Feeding your leopard gecko correctly ensures a long, healthy life for your scaly friend. I know many new gecko owners often wonder, "how much to feed a leopard gecko?" This question is crucial for their well-being. Proper nutrition directly impacts their growth, energy levels, and overall health. Overfeeding can cause obesity, while underfeeding leads to malnutrition.

In this guide, I will share comprehensive details on feeding amounts for leopard geckos of all ages. We will cover different feeder insects, essential supplements, and how to spot signs of good health. You will learn to create a feeding plan tailored to your gecko's specific needs. Let us make sure your leopard gecko thrives.

### Takeaway

*   **Age Matters:** Hatchlings and juveniles need more frequent feeding than adults.
*   **Portion Control:** Offer 2-4 appropriately sized insects per feeding, adjusting based on appetite.
*   **Insect Variety:** Provide different feeder insects to ensure balanced nutrition.
*   **Supplement Properly:** Dust feeders with calcium and multivitamin supplements regularly.
*   **Monitor Health:** Watch your gecko's weight and activity to adjust feeding as needed.

### Quick Answer: How Much To Feed A Leopard Gecko

Leopard geckos need 2-4 appropriately sized feeder insects per meal. The exact amount depends on their age, size, and activity level. Hatchlings eat daily, juveniles every other day, and adults 2-3 times a week. Always offer insects no larger than the space between their eyes.

## Understanding Your Leopard Gecko's Dietary Needs

Leopard geckos are insectivores. This means they eat insects as their main food source. Their natural diet in the wild includes various arthropods. In captivity, we must replicate this diet as closely as possible. Proper nutrition is key to their long-term health.

A balanced diet prevents many common health problems. Calcium deficiency, for example, can lead to metabolic bone disease. This is a serious condition that weakens their bones. Providing the right amount of food and supplements helps prevent such issues. Understanding their natural eating habits helps us provide the best care.

Leopard geckos have specific nutritional requirements. They need protein for growth and energy. They also need fats, vitamins, and minerals. Feeder insects alone may not provide all these nutrients. That is why dusting feeders with supplements is important. We must make sure they get everything they need to stay healthy and active. This guide helps you achieve that.

## Feeding Guidelines for Leopard Gecko Hatchlings and Juveniles

Hatchlings and juvenile leopard geckos grow very fast. They need more food and more frequent meals than adults. Their bodies are developing rapidly. This rapid growth requires a constant supply of energy and nutrients.

For hatchlings, which are typically under 3-4 months old, feeding them daily is important. They are small and have high metabolisms. Offer them small insects, such as 1/4 inch crickets or mealworms. The quantity should be about 2-3 insects per feeding. Watch their appetite; some may eat more, some less.

Juvenile geckos are usually between 4 months and 1 year old. You should feed these geckos every other day. They still grow, but at a slightly slower pace than hatchlings. The size of feeder insects can increase to 1/2 inch or 3/4 inch. Provide them with 3-4 insects per feeding. Always ensure the insects are not larger than the space between their eyes. This prevents choking and impaction.

Growth rates vary among individuals. Some geckos grow faster than others. Always observe your gecko's body condition. If they look too thin, offer a little more food. If they look too plump, reduce the amount slightly. Consistency in feeding times also helps establish a routine. Consider reading our guide on [how often should you feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-often-should-you-feed-a-leopard-gecko) for more details on feeding frequency. Remember, their small stomachs fill quickly. Small, frequent meals are best for young geckos.

## Feeding Adult Leopard Geckos: Quantity and Frequency

Adult leopard geckos are generally over one year old. Their growth slows down significantly once they reach maturity. This means their dietary needs change. They do not need as much food as often as juveniles. Overfeeding adult geckos can lead to obesity. Obesity causes many health problems for geckos.

For most adult leopard geckos, feeding them 2-3 times per week is appropriate. Some less active geckos may do well with just two feedings a week. Others, more active ones, might need three. Observe your gecko's body condition to find the right balance. A healthy adult gecko has a plump tail, which stores fat. Their body should be firm but not overly round.

The quantity per feeding for an adult gecko is typically 4-6 appropriately sized feeder insects. Insects should not be larger than the gecko's head. Common feeder insects include crickets, mealworms, and Dubia roaches. Varying the types of insects you offer is important. This provides a wider range of nutrients. For example, if you feed crickets on one day, try mealworms or Dubia roaches on the next feeding day.

Consider the activity level of your adult gecko. A very active gecko may burn more calories and need a little more food. A less active gecko will need less. Adjust the feeding amount and frequency slowly. Sudden changes can upset their digestive system. Monitoring their weight helps ensure they receive proper nutrition. Consistent care helps them maintain a healthy weight and stay vibrant.

## Ideal Feeder Insects: Quantity Breakdown

Choosing the right feeder insects is vital for your leopard gecko's health. Different insects offer different nutritional profiles. Providing a variety ensures your gecko gets all necessary vitamins and minerals. Here is a breakdown of common feeder insects and how much to offer.

### Crickets

Crickets are a common staple food for leopard geckos. They are active and stimulate a gecko's hunting instinct. For adult geckos, offer 4-6 crickets per feeding. For juveniles, 3-4 small crickets are sufficient. Always gut-load crickets before feeding them to your gecko. Gut-loading means feeding the crickets nutritious foods like fruits, vegetables, and commercial gut-load formulas. This passes the nutrients to your gecko. You can find more specific advice on [how many crickets to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-crickets-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) in our detailed guide.

### Mealworms

Mealworms are easy to keep and readily available. They are a good source of fat and protein. Some geckos love mealworms, while others prefer more active prey. For an adult, 5-7 mealworms per feeding is a good amount. For juveniles, 3-5 small mealworms work well. Mealworms have a harder exoskeleton than crickets. This can be harder for some geckos to digest. Do not feed too many mealworms at once. Ensure they are fresh and well-fed themselves. You can also explore if [can you feed a leopard gecko only mealworms](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-i-feed-my-leopard-gecko-only-mealworms) for more specific insights.

### Dubia Roaches

Dubia roaches are an excellent feeder insect choice. They are high in protein, low in fat, and easy to digest. They also live longer than crickets and do not smell. For adult geckos, 3-5 Dubia roaches per feeding are usually enough. For juveniles, 2-3 smaller ones work well. Ensure you get appropriately sized roaches for your gecko. Learn more about [how many Dubia roaches to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-dubia-roaches-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) in our dedicated article.

### Superworms

Superworms are larger than mealworms and have more fat content. They are a good treat or occasional feeder. Do not use them as a staple food due to their higher fat. For adults, 2-3 superworms per feeding, once or twice a week, is plenty. Juveniles should get smaller ones or none. Never feed superworms larger than your gecko's head. They have strong jaws and can bite if not eaten quickly. Our guide on [how many superworms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-superworms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) offers more detailed advice.

### Waxworms

Waxworms are very high in fat and should only be offered as an occasional treat. They are like "junk food" for geckos. Give them sparingly to promote healthy eating habits. For an adult, 1-2 waxworms once or twice a month is sufficient. They are useful for sick or underweight geckos to gain weight quickly, but not for regular feeding. Read our article on [how many wax worms to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-wax-worms-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko) to understand their specific use. Variety is key to providing all essential nutrients.

## Supplementation: Calcium and Multivitamins

Feeder insects alone cannot provide all the nutrients a leopard gecko needs. Calcium and vitamin D3 are particularly important. Without these, geckos can develop serious health issues. Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD) is a common and severe problem caused by calcium deficiency. This disease weakens bones, causes deformities, and can be fatal. Proper supplementation is a vital part of leopard gecko care.

You need two main types of supplements:

*   **Calcium with D3:** This powder ensures strong bones and proper metabolic function. Calcium without D3 is also important. D3 helps the gecko absorb calcium.
*   **Multivitamin powder:** This provides a range of essential vitamins and minerals. It fills any nutritional gaps from the feeder insects.

Here is a common supplementation schedule:

*   **Calcium with D3:** Dust feeder insects with calcium with D3 powder at most feedings. For hatchlings and juveniles, every feeding is a good rule. For adults, 2-3 times a week, or at every other feeding.
*   **Calcium without D3:** Keep a small dish of pure calcium powder without D3 in the gecko's enclosure at all times. Geckos can lick this as needed.
*   **Multivitamin:** Dust feeder insects with a multivitamin powder once or twice a week. Do not overuse multivitamins, as too many fat-soluble vitamins can be harmful.

To dust insects, place them in a small bag or container with a pinch of supplement powder. Gently shake the container until the insects are coated. Then, offer them to your gecko immediately. Always ensure your gecko has access to clean, fresh water. Water bowls also help them get hydration. Correct supplementation completes their diet and keeps them healthy.

## Monitoring Your Leopard Gecko's Weight and Health

Observing your leopard gecko's body condition is essential. It helps you adjust their feeding plan as needed. A healthy gecko will have a plump tail base. Their tail is where they store fat reserves. A thin tail indicates insufficient food or an underlying health issue. An overly large tail might suggest obesity.

Regularly weighing your gecko is a good practice. Use a small digital scale. Weigh them once a month or every few weeks. Keep a record of their weight. This helps you track their growth and detect any changes early. Significant weight loss or gain warrants a closer look at their diet and overall health.

Look at their body shape. Their body should be well-rounded but not excessively fat. The leg bones should not be prominent, nor should they be hidden under too much fat. Their eyes should be clear and alert. Their skin should be smooth and free of wrinkles. Shedding should occur regularly, and the skin should come off in one piece.

Changes in appetite or activity can also signal a problem. A sudden decrease in eating or becoming unusually lethargic may mean something is wrong. If your gecko seems unwell, consult a reptile veterinarian. Adjusting food quantity slowly is best. If your gecko is active and maintains a healthy weight, your feeding plan is likely correct. This observation helps you refine how much to feed a leopard gecko.

## When Your Leopard Gecko Refuses to Eat

It can be concerning when your leopard gecko stops eating. Several factors can cause a loss of appetite. Understanding these reasons helps you address the issue effectively. It is not always a sign of serious illness.

One common reason is stress. A new environment, changes in tank setup, or too much handling can stress a gecko. They might need time to adjust before eating normally again. Ensure their habitat is stable and secure. Another reason is temperature. Leopard geckos need specific temperatures to digest their food. If the tank is too cold, their metabolism slows down, and they will not want to eat. Make sure your heat source works correctly and temperatures are within the ideal range.

Shedding is another frequent cause of appetite loss. Geckos often stop eating a few days before and during a shed. They usually resume eating once the shed is complete. This is normal behavior. Impaction is a serious issue that can cause appetite loss. This happens when a gecko eats substrate or an insect too large to pass through their digestive system. Ensure your substrate is safe and insects are appropriately sized.

Parasites or other illnesses can also cause a gecko to refuse food. If your gecko shows other signs of illness, like lethargy, unusual stool, or swelling, contact a reptile veterinarian. Sometimes, geckos can be picky eaters. They might get bored with one type of feeder insect. Try offering a variety of insects to stimulate their appetite. ## FAQs About Feeding Leopard Geckos

### How often should I feed my leopard gecko?

Hatchlings and very young juveniles need food daily. Juveniles (4-12 months old) should eat every other day. Adult leopard geckos (over 1 year old) generally need feeding 2-3 times per week. Adjust frequency based on their age, activity level, and body condition. Always observe your gecko for cues. Our article [how often should you feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-often-should-you-feed-a-leopard-gecko) offers more in-depth advice on this.

### Can I feed my leopard gecko only mealworms?

Feeding a leopard gecko only mealworms is not ideal. Mealworms can be part of their diet, but they lack some essential nutrients found in other insects. A varied diet of crickets, Dubia roaches, and occasional mealworms provides balanced nutrition. Relying solely on one feeder type can lead to deficiencies over time. For more on this, see [can I feed my leopard gecko only mealworms](https://cosyhomecreation.com/can-i-feed-my-leopard-gecko-only-mealworms).

### What time of day should I feed my leopard gecko?

Leopard geckos are crepuscular. This means they are most active at dawn and dusk. The best time to feed your leopard gecko is usually in the late afternoon or early evening. This aligns with their natural active hours. Feeding them during this time encourages their hunting instincts. This also allows time for digestion before they become fully inactive during the night.

### How many crickets should I feed my leopard gecko?

For adult leopard geckos, offer 4-6 appropriately sized crickets per feeding. For juveniles, 3-4 smaller crickets are a good amount. Hatchlings typically eat 2-3 very small crickets daily. Always ensure the crickets are no bigger than the space between your gecko's eyes. This prevents choking and ensures safe digestion. Read more on [how many crickets to feed a leopard gecko](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-many-crickets-to-feed-a-leopard-gecko).

### What if my leopard gecko is not eating?

If your leopard gecko is not eating, check for common causes like shedding, stress from a new environment, or incorrect tank temperatures. Ensure the habitat is warm enough for digestion. Rule out impaction by checking insect size and substrate. If these checks do not resolve the issue, or if your gecko shows other signs of illness, consult a reptile veterinarian. For detailed steps, refer to [what to do if leopard gecko is not eating](https://cosyhomecreation.com/what-to-do-if-leopard-gecko-is-not-eating).

### How many superworms should I feed my leopard gecko?

Superworms are high in fat and should be offered as an occasional treat, not a staple. For an adult leopard gecko, 2-3 superworms, once or twice a week at most, is sufficient. Avoid feeding superworms to very young geckos. Always ensure the superworm is not too large for your gecko. Overfeeding can lead to obesity and digestive issues.
