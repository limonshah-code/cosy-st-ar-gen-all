## Understanding the Competition and Judging Process

To understand why some breeds have not won, we need to look at how Westminster works. The competition starts with individual breed judging. Each dog is evaluated against its breed standard. This standard describes the ideal characteristics of that breed. It covers everything from coat texture to tail set. The best dog in each breed wins "Best of Breed."

Next, all the Best of Breed winners move on to their respective groups. There are seven groups: Hound, Terrier, Working, Sporting, Non-Sporting, Toy, and Herding. Judges select one winner from each group. These seven group winners then compete for Best in Show. The Best in Show judge looks for the dog that best represents its breed standard and has the overall "type" and soundness.

The judging is subjective, but it follows strict rules. Judges are experts in canine anatomy and breed standards. They look for balanced movement, correct structure, and a confident presence. A dog must perform perfectly under pressure. The judge's personal preference for a certain "look" can sometimes play a small role. However, the core focus remains on how well a dog embodies its breed's ideal form. This complex system ensures only the most outstanding dogs advance.
