## Understanding What "Points" Are and Their Importance

You might wonder, "What exactly are points?" In many older vehicles, specifically those with traditional ignition systems, "points" refer to the distributor contact points. These are essential parts of your car's engine. They control the timing of the electrical current. This current then goes to the spark plugs. The spark plugs ignite the fuel mixture.

These contact points open and close thousands of times per minute. They do this as your engine runs. This action sends a signal to the ignition coil. The coil then creates a high-voltage spark. This spark travels to the correct spark plug. Proper timing is key for your engine to run well. Dirty or worn points can cause big problems.

Clean points ensure a strong, timely spark. This spark gives you efficient fuel combustion. This means your engine uses less fuel. It also runs with more power. Neglecting these points leads to a weak spark. A weak spark causes misfires and poor engine performance. This affects your driving experience. Taking time to clean points helps keep your classic car reliable.

Over time, these contact points can get dirty. Carbon builds up on their surfaces. Oxidation also forms due to electrical arcing. This buildup acts as an insulator. It prevents a good electrical connection. When the connection is poor, the spark becomes weak. This can make your car hard to start. It might also cause rough idling or power loss. Regular cleaning prevents these issues. This simple task maintains your ignition system.

## Gathering Your Tools: What You Need to Clean Points

Before you start, you need the right tools. Having everything ready makes the job easier. It also makes it safer. You probably have some of these tools already. Others are specialized for this task. Gather them all in one place.

First, you will need basic hand tools. A screwdriver set is often necessary. You might need both flathead and Phillips types. A wrench set can also be helpful. These are for removing parts to access the distributor. Make sure they are the right size for your car. Proper tools prevent damage to screws and bolts.

Next, you need specific items for cleaning points. A **point file** is essential. This is a small, fine-grained file. It is designed to clean the contact surfaces. Do not use sandpaper or emery cloth. These materials can leave abrasive particles. Such particles can damage the points. A point file cleans the surface without leaving residue. You might also want to read how to clean points on a distributor for more tool specifics.

You also need an **electrical contact cleaner**. This spray cleans and removes oil and grease. It evaporates quickly. It leaves no residue. This ensures a clean electrical path. Do not use general household cleaners. They can leave moisture or residue. This can cause more problems later. Isopropyl alcohol can be a decent substitute if contact cleaner is not available.

Finally, a **feeler gauge** is a must-have tool. This tool measures small gaps. You need it to set the point gap correctly. A precise gap ensures proper ignition timing. You will also need a **timing light** if you plan to reset the ignition timing. This tool helps confirm the timing is correct after the points are cleaned and gapped.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Safely Accessing Your Distributor Points

Accessing the points needs careful work. Safety is always first. You are working with electrical components. You are also near moving engine parts. Take your time with these steps.

Start by ensuring your car is off. The engine must be cool. Disconnect the car's battery. Remove the negative terminal first. This prevents accidental sparks. It also protects you from electrical shock. Use a wrench for this step. Store the cable away from the battery post. This ensures it stays disconnected.

Locate the distributor. It is usually a cylindrical component. It sits on top or side of your engine. The spark plug wires connect to its cap. These wires lead from the distributor cap to each spark plug. The distributor cap is often held by clips or screws. Release these clips or unscrew the screws. Then, gently lift off the distributor cap.

Once the cap is off, you will see the rotor. The rotor spins inside the distributor. It directs spark to the correct wire. Gently pull the rotor straight up. It should slide off its shaft. If it's stuck, wiggle it carefully. Do not twist it forcefully. Twisting can damage the rotor or shaft. Set the rotor aside in a clean, safe place.

Now you can see the contact points. They are small metal components. They sit on a plate inside the distributor. They usually have a spring arm and a fixed arm. You will see a small lever or cam that opens and closes them. The points might look discolored or pitted. This is normal buildup. This is what you need to clean. Sometimes it helps to turn the engine manually. You can do this to get the points to their most open position. This gives you better access for cleaning. You can often turn the crankshaft by hand or "bump" the starter for a second. ## The Cleaning Process: Detailed Steps for Effective Point Maintenance

Now comes the actual cleaning. This part requires a steady hand. It needs attention to detail. My goal is to make these points shine. Clean points give a strong spark.

First, ensure the points are open. You might need to rotate the engine slightly. This puts the rubbing block on a high spot of the distributor cam. This gives you access to the contact surfaces. You can often turn the engine by hand. Use a wrench on the crankshaft pulley bolt. Or, you can have someone briefly "bump" the starter. Just do it for a second. This helps position the points.

Take your specialized point file. Insert the file between the contact points. Gently rub the file back and forth. Apply light pressure. The goal is to remove the carbon buildup. You want to remove any oxidation or pitting. Focus on both contact surfaces. Work until the surfaces appear clean and smooth. Do not file too aggressively. You only need to remove the surface layer. Excessive filing can reduce the life of the points.

After filing, use your electrical contact cleaner. Spray a small amount onto a lint-free cloth. Then, wipe the contact surfaces. You can also spray directly into the points. Let the cleaner evaporate fully. This removes any debris from filing. It also cleans any oil or grease. Ensure no residue remains. The surfaces should be perfectly dry before moving on. Once clean, visually inspect the points. They should look shiny and free of pits. The surfaces should meet squarely. If they look badly worn or pitted, cleaning may not be enough. In such cases, replacement is the better option. However, for minor buildup, cleaning restores function. This step is crucial for efficient spark delivery. Good cleaning extends the life of your points.

*   **Key Cleaning Steps:**
    1.  Position points open using the distributor cam.
    2.  Use a point file to gently clean contact surfaces.
    3.  Remove carbon, oxidation, and pitting.
    4.  Wipe and spray with electrical contact cleaner.
    5.  Allow cleaner to evaporate completely.
    6.  Visually inspect for cleanliness and damage.

## Inspecting and Adjusting Points After Cleaning

Cleaning the points is only part of the job. You also need to inspect them. Then, you must adjust the gap. This step is critical for proper engine timing. Without the correct gap, your engine will not run well.

First, make sure the points are at their widest opening. Rotate the engine again if needed. This puts the rubbing block squarely on a high point of the distributor cam. This position gives you the maximum point opening. This is where you measure the gap.

Take your feeler gauge. Select the correct blade thickness for your car's specifications. Your car's service manual lists this value. It is usually found under the hood or in a repair guide. Slide the feeler gauge blade between the contact points. It should slide in with a slight drag. It should not be too loose or too tight. If it is too loose or too tight, you need to adjust the gap.

To adjust the gap, loosen the screw that holds the fixed point arm. Some systems have an eccentric screw for adjustment. Others require prying the fixed arm. Carefully move the fixed arm. Do this until the feeler gauge blade slides with the correct drag. Once the gap is perfect, tighten the screw firmly. Double-check the gap after tightening. Sometimes tightening can change the gap slightly.

After setting the gap, remove the feeler gauge. Rotate the engine slowly. Watch the points open and close. They should operate smoothly. The gap should remain consistent at the widest opening. Next, reinstall the rotor and distributor cap. Reconnect the negative battery cable. Finally, start your car. Your engine should run smoother. It should idle better too. For precision, check and set the ignition timing with a timing light. This confirms everything is perfectly aligned. For a detailed breakdown of this specific process, refer to [how to clean points on a distributor](https://cosyhomecreation.com/how-to-clean-points-on-a-distributor).

*   **Adjustment Steps:**
    1.  Position the points at their widest opening.
    2.  Measure the gap with the specified feeler gauge.
    3.  Loosen the fixed point arm screw.
    4.  Adjust the arm for the correct feeler gauge drag.
    5.  Tighten the screw and recheck the gap.
    6.  Reassemble and check ignition timing with a timing light.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Cleaning Points

Cleaning points is a straightforward task. Still, some common mistakes can happen. Avoiding these errors ensures a better outcome. It also protects your engine. I want you to succeed in this task.

One common mistake is using the wrong abrasive. Do not use sandpaper or emery cloth. These materials leave gritty residue. This residue gets stuck between the points. It causes accelerated wear. It also creates a poor electrical connection. Always use a dedicated point file. This file is designed for precise cleaning. It removes material without leaving harmful particles.

Another error is not disconnecting the battery. This is a big safety risk. Working on electrical systems with power can cause shocks. It can also damage your car's electrical components. Always disconnect the negative battery cable first. This protects both you and your vehicle. Safety should always be your top priority.

People sometimes over-file the points. You only need to remove the dirty surface. Excessive filing removes too much metal. This reduces the lifespan of the points. It can also change their shape. A point file requires light pressure. Work until the surfaces are clean. Stop as soon as you see clean metal.

Not setting the gap correctly is another frequent mistake. An incorrect point gap throws off your engine's timing. This leads to poor performance. Your car might misfire or lose power. Always use a feeler gauge. Always check your car's specific gap setting. This ensures the correct timing for a smooth-running engine. Recheck the gap after tightening the hold-down screw. The act of tightening can sometimes slightly alter the setting.

Finally, skipping the electrical contact cleaner is a mistake. Filing creates fine dust. This dust, combined with old grease, acts as an insulator. The cleaner removes this debris. It leaves a perfectly clean, conductive surface. Ensure the cleaner fully evaporates before reassembly. This prevents moisture issues. Avoiding these simple mistakes helps you get great results.

*   **Mistakes to Avoid:**
    *   Using sandpaper or emery cloth.
    *   Forgetting to disconnect the battery.
    *   Over-filing the contact surfaces.
    *   Incorrectly setting the point gap.
    *   Skipping the use of electrical contact cleaner.

## When to Replace Instead of Just Clean Points

Cleaning points is a good maintenance step. However, cleaning is not always enough. Sometimes, the points are too worn. At that point, replacement becomes necessary. Knowing when to replace saves you time and frustration.

Look closely at the contact surfaces. If they have deep pits, cleaning will not fix them. Deep pits mean a lot of material is gone. The electrical current cannot flow smoothly across such surfaces. They will continue to cause misfires. Pitting often looks like small craters. If you see these, it is time for new points.

Also, check the rubbing block. This is the plastic or fiber part. It rides on the distributor cam. This block controls the opening and closing of the points. If the rubbing block is severely worn, it changes the point gap. Worn blocks look flattened or grooved. A worn block makes it impossible to set the gap correctly. New points come with a fresh rubbing block. This ensures accurate timing.

Consider the age and mileage of your current points. Points are wear items. They wear out over time, usually every 10,000 to 12,000 miles. If it has been a long time since they were last replaced, new ones are a good idea. Even if they don't look too bad, old points might be brittle. They might also be less efficient. Prevention is better than continuous repair.

If your car still has ignition issues after cleaning and gapping, replace the points. A weak spark, misfires, or hard starting can persist. This means the points might be beyond simple repair. Sometimes the spring tension weakens. This causes erratic point operation. New points restore proper spring tension. This provides a consistent spark. Replacing points also often includes replacing the condenser. A new condenser ensures the entire system works correctly. It is a good idea to replace both as a set.

*   **Signs for Replacement:**
    *   Deep pitting on contact surfaces.
    *   Severely worn or grooved rubbing block.
    *   High mileage or age of existing points.
    *   Persistent ignition problems after cleaning and adjustment.
    *   Weakened spring tension in the point arm.

## FAQ Section

### Q1: Why do distributor points get dirty?

Distributor points get dirty from normal engine operation. Electrical arcing occurs when the points open. This creates tiny bits of carbon. Also, oxidation forms on the metal surfaces. Engine oil vapors can also contribute to buildup. These deposits hinder the electrical flow. They cause a weak or inconsistent spark to the plugs. This reduces engine efficiency over time.

### Q2: How often should I clean my points?

You should clean points as part of a regular tune-up. This typically occurs every 10,000 to 12,000 miles. However, check your car's service manual for specific recommendations. If you notice signs of poor engine performance, check them sooner. These signs include misfires, rough idle, or hard starting. Regular inspection helps maintain your ignition system.

### Q3: Can I use sandpaper to clean points?

No, you should not use sandpaper to clean points. Sandpaper leaves abrasive particles. These particles can embed in the contact surfaces. This causes accelerated wear. It can also create a poor electrical connection. Always use a specialized point file. This tool is designed to clean the metal without leaving harmful residue. It ensures a smoother, cleaner surface.

### Q4: What happens if I don't clean my points?

If you do not clean your points, engine performance suffers. Dirty points create a weak spark. This leads to misfires and rough idling. Your engine might lose power. It can also be harder to start. Fuel efficiency often decreases. Over time, the points can fail completely. This leaves your car unable to run. Regular cleaning prevents these problems.

### Q5: What tools are essential for cleaning points?

Essential tools for cleaning points include a point file and electrical contact cleaner. You also need a feeler gauge to set the gap. Basic hand tools like screwdrivers and wrenches are useful for accessing the distributor. Safety gear like gloves and glasses is important. A timing light is helpful for final ignition timing adjustment.

### Q6: How long does it take to clean points?

Cleaning points is a quick job. It usually takes about 30 minutes to an hour. This time includes accessing the distributor and putting everything back. The actual cleaning and gapping process is fast. It is a small time investment. This small effort provides big benefits for your engine's health.
