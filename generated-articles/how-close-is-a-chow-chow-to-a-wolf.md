Despite their ancient lineage, Chow Chows are domesticated dogs. This means significant differences exist between them and true wolves. Domestication involves thousands of years of human interaction. ### The Impact of Domestication: A Gradual Evolution

Domestication has profoundly changed the canine species. The process began tens of thousands of years ago. Early wolves that tolerated humans gained access to food scraps. This began a co-evolutionary path. Over generations, these animals became less fearful of humans. They developed traits useful for living alongside people.

Chow Chows represent an early branch of this evolutionary tree. They were among the first dogs to be domesticated from wolves in Asia. This early separation means they kept many primitive traits. Their genetic makeup reflects this shorter period of divergence. Many modern dog breeds have gone through much more selective breeding. This breeding emphasized specific traits or looks. This further diversified their genetic code from wolves.

The domestication process made dogs more amenable to human companionship. It also reduced their fear response. It changed their digestive systems to handle different foods. These changes are evident even in primitive breeds like the Chow Chow. They can thrive in a human home. A wild wolf cannot. This gradual evolution is what makes a Chow Chow a dog, not a wolf.
