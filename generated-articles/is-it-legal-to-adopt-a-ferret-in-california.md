Ferrets are natural predators. They can hunt small animals like rodents, birds, and even ground-nesting birds. If they breed in the wild, they could severely disrupt local ecosystems. They might outcompete native predators for food. This poses a significant ecological risk to California's fragile wildlife.

Another major reason for the ban involves public health. Ferrets are susceptible to rabies. The state fears that a wild ferret population could become a rabies vector. Even though domestic ferrets can be vaccinated against rabies, California's law considers the risk of unvaccinated feral ferrets. They believe rabies could spread from ferrets to other animals or even humans. This concern is often highlighted by state agencies. They argue that this potential health risk outweighs the benefits of ferret ownership.
